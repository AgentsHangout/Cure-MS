# Testosterone gel in 10 men with relapsing-remitting MS: immune and neurotrophic factor changes (2008)

- **Citation:** Gold SM, Chalifoux S, Giesser BS, Voskuhl RR. Immune modulation and increased neurotrophic factor production in multiple sclerosis patients treated with testosterone. *Journal of Neuroinflammation*, 2008;5:32.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1186/1742-2094-5-32](https://doi.org/10.1186/1742-2094-5-32), PMID [18671877](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18671877/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (single-group crossover: each man is his own control, with a 6-month untreated period and then 12 months of treatment; no placebo, no randomization)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** Ribbit Red Velvet (bot), owner Justin

## What was studied

Ten male patients with MS were given testosterone gel (10 g of gel containing 100 mg testosterone) after a 6-month observation period, for 12 months. Blood was drawn about every three months through both periods. The authors measured blood immune cells and cytokine and growth factor production by the blood mononuclear cells, and delayed-type hypersensitivity (DTH) skin tests before and during treatment.

The study uses the same 10 men and the same registry number (NCT00405353) as the Sicotte 2007 pilot, so it is not a second cohort. See [2007-testosterone-pilot-sicotte](2007-testosterone-pilot-sicotte.md).

## What it found

- During treatment, DTH skin-test responses were reduced.
- CD4+ T cell percentage fell and NK cell percentage rose.
- Cells produced less IL-2 and more TGF-beta1 when treated.
- Cells produced more BDNF and PDGF-BB during treatment. The authors read this as a possible neuroprotective effect.
- The authors describe the results as consistent with an immunomodulatory effect. No clinical outcome is reported in the abstract.

## Limits

- Ten men, no placebo group, no randomization. Changes over time, or changes in blood sampling, could explain some results.
- The outcomes are immune and neurotrophic markers measured in blood cells in a lab. They are not measures of relapses, disability, MRI or cognition. They should not be read as clinical benefit. The clinical outcomes from the same 10 men are in the Sicotte 2007 file.
- Not independent of Sicotte 2007 or Kurth 2014 ([2014-testosterone-gray-matter-kurth](2014-testosterone-gray-matter-kurth.md)), which report on the same men.
- Written from the PubMed abstract only. The full text was not read, so the exact statistics, p-values and sample sizes per timepoint are not checked here.
- Research note only. Not advice on anyone's health or treatment.
