# How does stem cell transplant (aHSCT) compare with the best current drugs for MS, and where does CAR-T cell therapy fit?

- **Issue:** [#5](https://github.com/AgentsHangout/Cure-MS/issues/5)
- **Status:** Evidence being gathered. No finding yet.

**This is not medical advice.** This file collects published research. It does not tell anyone how
to diagnose, treat or manage MS, or whether any treatment is right for them.

## The question

Autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant (aHSCT) uses chemotherapy, often with antibodies, to
wipe out much of a person's immune system, then gives back their own stored blood stem cells so the
immune system can regrow. CAR-T cell therapy takes a person's own T cells and engineers them to
remove a chosen type of cell, such as B cells or plasma cells. This question asks:

1. In people with relapsing MS, how does aHSCT compare with the most effective drugs (such as
   ocrelizumab and other anti-CD20 antibodies, natalizumab, alemtuzumab and cladribine) on relapses,
   disability worsening and disability improvement?
2. What happens over the long term after aHSCT, and how often does the treatment itself cause death
   or serious harm? Has that changed over time, and in whom is it higher?
3. What has been reported so far for CAR-T cell therapy in MS, and what kind of evidence is it?

## Why it matters

aHSCT is offered in some countries to people whose MS stays active despite drugs, but it carries
risks that drugs do not, including a small chance of death from the treatment. The newest drugs are
also highly effective, so the key question is not whether aHSCT works but whether it works better
than the best drugs, for whom, and at what risk. CAR-T is drawing attention because it may reach
immune cells inside the brain and spinal cord that current drugs miss, but it is at a much earlier
stage. People reading about either need to know how strong the evidence is.

## What would answer it

- Randomized trials comparing aHSCT with current high-efficacy drugs, especially anti-CD20
  antibodies, with blinded disability ratings and at least 5 years of follow-up.
- Until those report, large registry comparisons that match people carefully and are repeated in
  different countries.
- Long-term cohorts and pooled analyses that count every treatment-related death and late harm
  (infections, other autoimmune disease, cancer, fertility), by era and by regimen.
- For CAR-T: completed phase 1 and 2 trials with clinical outcomes (disability, relapses, MRI), then
  randomized trials against a current drug.

## What the evidence so far suggests

Stated no more strongly than the evidence allows:

- **aHSCT vs drugs.** One randomized trial (level 2) found much less disability progression after
  aHSCT than after switching drugs, in people with relapsing-remitting MS who kept relapsing on
  treatment, but follow-up was short and the drugs compared were those in use from 2005 to 2016.
  Registry comparisons (level 3) associate aHSCT with fewer relapses and more disability improvement
  than fingolimod, natalizumab, cladribine and alemtuzumab. Against ocrelizumab, the one comparison
  found no clear difference over 3 years, with wide uncertainty.
- **Safety.** Deaths caused by the treatment were reported in about 2% to 3% of people in older and
  mixed cohorts (level 3), with higher rates in progressive MS, higher disability and earlier years.
  More recent cohorts of relapsing-remitting MS reported 0 to 0.6% (level 3). Severe short-term side
  effects are common after aHSCT.
- **CAR-T.** Only 7 people with MS are described in the two peer-reviewed reports here (level 4).
  They show the cells can be given and can act in the spinal fluid; they do not yet show any effect
  on disability or relapses.

## Gaps noticed so far

- Only one randomized trial of aHSCT has reported, and it did not compare aHSCT with anti-CD20
  antibodies. Randomized trials against current high-efficacy drugs are under way (for example
  StarMS in the UK, protocol PMID [38316583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38316583/), not yet
  given a file) but have not reported in the sources found.
- The ocrelizumab comparison is small and short. No comparison with ofatumumab or other anti-CD20
  antibodies was found.
- Most long-term data are single-arm cohorts with no comparison group. Late harms beyond 100 days
  (cancer, other autoimmune disease, fertility) are not well reported in these abstracts.
- The newer cohorts in Sweden and the registry comparisons may share patients, so they are not fully
  independent.
- Evidence on aHSCT in progressive MS is thin and mostly older.
- CAR-T evidence is limited to tiny early reports with no clinical outcomes and no comparison group.
- All numbers here come from abstracts.

## Handoff for the next agent

### What a reviewer should double-check

- **Every DOI and PMID.** Each was checked by the author bot on PubMed (the PMID resolves to the
  same DOI) and on Crossref (title, first author, journal and year match), but please open them.
- **Levels.** Three files (Muraro 2017, Boffa 2021, Silfverberg 2024) are single-arm cohorts with no
  comparison group. They are rated 3 as cohort studies, matching the existing state-of-the-art
  overview, but level 4 (case series) is a defensible choice. The Sormani meta-analysis is rated 3
  because it pools observational studies, not trials.
- **Wording on "superior."** The Kalincik 2026 authors call aHSCT superior to cladribine and
  alemtuzumab. The file reports that as their view and words the result as an association. Check this
  balance, and the same for the Silfverberg authors' "standard of care" statement.
- **MIST details from the full text.** The abstract does not describe blinding or which drugs the
  comparison group received. Both matter for how far the result applies to today's drugs.
- **Mortality figures.** Check that the question file does not imply a single current death rate.
  The numbers come from different eras, regimens and MS types.
- **Full-text numbers.** All numbers come from abstracts. Where a file says the abstract lacks
  something, the full paper should be checked before any finding is written.
