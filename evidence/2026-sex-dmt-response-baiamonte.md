# Sex differences in MS response to disease-modifying drugs, Palermo registry (abstract only)

- **Citation:** Baiamonte L, Enea M, Vaccaro M, Ragonese P, Salemi G. Sex differences in multiple sclerosis treatment with disease modifying drugs: a step towards gender neurology. *Mult Scler Relat Disord*. 2026;105:106869. Epub 2025 Nov 23.
- **DOI or PMID:** doi:10.1016/j.msard.2025.106869 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2025.106869); PMID 41349231 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41349231/).
- **Evidence level:** 3 (cohort study, registry-based, observational)

## What was studied

People with relapsing-remitting MS or clinically isolated syndrome from a registry at one reference center in Palermo, Italy. The registry had 1,805 records, but only some drugs had enough patients to analyze: dimethyl fumarate, glatiramer acetate, beta interferon, and partly natalizumab and S1PR modulators. Cox multivariable survival models were used to compare women and men on confirmed disease progression, first relapse, MRI active lesions, and withdrawal due to adverse events. Annualized relapse risk was an exploratory analysis.

## What it found

- No significant sex differences were found for the efficacy endpoints (confirmed progression, first relapse, MRI active lesions).
- For glatiramer acetate, men had a lower risk of withdrawal due to adverse events than women: HR 0.32, 95% CI 0.16 to 0.65, p = 0.002. The direction is men lower, women higher.
- Dyspnea was the most common reason for stopping in both sexes.

The authors conclude that differences in response to these drugs between men and women are limited.

## Limits

This is a single-center registry study, so the results are associations and not causes. The abstract says the sample was small, which is why most drugs, especially the high-efficacy ones, could not be analyzed. The glatiramer result is one drug and one outcome, and it could be a chance finding. This file is based on the PubMed abstract only; the full text was not read. Nothing here is advice about treatment, and it is not a comment on anyone's own care.
