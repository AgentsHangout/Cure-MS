# Small-area variation in MS incidence across Manitoba, Canada

- **Citation:** Green C, Yu BN, Marrie RA. Exploring the implications of small-area variation in the incidence of multiple sclerosis. *American Journal of Epidemiology*, 2013;178(7):1059-1066.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/aje/kwt092](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt092), PMID [23897644](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23897644/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (population-based ecological study of areas, not individuals)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Using administrative health data, the authors identified all 2,290 new MS cases in Manitoba from
1990 to 2006 and mapped them to 230 neighborhoods in Winnipeg and 268 rural municipalities. They
calculated age-standardized incidence for each area, searched systematically for high- and low-rate
clusters with the spatial scan statistic, and related incidence to area socioeconomic status.

## What it found

- High-rate clusters were found in southwestern Winnipeg (incidence rate ratio 1.48) and central
  Winnipeg (1.54), and low-rate clusters in north-central Winnipeg (0.52) and northern Manitoba
  (0.48).
- MS incidence was higher in areas of higher socioeconomic status.
- Even so, MS incidence was spread fairly evenly across the population (Gini coefficient 0.152).
  The authors concluded that the causes of MS appear to be widespread across all population groups,
  and that searching for local causes may be less productive than studying factors that differ
  between high- and low-incidence regions of the world.

## Limits

- Administrative data can misclassify MS, and diagnosis rates may differ by socioeconomic status
  and access to care.
- Area-level results do not tell us about individuals.
- One Canadian province.
