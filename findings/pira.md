# Does PIRA reflect real worsening, and how much does the definition change the count?

- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Overall strength:** 3 (observational registry and cohort studies, a systematic review of observational studies, and one reanalysis of two trials; no trial uses PIRA as its main outcome)
- **Last reviewed:** 11/25/2026

## In short

Confirmed worsening that falls outside a study's relapse windows usually does not reverse: in the MSBase registry, 83.5% of PIRA events with at least 5 years of follow-up had no later improvement. How many PIRA events get counted depends a lot on the definition, though. Across 360 definitions tested in 33,303 people, the rate ranged from 0.141 to 0.658 events per patient-decade, mostly driven by the confirmation period. Whether PIRA is separate from inflammation is still open: in a reanalysis of two natalizumab trials, many PIRA events came with new MRI activity.

## Where it disagrees, and what's missing

- The definition changes the answer more than the disease does. A 4.7-fold spread in incidence from definition choice alone means PIRA percentages from different studies (about 5% per year in the 2023 review, 72% of worsening in the Italian registry, 6.8% over 2 years in the trials) cannot be compared directly.
- The registry studies (level 3) follow people for years, while the trial reanalysis has 2 years and few events. Persistence is high in the registry, but the trial data show improvement events outnumbering PIRA, which is what measurement noise would look like. The two are not the same population or measure.
- The Müller 2025 persistence figure is a registry measure of whether a worsening stayed. It does not tell us whether the worsening came from inflammation or from loss of nerve tissue. Strijbis et al. conclude that PIRA and RAW as currently defined do not reliably separate those two causes.
- Portaccio's link between PIRA and moving to secondary progressive MS is partly built into how that move is defined, so it is not independent evidence.
- Missing: a trial using PIRA with a harmonized definition as its main outcome, and cohorts that test whether the harmonized definition still shows persistence once MRI activity is taken into account.

## What would change this

A prospective cohort or trial using the harmonized definition that shows PIRA events persisting in people with no MRI activity would make a stronger case that PIRA reflects worsening independent of inflammation. Confirmation that the Strijbis 6.8% and 0.9% figures are EDSS-only, and a resolved MRI figure (42.2% vs 42.6%), would change how much weight the trial reanalysis carries.
