# Blood neurofilament light in two fingolimod trials: activity and treatment response

- **Citation:** Kuhle J, Kropshofer H, Haering DA, et al. Blood neurofilament light chain as a biomarker of MS disease activity and treatment response. *Neurology*, 2019;92(10):e1007-e1015.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000007032](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000007032), PMID [30737333](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30737333/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 for the effect of fingolimod on NfL levels, which compares randomized groups. The links between NfL and later activity are post hoc analyses of trial participants and read as level 3.
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Blood neurofilament light (NfL) was measured in stored samples from 589 people with
relapsing-remitting MS in two phase 3 trials: FREEDOMS (fingolimod vs placebo) and TRANSFORMS
(fingolimod vs interferon beta-1a), plus 35 healthy controls. NfL was compared with relapses, MRI
lesions, brain volume loss and disability worsening.

## What it found

- At the start, NfL was higher in people with MS than in controls (30.5 and 27.0 vs 16.9 pg/mL) and
  went with more T2 lesions and more gadolinium-enhancing lesions.
- People with high vs low NfL at the start had more new or enlarging T2 lesions (ratio 2.64), more
  relapses (rate ratio 2.53) and more brain volume loss (difference -0.78%). The link with confirmed
  disability worsening (hazard ratio 1.94, 95% CI 0.97-3.87) was not statistically significant.
- Fingolimod lowered NfL compared with placebo and with interferon by 6 months (ratios 0.73 and
  0.789), and the difference lasted to the end of the trials (ratios 0.628 and 0.794).

## Limits

- The trials randomized people to drugs, not to NfL-guided care. This shows that an effective drug
  lowers NfL, not that measuring NfL and acting on it improves outcomes.
- Post hoc analysis of a subset of trial participants, with a small control group (35 people).
- Trial populations are selected and may differ from people seen in routine care.
- Funding and conflicts of interest are not given in the abstract and should be checked in the full
  paper.
- All numbers come from the abstract.
