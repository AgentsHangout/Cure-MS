# Domperidone add-on, lesion repair pilot RCT (Zhang 2024)

- **Citation:** Zhang Y, Liu WQ, Hosseinpour Z, Pike GB, Cerchiaro G, Greenfield J, Yong VW, Metz LM. Feasibility study to assess lesion repair in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis: A randomized controlled pilot clinical trial of domperidone add-on treatment. *Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*, 2024;85:105525.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2024.105525](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2024.105525), PMID [38461731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38461731/). Trial registration: [NCT02493049](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02493049).
- **Evidence level:** 2. It is a randomized controlled trial, but a small single-site pilot with a feasibility aim, so its results are hypothesis-generating only.
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)

## What was studied

A single-site randomized pilot in people with relapsing-remitting MS who were already on a disease-modifying treatment and had at least one gadolinium-enhancing lesion on clinical MRI (aged 18 to 60). Recruitment ran from November 2015 to January 2019. Participants were randomized 2:1 to oral domperidone 10 mg three times a day for 16 weeks, or to no add-on treatment, with MRI at 16 and 32 weeks. The main aims were feasibility of the trial model and MRI estimates of lesion repair (texture analysis, magnetization transfer imaging and diffusion tensor imaging). Serum prolactin was also measured.

## What it found

- Of 237 people screened, 17 were randomized: 12 to domperidone and 5 to control. All 17 completed the study.
- The feasibility target was not met. The trial aimed to recruit 24 people within 36 months, and the authors conclude the model was not feasible.
- No MRI outcome differed significantly between groups. Both groups improved on MRI texture and fractional anisotropy at 32 weeks, and the domperidone group showed additional recovery on texture and fractional anisotropy at 16 weeks.
- Seven of the 12 domperidone participants had serum prolactin at least four times the normal level. The abstract reports no serious adverse events.
- The authors write that prolactin is safe and may hold promise as a remyelination therapy. That is their hypothesis, not a result this trial shows.

## Limits

- Only 17 people, and only 5 in the control group, so the study was not powered to detect a difference in MRI measures.
- No clinical outcome is reported as a finding: no disability, walking, vision or cognition result is given. The MRI measures used are not shown to predict changes people feel.
- Participants were already on a disease-modifying treatment and were selected for enhancing lesions, so the results do not apply to other stages of MS, including progressive MS.
- Seven of 12 treated participants had prolactin at least four times normal. The trial ran for 16 weeks, so the abstract says nothing about longer use.
