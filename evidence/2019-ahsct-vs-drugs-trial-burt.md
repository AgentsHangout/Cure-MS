# MIST: stem cell transplant vs switching drugs in relapsing-remitting MS

- **Citation:** Burt RK, Balabanov R, Burman J, et al. Effect of Nonmyeloablative Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation vs Continued Disease-Modifying Therapy on Disease Progression in Patients With Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis: A Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA*, 2019;321(2):165-174.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/jama.2018.18743](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.18743), PMID [30644983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30644983/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A randomized trial (MIST, NCT00273364) at 4 centers in the US, Europe and South America, enrolling
from 2005 to 2016. It included 110 people with relapsing-remitting MS who had at least 2 relapses in
the prior year while on a disease-modifying drug, and an EDSS disability score of 2.0 to 6.0. They
were randomly assigned to:

- autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant (aHSCT) with cyclophosphamide and antithymocyte
  globulin (55 people), or
- a disease-modifying drug of higher efficacy or a different class than the one taken in the prior
  year (55 people).

The main outcome was disability progression: a rise in EDSS of at least 1.0 point after at least 1
year, confirmed on 2 visits 6 months apart.

## What it found

- In a trial of 110 people, disability progressed in 3 people after aHSCT and in 34 people in the
  drug group (hazard ratio 0.07, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.24).
- In the drug group, median time to progression was 24 months. Too few people progressed after aHSCT
  to calculate a median.
- In the first year, mean EDSS fell from 3.38 to 2.36 after aHSCT and rose from 3.31 to 3.98 in the
  drug group (difference -1.7 points).
- There were no deaths, and no one given aHSCT had grade 4 toxicity outside the blood system (such as
  heart attack or sepsis).
- The authors call it a preliminary study and say the results need to be repeated, with long-term
  outcomes and safety assessed.

## Limits

- Small trial. 103 of 110 stayed in the trial, 98 were assessed at 1 year, and only 23 were followed
  every year for 5 years (median follow-up 2 years).
- The abstract does not describe blinding. A transplant cannot easily be hidden from participants or
  doctors, which can affect a rated outcome like EDSS. Check the full text.
- The comparison arm was a switch to another drug chosen at the time (2005 to 2016). The abstract
  does not say which drugs were used, so it is not clear how many people got the high-efficacy drugs
  used today, such as ocrelizumab. Check the full text.
- Only people with frequent relapses on treatment and moderate disability were included, so it says
  nothing about progressive MS or milder disease.
- Funding is not described in the abstract.
