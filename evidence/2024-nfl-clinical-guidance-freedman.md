# Expert guidance on using neurofilament light in MS care

- **Citation:** Freedman MS, Gnanapavan S, Booth RA, et al. Guidance for use of neurofilament light chain as a cerebrospinal fluid and blood biomarker in multiple sclerosis management. *eBioMedicine*, 2024;101:104970.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.ebiom.2024.104970](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2024.104970), PMID [38354532](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38354532/)
- **Evidence level:** 6 (expert review and guidance; the abstract does not describe a systematic search)
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A review by nine authors on how neurofilament light (NfL), measured in spinal fluid or blood, could
be used in managing MS. It is a guidance paper, not a new study.

## What it found

The authors propose that:

- There is now enough evidence to use serum NfL alongside MRI and clinical measures as a tool when
  making decisions about MS care.
- Serum NfL is less invasive and cheaper than MRI and can be repeated often to follow activity and
  treatment effect. Repeated values can be compared with a person's own baseline to flag silent
  activity, relapse risk and new gadolinium-enhancing lesions.
- Serum NfL does not replace MRI, which shows where lesions are and how old they are.
- More work is needed on differences between lab platforms (absolute values) and on reference
  ranges before results can be read reliably.

## Limits

- Expert opinion. It does not report a trial showing that NfL-guided care improves outcomes, and the
  abstract does not cite one.
- The abstract does not describe how the evidence was searched or graded.
- Conflicts of interest are not given in the abstract and should be checked in the full paper.
