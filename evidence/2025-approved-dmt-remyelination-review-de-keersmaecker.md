# Systematic review of whether approved MS treatments promote remyelination

- **Citation:** De Keersmaecker AV, van Doninck E, Wens I, et al. Recent Advances in Interventions Targeting Remyelination and a Systematic Review of Remyelinating Effects of Approved Disease-Modifying Treatments for Multiple Sclerosis. *European Journal of Neurology*, 2025;32(11):e70397.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1111/ene.70397](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.70397), PMID [41216863](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41216863/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a systematic review without meta-analysis; most included studies are animal, cell or non-randomized, with only 3 randomized trials)
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A systematic review, registered in advance on PROSPERO and reported with the "Synthesis without
meta-analysis" (SWiM) guideline. It asked which FDA- or EMA-approved disease-modifying treatments
show evidence of promoting remyelination, to inform combinations with future repair drugs. It also
gives a narrative overview of newer remyelination approaches.

## What it found

- 57 preclinical articles, 3 randomized trials, 29 non-randomized clinical studies and 8 reviews
  were included.
- In animal and cell studies (level 5), convincing evidence of remyelination was found only for
  glatiramer acetate, teriflunomide, fingolimod, siponimod, ponesimod and alemtuzumab.
- Clinical studies using imaging and nerve-signal measures of myelin repair showed promising results
  for glatiramer acetate, fingolimod, siponimod, natalizumab, alemtuzumab and ocrelizumab.
- The authors found insufficient proof that these measures change because of remyelination alone.
  They suggest future trials combine several measures into a composite endpoint.

## Limits

- Most of the evidence is preclinical or non-randomized. Only 3 randomized trials were included.
- No meta-analysis, so there is no pooled estimate of effect.
- In people, the signals come from surrogate measures that the authors themselves say may not
  reflect remyelination. Less inflammation or less tissue loss could also change them.
- The review asks about approved treatments only, not new repair drugs like those in this
  question's trial table.
- Numbers come from the abstract; the open-access full text (PMC12603786) was not read.
