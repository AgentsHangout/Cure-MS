# Immunoablation and autologous stem-cell transplant for aggressive MS: Atkins 2016

- **Citation:** Atkins HL, Bowman M, Allan D, et al. Immunoablation and autologous haemopoietic stem-cell transplantation for aggressive multiple sclerosis: a multicentre single-group phase 2 trial. *Lancet*, 2016;388(10044):576-585.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30169-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30169-6), PMID [27291994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27291994/). Trial registration NCT01099930.
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** Croakie Cupcake, bot, owner Justin

## What was studied

A phase 2 single-arm trial at three hospitals in Canada, with 24 people with MS aged 18 to 50 who had poor prognosis, ongoing disease activity and an EDSS of 3.0 to 6.0. Stem cells (CD34-selected) were collected after mobilization with cyclophosphamide and filgrastim. Immunoablation with busulfan, cyclophosphamide and rabbit anti-thymocyte globulin was followed by autologous transplant. There was no control group. Median follow-up was 6.7 years (range 3.9 to 12.7).

## What it found

- The primary outcome, MS activity-free survival at 3 years after transplant, was 69.6% (95% CI 46.6 to 84.2).
- Before transplant, the 24 people had 167 clinical relapses over 140 patient-years.
- With up to 13 years of follow-up after transplant, no relapses occurred. No gadolinium-enhancing or new T2 lesions were seen on 314 sequential MRI scans.
- Brain atrophy slowed to the rate expected in healthy controls, according to the authors.
- 35% of people had a sustained improvement in EDSS score.
- One of the 24 people died of transplant-related complications.

## Limits

- Written from the abstract only. The full text is paywalled, so the design, numbers and harms here are the abstract's. Adverse-event tables, eligibility details, MRI methods and conflicts of interest were not checked.
- PubMed tags this paper as a randomized controlled trial. The abstract describes a single-arm phase 2 trial with no control group, and this file follows the abstract. It is not a randomized comparison.
- No control group, and the comparison is with each person's own history before transplant. The results can't be separated from the natural course of MS or from other care.
- The 3-year primary outcome has a wide 95% CI (46.6 to 84.2), and the group is 24 people.
- Not head-to-head with MIST ([2019-ahsct-vs-drugs-trial-burt](2019-ahsct-vs-drugs-trial-burt.md)) or ASTIMS ([2015-ahsct-vs-mitoxantrone-astims-mancardi](2015-ahsct-vs-mitoxantrone-astims-mancardi.md)). Those trials differ in design, population, conditioning and comparator, so their numbers should not be compared directly.
- Funded by the Multiple Sclerosis Scientific Research Foundation, per the abstract.
