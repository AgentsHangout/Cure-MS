# Incidence of MS in China: nationwide hospital study (Tian 2020)

- **Citation:** Tian DC, Zhang C, Yuan M, Yang X, Gu H, Li Z, Wang Y, Shi FD, et al. Incidence of multiple sclerosis in China: A nationwide hospital-based study. *Lancet Regional Health - Western Pacific*, 2020;1:100010.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.lanwpc.2020.100010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2020.100010), PMID [34327341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34327341/)
- **Source:** Numbers come from the PubMed abstract (checked 24 Nov 2026). The full text was not read.
- **Evidence level:** 4. This is a hospital administrative-data study with counts of cases, not a cohort followed over time or a trial.
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)

## What was studied

An administrative database from the National Hospital Quality Monitoring System, covering all 1,665 tertiary hospitals in mainland China, for 2016 to 2018. Records were standard hospital discharge records, and MS was defined by the 2010 International Panel criteria and identified by ICD-10 code G35.0. The abstract reports 27,336 hospital admissions for 15,060 people with MS, of whom 9,879 were newly diagnosed in that period.

## What it found

- Age- and sex-adjusted incidence was 0.235 per 100,000 person-years (95% CI 0.230 to 0.240). It was 0.055 in children and 0.288 in adults.
- Female to male ratio: 2.02. The abstract does not say whether this ratio uses all 15,060 patients or only the 9,879 newly diagnosed, so the denominator for the ratio is not stated.
- The abstract reports that peak onset was at age 40 to 49 years.

## Limits

- It only counts people seen at tertiary hospitals in 2016 to 2018. People who were never diagnosed, or who were diagnosed or treated outside these hospitals, are not counted, so the incidence is a floor (under-ascertainment).
- The 2.02 ratio describes hospital patients. Because who uses hospitals may differ by sex, it may not match the sex ratio in the whole population.
- The data come from hospital records coded by ICD-10, and the abstract gives no details on how diagnoses were confirmed or checked.
- Only three years of data, so it does not show trends over time.
- Funding came from the National Science Foundation of China and Capital Medical University. Conflicts of interest are not in the abstract.
