# Tehran MS sex ratio and prevalence, 1999-2018 (registry study)

- **Citation:** Almasi-Hashiani A, Sahraian MA, Eskandarieh S. Evidence of an increased prevalence of multiple sclerosis: a population-based study of Tehran registry during 1999-2018. BMC Neurology, 2020.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI [10.1186/s12883-020-01747-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12883-020-01747-8); PMID [32359352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32359352/). Checked in PubMed (efetch) and Crossref (title, all three authors, journal, 2020).
- **Evidence level:** 4 (population registry, descriptive prevalence study with no comparison group)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md), under the search on sex ratio trends outside Europe
- **Added by:** Tadpole Tiramisu, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

People registered with the Iranian MS Society (IMSS) in Tehran province, 1999 to 2018: 21,580 cases, 16,187 women and 5,393 men, mean age at onset 28.8 years. Diagnosis was confirmed by neurologists using "the latest version of the McDonald criteria"; the version is not named in the text (the reference given is Polman 2005). Rates use population data from the Statistical Centre of Iran (censuses of 2006, 2011 and 2016; other years estimated) and are age-standardized. The paper is about prevalence, meaning people living with MS in a given year, not new cases.

## What it found

In the abstract, the age-standardized female-to-male ratio "ranged from 3.7 (in 2010) to 2.06 (in 2017)", and was 2.14 in 2018. The Discussion gives 3.20 in 2006 and 2.14 in 2018. A Poisson model found a significant change over time in the sex ratio (p=0.001). Age-standardized prevalence rose from 73.7 per 100,000 in 2006 to 137.6 in 2018 (67.9 in men and 207.3 in women in 2018).

## Limits

- Prevalence only. A falling ratio of people living with MS can reflect changes in survival and registration as well as in new cases. No incidence ratio is given here.
- Registry of IMSS members. People who register may not represent everyone with MS in Tehran.
- Most years' denominators are estimated from census figures, so the yearly ratios depend on those estimates.
- The paper's explanations (more men in the workforce, more outdoor air pollution exposure) are not tested in this study.
- The paper's own numbers are not fully consistent: the Discussion's 3.20 for 2006 does not appear in the abstract.
- A related Tehran population-based study, [Kamali et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.18502/ijph.v51i5.9439) (PMC9643219, Iranian Journal of Public Health, vol 51 issue 5), says the female-to-male ratio went "from 3; 1 in 2006 into 2.2:1 in 2017" among 11,152 patients evaluated 2006 to 2017. The study's stated aim is incidence and the female-to-male ratio (its title is "Increasing Incidence and Male to Female Sex Ratio of Multiple Sclerosis in Tehran, Iran: A Population-Based Study"); it does not separate the ratio into prevalence or incidence in the text read. The 2.2 figure is from that study. Its denominators were not checked here, and its 2017 value differs from this paper's abstract value of 2.06. The two are not combined.
- A different Tehran registry analysis of prevalence and incidence, [Eskandarieh et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5442284/) (PMID 28560202, 1991-2014), reports a rising female-to-male ratio over its period (mean 3.18:1; 3.07 in 2013 and 2.61 in 2014). Another, [Mohebi et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31679345/) (PMID 31679345, 1989-2016), reports that the female-to-male ratio was "generally 3.06:1, showing a general decreasing trend" in its abstract. These are different periods and measures. They are not combined here, and the direction of the Tehran ratio across sources is not settled.
- Research note only. This describes one registry's counts and does not show why the ratio changed.
