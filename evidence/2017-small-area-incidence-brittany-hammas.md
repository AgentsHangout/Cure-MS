# Small-area MS incidence in Brittany: no significant cluster

- **Citation:** Hammas K, Yaouanq J, Lannes M, et al. Small-area distribution of multiple sclerosis incidence in western France: in search of environmental triggers. *International Journal of Health Geographics*, 2017;16(1):35.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1186/s12942-017-0108-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12942-017-0108-6), PMID [28934989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28934989/)
- **Evidence level:** 4
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A population-based study of MS onset in the French region of Brittany from 2000 to 2004. Each
person's municipality at onset was geocoded, and a spatial scan statistic looked for areas with
unusually high or low incidence, adjusted for socioeconomic deprivation. Sensitivity analyses
focused on relapsing-remitting MS and on residence within a year of onset.

## What it found

- 848 people had MS onset in the period, a crude yearly incidence of 5.8 per 100,000.
- No significant cluster was found in the main analysis (p ≥ 0.56) or in the sensitivity analyses
  (p ≥ 0.16).
- The authors conclude that no local environmental trigger appears to act at the small-area level
  in Brittany.

## Limits

- Residence at onset may not reflect where people lived when a trigger acted, which may be years
  earlier (compare the adolescent-residence finding in
  [1991-adolescent-residence-clustering-hordaland-riise](1991-adolescent-residence-clustering-hordaland-riise.md)).
- A null result in one region and five years does not rule out small or very local effects.
- An example of an investigation that found no excess; the clustering question notes that such
  results are probably under-published.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
