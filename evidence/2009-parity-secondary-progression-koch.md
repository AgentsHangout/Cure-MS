# Number of births and secondary progression (Dutch hospital cohort)

- **Citation:** Koch M, Uyttenboogaart M, Heersema D, et al. Parity and secondary progression in multiple sclerosis. *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry*, 2009;80(6):676-678.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1136/jnnp.2008.160911](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.2008.160911), PMID [19448093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19448093/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A hospital-based cohort of 277 women with MS. The authors tested whether the number of births was
linked to secondary progression, using multivariable logistic regression adjusted for possible
confounders.

## What it found

- Number of births was not independently associated with secondary progression.
- Longer disease duration (OR 1.05 per year, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.09) was associated with more secondary
  progression, and use of immunomodulatory treatment (OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.08 to 0.65) with less.

## Limits

- 277 women in one hospital, analyzed cross-sectionally with logistic regression rather than
  time-to-event methods, so it may miss an effect.
- "Not independently associated" is not proof of no effect.
- The authors call for larger population-based studies.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
