# Anti-EBV antibodies during ocrelizumab treatment and MS outcomes

- **Citation:** Esposito A, Corsaro L, Delle Cave I, et al. Anti-EBV antibody reduction during ocrelizumab treatment is not associated with multiple sclerosis outcomes. *Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*, 2025;104:106830.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2025.106830](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2025.106830), PMID [41202469](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41202469/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

58 people with MS treated with ocrelizumab, an anti-CD20 drug, had blood antibody levels measured
before treatment and again after a mean of 4.8 years (SD 1.5). The authors measured total IgG and IgG
against EBV and against cytomegalovirus (CMV), another herpesvirus. They then tested whether changes
in anti-EBV IgG were linked to relapses, MRI activity, EDSS worsening or these combined.

## What it found

- Anti-EBV IgG fell by a mean of 8.2% (p = 0.03). This was similar to the fall in total IgG (8.8%,
  p < 0.01) and in anti-CMV IgG (7.8%, p < 0.01).
- Changes in anti-EBV IgG were not significantly associated with any of the MS outcomes tested.

## Limits

- Antibody levels are a sign of past and ongoing immune response to EBV, not a direct measure of
  how active the virus is. The study did not measure EBV DNA in blood or saliva.
- The fall in anti-EBV IgG matched the fall in total IgG and anti-CMV IgG, so it looks like a general
  effect of the drug on antibodies, not something specific to EBV.
- 58 people with no untreated comparison group. "No significant association" in a group this size
  does not show that there is no link.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
