# Updated meta-analysis of latitude and MS prevalence

- **Citation:** Simpson S Jr, Wang W, Otahal P, Blizzard L, van der Mei IAF, Taylor BV. Latitude continues to be significantly associated with the prevalence of multiple sclerosis: an updated meta-analysis. *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry*, 2019;90(11):1193-1200.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1136/jnnp-2018-320189](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2018-320189), PMID [31217172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31217172/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (meta-regression of prevalence surveys, which are cross-sectional; it compares places, not people)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors searched Embase, Web of Knowledge and PubMed through December 2018 and added 94 new
studies (230 prevalence points) to their 2011 analysis, for 880 prevalence points in all. Where
possible, prevalence was age- and sex-standardized to the 2009 European population and adjusted
for survey year and how cases were found. They used meta-regression to test the link with
latitude.

## What it found

- MS prevalence rose with distance from the equator: by 5.27 per 100,000 people per degree of
  latitude (time-corrected), and 4.34 per 100,000 after age-standardization. The link held after
  adjusting for how cases were found.
- The age-standardized gradient was steeper than in the authors' 2011 analysis.
- Some regions (Scandinavia, and the Atlantic Coast/Central Europe) showed changes in the gradient
  over time.
- The authors suggest that modifiable environmental factors such as sun exposure remain associated
  with MS risk. That is their interpretation.

## Limits

- An ecological comparison of places: it cannot show that a person's latitude, or sunlight, changes
  their risk. Ancestry, genes, diet, infections and access to diagnosis all vary with latitude.
- Prevalence surveys differ in methods and quality, and prevalence depends on survival and
  diagnosis, not only on how many people develop MS.
