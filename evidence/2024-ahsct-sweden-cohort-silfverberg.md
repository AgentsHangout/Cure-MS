# Stem cell transplant for relapsing-remitting MS in routine care in Sweden

- **Citation:** Silfverberg T, Zjukovskaja C, Ljungman P, et al. Haematopoietic stem cell transplantation for treatment of relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis in Sweden: an observational cohort study. *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery and Psychiatry*, 2024;95(2):125-133.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1136/jnnp-2023-331864](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2023-331864), PMID [37748927](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37748927/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

An observational cohort of people with relapsing-remitting MS who had autologous hematopoietic stem
cell transplant (aHSCT) in routine care in Sweden before 2020. Of 231 people assessed, 174 were
included. Effectiveness came from the Swedish MS registry (data collected as care went on, analyzed
afterwards). Safety came from patient records for the 100 days after transplant. Median follow-up was
5.5 years. There was no comparison group.

## What it found

- An estimated 73% (95% CI 66% to 81%) had no evidence of disease activity at 5 years, and 65% (95%
  CI 57% to 75%) at 10 years.
- Of 149 people with some disability before treatment, 54% improved, 37% were stable and 9% worsened.
- On average, each person had 1.7 grade 3 side effects and 0.06 grade 4 side effects.
- The most common side effect was fever with low white blood cell counts (febrile neutropenia), in
  68% of people.
- There were no treatment-related deaths.
- The authors conclude aHSCT should be considered a standard of care for highly active
  relapsing-remitting MS. That is their view; this study alone cannot show aHSCT is better than drugs.

## Limits

- No comparison group, so it cannot show whether aHSCT did better than drugs.
- Safety was only recorded for the first 100 days; later problems are not covered by these numbers.
- 57 of 231 people assessed were not in the final analysis; the abstract does not say why.
- Single-arm cohort; a reviewer may judge level 4 (case series) a better fit than 3.
- Some people in this cohort are likely also in the Swedish comparison with alemtuzumab
  ([2021-ahsct-vs-alemtuzumab-zhukovsky](2021-ahsct-vs-alemtuzumab-zhukovsky.md)).
