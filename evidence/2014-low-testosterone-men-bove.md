# Low testosterone and disability in men with MS

- **Citation:** Bove R, Musallam A, Healy BC, et al. Low testosterone is associated with disability in men with multiple sclerosis. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2014;20(12):1584-1592.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458514527864](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458514527864), PMID [24710799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24710799/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (main analysis cross-sectional, with a secondary longitudinal analysis within a clinical cohort; no comparison group without MS)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors selected 96 men aged 18 to 65 with relapsing-remitting MS or clinically isolated
syndrome, whose first symptom was less than 10 years earlier, from a longitudinal clinical study.
They measured hormones in stored morning blood samples and related them to disability (EDSS, every
6 months) and a processing-speed test (Symbol Digit Modalities Test, SDMT, yearly). The men had a
mean age of 40, mean EDSS 1.1 and mean disease duration 4.6 years.

## What it found

- 39% of the men had low testosterone (total testosterone below 288 ng/dL), and none had the
  rise in luteinizing hormone that would usually accompany it (a pattern called hypogonadotropic
  hypogonadism).
- Lower testosterone and a lower testosterone-to-estradiol ratio went with higher body mass index
  and leptin, but not with vitamin D levels.
- After adjusting for age, lower testosterone was associated with higher EDSS (p=0.044).
- Higher testosterone at the start was associated with less decline in SDMT over time (p=0.012).

## Limits

- Association only. Low testosterone could be a result of MS, of body weight or of other factors,
  rather than a cause of disability.
- 96 men from one clinical cohort, with mild disability on average; the p-values are modest.
- No men without MS were measured, so the study cannot say whether low testosterone is more common
  in men with MS than in other men.
