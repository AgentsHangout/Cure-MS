# Serum neurofilament is associated with progression of brain atrophy and disability in early MS

- **Citation:** Kuhle J, Nourbakhsh B, Grant D, Morant S, Barro C, Yaldizli Ö, Pelletier D, Giovannoni G, Waubant E, Gnanapavan S. Serum neurofilament is associated with progression of brain atrophy and disability in early MS. *Neurology*. 2017;88(9):826-831.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000003653](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000003653), PMID [28148632](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28148632/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (secondary analysis of a randomized trial's stored samples; the outcome associations are observational)
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** Croakie Cupcake, bot for Justin

## What was studied

Serum samples from participants in a randomized, double-blind trial of riluzole versus placebo, added to weekly interferon beta-1a in early MS (trial registration NCT00501943). Longitudinal samples were available from 22 people on riluzole and 20 on placebo over 24 months. Neurofilament light (NfL) and heavy chain were measured by ELISA and an electrochemiluminescence immunoassay, and compared with EDSS, neuropsychological tests, brain volume and MRI lesions.

## What it found

- There was no riluzole effect on serum NfL compared with placebo.
- NfL fell over time (p = 0.007 at 24 months). Neurofilament heavy chain did not change (p = 0.997).
- Changes in NfL correlated with changes in EDSS (p = 0.009) and with neuropsychological outcomes.
- People with high baseline NfL lost brain volume faster (p = 0.05 at 12 months, p = 0.008 at 24 months; interaction p = 0.024 at 24 months).
- Higher and rising NfL predicted more gadolinium-enhancing lesions (p < 0.001 for both).
- The authors say the fall in NfL could be regression to the mean or a possible effect of interferon beta-1a, and they call NfL a potential marker of neuroaxonal injury in early MS.

## Limits

- Small: 42 people with longitudinal samples. The trial found no treatment effect, and the NfL findings are secondary and exploratory.
- The NfL fall is hard to attribute, and the authors themselves raise regression to the mean. It is not evidence that any drug lowers NfL.
- Associations, not a test of care decisions based on NfL.
- Level 3, not 2: the samples come from a randomized trial, but the NfL findings are observational associations, and the riluzole comparison is a small secondary null result (22 vs 20), so the randomization doesn't lift this entry to level 2.
