# aHSCT vs natalizumab in progressive MS (matched observational study)

- **Citation:** Kalincik T, Sharmin S, Roos I, et al. Effectiveness of autologous haematopoietic stem cell transplantation versus natalizumab in progressive multiple sclerosis. *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry*, 2024;95(8):775-783.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1136/jnnp-2023-332790](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2023-332790), PMID [38538060](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38538060/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

People with primary or secondary progressive MS treated with autologous haematopoietic stem cell
transplantation (aHSCT) at seven aHSCT centers, or with natalizumab in the MSBase registry, were
matched on a propensity score (sex, age, EDSS, relapses in the prior 12 and 24 months, time since
onset, most effective prior therapy and country). 39 aHSCT patients (37 with secondary progressive
MS; mean age 37, mean EDSS 5.7, 28% with recent disability progression, prior-year relapse rate 0.54)
were matched with 65 natalizumab patients and followed for up to 4 years.

## What it found

- No evidence of a difference in 6-month confirmed EDSS worsening (HR 1.49, 95% CI 0.70 to 3.14) or
  improvement (HR 1.50, 95% CI 0.22 to 10.29).
- Relapse rates were similar (mean 0.08 on both; HR 1.05, 95% CI 0.39 to 2.82).
- In the aHSCT group, 3 had febrile neutropenia during mobilization, 9 had serum sickness, 6 needed
  intensive care and 36 had complications after discharge. No treatment-related deaths were
  reported.
- The authors conclude the study does not support aHSCT to control disability in progressive MS
  with advanced disability and low relapse activity.

## Limits

- Observational, with only 39 aHSCT patients; the wide confidence intervals are consistent with
  either benefit or harm, so "no evidence of a difference" is not evidence that the two are equal.
- Natalizumab was itself not shown to change disability in progressive MS, so this compares aHSCT
  with a treatment of uncertain effect in this group, not with no treatment.
- Matching cannot remove all differences between people chosen for aHSCT and those given
  natalizumab.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
