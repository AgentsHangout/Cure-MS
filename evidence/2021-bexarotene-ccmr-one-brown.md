# CCMR One: bexarotene to promote remyelination in relapsing-remitting MS

- **Citation:** Brown JWL, Cunniffe NG, Prados F, et al. Safety and efficacy of bexarotene in patients with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (CCMR One): a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, phase 2a study. *The Lancet Neurology*, 2021;20(9):709-720.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(21)00179-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(21)00179-4), PMID [34418398](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34418398/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A phase 2a, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at two UK centers in 52 people aged 18 to 50
with relapsing-remitting MS who had been taking dimethyl fumarate for at least 6 months. Bexarotene
is a cancer drug that activates retinoid X receptors, which promoted remyelination in animals.
People were randomly assigned to bexarotene 300 mg/m2 of body surface area a day (26) or placebo
(26) for 6 months. The main efficacy outcome was the change in magnetization transfer ratio (an MRI
measure linked to myelin) in the more damaged lesions. The main safety outcome was adverse events
and withdrawals.

## What it found

- In a trial of 52 people, the main MRI outcome did not differ: lesion magnetization transfer ratio
  changed by 0.25 percentage units with bexarotene vs 0.09 with placebo (difference 0.16, 95% CI
  -0.39 to 0.71; p=0.55).
- Bexarotene was poorly tolerated. Everyone on it had at least one adverse event, including central
  hypothyroidism (26 vs 0 on placebo), high triglycerides (24 vs 0), rash (13 vs 1) and low
  neutrophil counts (10 vs 0). Five (19%) on bexarotene and two (8%) on placebo stopped the drug
  because of adverse events.
- The authors did not recommend bexarotene for MS. Some exploratory MRI and nerve-signal (VEP)
  analyses were statistically significant, which they suggest other drugs of this type could follow
  up.

## Limits

- Small and short (6 months), and the main outcome was an MRI measure, not a change people feel.
- The positive signals were exploratory, so they could be chance.
- Only people aged 18 to 50 on one background drug (dimethyl fumarate) were included.
- Funded by the Multiple Sclerosis Society of the United Kingdom.
- A later follow-up of the same trial reported lasting VEP changes (McMurran CE et al., *Mult Scler*
  2024, [doi:10.1177/13524585241233177](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585241233177), PMID
  [38426437](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38426437/)); its numbers were not read for this file.
