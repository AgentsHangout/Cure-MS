# PIRA in the Italian MS Registry: how much worsening happens without relapses

- **Citation:** Portaccio E, Betti M, De Meo E, et al. Progression independent of relapse activity in relapsing multiple sclerosis: impact and relationship with secondary progression. *Journal of Neurology*, 2024;271(8):5074-5082.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1007/s00415-024-12448-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-024-12448-4), PMID [38805052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38805052/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

16,130 people with relapsing-onset MS in the Italian MS Registry, each followed for at least 5 years
(average about 11.8 years). Disability worsening was a 6-month confirmed rise in the Expanded
Disability Status Scale (EDSS) score. Each worsening event was sorted as relapse-associated worsening
(RAW) or PIRA. "Sustained" worsening meant no later improvement at any visit. The authors also looked
at who went on to secondary progressive MS.

## What it found

- 16,731 confirmed worsening events happened in 8,998 people (55.8%).
- PIRA made up 72.3% of these events (12,175).
- PIRA events were more often sustained than RAW events (73.2% vs 56.7%).
- 27.6% of people moved to secondary progressive MS. This happened in 73.2% of people with sustained
  PIRA and 24.8% of people with non-sustained PIRA.
- PIRA was associated with a higher final EDSS score after adjusting for other factors.

## Limits

- Registry data from routine care: visits were not at fixed intervals, and relapses or worsening can
  be missed or misclassified.
- Only people followed for 5 or more years were included, which may not reflect everyone with MS.
- Association only; it does not show what causes PIRA.
- The journal later published a correction to this article (PMID
  [39231861](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39231861/)); the numbers above are from the PubMed
  abstract of the original article.
