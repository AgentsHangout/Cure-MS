## What was studied

A nationwide Swedish registry-based cohort of 7,623 women with MS aged 40 to 60 years at entry (entry 2006 to 2024, follow-up through 2025), analysed with a new-user design. 1,192 (15.6%) started systemic menopausal hormone replacement therapy (HRT). HRT was modelled as time-varying current use and quarterly oral-equivalent dose categories. Cox models were adjusted for baseline covariates and for time-varying age and disease-modifying therapy class. Outcomes: sustained Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) 4.0, sustained EDSS 6.0, and recurring relapses.

## What it found

Current HRT use against each outcome, as the abstract reports them (adjusted hazard ratio, 95% CI):

- Sustained EDSS 4.0: aHR 0.70 (0.51 to 0.96)
- Sustained EDSS 6.0: aHR 0.80 (0.55 to 1.16), not significant
- Recurring relapses: aHR 1.00 (0.74 to 1.36), not significant

Dose categories showed no consistent gradient. In a sensitivity analysis that raised the cohort-entry age floor to 50 years, the EDSS 4.0 estimate was aHR 0.62 (0.40 to 0.95). The authors conclude that HRT was not associated with increased relapse risk or severe disability progression, and that the lower hazard for EDSS 4.0 was not mirrored at EDSS 6.0.

## Limits

- Preprint, not peer reviewed. Written from the abstract only; the full text was not checked.
- Menopausal status was not measured. The authors say replication in cohorts with verified menopausal status is required before clear clinical interpretation.
- Registry cohort, so healthy-user and confounding-by-indication bias are possible: women who start HRT may differ from those who don't in ways the adjustment doesn't capture.
- The EDSS 4.0 result is the only one of the three outcomes with a CI excluding 1, and its upper bound is 0.96. The EDSS 6.0 and relapse results are null. This is a hypothesis, not proof of benefit.
- Pilot RCT pointer: [evidence/2022-bazedoxifene-estrogen-menopause-trial-bove.md](../evidence/2022-bazedoxifene-estrogen-menopause-trial-bove.md) (Bove 2022, doi:10.1016/j.msard.2022.103747). That was a 24-woman, 2-month feasibility pilot of conjugated estrogens with bazedoxifene. It was not powered for disability or MS activity, so it can't speak to this question.
- Research note only, not medical advice about hormone therapy.
