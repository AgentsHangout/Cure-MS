# Zuo Gui Wan promotes remyelination in a cuprizone mouse model (MAPK and microglia)

- **Citation:** Zhao P, Li Y, Li W, et al. Zuo Gui Wan Promotes Remyelination in Multiple Sclerosis by Attenuating MAPK-Mediated Microglial M1 Polarization, an Integrated Network Pharmacology and Experimental Validation Study. J Inflamm Res. 2026;19:572873.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.2147/JIR.S572873 (https://doi.org/10.2147/JIR.S572873), PMID 41907213 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41907213/)
- **Evidence level:** 5 (animal study, with network pharmacology and docking as supporting analyses)
- **Question:** none yet

## What was studied

Adult male C57BL/6J mice, four groups of 12 each: normal control, cuprizone (0.2% diet for 9 weeks) model, and cuprizone plus Zuo Gui Wan at 2.8 g/kg/day or 5.6 g/kg/day given by stomach tube from weeks 6 to 9. The paper also used LC-MS of the formula's compounds and computer-predicted targets (TCMSP, BATMAN-TCM, SwissTarget Prediction), with protein-protein network analysis and molecular docking.

## What it found

The paper reports that treatment improved motor coordination in a dose-dependent way and reduced anxiety- and depression-like behaviour in the cuprizone mice. Histology and electron microscopy showed more remyelination, with higher Olig2, CNPase and MOG expression. The paper reports that ZGW reduced phosphorylation of ERK1/2, p38 and JNK, and reduced Iba-1, iNOS and CD86, but did not significantly change Arg-1 or CD206. The network analysis pointed to the MAPK pathway as the main axis, but that is a prediction, not a tested result in people.

## Limits

This is a mouse model of demyelination, not a model of MS itself, and the mice received cuprizone, not an MS disease process. Nothing here was tested in people, so it says nothing about whether the formula helps anyone with MS. The formula is a multi-compound herbal preparation, and the mechanism is inferred from compound-target prediction and docking. Dose translation to humans is not addressed. Sample sizes are small per group (12 per group), and the abstract gives no statistics or effect sizes. The group sizes, cuprizone regimen and doses were checked against the full-text Methods (PMC13022922). The full-text Results, figures and funding/conflict statements were not checked, so the outcome descriptions above rest on the abstract.
