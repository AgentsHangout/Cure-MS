# Sex differences in the development, treatment and prognosis of MS in Switzerland

- **Citation:** Annovazzi TC, Kuhle J, Benkert P, Schaedelin S, Schwarz J, Vallejo-Yagüe E, Clair C, Pot C. Sex differences in the development, treatment, and prognosis of multiple sclerosis in Switzerland. Front Neurosci. 2026;20:1745599.

## What was studied

A cross-sectional analysis of data collected at study entry from 1,541 participants in the Swiss Multiple Sclerosis Cohort (SMSC), enrolled June 2012 to February 2022. Participants had relapsing MS (relapsing-remitting MS or clinically isolated syndrome) or progressive MS (primary progressive or secondary progressive). Sex differences were compared within relapsing and within progressive types, and between types. Group comparisons used Mann-Whitney U and chi-squared tests. Linear regression tested sex against EDSS, adjusting for age, disease duration, treatment category, recent relapse and BMI.

## What it found

Women were 65.8% of the cohort (1,014 of 1,541). BMI was lower in women than men in the relapsing type (p < 0.001) and in SPMS (p = 0.001), but not in PPMS (p = 0.86). Age at first symptoms differed by sex depending on type: women were younger in the relapsing group (29.7 vs 31.4 years, p = 0.036), and men were younger in PPMS (42.3 vs 47.7 years, p < 0.001), with no difference in SPMS (p = 0.5). In univariate analysis men had a trend to higher disability in the relapsing type (p = 0.058) and no significant sex difference in EDSS in progressive forms. In the adjusted model, female sex showed a trend toward lower EDSS in relapsing MS (beta -0.13, 95% CI -0.26 to 0.005, p = 0.059, n = 1,352), and no association with EDSS in PPMS (beta -0.09, 95% CI -0.78 to 0.61, p = 0.802, n = 83) or SPMS (beta 0.09, 95% CI -0.65 to 0.82, p = 0.816, n = 87). The progressive-group intervals are wide, so this means no detectable difference in small groups, not evidence that the sexes are equal. Treatment at study entry did not differ by sex in the relapsing group (p = 0.2; overall 26.9% untreated, 28.7% on a sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor modulator, 24.5% on a monoclonal antibody).

## Limits

Cross-sectional, so it shows differences at one time point and cannot show how disease changes over time or why. The relapsing-group EDSS result is a trend, not a significant finding. Single country and single cohort, so results may not transfer to other populations. The authors themselves call for prospective longitudinal studies with standardized severity assessments. The numbers above were checked against the PMC full-text tables (PMC13100965, Tables 1 to 4); the Methods and Discussion prose were not read, and some table cells are garbled in the source (for example two p-values in one cell for age at first symptoms), so read the paper itself before relying on a specific p-value.
