# Evobrutinib and slowly expanding lesion volume (phase 2 post hoc analysis)

- **Citation:** Arnold DL, Elliott C, Martin EC, et al. Effect of Evobrutinib on Slowly Expanding Lesion Volume in Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis: A Post Hoc Analysis of a Phase 2 Trial. *Neurology*, 2024;102(5):e208058.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000208058](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000208058), PMID [38335474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38335474/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (a post hoc analysis of a randomized trial)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A post hoc analysis of a 48-week, double-blind, randomized phase 2 trial in relapsing MS
(NCT02975349). People took evobrutinib, a brain-penetrant BTK inhibitor (25 mg once daily, 75 mg once
daily or 75 mg twice daily), placebo (switched to evobrutinib 25 mg once daily after week 24) or
open-label dimethyl fumarate. 223 people were in the slowly expanding lesion (SEL) analysis (mean age
42; 87% relapsing-remitting). SELs were old lesions that grew slowly and steadily outward from
baseline to week 48.

## What it found

- SEL volume was lower with higher evobrutinib doses than in the placebo-then-low-dose group.
- With 75 mg twice daily vs the placebo-then-25 mg group (one sequence group that took placebo for
  24 weeks and then low-dose evobrutinib, not separate placebo and low-dose arms; as reported by Dotliver Tree (bot) from the full paper; not checked here),
  the shift in SEL volume was -474.5 mm³
  (95% CI -1,098.0 to -3.0; p = 0.047). Compared with dimethyl fumarate it was -711.6 mm³ (95% CI
  -1,290.0 to -149.0; p = 0.011). Dimethyl fumarate did not differ from placebo or low dose.
- In subgroups, the high vs low dose difference was significant in people with EDSS 3.5 or more and
  in those with disease for 8.5 years or more.

## Limits

- Post hoc, so these comparisons were not planned when the trial was designed, and the borderline
  p = 0.047 is not corrected for the many comparisons made. The placebo group switched to evobrutinib
  halfway through, and the dimethyl fumarate arm was open label.
- It measured lesion volume, not disability or PIRA, over 48 weeks.
- Evobrutinib later did not beat teriflunomide on relapses in two phase 3 trials and raised liver
  enzymes more often (evolutionRMS; see the
  [state-of-the-art finding, section 3](../findings/state-of-the-art-2026.md#3-btk-inhibitors-and-other-late-stage-therapies-2024-2026)),
  and those authors concluded the results do not support its use in relapsing MS. So a lower SEL
  volume here did not lead to a better treatment, and the phase 3 abstracts do not report whether it
  slowed PIRA.
- The authors include employees of Merck, which made evobrutinib.
