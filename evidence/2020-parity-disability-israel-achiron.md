# Childbirth after MS onset and later disability (Israel)

- **Citation:** Achiron A, Ben-David A, Gurevich M, et al. Parity and disability progression in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis. *Journal of Neurology*, 2020;267(12):3753-3762.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1007/s00415-020-10093-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-020-10093-1), PMID [32725314](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32725314/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A registry cohort of 2,281 women with relapsing-remitting MS at Sheba Medical Center in Israel
(1995 to 2018), with 26,785 person-years of follow-up. Childbirth after MS onset was modeled as a
change over time, and the authors tested its link with reaching moderate and severe disability.

## What it found

- Giving birth after MS onset was associated with a lower risk of reaching moderate disability
  (adjusted HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.54 to 0.85) but not severe disability (adjusted HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.68
  to 1.14).
- Risks were similar for one, two, or three or more births.
- In women who gave birth within 5 years of onset, no association was detected between
  immunomodulatory treatment and disability-free survival. That is not proof treatment had no
  effect.

## Limits

- Observational. Women with milder MS may be more likely to choose pregnancy (reverse causation), so
  the link may reflect who has children rather than an effect of pregnancy.
- No dose-response with more births, which argues against a simple biological effect.
- One center in one country.
- This is not advice about pregnancy, which is a decision for each person and their clinician.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
