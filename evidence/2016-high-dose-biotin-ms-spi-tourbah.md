# MS-SPI: high-dose biotin (MD1003) in progressive MS

- **Citation:** Tourbah A, Lebrun-Frenay C, Edan G, et al.; MS-SPI study group. MD1003 (high-dose biotin) for the treatment of progressive multiple sclerosis: A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2016;22(13):1719-1731.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458516667568](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458516667568), PMID [27589059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27589059/), full text [PMC5098693](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5098693/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** aionoro (bot), owner morgkorg

Read from the full text in PubMed Central, including Tables 1 to 4. The supplementary materials
(eligibility details, Table S1, Table S2) were not opened.

## What was studied

A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at 16 MS centers in France in 154 people aged 18 to 75
with primary or secondary progressive MS, an EDSS disability score of 4.5 to 7, worsening over the
previous 2 years and no sign of inflammatory activity in the previous year. MD1003 is
pharmaceutical-grade biotin at a very high dose (100 mg three times a day). People were assigned
2:1 to MD1003 (103) or placebo (51) for 12 months, and then everyone took MD1003 for a further 12
months. The main outcome was the share of people whose disability improved at month 9 and was
still improved at month 12. Improved meant a lower EDSS score (by 1 point, or by 0.5 from a
starting score of 6 to 7) or a timed 25-foot walk at least 20% faster, compared with the better of
two visits before treatment.

## What it found

- In a trial of 154 people, 13 of 103 (12.6%; 95% CI 6.9% to 20.6%) on high-dose biotin met the
  main outcome, vs none of 51 on placebo (p = 0.005). Ten of the 13 improved on the EDSS, five on
  the timed walk and two on both.
- Mean EDSS change over 12 months was -0.03 on biotin vs +0.13 on placebo (p = 0.01). Clinicians'
  and patients' overall impression scores also favored biotin (p < 0.001 and p = 0.009).
- Several other secondary outcomes showed no significant difference: mean timed walk change
  (p = 0.64), the walking scale, fatigue, and the hand function test. Confirmed EDSS worsening was
  4.2% vs 13.6% (p = 0.07). Quality-of-life subscores were not consistent, and one (general
  health) favored placebo (p = 0.03).
- Among people on biotin, the main outcome was met more often by those not taking fampridine (12
  of 58) than by those taking it (1 of 45), in a pre-planned subgroup analysis.
- Adverse events were similar (81.6% vs 84.3%), as were serious adverse events (19.4% vs 23.5%).
  One person on biotin died by suicide, which was judged not related to treatment.
- High-dose biotin interfered with lab tests that use biotin. Six people on biotin were recorded
  as having an overactive thyroid during the first 12 months, and only one of the six was confirmed
  to have thyroid disease.
- MRI was done in a subset. New MS lesions at month 12 were seen in 11 of 47 (23.4%) on biotin vs
  3 of 23 (13.0%) on placebo (p = 0.36), and enlarging lesions in 4 (8.5%) vs none (p = 0.30).
  The differences were not significant, and the authors say later trials should check whether
  biotin could increase inflammation.
- The later, larger trial [SPI2](2020-high-dose-biotin-spi2-cree.md) (642 people) did not confirm
  the benefit.

## Limits

- **Small, with only 13 people meeting the main outcome**, fewer than the authors expected. They
  had planned for more than 40% on biotin and 10% on placebo.
- **The groups differed at the start.** More people on biotin had primary progressive MS (40.8%
  vs 25.5%) and a starting EDSS of 4.5 to 5.5 (27.2% vs 13.7%), and their mean walk time was
  shorter (21.8 vs 30.6 seconds). The paper calls the first difference non-significant and lists
  baseline imbalance among its limitations.
- **Blinding was not checked**, and the same neurologist could both treat and examine a patient.
  The authors list both as limitations.
- People who dropped out (11.7% on biotin, 17.6% on placebo) were not counted as improved, and no
  missing values were filled in for the main outcome.
- The placebo-controlled part lasted 12 months. After that everyone took biotin, so the second
  year has no untreated comparison.
- **A printed percentage doesn't match its count.** The text gives 12 of 58 as 20.3% and 1 of 45
  as 2.3%. Those work out to 20.7% and 2.2%. This file reports the counts only.
- Funded by MedDay Pharmaceuticals, the study sponsor, which also supplied MD1003. One author was
  the company's chief executive and a shareholder, and the company paid for editorial support.
- A positive result here was not repeated in the phase 3 trial, so on its own this is not evidence
  that high-dose biotin helps people with MS.
