# Sex chromosome contributions to sex differences in MS susceptibility and progression (mouse EAE, X-chromosome genes)

- **Citation:** Voskuhl RR, Sawalha AH, Itoh Y. Sex chromosome contributions to sex differences in multiple sclerosis susceptibility and progression. *Mult Scler*. 2018;24(1):22-31.
- **DOI or PMID:** doi:10.1177/1352458517737394 (https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458517737394); PMID 29307297 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29307297/); PMC5823689.
- **Evidence level:** 5 (animal study: sex chromosome transgenic mice in EAE, with bone marrow chimeras). Read from the abstract only.
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** Ribbit Red Velvet, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

Sex chromosome transgenic mice were used in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), a mouse model of MS, so that sex chromosomes could be studied apart from sex hormones. The authors looked at X-chromosome genes. They did DNA methylation studies of the X-linked gene Foxp3 in T lymphocytes, and bone marrow chimeras that had the same immune system but different sex chromosomes in the central nervous system. The abstract does not give group sizes.

## What it found

- DNA methylation of Foxp3 in T lymphocytes suggested that maternal versus paternal imprinting of X-chromosome genes may underlie sex differences in autoimmunity.
- In the chimeras, differential expression of the X-linked gene Toll-like receptor 7 in neurons may contribute to sex differences in neurodegeneration.
- The authors conclude that mapping the transcriptome and methylome in T cells and neurons in females versus males could reveal the mechanisms.

## Limits

This is a mouse model, not MS in people, and the abstract's wording is tentative ("suggested", "may"). The abstract gives no group sizes or effect sizes. The full text was not read for this file, so the methods and statistics are not checked here. Findings are about mechanisms in mice and do not show how much the X chromosome contributes to women's MS risk in people.
