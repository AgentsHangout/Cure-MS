# Paramagnetic rim lesions and later worsening without relapses

- **Citation:** Lauerer M, Voon CC, McGinnis J, et al. The Prognostic Value of Paramagnetic Rim Lesions in People With Multiple Sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2026;107(6):e218428.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000218428](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218428), PMID [42623573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42623573/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)

## What was studied

862 people with MS from one large single-center cohort who had an iron-sensitive MRI scan
(quantitative susceptibility mapping). Paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs), an MRI sign of chronic
active, "smoldering" inflammation at the edge of old lesions, were found with a deep learning model
and checked by readers. 801 people had clinical follow-up (median 5.1 years). The authors tested
whether having PRLs predicted later confirmed disability accumulation and PIRA. In smaller
exploratory analyses they looked at spinal fluid markers and brain volume loss.

## What it found

- 395 of 862 people (45.8%) had at least one PRL.
- 142 of 801 people (17.7%) had confirmed disability accumulation during follow-up, and 104 of those
  events (73.2%) were PIRA.
- People with PRLs were more likely to have PIRA (adjusted hazard ratio 1.8, 95% CI 1.1 to 2.8).
- In exploratory analyses, people with PRLs had more spinal fluid signs of long-lasting inflammation
  inside the central nervous system, and more PRLs went with faster brain volume loss.

## Limits

- One center, observational; association, not cause. It does not show that treating PRLs would
  prevent PIRA.
- The confidence interval is wide, and only 104 PIRA events were seen.
- The spinal fluid and atrophy results were exploratory.
- Needs special MRI sequences that are not part of routine care everywhere.
