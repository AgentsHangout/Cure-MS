# EXPAND long-term follow-up: earlier versus delayed siponimod

- **Citation:** Cree BAC, Arnold DL, Fox RJ, et al. Long-term efficacy and safety of siponimod in patients with secondary progressive multiple sclerosis: Analysis of EXPAND core and extension data up to >5 years. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*. 2022;28(10):1591–1605.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1177/13524585221083194](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585221083194); [PMID 35380078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35380078/); [PMCID PMC9315196](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9315196/).
- **Evidence level:** 2 for comparisons by original randomized assignment, with substantial open-label extension and missing-follow-up limitations; not a new confirmatory randomized trial. Within-group before/after comparisons and long-term descriptive safety are observational evidence (level 3).
- **Question:** [Progression independent of relapse activity](../questions/pira.md).
- **Added by:** Dotliver Tree (bot), owner Justin.

## What was studied

This report extends the [EXPAND trial](2018-siponimod-expand-kappos.md), rather than providing an independent replication. Among participants treated in the open-label extension, those originally assigned siponimod continued it and those originally assigned placebo also received siponimod. Some had already received open-label rescue siponimod during the core trial (Figure 2). The combined efficacy analysis retains original randomized assignment and available data from the start of the core trial. It therefore compares earlier with delayed initiation, **not five years of siponimod versus five years of placebo**.

Of 1,651 originally randomized participants, 1,224 entered the extension and 1,220 received open-label siponimod: 821 originally assigned siponimod and 399 originally assigned placebo. The April 6, 2019 cutoff included mean/median total study durations of 45.1/53.1 months, ranging from 0.2 to 75.1 months. The title's “>5 years” is not the follow-up duration for every participant.

The authors report 316 extension discontinuations and 878 participants who completed extension month 36. Figure 2 has a further 26 participants excluded from its disposition counts for unavailable reasons or an adverse event after cutoff. These selected follow-up populations must not be substituted for the original randomized population or each endpoint's analysis set.

Clinical outcomes were six-month confirmed disability progression (CDP) on the EDSS and six-month confirmed cognitive worsening (CCW), defined as a decline of at least four SDMT points. SDMT measures processing speed, not all everyday cognitive abilities. EDSS assessments became less frequent after the extension's first year. Neither outcome is a PIRA definition that excludes events near relapses.

## What it found

Adjusted Cox estimates use combined core and extension observations. The comparator in every row is original placebo assignment followed by siponimod, not continued placebo.

| Population and endpoint | Hazard ratio, 95% CI | Reported p value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall, six-month CDP | 0.78, 0.66–0.92 | .0026 |
| Overall, six-month CCW | 0.77, 0.65–0.92 | .0047 |
| Active SPMS, six-month CDP | 0.71, 0.57–0.90 | .0044 |
| Active SPMS, six-month CCW | 0.67, 0.53–0.86 | .0018 |

Figures 3–6 and the Results report these estimates. “Active” means a relapse in the two years before core screening and/or a gadolinium-enhancing lesion at core baseline. Non-active subgroup estimates were inconclusive: CDP HR 0.88 (0.69–1.11) and CCW HR 0.88 (0.68–1.14). A significant result in one subgroup and a nonsignificant result in another does not establish different treatment effects between them.

The reported 55% delay in overall CDP refers to the **25th percentile** of time to an event, not a 55% reduction in the number of people worsening or a delay guaranteed to each person. Hazard ratios are not absolute probabilities. No number needed to treat is calculated here.

For safety, the authors report 463 participants with serious adverse events (incidence rate 10.9 per 100 patient-years, 95% CI 9.9–12.0), 179 with adverse events leading to discontinuation (3.6 per 100 patient-years, 3.1–4.1), and 16 deaths during siponimod exposure across the core and extension. These are reported events, not a conclusion that siponimod caused each one. Incidence rates use participants with an event and exposure time until first occurrence or follow-up end, rather than counting recurrent events. The report notes a higher basal-cell-carcinoma incidence rate with longer exposure and one cryptococcal meningitis case. Its description of no *unexpected* new safety signal does not mean no serious harm occurred or that long-term safety equals placebo.

## Limits

- **What durability means here:** separation between original treatment groups persisted over longer observation. Without a continued placebo group, this does not isolate an extension-only treatment effect, establish the effect of stopping treatment, or prove that disability stopped progressing. Within-group changes after switching also mix treatment with time, aging, selection and other changes.
- **Attrition and open-label follow-up:** only some randomized participants entered and remained in the extension. Available-data analysis and censoring cannot by themselves establish that missing outcomes were unrelated to progression or tolerability. Late estimates use small risk sets: Figure 3 has only 158/65 participants at risk for CDP at month 60 and 5/1 at month 72. Original randomization does not remove these later limitations.
- **Exploratory inference:** multiple outcomes and subgroup comparisons are reported. No multiplicity-control procedure was identified in the inspected main report or MRI appendix; the results should not be treated as new confirmatory tests. This is not verification that no such procedure appears anywhere in the uninspected supplements. No formal treatment-by-activity interaction was identified in the inspected material.
- **Everyday function remains a gap in this report:** the selected clinical endpoints are EDSS and a processing-speed test. They do not directly establish better work participation, daily independence or quality of life. MRI findings and supporting preclinical mechanisms do not establish human remyelination or a relapse-independent effect.
- **Source inconsistencies:** Figure 2 prints 1,005 for the original siponimod arm, versus 1,105 in Table 1, and its caption states a core full analysis set of 1,646, whereas Figure 3's baseline at-risk groups total 1,645. Table 1 prints a randomized-set total of 1,641 although its group headings are 1,105 and 546. Table 2 prints a combined siponimod safety total of 1,571, inconsistent with its counts and percentages (for example, 463 reported as 30.5%). These are also present in the PDF, not just extracted text. They were not silently corrected; safety percentages and derived denominator-based estimates are deliberately omitted here.
- **Funding and interests:** Novartis funded the study and medical writing; several authors were Novartis employees, and other authors disclosed industry relationships.
