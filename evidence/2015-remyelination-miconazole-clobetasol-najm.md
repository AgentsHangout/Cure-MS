# Miconazole and clobetasol promote remyelination in mice and human cells in vitro (Najm 2015)

- **Citation:** Najm FJ, Madhavan M, Zaremba A, et al. Drug-based modulation of endogenous stem cells promotes functional remyelination in vivo. *Nature*, 2015 Jun 11;522(7555):216-220.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1038/nature14335](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14335), PMID [25896324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25896324/). Full text open: [PMC4528969](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4528969/).

## What was studied

The authors screened 727 drugs from the NIH Clinical Collection I and II for their ability to turn mouse oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs, made from mouse epiblast stem-cell lines) into myelin-making cells in a dish. The top hits were tested further. Miconazole (an antifungal) and clobetasol (a topical corticosteroid) were chosen for in vivo tests, after both were shown to cross the blood-brain barrier in mice. The in vivo work used mice: a lysolecithin model of focal spinal cord demyelination in adult mice, a developmental myelination model in newborn mice, and two EAE models (PLP139-151 relapsing-remitting EAE and MOG35-55 chronic progressive EAE). Human OPCs made from human stem cells were used in a dish.

## What it found

- In a screen of 727 drugs, 22 enhanced oligodendrocyte formation in mouse cells more than thyroid hormone did, and 14 of 16 hits tested at an outside lab were confirmed.
- In mouse spinal cord lesions, miconazole and clobetasol both increased remyelination. At 12 days after lesion, more than 70% of axons in treated lesions were remyelinated, against 6% in vehicle-treated mice.
- In the MOG35-55 chronic progressive EAE model, both drugs given from the peak of disease reduced disease severity. Miconazole's effect was validated by a separate contract lab.
- In the PLP139-151 relapsing-remitting EAE model, only clobetasol reduced disease severity. The authors say its immune-suppressing effect makes it hard to read its remyelinating effect in that model.
- Both drugs increased differentiation of human OPCs into oligodendrocytes in a dish, with miconazole the most reproducible.

## Limits

- Mice and cells only. Nothing here was tested in people, so it does not show that either drug helps anyone with MS. The authors describe the step to clinical trials as a possibility.
- Clobetasol is a corticosteroid with immune-suppressing effects, so its EAE results mix remyelination with immune effects. The authors say so.
- EAE is a model of inflammation, not MS itself.
- The drugs were dosed in mice at specific doses (miconazole 10 or 40 mg/kg, clobetasol 2 mg/kg). Doses in people, safety and long-term effects were not studied here.
- Human OPC work was in cells in a dish, not in human tissue or people.
- The paper reports no competing financial interests. The full funding list was not checked in full for this file.
