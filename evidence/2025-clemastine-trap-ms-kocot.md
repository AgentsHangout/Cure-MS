# TRAP-MS: clemastine arm stopped for safety in people with progressive MS

- **Citation:** Kocot J, Kosa P, Ashida S, et al. Clemastine fumarate accelerates accumulation of disability in progressive multiple sclerosis by enhancing pyroptosis. *Journal of Clinical Investigation*, 2025;135(10):e183941.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1172/JCI183941](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI183941), PMID [40371642](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40371642/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 for the clinical result (9 treated people with follow-up, compared with other trial participants, not with a randomized placebo group); 5 for the lab work in cells
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A clemastine arm was added to TRAP-MS, an ongoing NIH platform trial (NCT03109288) that tests
biomarker-guided add-on treatments. The aim was to find a spinal fluid "signature" of remyelination
and to collect safety data on clemastine in people whose disability was worsening without relapses
(progression independent of relapse activity, PIRA). The researchers also measured about 7,000
proteins in spinal fluid before and after treatment, and did lab experiments in immune cells
(macrophages) and in lab-grown human oligodendrocytes (myelin-making cells made from stem cells).

## What it found

- Sixteen people started clemastine. Nine had reached at least one six-month follow-up visit when 3
  of those 9 met individual safety stopping criteria, and the arm was stopped under the trial's own
  rules.
- The individual stopping rule was an on-treatment yearly progression slope on CombiWISE (a combined
  disability scale) more than five times the person's own baseline slope (or, separately, new or
  enlarging lesions over three times baseline).
- Reported in 9 cases: people on clemastine had significantly steeper disability worsening during
  treatment than the other TRAP-MS participants. The comparison was with 63 treatment-specific
  slopes from 42 people in 6 other treatment arms (none of which met stopping criteria), not with 63
  separate people; some people contributed more than one slope.
- In spinal fluid, the authors report that markers of purinergic signaling and pyroptosis (an
  inflammatory type of cell death) rose after clemastine. These are exploratory pathway results: of
  about 7,000 proteins measured in the 9 people, none of the individual changes passed the
  correction for multiple testing (false discovery rate), and the pathway signal came from the
  proteins with the largest changes.
- In cells: clemastine together with low levels of extracellular ATP triggered pyroptosis in
  macrophages and in lab-grown human oligodendrocytes; blocking the P2RX7 channel prevented this.
- A spinal fluid pyroptosis score was higher in untreated progressive than in relapsing-remitting MS
  and correlated with the rate of progression. The authors propose that pyroptosis is a likely
  mechanism of injury in PIRA more broadly; that is a hypothesis, not tested here.

## Limits

- Very small (16 started clemastine, 9 with follow-up) and not a randomized comparison with
  placebo, so chance and differences between groups cannot be ruled out. The comparison group was
  treatment slopes from other arms of the same trial, with some people counted more than once.
- The mechanism findings come from cells and from exploratory protein patterns; no single protein
  change survived correction for multiple testing, and they do not show that this is what caused
  the worsening in people.
- It does not tell us whether clemastine is harmful in relapsing MS, where ReBUILD reported no
  serious adverse events (see [2017-clemastine-rebuild-green](2017-clemastine-rebuild-green.md)).
- An earlier version was posted as a preprint; this file uses only the peer-reviewed paper. The
  numbers on enrollment, the comparison group, the stopping rule and multiple testing were checked
  in the full text (Europe PMC, PMC12077908) on 11/03/2026.
- The authors declared no conflicts of interest.
