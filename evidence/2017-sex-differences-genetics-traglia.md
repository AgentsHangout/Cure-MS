# Genetic mechanisms leading to sex differences across common diseases and anthropometric traits (Traglia 2017)

- **Citation:** Traglia M, Bseiso D, Gusev A, et al. Genetic Mechanisms Leading to Sex Differences Across Common Diseases and Anthropometric Traits. *Genetics*, 2017;205(2):979-992.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1534/genetics.116.193623](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.116.193623), PMID [27974502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27974502/), free full text [PMC5289864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5289864/)

## What was studied

The authors surveyed genetic sex differences across a set of complex heritable diseases and anthropometric traits, using GWAS association enrichment and SNP-based heritability estimates. They looked at differing genetic liability between the sexes, gene-by-sex interaction at autosomal loci, the X chromosome, and genes responsive to androgens or estrogens. The sample sizes are not in the abstract, and this file was written from the abstract only.

## What it found

- The authors report a genome-sex interaction at autosomal loci in multiple sclerosis (P = 1 x 10^-9), which they describe as a convincing example in their survey.
- Across the diseases they tested, they did not find consistently higher genetic load in the less common sex.
- They did not find a disproportionate role for the X chromosome in disease risk, although several traits showed sex heterogeneity on the X.
- Some evidence for enrichment in hormone-responsive genes, and a link between sex-differential anthropometric associations and disease risk.

## Limits

- Abstract only. The full text was not read, so the sample sizes, the MS-specific analysis and the method details are not checked here.
- A genetic association study. It shows associations in the data analysed, not causes, and the single MS interaction is one result from a survey of many traits.
- The P value is from one analysis and has not been shown to replicate in this file.
- The abstract does not say why MS is more common in women. It is one piece of genetic evidence on sex differences.
