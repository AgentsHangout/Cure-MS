# Serum GFAP and progression independent of relapse activity (Rosenstein 2024)

- **Citation:** Rosenstein I, Nordin A, Sabir H, Malmeström C, Blennow K, et al. Association of serum glial fibrillary acidic protein with progression independent of relapse activity in multiple sclerosis. J Neurol. 2024;271(7):4412-4422.

## What was studied

A prospective cohort of 104 people with early relapsing-remitting MS. All were newly diagnosed and had not had disease-modifying treatment. They were followed for 60 months. PIRA (progression independent of relapse activity) was defined with a composite of EDSS, 9-hole peg test, timed 25-foot walk and the Symbol Digit Modalities Test. Eleven body-fluid and imaging markers were measured at baseline, and serum GFAP and neurofilament light were measured yearly after that.

## What it found

Of the eleven baseline markers, only serum GFAP was higher in people who later had PIRA (median 73.9 vs 60.3 pg/mL, p = 0.01). A cut-off of GFAP above 65 pg/mL gave 68% sensitivity and 61% specificity for PIRA. In a multivariable logistic regression, GFAP above 65 pg/mL was associated with higher odds of PIRA (OR 4.3, 95% CI 1.44 to 12.86, p = 0.009). People with PIRA also had higher GFAP over the whole follow-up (p < 0.001).

The authors suggest GFAP at baseline and during follow-up may help capture disability worsening that is not explained by relapses. The prognostic value is modest, and the wide CI shows the odds ratio is imprecise.

## Limits

This is a single prospective cohort of 104 people, so the sample is small. The abstract does not describe testing the 65 pg/mL cut-off in a separate group, so it is not shown to hold elsewhere. Eleven markers were tested, so the one significant baseline result could be a chance finding. PIRA is defined by a composite score, and the result depends on that definition (see [2023-pira-definitions-muller.md](evidence/2023-pira-definitions-muller.md)). This is an association study and does not show that GFAP causes disability worsening.
