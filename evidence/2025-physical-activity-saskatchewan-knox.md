## What was studied

People with MS who, when they first registered with a provincial MS drugs program in Saskatchewan (Sept 1, 2014 to Feb 22, 2022), were mailed the Godin Leisure-Time Exercise Questionnaire. There were 505 responses from 993 requests (50.8%). Of the respondents, 69.7% were female, mean age 42.8 years, median disease duration 6.0 years, and 47.4% had prior disease-modifying therapy (DMT). The authors ran linear regression of total physical activity (TPA) and health contribution (HC) scores on DMT, sex, age, age at onset and disease duration.

## What it found

Activity was self-reported on the questionnaire, not measured by a device. Among new registrants there was no significant change in activity scores over 7 years (slope TPA 0.71, P = .26; HC 0.61, P = .21). Men reported higher activity than women (TPA beta 11.95, P < .001; HC beta 6.65, P < .001). There were interactions between age and disease duration on activity scores. DMT exposure was not associated with physical activity. The authors conclude that activity levels stayed suboptimal for health benefits over the period.

## Limits

Activity was self-reported by mail, so it may be inaccurate. Only half of those asked responded, and responders may differ from non-responders. The group is one provincial drugs-program population in Canada. The analysis covers people when they first joined the program, not the same people over time. The abstract does not show the full models, so the regression results should be read from the full paper. This note is about association only. It does not show that physical activity changes MS outcomes.
