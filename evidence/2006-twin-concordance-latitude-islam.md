# Twin concordance for MS by latitude of birthplace (Islam, 2006)

- **Citation:** Islam T, Gauderman WJ, Cozen W, Hamilton AS, Burnett ME, Mack TM. Differential twin concordance for multiple sclerosis by latitude of birthplace. *Annals of Neurology*, 2006;60(1):56-64.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1002/ana.20871](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.20871), PMID [16685699](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16685699/)
- **Evidence level:** 3

## What was studied

A twin series of 418 monozygotic (MZ) and 380 same-sex dizygotic (DZ) pairs with MS, ascertained from 1980 to 1992 and followed. The twins were from Canada and adjacent US states. Within that series, twins born at or above about 41-42 degrees N are labelled "northern"; the label marks that birthplace subgroup, not where the whole series came from. Concordance was compared by zygosity, sex, latitude of birthplace, ancestry (descent from high-risk populations) and early diagnosis (before a median age of 29.3 years).

## What it found

- The MZ/DZ concordance ratio was 2.9 (95% CI 1.0-8.9) in men and 2.6 (95% CI 1.5-4.5) in women.
- Among MZ twins, concordance was 1.9 times greater in northern-born twins (95% CI 1.2-3.2). It was also 1.9 times greater with high-risk ancestry and 2.1 times greater with early diagnosis.
- Among DZ twins, concordance did not depend on these characteristics.
- The average age at diagnosis in northern twins was about 2 years earlier, for both MZ and DZ twins.

The paper reads the latitude difference as partly environmental and partly genetic. It is an observational study, so it does not show which factor causes the difference.

## Limits

- Twins were ascertained and followed, not drawn at random from the general population. The authors describe the sample as representative of twins with MS. That is not the same as representative of all twins, so concordance may be biased.
- The latitude split is a single cut-off (about 41-42 degrees N) on birthplace in one region, and latitude stands in for unmeasured environmental exposures.
- The result is a ratio of concordance, with wide confidence intervals (for example 1.2-3.2 for northern MZ twins, and 1.0-8.9 for men's MZ/DZ ratio). The CIs are the main guide to how precise each subgroup estimate is.
- The result is about association within twin pairs. It does not show that living at a given latitude causes MS, and it does not measure any individual's risk.
