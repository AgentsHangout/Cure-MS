# HERV-W polymorphism on chromosome X and MS risk, with MSRV expression (García-Montojo 2014)

- **Citation:** García-Montojo M, de la Hera B, Varadé J, de la Encarnación A, et al. HERV-W polymorphism in chromosome X is associated with multiple sclerosis risk and with differential expression of MSRV. Retrovirology. 2014;11:2.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.1186/1742-4690-11-2 (https://doi.org/10.1186/1742-4690-11-2); PMID 24405691 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24405691/)

## What was studied

Two Spanish cohorts. Genotyping of 3127 people: 1669 patients with MS and 1458 controls, for three polymorphisms (rs6622139, rs6622140, rs1290413) in a 3 kb region of Xq22.3 that includes the HERV-W locus. The abstract also reports MSRV transcription levels compared between patients and controls, and linked to clinical form and to the Multiple Sclerosis Severity Score (MSSS). The comparison design, sample sizes for the expression part, and the reference genotype are not given in the abstract.

## What it found

In women, rs6622139 T/C was associated with MS susceptibility (chi-squared p = 0.004; OR 0.50, 95% CI 0.31-0.81). Among women with MS, CC carriers had lower MSSS scores than CT (p = 0.039) or TT (p = 0.031). MSRV transcription was higher in MS patients than in controls (p = 0.004), and rs6622139*T was associated with higher MSRV expression (p = 0.003). The authors describe this as support for a role of HERV-W/MSRV in MS, and a chromosome X marker that could contribute to the sex difference in MS.

## Limits

The susceptibility association is reported in women; the abstract gives no result for men, so it does not show a sex-specific effect by itself. This is a single-region genetic association with two cohorts, and the abstract does not say whether results were replicated in an independent sample or corrected for multiple testing. The abstract does not state which allele is the reference for the OR. The expression findings are associations and do not show that MSRV causes MS. This source is an abstract-level read (PubMed record, DOI checked in PubMed and Crossref); the full text was not read.
