# Meta-analysis of smoking and MS risk

- **Citation:** Handel AE, Williamson AJ, Disanto G, Dobson R, Giovannoni G, Ramagopalan SV. Smoking and multiple sclerosis: an updated meta-analysis. *PLoS One*, 2011;6(1):e16149.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0016149](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0016149), PMID [21249154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21249154/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a meta-analysis, but of observational studies, not of randomized trials, so it is no stronger than the studies it pools)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors searched Medline for studies of MS risk after cigarette smoking and pooled 14 articles,
covering 3,052 people with MS and 457,619 controls. They ran a conservative analysis (only studies
where smoking was recorded before MS onset) and a broader one. They also pooled 4 studies on the
risk of moving to secondary progressive MS.

## What it found

- Smoking was associated with higher MS risk: risk ratio 1.48 (95% CI 1.35-1.63) in the
  conservative analysis, and 1.52 (95% CI 1.39-1.66) in the broader one.
- The link between smoking and secondary progression fell just short of statistical significance,
  with large differences between studies (risk ratio 1.88, 95% CI 0.98-3.61, p = 0.06).

## Limits

- Pooled observational studies; association, not cause. Smokers may differ from non-smokers in
  other ways.
- Only Medline was searched, and the abstract does not describe a formal quality rating of the
  included studies.
- Relevance to this question: smoking habits and secondhand smoke are often shared within
  households and communities, but this study does not test whether smoking explains clustering.
