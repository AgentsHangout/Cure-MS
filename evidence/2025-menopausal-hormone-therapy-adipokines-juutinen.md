# Menopausal hormone therapy and inflammation markers in women with MS (small pilot)

- **Citation:** Juutinen L, Ahinko K, Hagman S, et al. Immunometabolic profiling in menopausal women with multiple sclerosis: the role of adipokines and hormone therapy. *BMJ Neurology Open*, 2025;7(2):e001295.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1136/bmjno-2025-001295](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjno-2025-001295), PMID [41368034](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41368034/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (a small study with before-and-after measurements and no randomized comparison)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

16 menopausal women with MS and 15 age-matched healthy women had adipokines (hormones made by fat
tissue), inflammation markers and MS severity markers measured. In the women with MS, adipokines were
followed over 1 year of oral menopausal hormone therapy, compared with their own baseline.

## What it found

- In women with MS, body mass index and the leptin-to-adiponectin ratio correlated with blood
  inflammation markers (hs-CRP, TNF-α and IL-6; ρ 0.51 to 0.66).
- Lower vitamin D correlated with higher inflammation markers.
- Adipsin correlated strongly with neurofilament light chain (ρ = 0.72), a marker of nerve damage,
  and fell during hormone therapy (at 3 and 12 months).

## Limits

- 16 women with MS, no randomized or untreated comparison over the year, so changes during hormone
  therapy cannot be attributed to it.
- Correlations between markers, not effects on relapses, disability or symptoms.
- The authors' description of adipsin as a promising biomarker is a proposal.
- This is not advice about hormone therapy, which is a decision for each person and their clinician.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
