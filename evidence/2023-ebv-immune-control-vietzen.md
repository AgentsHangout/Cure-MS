# Immune control of EBV-induced autoreactive cells and MS risk

- **Citation:** Vietzen H, Berger SM, Kühner LM, et al. Ineffective control of Epstein-Barr-virus-induced autoimmunity increases the risk for multiple sclerosis. *Cell*, 2023;186(26):5705-5718.e13.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2023.11.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2023.11.015), PMID [38091993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38091993/)
- **Evidence level:** 5 (mainly a laboratory study of immune cells; its risk estimates come from comparing people with and without MS, which is level 3 at best)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The study built on the finding that antibodies against part of the EBV protein EBNA1 can cross-react
with the brain protein GlialCAM (see
[2022-ebna1-glialcam-mimicry-lanz](2022-ebna1-glialcam-mimicry-lanz.md)). It asked why only some
people with high levels of these antibodies get MS. The authors tested, in laboratory experiments,
which immune cells kill GlialCAM-reactive cells, and looked at genetic differences in the person, in
the infecting EBV strain and in a second herpesvirus, cytomegalovirus (CMV).

## What it found

- In the laboratory, certain natural killer (NK) cells (NKG2C+ and NKG2D+) and certain EBV-specific
  T cells killed cells that react to GlialCAM.
- Some EBV variants raised the level of HLA-E, a molecule that helped these autoreactive cells
  escape being killed.
- Combinations of host and virus genetic features were associated with up to 260 times higher risk
  of MS.
- The authors suggest this could help identify people at risk early and point to new treatments.
  That is a proposal, not a tested result.

## Limits

- The killing experiments were done in cells (level 5), not in people.
- The abstract does not give how many people were studied or how they were chosen, so the "up to
  260-fold" figure cannot be judged from the abstract. It is the top of a range for specific
  combinations and is an association, not a measured cause. Check the full paper.
- Not yet repeated by an independent group, as far as this search found. A 2026 paper by the same
  first author on pediatric MS (*PNAS*, PMID 42821346) was seen in PubMed but not reviewed or
  verified.
