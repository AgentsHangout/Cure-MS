# Sun and UV-B exposure over the life course and MS in the Nurses' Health Studies

- **Citation:** Tremlett H, Zhu F, Ascherio A, Munger KL. Sun exposure over the life course and associations with multiple sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2018;90(14):e1191-e1199.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000005257](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000005257), PMID [29514944](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29514944/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (case-control study within cohorts; observational)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

151 women with MS and 235 age-matched controls from the Nurses' Health Study cohorts filled in
questionnaires on summer, winter and lifetime sun exposure, from ages 5-15 onward. Ambient UV-B
exposure was estimated from where they lived (latitude, altitude and cloud cover). Results were
adjusted for body mass index, ancestry, smoking and vitamin D supplement use.

## What it found

- Living in high (vs low) ambient UV-B areas before MS onset was associated with a 45% lower MS
  risk (adjusted relative risk 0.55, 95% CI 0.42-0.73).
- Similar lower risks (51%-52%) were seen for medium or high exposure at ages 5-15 and in the 5-15
  years before MS onset.
- At ages 5-15, living in a high UV-B area and having high summer sun exposure was associated with
  lower MS risk (relative risk 0.45, 95% CI 0.21-0.96).

## Limits

- Sun exposure was recalled years later, which can be inaccurate and may differ between people with
  and without MS.
- Small study; all participants were women and 98% were white.
- Where people lived is tied to many factors besides UV-B, so the association does not show that
  sunlight itself lowers risk.
