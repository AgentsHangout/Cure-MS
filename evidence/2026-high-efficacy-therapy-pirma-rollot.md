# High-efficacy vs moderate-efficacy first treatment and worsening without relapses or MRI activity

- **Citation:** Rollot F, Casey R, Kerbrat A, et al. Progression independent of relapse and MRI activity and treatment strategies in multiple sclerosis. *Brain*, 2026;149(6):2022-2031.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/brain/awag060](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag060), PMID [41689266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41689266/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

10,499 people with relapsing-onset MS in the French MS registry (2010 to 2023), followed on average
3.7 years, who started on either a high-efficacy therapy (2,666) or a moderate-efficacy therapy
(7,833) and had EDSS and MRI checks at least every 2 years. The main outcome was PIRMA: worsening with
neither relapses nor new MRI activity, a stricter version of PIRA. Groups were compared using
propensity scores.

## What it found

- Time to first PIRMA was slightly shorter with high-efficacy first treatment than with
  moderate-efficacy treatment (restricted mean 8.7 vs 8.9 years). The difference was statistically
  significant but small.
- Relapse-associated and MRI-associated worsening came sooner with moderate-efficacy treatment.
- Time to any confirmed disability progression tended to be longer with high-efficacy treatment, but
  this did not reach significance (7.6 vs 7.3 years, P = 0.071).
- Higher EDSS, older age and spinal cord lesions at baseline were associated with more PIRMA.
- The authors conclude that, in routine care, high-efficacy drugs gave better control of worsening
  tied to relapses and MRI activity, but did not appear to act differently on PIRMA.

## Limits

- Observational registry data; despite propensity scores, people given high-efficacy drugs may
  differ in ways not measured (for example, more active disease at the start).
- Short average follow-up (3.7 years) and MRI only every 2 years, so some MRI activity may have been
  missed.
- Association, not cause.
