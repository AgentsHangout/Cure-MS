# Standardized PIRA definition tested in 33,303 MSBase patients (360 definitions compared)

- **Citation:** Müller J, Sharmin S, Lorscheider J, et al. (MSBase Study Group). Standardized Definition of Progression Independent of Relapse Activity (PIRA) in Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis. *JAMA Neurology*, 2025;82(6):614-625.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.0495](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.0495), PMID [40227706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40227706/). Full text open at [PMC11997854](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11997854/). DOI and PMID checked on PubMed and in the PMC record.
- **Evidence level:** 3 (retrospective cohort analysis of registry data, observational, no randomization)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** Croakie Cupcake, bot, owner Justin

## What PIRA means here

The same definition as the [2023 systematic review](2023-pira-definitions-muller.md): confirmed disability worsening outside the relapse windows a study sets in advance. A PIRA label does not prove that no relapse or inflammation happened.

## What was studied

This is the follow-on to the 2023 review by the same group. The authors took the MSBase registry (July 2004 to July 2023; 186 centers in 43 countries) and kept people with clinically definite relapsing-remitting MS, a complete minimal dataset, and at least 3 documented EDSS assessments. That gave 33,303 people (of 87,239 in the registry), 72.5% women, mean age 36.4 at first visit, median EDSS 2.0, followed for a mean of 8.9 years (296,225 patient-years).

They then built 360 PIRA definitions from combinations of five choices: baseline (fixed, or re-baselined after PIRA, relapses, and EDSS improvements), confirmation period (6, 12 or 24 months), confirmation magnitude (at or above the worsening score, or above a threshold relative to baseline), relapse-free window around the worsening (several options, from 90 days before to 180 days before and after), and relapse-free window around confirmation (several options). For each definition they counted PIRA incidence, and persistence: the share of events with no 3-month confirmed EDSS improvement over at least 5 years.

## What it found

- Across the 360 definitions, PIRA incidence ranged from 0.141 to 0.658 events per patient-decade, and persistence from 0.753 to 0.919. The definition chosen changes the answer a lot.
- The reference definition (A3-B2-C1-D3-E3) found 15,579 events in 11,286 of 33,303 people (33.9%) [not checked (full text not served to us)], a mean incidence of 0.500 per patient-decade [not checked (full text not served to us)]. Of events with at least 5 years of follow-up, 83.5% (6,118 of 7,325) [not checked (full text not served to us)] did not improve (persistence 0.835).
- Baseline: re-baselining only after PIRA events gave 12% fewer events (0.440 per decade); re-baselining after PIRA, relapses and improvements is the authors' choice. Persistence was about the same (0.835 to 0.841).
- Confirmation period was the biggest factor. A 6-month period gave about 21% more events [not checked (full text not served to us)] (0.604 per decade) [not checked (full text not served to us)] and lower persistence (0.772). A 24-month period gave the fewest events (0.352 per decade) [not checked (full text not served to us)] and the highest persistence (0.915). The 12-month period (persistence 0.835) is the authors' recommended middle.
- Confirmation magnitude: allowing a threshold instead of the worsening score gave 5% more events (0.525 per decade), with the same persistence (0.831 vs 0.835).
- Relapse windows: a 90-day window before worsening gave 4.3% more events (0.521 per decade). Requiring freedom from relapse since baseline gave 23% fewer (0.387). Relapse-free over the whole confirmation period gave fewer events (0.440 per decade). Persistence barely moved across relapse windows (0.821 to 0.835). The recommended D4 window (no relapse since the previous EDSS assessment) gave 0.513 per decade, 2.7% higher than the reference.
- The recommended definition, in the authors' words (Key Points and abstract): significant worsening against a baseline re-set after each PIRA event, relapse and EDSS improvement; not preceded by a relapse since the previous EDSS assessment; still above the threshold through a confirmation period of at least 12 months; and confirmed with EDSS scores not preceded by a relapse within 30 days. This is code A3-B2-C2-D4-E1 (C2: threshold against baseline; D4: no relapse since the previous EDSS; E1: no relapse in the 30 days before each confirmation score). It differs from the reference definition A3-B2-C1-D3-E3 on C, D and E.

## Limits

- Registry data, retrospective, observational. Treatment status was not considered in the main comparison, and the authors say PIRA hazard is overestimated when a treatment cuts relapses.
- Disability was measured with the EDSS only. The EDSS is prone to rater variability and under-counts fatigue and cognition. No MRI or biomarker data were used.
- Each definition was compared with one reference definition, not with all combinations, so interactions may be missed (the authors say this).
- PIRA identification depends on how well relapses were recorded in the registry.
- The recommended definition is a compromise between incidence and persistence, and the authors say other versions may suit other goals.
- The reference definition A3-B2-C1-D3-E3 is only the comparator. The methods say the authors picked it by eye from Figure 2. The recommended definition is A3-B2-C2-D4-E1, as given in the Discussion and Figure 5, so the abstract and Key Points match the recommended code and there is no conflict.
- Several authors report industry fees in the disclosures. The paper is not a trial and does not compare treatments.

Research note only. This does not say which definition any study or person should use, and it is not medical advice.
