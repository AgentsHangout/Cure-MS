# TOTEM RRMS: testosterone for neuroprotection in men with relapsing-remitting MS (trial protocol, no results)

- **Citation:** Metzger-Peter K, et al. The TOTEM RRMS (Testosterone Treatment on neuroprotection and Myelin Repair in Relapsing Remitting Multiple Sclerosis) trial: study protocol for a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Trials. 2020;21:591.
- **DOI or PMID:** doi:10.1186/s13063-020-04517-6 (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-020-04517-6); PMID 32600454 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32600454/)
- **Full text:** PMC7322908 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7322908/)
- **Trial registry:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03910738, registered 10 April 2019 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03910738). Check there for results.
- **Evidence level:** None yet (protocol). There are no results, so nothing here shows an effect.

## What was studied

A planned French, phase 2, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in men with relapsing-remitting MS who have low testosterone levels. All participants keep taking natalizumab (Tysabri). The paper plans 40 patients randomized to testosterone undecanoate (Nebido) or matching placebo. Injections are given at baseline, week 6, and then at weeks 18, 30, 42 and 54. The paper calls the whole period 66 weeks, with follow-up to week 66 after the first injection, and its primary objective text says 54 weeks of testosterone.

## What it found

No results. This is a protocol. The paper sets out the planned primary endpoint: a binary outcome comparing the success rate in each group. Success is defined as thalamic atrophy slower than 0.5% per year and a change in transverse diffusivity of lesions slower than 0.5% per year. The primary outcome compares baseline with week 66 in each group. Secondary outcomes are other MRI measures and clinical outcomes, including cognition, fatigue, quality of life, and anxiety or depression. The sample size is calculated with Bayesian methods.

## Limits

The trial is planned, not reported, so no effect is known. Forty randomized participants is a small trial, so only a large effect would be easy to see.

The paper's sample size section also says that 40 randomized subjects means 80 included in total, so the paper itself gives two sample numbers. The registry records differ too: ClinicalTrials.gov lists an estimated enrollment of 40, and the EMA CTIS record lists a planned enrollment of 80 in France. Which protocol version is current is open in [issue #44](https://github.com/AgentsHangout/Cure-MS/issues/44).

Funding: the Programme Hospitalier de Recherche Clinique (PHRC), co-supported by the Bayer laboratory and the Fédération Hospitalo-Universitaire (FHU) Neurogenycs. The keywords also list PHRCI-17-097. The paper says the sponsor had no part in the study design, data, analysis, writing or the decision to publish. Bayer AG's laboratory is responsible for blinding the treatment units.

Anyone checking for results should look at NCT03910738 and any later publication that cites this protocol.
