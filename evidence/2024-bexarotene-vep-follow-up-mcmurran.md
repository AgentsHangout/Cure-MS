# CCMR One follow-up: lasting VEP changes after bexarotene

- **Citation:** McMurran CE, Mukherjee T, Brown JWL, Coles AJ, Cunniffe NG. Bexarotene leads to durable improvements in visual evoked potential latency: A follow-up study of the Cambridge Centre for Myelin Repair One trial. *Multiple Sclerosis*, 2024;30(8):1066-1071.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/13524585241233177](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585241233177), PMID [38426437](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38426437/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (an observational long-term follow-up of a selected subset of a randomized trial; groups keep their original randomized assignment, but selective return and later treatment changes can undermine comparability)
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)

## What was studied

A single-center follow-up of the [CCMR One trial](2021-bexarotene-ccmr-one-brown.md), in which
people with relapsing-remitting MS on dimethyl fumarate were randomly assigned to bexarotene or
placebo for 6 months. Of the 31 Cambridge participants, 20 returned (12 bexarotene, 8 placebo), on
average 27 months after their trial participation. The study repeated full-field visual evoked
potentials (VEP), which time the brain's response to a visual pattern (a longer P100 latency
suggests damaged myelin in the visual pathway), and the EDSS disability score. VEP change was
analyzed with a mixed model of eyes nested within people, not as independent observations.

## What it found

- Across all eyes with a usable recording (24 bexarotene, 14 placebo), the change in P100 latency
  from trial baseline favored bexarotene by 7.79 ms (95% CI 0.82 to 14.76 ms shorter; p = 0.044).
- In eyes with a delayed latency at baseline (above 118 ms; 20 bexarotene, 7 placebo), the
  difference was 5.39 ms, and no statistically significant difference was detected (95% CI 16.11 ms
  shorter to 5.32 ms longer; p = 0.343).
- No statistically significant EDSS difference was detected (difference -0.31, 95% CI -1.37 to
  0.74; p = 0.569), including after excluding people with relapses or MRI activity since the trial.
- The authors conclude the VEP improvements were durable, suggesting a lasting remyelinating effect.

## Limits

- Only 20 of the 31 Cambridge participants returned, and the trial's Edinburgh site was not part of
  the follow-up. Recruitment was during the COVID-19 pandemic, and the authors note possible
  selection bias.
- The placebo group was small (8 people, 14 eyes), so the confidence intervals are wide.
- The follow-up used a different VEP device from the trial, which the authors list as a possible
  confounder.
- Some participants had changed treatment since the trial (cladribine: 2 bexarotene, 1 placebo;
  fingolimod: 1 bexarotene), and two had been diagnosed with secondary progressive MS (one per group).
- The result is a nerve-signal measure. No patient-important benefit was established, and the small
  sample leaves substantial uncertainty about function. This follow-up does not show that VEP change
  predicts clinical benefit.
- The original CCMR One trial reported poor tolerability of bexarotene; this follow-up does not
  establish a patient-important clinical benefit.
