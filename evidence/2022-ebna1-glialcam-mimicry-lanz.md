# Antibodies from MS spinal fluid that bind both EBV EBNA1 and the brain protein GlialCAM

- **Citation:** Lanz TV, Brewer RC, Ho PP, et al. Clonally expanded B cells in multiple sclerosis bind EBV EBNA1 and GlialCAM. *Nature*, 2022;603(7900):321-327.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1038/s41586-022-04432-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04432-7), PMID [35073561](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35073561/)
- **Evidence level:** 5 (laboratory study of antibodies and cells, plus a mouse model; the comparison of antibody levels in people is cross-sectional)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors sequenced B cells (the immune cells that make antibodies) from the blood and spinal
fluid of people with MS. They made antibodies from B cells that had multiplied in the spinal fluid
and tested them against viruses linked to MS. They then studied one antibody in detail, including
its crystal structure bound to its target, and tested whether immunizing mice with the EBV protein
EBNA1 made disease worse in a mouse model of MS. They also measured antibodies to EBNA1 and
GlialCAM in people with MS.

## What it found

- An antibody from MS spinal fluid bound tightly to both EBNA1, an EBV protein, and GlialCAM, a
  protein made in the brain and spinal cord. This kind of cross-reaction is called molecular
  mimicry: the immune system mistakes a body protein for a viral one.
- The cross-reaction depended on a chemical change (a post-translational modification) to GlialCAM.
- In mice, EBNA1 immunization made the MS-like disease worse.
- Antibodies to EBNA1 and to GlialCAM were common in people with MS.
- The authors say this gives a mechanism for the link between EBV and MS. That is their
  interpretation; the study shows one possible mechanism, not that it causes MS in most people.

## Limits

- Mostly a laboratory and mouse study (level 5). Mouse results are not yet shown in people.
- The abstract gives no counts of people studied or how often the cross-reactive antibodies occur,
  so how many people with MS carry them needs checking in the full paper.
- Molecular mimicry with GlialCAM is one of several proposed mechanisms, and cross-reactive
  antibodies may also occur in some people without MS.
- Conflicts: two authors own equity in, or consult for, a company (Atreca), and two are named on a
  patent application that includes antibodies to EBV. One author is from Atara Biotherapeutics.
