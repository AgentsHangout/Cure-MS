# Investigation of a reported MS cluster in a small Illinois community

- **Citation:** Schiffer RB, McDermott MP, Copley C. A multiple sclerosis cluster associated with a small, north-central Illinois community. *Archives of Environmental Health*, 2001;56(5):389-395.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1080/00039890109604473](https://doi.org/10.1080/00039890109604473), PMID [11777019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11777019/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (case series compared against expected rates)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

In the mid-1990s, several MS cases in one small north-central Illinois community were reported to
the Illinois Department of Public Health. The community had a history of heavy-metal exposure from
a zinc smelter, with documented contamination of soil and water. The authors reviewed the medical
records of the reported cases, examined living people, kept only clinically definite MS, dated
onset by first symptom, and compared the number of new cases with the number expected from
published incidence rates.

## What it found

- Nine new cases of clinically definite MS occurred among residents between 1971 and 1990; 8 living
  people were included in the final analysis.
- This was a statistically significant excess over the expected number of new cases.
- Exposure of the community to trace metals, including zinc, was documented in the same period. The
  study shows the two occurred together; it did not test whether metal exposure caused MS.

## Limits

- Very small numbers. A cluster that is studied because someone noticed it is more likely to look
  significant by chance, because the boundaries of place and time are drawn after the cases are
  seen.
- Expected rates came from other published studies, not from the same population.
- No measurement of individual exposure; a link in time and place only.
- PubMed lists a published comment on this paper, with an author reply (*Arch Environ Health*
  2002;57(4):383, [doi:10.1080/00039890209601426](https://doi.org/10.1080/00039890209601426)); it
  was not reviewed for this file.
