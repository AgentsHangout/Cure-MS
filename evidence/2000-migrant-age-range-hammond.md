# MS in migrants from the UK and Ireland to Australia, by age at migration

- **Citation:** Hammond SR, English DR, McLeod JG. The age-range of risk of developing multiple sclerosis: evidence from a migrant population in Australia. *Brain*, 2000;123(Pt 5):968-974.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/brain/123.5.968](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/123.5.968), PMID [10775541](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10775541/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (prevalence comparison between groups; it also includes a case-control analysis, which on its own would be level 3)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors compared MS prevalence among people born in Australia and among migrants from the UK
and Ireland (a higher-risk region) living in five regions of Australia at different latitudes.
They compared migrants who moved before age 15 with those who moved at 15 or older, and ran a
case-control study on age at migration.

## What it found

- In people born in Australia, MS prevalence rose strongly with distance south from the equator.
- In migrants from the UK and Ireland, prevalence also tracked latitude, but this was strongly
  driven by high prevalence in Hobart, the southernmost region.
- Except in Hobart, prevalence among migrants was much lower than in their countries of origin.
- Prevalence did not differ significantly between those who migrated before age 15 and those who
  migrated later, and the case-control study found little link between age at migration and risk.
- The authors suggest environmental risk may act over many years, not only in childhood and early
  adulthood.

## Limits

- The abstract does not give case numbers; a reviewer should check the full paper.
- Migrants are a selected group (healthier people may be more likely to migrate), and prevalence
  depends on diagnosis and survival.
- Older migrant studies are often read as pointing to a childhood window of risk; this study does
  not support a sharp cutoff at age 15. A later reassessment of the same migrant population by
  McLeod and colleagues (*J Neurol* 2011,
  [doi:10.1007/s00415-010-5898-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-010-5898-4), PMID
  [21264474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21264474/)) was found in PubMed but not reviewed for
  this file.
