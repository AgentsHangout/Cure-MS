## What was studied

Adults with clinically isolated syndrome or relapsing-onset MS seen at the Brigham Multiple Sclerosis Center in Boston. Patients were split into an "early" cohort with a first clinic visit from 1997 to 2010 (1,536 patients) and a "recent" cohort with a first visit from 2010 to 2020 (656 patients), 2,192 in total. For each cohort, the authors used multivariable logistic regression to find predictors of a clinical relapse in the 3 years after the first visit, and tested interaction terms to see whether predictors differed between eras.

## What it found

- In the early cohort, younger age, female sex, relapsing-remitting disease, more prior relapses and platform therapy use were associated with a relapse in the following 3 years.
- In the recent cohort, age, family history of MS and platform therapy use were predictive.
- The interaction terms for all variables were not significant, and model accuracy was similar across the two eras.
- The authors conclude that predictors did not differ substantially between treatment eras, and that younger age and use of "less effective therapies" were strong risk factors at the first visit. They also stress wide variation in individual relapse risk.

## Limits

- Abstract only. The full text was not read, so the covariates, how "platform therapy" was defined, model checks and the size of each effect are not checked here. The abstract gives no odds ratios.
- Single centre, retrospective, and the cohorts were treated in different eras, so era is tangled with clinic practice and who was seen.
- The question is about relapse prediction, not about whether any therapy changes relapse risk. Nothing here says what anyone should take, stop or do.
