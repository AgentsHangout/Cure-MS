# Pregnancy-related relapses in a contemporary US cohort

- **Citation:** Langer-Gould A, Smith JB, Albers KB, et al. Pregnancy-related relapses and breastfeeding in a contemporary multiple sclerosis cohort. *Neurology*, 2020;94(18):e1939-e1949.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000009374](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000009374), PMID [32284359](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32284359/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (cohort study from electronic health records)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors used prospectively recorded electronic health records at Kaiser Permanente Southern and
Northern California from 2008 to 2016, covering 466 pregnancies in 375 women with MS diagnosed by
current criteria, and their infants. They looked at relapses before, during and after pregnancy,
breastfeeding and use of disease-modifying therapies (DMTs), with models that accounted for
measures of disease severity.

## What it found

- Annualized relapse rates fell from 0.37 before pregnancy to between 0.14 and 0.07 during
  pregnancy (p<0.0001).
- After birth the rate was 0.27 in the first 3 months and back to the prepregnancy rate (0.37) at
  4 to 6 months; the authors saw no "rebound" above the prepregnancy rate.
- In the year after birth, 26.4% of women relapsed. 87% breastfed, 36% breastfed exclusively for at
  least 2 months, and 58.8% did not use DMTs.
- At the start of pregnancy, 67.2% had disease that the authors judged suboptimally controlled.
- Exclusive breastfeeding was associated with a lower risk of early postpartum relapse (adjusted
  hazard ratio 0.37, p=0.009). Measures of disease severity were associated with higher risk.
  Restarting modestly effective DMTs showed no association (p=0.62).
- The authors draw clinical conclusions in their abstract; this file does not repeat them, and
  nothing here is advice.

## Limits

- Observational. Women who breastfed exclusively may have differed in disease activity or other
  ways, even after adjustment, so the breastfeeding result is an association, not a shown effect.
- Most women did not use highly effective DMTs, so this cohort says little about them.
- Members of one health care system (Kaiser Permanente) in California only.
