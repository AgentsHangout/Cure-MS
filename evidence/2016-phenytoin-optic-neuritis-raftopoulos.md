## What was studied

A double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial at two UK academic hospitals, London and Sheffield. People aged 18-60 with acute optic neuritis, seen within 2 weeks of onset, with visual acuity of 6/9 or worse, were randomised 1:1 by minimisation to oral phenytoin or placebo for 3 months. The trial is registered as NCT01451593. Recruitment ran from February 2012 to May 2014.

The maintenance dose was 4 mg/kg per day for people randomised on or before July 16, 2013, and 6 mg/kg per day for those randomised on or after July 17, 2013. The abstract reports 29 phenytoin participants on 4 mg/kg and 13 on 6 mg/kg. So the regimen changed during recruitment, not partway through any one person's treatment.

## What it found

Of 86 randomised (42 phenytoin, 44 placebo), 81 were in the primary analysis (39 phenytoin, 42 placebo). The primary outcome was retinal nerve fibre layer (RNFL) thickness in the affected eye at 6 months, adjusted for fellow-eye RNFL at baseline. The abstract reports a mean 6-month RNFL of 81.46 um (SD 16.27) with phenytoin versus 74.29 um (SD 15.14) with placebo. The mean decrease from baseline was 16.69 um (SD 13.73) with phenytoin and 23.79 um (SD 13.97) with placebo (our arithmetic: 1 - 16.69/23.79 is about 30%, the abstract does not show this calculation). The adjusted 6-month difference was 7.15 um (95% CI 1.08 to 13.22; p=0.021). The authors describe this as "a 30% reduction in the extent of RNFL loss with phenytoin compared with placebo".

Serious adverse events: "five (12%) of 42 patients" on phenytoin versus "two (5%) of 44" on placebo. The abstract says "only one, severe rash, was attributable to phenytoin".

The abstract reports no visual function result (no visual acuity, VEP or other visual outcome). It does not show whether the RNFL difference carried over to what people could see.

The authors conclude that the findings "support the concept of neuroprotection with phenytoin" at concentrations that block sodium channels, and that larger trials in optic neuritis and relapsing MS are warranted.

## Limits

- Small phase 2 trial: 81 analysed, with 5 lost to follow-up. The primary analysis is a modified intention-to-treat population of those followed up at 6 months.
- The primary outcome is a structural measure (RNFL thickness on OCT). It is not remyelination, and the abstract does not show it was felt by people.
- The dose schedule changed during recruitment (4 mg/kg, then 6 mg/kg by randomisation date).
- Only the abstract was read. Visual function, subgroup results and the full adverse-event table may be in the full paper, which is not in PMC.
- Funding: the abstract lists the US National MS Society, the MS Society of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, Novartis, and NIHR and NIHR UCLH/UCL Biomedical Research Centre support.

This is a research note, not medical advice.
