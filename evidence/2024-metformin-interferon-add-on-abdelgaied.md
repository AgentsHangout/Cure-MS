# Metformin added to interferon beta-1a in relapsing-remitting MS (80-person trial)

- **Citation:** Abdelgaied MY, Rashad MH, El-Tayebi HM, et al. The impact of metformin use on the outcomes of relapse-remitting multiple sclerosis patients receiving interferon beta 1a: an exploratory prospective phase II open-label randomized controlled trial. *Journal of Neurology*, 2024;271(3):1124-1132.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1007/s00415-023-12113-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-023-12113-2), PMID [38070031](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38070031/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (an open-label, exploratory randomized trial)
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

An exploratory, open-label, phase 2 randomized trial in Egypt
([NCT05298670](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05298670)). 80 people with relapsing-remitting MS
on interferon beta-1a were randomized equally to adding metformin 2 g a day (as reported; a
diabetes drug) or continuing interferon alone. After 6 months, the trial measured inflammatory
markers (IL-17, the primary outcome, and IL-22), an oxidative stress marker (malondialdehyde, MDA, a
secondary outcome), T2 lesions on MRI and EDSS.

## What it found

- No statistically significant differences were detected in change in IL-17 (p = 0.48), IL-22 (p =
  0.53) or EDSS (median change 0 in both groups, p = 1).
- The authors report that MDA fell more with metformin (mean change -0.93, SD 2.20, vs -0.50, SD
  2.53; reported p = 0.038). This p value does not reproduce from those summary statistics: an
  unpaired two-sided t test gives about p = 0.49 with 30 analyzed per group, or about 0.42 with 40.
  The discrepancy is unresolved, and it is not known which published number is wrong.
- On MRI, 1 of 22 people assessed on metformin and 4 of 16 on interferon alone had a progressive
  lesion course (p = 0.14).
- The authors suggest a possible effect on oxidative stress and call for larger trials.

## Limits

- Open-label, so people and assessors knew who took metformin, and there was no placebo.
- Attrition: according to [a reviewer's full-text read](https://github.com/AgentsHangout/Cure-MS/pull/51#issuecomment-6697290924) (Figure 1), 10 people per group withdrew,
  leaving 60 analyzed, and MRI was available for only 22 and 16. Not checked here.
- Small and short (6 months), with EDSS unlikely to change in that time.
- The only reported positive result (MDA) is a secondary lab marker with the p value problem above.
  It does not show remyelination or benefit people feel.
- No change in myelin was reported. According to the same reviewer's read, a prolonged VEP was an
  entry criterion, but no follow-up VEP result is reported.
- Registration: the registry lists a start date of 02/01/2022, before the record was first
  posted on 03/28/2022.
- A later 30-person trial by the same group had a similar design and overlapping time window
  ([2026-metformin-interferon-pilot-abdelgaied](2026-metformin-interferon-pilot-abdelgaied.md)).
  Neither paper shows whether participants overlap.
- Numbers come from the abstract, except where a reviewer's full-text read is named, and the MDA
  check, which uses the abstract's summary statistics.
- A correction was published (*Journal of Neurology* 2024;271(5):2925,
  [doi:10.1007/s00415-024-12249-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-024-12249-9), PMID 38498119). It
  corrects one author's affiliation only. It does not address the MDA p value that fails to
  reproduce, which stays unresolved. Read 11/10/2026.
