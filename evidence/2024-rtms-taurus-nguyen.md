# Low-intensity rTMS in MS: TAURUS phase I safety and feasibility trial

- **Citation:** Nguyen PT, Zarghami A, Makowiecki K, et al. Low-intensity repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation is safe and well tolerated by people living with MS - outcomes of the phase I randomised controlled trial (TAURUS). Mult Scler J Exp Transl Clin. 2024;10(2):20552173241252571.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.1177/20552173241252571 (https://doi.org/10.1177/20552173241252571); PMID 38756414 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38756414/); PMCID PMC11097717
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial). Phase I safety and feasibility trial, small sample.

## What was studied

People living with MS in a randomised (2:1), placebo-controlled, single-blind, parallel-group phase I trial. Twenty participants were randomised: 13 to active low-intensity rTMS and 7 to sham, with the coil at 0 degrees (active) or 90 degrees (sham). Active rTMS was intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS), 20 sessions over 4-5 weeks, 600 iTBS pulses per hemisphere per session, at 25% of maximum stimulator output. The primary aim was safety and tolerability. Secondary aims were feasibility, blinding, and exploratory MRI, patient-reported outcome (PROM) and cognitive or motor measures. The protocol paper describes eligibility as relapsing-remitting or secondary progressive MS, aged 18-65.

## What it found

Five adverse events were reported, including one serious adverse event. None were related to treatment. Protocol compliance was 85%, and the authors describe blinding as successful. Within-participant MRI metrics, PROMs and cognitive or motor performance were unchanged over time. The authors conclude that 20 sessions of rTMS were safe and well tolerated in a small group, and that the protocol and procedures are feasible.

The remyelination rationale comes from mouse data, where iTBS increased new oligodendrocytes in the adult mouse brain. This trial did not show remyelination in people.

## Limits

- Only 20 participants (13 active, 7 sham). Too small to rule out rarer harms or to show any effect on disability or MRI.
- Phase I safety and feasibility design. It was not designed to test efficacy, and the authors say the sham needs improving before further efficacy or safety work.
- The sham uses a 90 degree coil position. The authors state that improving the sham is warranted before further investigation.
- The MRI, PROM and cognitive or motor measures were exploratory. No change does not show absence of effect in a trial this size.
- Follow-up covers only the 4-5 week treatment period.
- Rationale rests on mouse data (level 5), which is not evidence in people.
