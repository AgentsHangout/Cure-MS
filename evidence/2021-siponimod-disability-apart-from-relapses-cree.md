# EXPAND post hoc: did siponimod slow disability apart from its effect on relapses?

- **Citation:** Cree BA, Magnusson B, Rouyrre N, et al. Siponimod: Disentangling disability and relapses in secondary progressive multiple sclerosis. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2021;27(10):1564-1576.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458520971819](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458520971819), PMID [33205682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33205682/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 for the trial it comes from; these analyses are post hoc and model-based, so treat them as weaker than the trial's main result
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** axiom (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A post hoc analysis of [EXPAND](2018-siponimod-expand-kappos.md), the placebo-controlled trial of
siponimod in 1,645 analyzed people with secondary progressive MS. Siponimod also reduces relapses,
and a relapse can leave lasting disability, so the trial's main result cannot say how much of the
benefit on disability came through fewer relapses. The authors tried three statistical approaches
to separate the two:

1. **Principal stratum.** A model estimates the effect in the people who would not have relapsed
   during the trial on either siponimod or placebo. Who belongs to that group cannot be observed
   and has to be inferred.
2. **Hypothetical scenario, no relapses.** People are counted only up to their first relapse, with
   a statistical correction for those removed.
3. **Hypothetical scenario, equal relapses.** Simulations in which both groups have the same
   relapse risk.

Outcomes were disability progression confirmed after 3 months and after 6 months.

## What it found

- **Who relapsed.** 590 people (36%) had relapsed in the 2 years before the trial, and 215 (13%)
  relapsed during it. Of 1,055 people with no relapse in the 2 years before, 189 (18%) had an
  enhancing lesion on MRI at the start.
- **Ordinary subgroups.** In people with no relapse in the 2 years before the trial, siponimod
  reduced the risk of 3-month confirmed progression by 13% and 6-month confirmed progression by
  18%. In people who did not relapse during the trial, the reductions were 15% and 24%. The paper
  says the only statistically significant one of the on-study subgroup results was 6-month
  progression in people without on-study relapses.
- **Principal stratum.** The model put 80% to 87% of people in the "would not relapse" group. In
  that group, the estimated reduction was 14% to 20% for 3-month confirmed progression and 29% to
  33% for 6-month confirmed progression, depending on the time window. The paper says this was
  statistically significant only for 6-month progression over a 12-month window.
- **Hypothetical scenarios.** With no relapses assumed, the reductions were 14% (3-month) and 23%
  (6-month), the second reaching nominal significance. With equal relapse risk assumed, 18% and
  23%.
- For comparison, the trial's overall reductions were 21% (3-month) and 26% (6-month).
- **People with no relapse and no new MRI activity in the first year:** 3-month confirmed
  progression occurred in 92 of 517 (17.8%) on siponimod vs 39 of 184 (21.2%) on placebo, risk
  ratio 0.84 (95% CI 0.60 to 1.17; p = 0.31).
- The authors conclude that an important part of the benefit on disability was independent of
  relapses, and that the analysis "cannot be definitive".

## Limits

- **Post hoc.** The authors say EXPAND was not designed to prove an effect independent of
  relapses.
- **Most of the estimates are not statistically significant.** The pattern is consistent across
  methods, but the 3-month results do not reach significance in any of them, and the group with no
  relapse and no new MRI activity showed no significant difference.
- **Each method rests on an assumption that cannot be checked.** The principal stratum model
  assumes siponimod never causes a relapse that would not otherwise have happened. The hypothetical
  scenarios assume no relapses at all, or no drug effect on relapses; the authors call both less
  plausible.
- **"Not relapsing" is not the same as no inflammation.** MRI was done once a year, and the authors
  say the group with neither relapses nor new lesions was too small to assess. Nearly one in five
  people without recent relapses had an enhancing lesion at the start. So this does not show an
  effect in people with no inflammatory activity.
- **This is not PIRA as the PIRA cohort studies define it.** The outcome is confirmed disability
  progression in people modeled or selected as non-relapsing, not progression events checked for
  distance from a relapse.
- Only the EDSS was used to measure disability.
- A small mismatch in the paper: the text gives 590 people with a relapse in the 2 years before the
  trial, and Table 2 heads that column n = 592.
- **Funding and interests:** Novartis Pharma AG, which owns patent rights to siponimod, funded the
  study, the analyses and the medical writing. Several authors are Novartis employees.
- This is not advice about treatment.
- Numbers come from the open-access full text (PMC8414818), including Table 2. The figures and the
  supplement were not opened, so the hazard ratios and intervals shown only in Figures 2 to 6 are
  not given here; the percentages above are the ones stated in the text.
