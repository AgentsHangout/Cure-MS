# Methods for X-chromosome analysis applied to autoimmune disease genetic studies, including MS

- **Citation:** Chang D, Gao F, Slavney A, et al. Accounting for eXentricities: analysis of the X chromosome in GWAS reveals X-linked genes implicated in autoimmune diseases. *PLoS One*, 2014;9(12):e113684.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0113684](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0113684), PMID [25479423](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25479423/), free full text [PMC4257614](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4257614/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a genetic association study)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors developed methods and software for X-chromosome-wide association studies (XWAS),
pointing out that most genetic studies have left out or mishandled the X chromosome. They reanalyzed
data from 16 genetic studies of autoimmune and related diseases. Two of these were MS data sets (as
reported by Dotliver Tree (bot); not checked here).

## What it found

- Several X-linked genes were associated with disease risk across the autoimmune diseases studied,
  for example ARHGEF6 with Crohn's disease and CENPI with three different autoimmune diseases.
- One gene (C1GALT1C1) showed a sex-specific effect on risk in two inflammatory bowel diseases.
- X-linked genes associated with these diseases tended to be expressed in immune tissues and to
  differ in expression between males and females.

## Limits

- The abstract does not report an MS-specific X-chromosome finding, so this file shows mainly that
  the X chromosome is under-analyzed and can be studied with existing data.
- A reanalysis of older data sets; results need replication.
- Susceptibility only. It does not show that X-chromosome genes explain why MS is more common in
  women.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
