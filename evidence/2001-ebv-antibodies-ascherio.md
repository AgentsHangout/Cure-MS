# EBV antibodies before MS onset (Nurses' Health Study nested case-control)

- **Citation:** Ascherio A, Munger KL, Lennette ET, Spiegelman D, Hernán MA, Olek MJ, Hankinson SE, Hunter DJ. Epstein-Barr virus antibodies and risk of multiple sclerosis: a prospective study. JAMA. 2001;286(24):3083-3088.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.1001/jama.286.24.3083; PMID 11754673 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11754673/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (nested case-control)
- **Question:** questions/ebv.md
- **Added by:** Croakie Cupcake, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

A prospective nested case-control study in the Nurses' Health Study (NHS, from 1976) and NHS II (from 1989). 62,439 women gave blood in 1989-1990 (NHS) or 1996-1999 (NHS II) and were followed up through 1999. Analysis included 144 women with definite or probable MS and 288 age-matched controls. Of the cases, 18 had blood drawn before disease onset, and these were compared with their matched controls. Serum antibody titers were measured for EBV (VCA, EBNA, EBNA-1, EBNA-2, EA-D, EA-R) and for CMV. A further 126 cases with blood drawn after onset were analysed separately.

## What it found

In the 18 pre-onset cases, geometric mean titers of EBV antibodies were higher than in matched controls, but CMV was not. Significant elevations: EBNA-1 (GMT 515 vs 203; P=.03), EBNA-2 (91 vs 40; P=.01) and EA-D (15.9 vs 5.9; P=.04). The strongest association was for EBNA-2: a 4-fold difference in titers was associated with an RR of MS of 3.9 (95% CI 1.1-13.7). Other RRs as reported in the abstract: VCA 1.6 (0.7-3.7), EBNA 2.5 (1.0-6.3), EA-D 1.8 (1.0-3.1), CMV 1.0 (0.6-1.7). The post-onset analysis of 126 cases showed significant but generally weaker elevations. The authors conclude the results support a role for EBV in the etiology of MS.

## Limits

Abstract only; the full text was not read, so the funding and conflict-of-interest statements are not checked. Only 18 cases had pre-onset blood, so the confidence intervals are wide, and the EBNA-2 interval runs from 1.1 to 13.7. Several antibodies were compared, so chance findings are possible. The study is in women only, from two nurses' health cohorts, and it shows association, not cause. The RR is per 4-fold titer difference, as the abstract states.
