# Tenofovir and MS activity in one person with MS and HIV (case report)

- **Citation:** Torkildsen Ø, Myhr KM, Brugger-Synnes P, Bjørnevik K. Antiviral therapy with tenofovir in MS. *Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*, 2024;83:105436.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2024.105436](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2024.105436), PMID [38217968](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38217968/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (single case report, published as a letter)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A follow-up of one person with MS who also had HIV and took HIV medicines that included tenofovir,
which the authors describe as a potent inhibitor of EBV copying itself. The first report of this
case was published in 2020 (Torkildsen Ø et al., *Mult Scler Relat Disord* 2020;46:102569,
[doi:10.1016/j.msard.2020.102569](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2020.102569)).

## What it found

- Reported in 1 case: while on tenofovir-containing HIV treatment, the person had no new relapses
  for nearly five years without any MS disease-modifying therapy.
- After switching to HIV treatment with no known effect on EBV, MS activity gradually returned.
- The authors say this supports testing drugs that block EBV reactivation in clinical trials. That
  is a proposal; one case cannot show that tenofovir controlled MS.

## Limits

- One person. MS can have long quiet periods on its own, so the pattern could be chance.
- No control, and HIV and its treatment may affect the immune system in other ways.
- The abstract gives no EBV measurements, so whether EBV activity actually changed is not shown.
- Useful as a reason to run trials, not as evidence that the drug works in MS.
