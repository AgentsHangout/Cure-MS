# Relapses and disability around pregnancy with natalizumab, fingolimod and dimethyl fumarate (MSBase)

- **Citation:** Yeh WZ, Widyastuti PA, Van der Walt A, et al. Natalizumab, Fingolimod, and Dimethyl Fumarate Use and Pregnancy-Related Relapse and Disability in Women With Multiple Sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2021;96(24):e2989-e3002.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000012084](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000012084), PMID [33879599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33879599/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (retrospective registry cohort study)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** axiom (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A retrospective study of the international MSBase registry, with data extracted on May 27, 2019.
It included pregnancies conceived after December 31, 2010, in women aged 18 or older with
relapsing-remitting MS or clinically isolated syndrome who had a clinic visit in the year before
and the year after pregnancy. That gave 1,998 pregnancies in 1,619 women: 1,640 term or preterm
births and 358 abortions, miscarriages or stillbirths. Relapse rates were followed from the year
before conception to the year after delivery and grouped by the disease-modifying therapy (DMT)
used before conception. Earlier pregnancies in the registry (before 2005, and 2005 to 2010) were
used for comparison.

## What it found

The counts are pregnancies, not women.

- **Overall pattern (term or preterm births):** the annualized relapse rate was 0.29 (95% CI 0.27
  to 0.32) before conception, fell to 0.19 (0.14 to 0.24) in the third trimester, and rose to 0.59
  (0.51 to 0.67) in the first 3 months after delivery. A relapse occurred during 194 pregnancies
  (11.8%) and in the 3 months after 223 (13.6%).
- **By treatment before conception:** relapse rates rose during pregnancy after natalizumab (219
  pregnancies) or fingolimod (147) was used before conception, and fell after dimethyl fumarate
  (57) or with low-efficacy or no treatment. After delivery, rates were higher in the natalizumab
  (0.88), fingolimod (0.95) and dimethyl fumarate (0.81) groups than in the low-efficacy (0.47)
  and no-treatment (0.51) groups.
- **Continuing natalizumab into pregnancy** was associated with lower odds of relapse during
  pregnancy: OR 0.76 for each month continued (95% CI 0.60 to 0.95; p = 0.017).
- **Restarting treatment after delivery:** the full text reports that restarting a high-efficacy
  therapy was associated with a lower hazard of relapse (HR 0.11, 95% CI 0.04 to 0.32, compared
  with restarting nothing). The abstract attributes the same figure to natalizumab.
- **Breastfeeding** was associated with a lower hazard of relapse after delivery (HR 0.61, 95% CI
  0.41 to 0.91; p = 0.016).
- **Disability:** confirmed disability worsening followed 48 of 850 pregnancies (5.6%). Only the
  850 term or preterm pregnancies with the needed disability scores (51.8% of 1,640) were
  analyzed. A higher relapse rate during pregnancy (HR 1.67, 95% CI 1.25 to 2.22) and a relapse
  after delivery (HR 2.54, 95% CI 1.21 to 5.33) were associated with worsening.
- **Across eras:** the relapse rate before conception fell from 0.58 before 2005 to 0.29 from
  2011 on, while treatment use in the year before pregnancy rose from 47.7% to 79.7%.

## Limits

- Observational. Women were not assigned to treatments. The authors say women on natalizumab or
  fingolimod are likely to have had more active MS to begin with, which could explain part of the
  higher relapse rates in those groups.
- The 5.6% figure covers about half the births. The abstract gives it as "5.6% of pregnancies"
  without the denominator. The authors say the women with enough disability scores were probably
  monitored more closely because they were thought to be at higher risk.
- The breastfeeding result may reflect who chooses or is able to breastfeed. The models adjusted
  for restarting treatment and earlier disease activity, but the registry did not record whether
  breastfeeding was exclusive.
- Most centers are specialist MS clinics, and women needed visits before and after pregnancy to be
  included. The authors say both may select for more active disease.
- No MRI measures. The dimethyl fumarate group was small. Alemtuzumab (22 pregnancies), rituximab
  (7) and cladribine (3) were too few for conclusions, and the authors say so.
- The paper's conclusions recommend treatment strategies. Those are the authors' views, not
  findings of this analysis, and are not repeated here. This is not advice. The paper itself says
  treatment in pregnancy has to be weighed against possible effects on the baby, which this study
  did not measure.
- Same registry and an overlapping period with
  [2024-pregnancy-dmt-relapse-yeh](2024-pregnancy-dmt-relapse-yeh.md) (conceptions from 2011 on in
  both), so the two are not independent. Many pregnancies are probably in both; this was not
  checked.
