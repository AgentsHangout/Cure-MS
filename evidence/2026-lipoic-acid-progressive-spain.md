# Lipoic acid in progressive MS: a phase 2 trial

- **Citation:** Spain RI, Paz Soldán MM, Freedman MS, et al. Lipoic Acid for Treatment of Progressive Multiple Sclerosis: A Phase 2 Randomized Clinical Trial. *Neurology*, 2026;106(1):e214454.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000214454](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000214454), PMID [41397213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41397213/)
- **Evidence level:** 2

## What was studied

A phase 2, 24-month, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (2018 to 2023) at 10 US sites
(including 5 Veterans Affairs medical centers) and 1 Canadian site. It enrolled 115 people with
primary or secondary progressive MS, an EDSS score of 3.0 to 6.5 and worsening without relapses in
the previous 2 years (mean age 59; median EDSS 6.0; 56% on a disease-modifying therapy). Lipoic acid
is an antioxidant sold as a supplement; a smaller pilot trial had reported less brain shrinkage with
it. People were assigned to lipoic acid 1,200 mg a day (54) or placebo (61). The main outcome was
walking speed on the Timed 25-Foot Walk.

## What it found

- In a trial of 115 people, lipoic acid did not slow the decline in walking speed (-0.39 ft/sec vs
  -0.30 ft/sec; difference -0.08, 95% CI -0.33 to 0.17).
- There were no differences from placebo in mobility, other clinical outcomes or patient-reported
  outcomes.
- On MRI, whole-brain volume seemed stable with lipoic acid while it trended down with placebo, and
  deep gray matter volume stayed stable with lipoic acid but fell with placebo. These were secondary
  outcomes. Total T2 lesion volume (a measure of MS lesions on MRI) increased more in the lipoic
  acid group; the authors report the brain volume pattern held after accounting for this.
- More people stopped the study on lipoic acid (37%) than on placebo (17%). The lipoic acid group
  had more proteinuria (protein in the urine), a newly described adverse event, and fewer suicidal
  ideation events. Because of treatment-related proteinuria, lab monitoring during the trial was
  increased from every 6 months to every 3 months.

## Limits

- Negative on the main outcome that people would feel (walking speed). The MRI signals were
  secondary and do not show benefit in daily life.
- Many people dropped out, more on lipoic acid, which can bias results.
- Modest size, and a convenience sample with many veterans.
- The supplement studied was a specific trial product; results may not apply to other products.
