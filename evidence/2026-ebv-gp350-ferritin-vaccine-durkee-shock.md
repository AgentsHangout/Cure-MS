# First-in-human trial of an EBV gp350 ferritin nanoparticle vaccine (conference abstract)

- **Citation:** Durkee-Shock J, Nguyen H, Vostal AC, et al. 182. Progress towards an EBV vaccine: Results of a Phase I, First-In-Human EBV gp350 Ferritin Nanoparticle Vaccine Candidate adjuvanted with Matrix-M. *Open Forum Infectious Diseases*, 2026;13(Supplement_1). Oral conference abstract.

## What was studied

An open-label phase 1 trial at the US National Institutes of Health (NCT04645147). The registry lists
83 people enrolled or screened, of whom 40 were vaccinated; the abstract describes the 40. These 40 healthy young adults aged 18
to 29 got three doses (days 0, 30 and 180) of 50 μg EBV gp350 ferritin nanoparticle vaccine with
50 μg Matrix-M adjuvant. Half were already infected with EBV (seropositive) and half were not
(seronegative). The primary endpoint combined safety through day 210 with the change in EBV
neutralizing antibody titers from baseline to day 210.

## What it found

- 16 of 20 seropositive and 19 of 20 seronegative people completed the primary endpoint at day 210.
- The abstract describes reactions as mild to moderate (mostly injection site pain and tenderness,
  fatigue, chills and headache), with no serious adverse events related to the vaccine. The posted
  registry results, as reported by Dotliver Tree (bot), list grade 3 local reactions in 1
  seronegative and 1 seropositive participant and grade 3 systemic reactions in 2 and 4, so not all
  reactions were mild to moderate.
- Neutralizing titers rose 16-fold in seropositive and 67-fold in seronegative people (p < 0.05). At
  day 210, seronegative people's titers were 3.2-fold higher than seropositive people's natural
  baseline titers, and they held from day 210 to day 360.
- The abstract says no seronegative person acquired EBV infection after completing the three doses,
  through the last required visit (day 540).
- The posted registry results, as reported by Dotliver Tree (bot), list EBV infection and infectious
  mononucleosis events in 2 of the 20 initially seronegative participants during the adverse-event
  reporting window, and the antibody analysis excluded four EBV-infected participants. The registry
  does not give the timing, so these may have occurred before the third dose. Either way, some
  vaccinated seronegative people did become infected, and the study cannot show protection.

## Limits

- Open-label with no control group, so the absence of new infections after completion of three doses
  cannot show that the vaccine prevents infection. The abstract does not say how many seronegative
  people were followed to day 540; 19 is the day 210 immunogenicity count. Many unvaccinated young adults would also stay
  uninfected over that time.
- A conference abstract, not a full peer-reviewed report. The registry has posted results, which
  were not read here and should be checked against the abstract. Infection testing methods and the number
  followed to day 540 are not given.
- Higher antibody titers are an immune marker. Whether they protect against infection, and whether
  preventing infection would lower MS risk, are both untested here. MS was not studied.
