# Systematic review of clinical trials of remyelination-promoting treatments

- **Citation:** Makhzoum H, El Majzoub R, Ismail A, et al. Interventions promoting remyelination in multiple sclerosis: a systematic review of clinical trials. *Neurological Research*, 2025;47(10):901-918.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1080/01616412.2025.2507756](https://doi.org/10.1080/01616412.2025.2507756), PMID [40393003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40393003/)
- **Evidence level:** 3, provisional. It is a systematic review of mixed designs: the abstract says both randomized trials (rated with RoB 2.0) and non-randomized trials (rated with ROBINS-I) were included, but not how many of each. It is not treated here as a level-1 synthesis of randomized trials, and the rating should be revisited once the full text is checked.

## What was studied

A systematic review of clinical trials of treatments thought to promote remyelination in MS. It
searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science and the Cochrane Library in May 2024 and included trials
that measured remyelination with magnetization transfer ratio, VEP, myelin water fraction, diffusion
imaging or optical coherence tomography. The abstract reports no pooled estimate; whether the full
paper contains any meta-analysis has not been checked.

## What it found

- 25 studies of 17 interventions, with 3,341 participants in total, were included from 1,615
  screened records.
- The authors report that most studies had a moderate risk of bias and that all interventions but
  one were generally safe and well tolerated.
- The authors list rHIgM22, liothyronine (L-T3), opicinumab, clemastine, phenytoin, domperidone,
  GSK239512, human fetal neural precursor cells and low-intensity transcranial magnetic stimulation
  as showing "remyelination potential," along with some approved treatments (ocrelizumab,
  fingolimod and natalizumab).
- They name small samples, short follow-up and no standard clinical endpoints showing that
  remyelination improves function as the main limits of the field.

## Limits

- "Remyelination potential" rests on imaging and nerve-signal measures, without established
  patient-important functional benefit.
- External context, not necessarily part of this review's search: in trials this repo has files
  for, opicinumab did not meet its main outcomes ([RENEW](2017-opicinumab-renew-cadavid.md),
  [SYNERGY](2019-opicinumab-synergy-cadavid.md), and the later
  [AFFINITY](2026-opicinumab-affinity-calabresi.md)). The search ended in May 2024, before the
  peer-reviewed TRAP-MS report ([2025-clemastine-trap-ms-kocot](2025-clemastine-trap-ms-kocot.md)),
  in which the clemastine arm in progressive MS was stopped for safety.
- Not verified here: the exact split of randomized and non-randomized studies, which intervention
  was the safety exception, study-level risk of bias, whether participants are counted only once,
  and whether any results were pooled.
- Numbers come from the abstract; the publisher full text was not accessible.
