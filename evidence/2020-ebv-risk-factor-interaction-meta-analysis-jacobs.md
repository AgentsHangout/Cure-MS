# Systematic review: does EBV interact with other MS risk factors?

- **Citation:** Jacobs BM, Giovannoni G, Cuzick J, Dobson R. Systematic review and meta-analysis of the association between Epstein-Barr virus, multiple sclerosis and other risk factors. *Multiple Sclerosis*, 2020;26(11):1281-1297.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458520907901](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458520907901), PMID [32202208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32202208/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies, not of randomized trials)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A systematic review and meta-analysis asking whether EBV combines with other MS risk factors, such
as the HLA-DRB1*1501 risk gene and smoking, to raise risk by more than each would alone. PubMed was
searched for papers on EBV and MS or clinically isolated syndrome. Interaction was tested on two
scales: additive (do the two risks together add up to more than their sum?) and multiplicative.

## What it found

- 262 full-text papers were reviewed.
- EBV antibody level and HLA genotype interacted on the additive scale (attributable proportion due
  to interaction 0.48, p < 0.0001).
- Past infectious mononucleosis was associated with higher odds of MS in people with HLA-DRB1*1501,
  but not in people without it.
- Smoking was associated with higher MS risk in people with high EBV antibody levels (OR 2.76) but
  not in people with low levels (OR 1.16).
- No interaction was found on the multiplicative scale.
- The authors conclude that EBV appears to interact with some established risk factors, and that
  how EBV influences MS risk is still unknown.

## Limits

- The underlying studies are observational, so these are associations. An interaction in the
  statistics does not show that the factors act together biologically.
- The finding depends on the scale: additive interaction was found, multiplicative was not.
- The abstract gives no confidence intervals for the smoking odds ratios and no numbers for the
  mononucleosis and HLA result.
- One database (PubMed) was searched, per the abstract.
- EBV antibody levels were often measured after MS began, so high levels could partly follow the
  disease.
- Numbers come from the abstract. The open-access full text (PMC7543008) was not read, so the
  number of studies in each pooled estimate is not given here.
