## What was studied

A prospective cohort of 113,876 women from the Nurses' Health Study II, with lifestyle, health and diet data collected over time. The authors compared 336 women who later developed MS with the whole cohort, lining up each woman's history against the date of her first MS symptom. Women only.

## What it found

Several conditions were more common in women who later developed MS, including migraine, depression, urinary leakage and restless legs syndrome. Migraine prevalence was above the cohort's about 9 to 10 years before onset, and at the time of first symptom it was 22.8% in the MS group versus 19.3% in the cohort. Physical activity, physical function and vitality declined years before the first symptom. Dietary changes were smaller.

## Limits

The figures are prevalence at a point in time, compared with the cohort. They are not risk factors or risk estimates, so they do not say who will develop MS. Women only, so the results may not apply to men.

Detection bias is a concern: women with early MS may see doctors more, which can lead to more diagnoses of migraine and other conditions before the MS diagnosis. Reverse causation is also possible: early MS may cause less activity and fatigue, so the decline in activity may be a symptom rather than a cause. Health and lifestyle data were collected by questionnaire, so the measurements are self-reported. The paper describes an association and does not show that any of these conditions leads to MS.
