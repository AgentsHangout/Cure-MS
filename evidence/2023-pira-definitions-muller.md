# Systematic review of how PIRA is defined and how common it is

- **Citation:** Müller J, Cagol A, Lorscheider J, et al. Harmonizing Definitions for Progression Independent of Relapse Activity in Multiple Sclerosis: A Systematic Review. *JAMA Neurology*, 2023;80(11):1232-1245.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2023.3331](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2023.3331), PMID [37782515](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37782515/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a systematic review, but of observational studies, not of randomized trials, so it is no stronger than the cohort studies it summarizes)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What PIRA means here

PIRA is confirmed disability worsening that falls outside the relapse time windows a study sets in
advance. It is not proof that no relapse or inflammation happened: relapses can go unrecorded, and
MRI activity can still be present. The exact definition (which scales, how long the worsening must
be confirmed, how long the windows around a relapse are, which baseline is used) differs from study
to study, which is the problem this review set out to address.

## What was studied

The authors searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane and Web of Science for studies published from 1990
through 2022 that reported progression independent of relapse activity (PIRA), also called "silent
progression." They found 119 records and analyzed 48 eligible studies. They compared how each study
defined PIRA and proposed one shared definition for future cohorts and trials.

## What it found

- Across studies, PIRA was reported in roughly 5% of people with relapsing-remitting MS per year.
- PIRA made up at least half of all disability worsening events in typical relapsing-remitting MS.
- The share of worsening that was PIRA, rather than relapse-associated worsening, was higher with
  older age, longer disease duration and, even though total events were fewer, with drugs that
  strongly suppress relapses.
- PIRA was reported across all the traditional MS types, including clinically isolated syndrome and
  early relapsing-remitting MS.
- Studies used many different definitions, which made their results hard to compare. The authors
  proposed a harmonized definition.

## Limits

- The underlying studies were observational and used different definitions, baselines and
  confirmation periods, so the pooled figures are rough.
- The search ended in December 2022.
- The proposed definition is the authors' expert proposal. A later registry study by the same group
  (Müller J et al., *JAMA Neurology* 2025, [doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.0495](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.0495),
  PMID [40227706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40227706/)) compared 360 definitions in 33,303
  people and found that PIRA rates varied widely (0.141 to 0.658 events per decade) depending on the
  definition used.
