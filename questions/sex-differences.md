# Why is MS two to three times more common in women than in men, and what do pregnancy, sex hormones and the X chromosome tell us about what drives it?

- **Issue:** not yet opened (question G)
- **Status:** Evidence being gathered. No finding yet.

**This is not medical advice.** This file collects published research. It does not tell anyone how
to diagnose, treat or manage MS, and it says nothing about what anyone should do about pregnancy,
contraception, breastfeeding, hormones or treatment. Those are decisions for each person and their
own clinicians.

## The question

In most countries, women are diagnosed with MS two to three times as often as men. This question
asks:

1. How big is the difference between women and men, and has it changed over time?
2. What happens to MS during pregnancy and after birth, and does modern treatment change that
   pattern?
3. Do sex hormones (such as estriol, a pregnancy estrogen, or testosterone) change MS activity, and
   what have trials of them found?
4. Do the X chromosome, or other genetic differences between the sexes, help explain the
   difference?
5. Once someone has MS, does the course of the disease differ by sex?

## Why it matters

The sex difference is one of the largest and most consistent facts about MS, and it is not
explained. If the ratio has changed within a few generations, genes alone are unlikely to explain
it, since they do not change that fast. But a changing ratio does not by itself prove an
environmental cause: changes in ascertainment (how cases are found and diagnosed) and in the
population have to be considered first. Pregnancy is a natural experiment:
relapses fall late in pregnancy and rise after birth, which points to hormones or immune changes
that might be studied. Sex chromosomes and hormones can be separated in animal studies, which helps
decide which to study in people. And if men and women differ in how MS progresses, that matters for
how studies are designed and read.

## What would answer it

- Population-based incidence data (new cases, not just people living with MS) by sex, over many
  decades and in more than one country and ancestry, with attention to changes in diagnosis.
- Prospective studies that measure candidate exposures (for example smoking, body size, age at
  first period, number of pregnancies, hormone use) before MS begins, separately in women and men,
  and Mendelian randomization studies where possible.
- Large, recent pregnancy cohorts that report relapses and disability by treatment, and studies
  that measure hormones or immune changes across pregnancy.
- Randomized trials of sex hormones that are large enough to give clear answers, ideally phase 3,
  and in both women and men.
- Genetic studies that analyze the X chromosome properly (it is often left out of genome-wide
  studies) and look for effects that differ by sex, plus studies of X-inactivation and of people
  with unusual numbers of sex chromosomes.
- Human studies that test what mouse studies separating hormones from chromosomes suggest.

### How big the sex difference is, and whether it changed over time

| --- | --- | --- |
| [2006-sex-ratio-canada-orton](../evidence/2006-sex-ratio-canada-orton.md) | 4 | In 27,074 Canadians with MS, the female-to-male ratio by year of birth rose for at least 50 years and exceeded 3.2 to 1 (r=0.84). |
| [2018-incidence-by-sex-denmark-koch-henriksen](../evidence/2018-incidence-by-sex-denmark-koch-henriksen.md) | 4 | In 19,536 Danes with MS onset 1950-2009, incidence in women more than doubled (5.91 to 12.33 per 100,000 per year); in men it rose from 4.52 to 6.08. | [2020-global-prevalence-atlas-walton](../evidence/2020-global-prevalence-atlas-walton.md) | 4 | Country-reported data: about 2.8 million people live with MS worldwide, and females are twice as likely as males to live with MS. |

### Pregnancy and relapses after birth

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1998-pregnancy-relapse-prims-confavreux](../evidence/1998-pregnancy-relapse-prims-confavreux.md) | 3 | In 254 women (269 pregnancies), relapse rates fell from 0.7 per year before pregnancy to 0.2 in the third trimester, rose to 1.2 in the first 3 months after birth, then returned to the prepregnancy rate. |
| [2020-postpartum-relapse-breastfeeding-langer-gould](../evidence/2020-postpartum-relapse-breastfeeding-langer-gould.md) | 3 | In 466 pregnancies (2008-2016), relapse rates fell from 0.37 before to 0.14-0.07 during pregnancy and were 0.27 in the first 3 months after birth, with no rise above the prepregnancy rate. |
| [2021-pregnancy-relapse-disability-msbase-yeh](../evidence/2021-pregnancy-relapse-disability-msbase-yeh.md) | 3 | In 1,998 pregnancies in the MSBase registry, relapse rates rose during pregnancy after natalizumab or fingolimod was used before conception and rose after delivery in every treatment group; breastfeeding was associated with fewer relapses after delivery (HR 0.61); confirmed disability worsening followed 48 of the 850 births with enough disability scores (5.6%). Not independent of Yeh 2024 below. |
| [2024-pregnancy-dmt-relapse-yeh](../evidence/2024-pregnancy-dmt-relapse-yeh.md) | 3 | In 2,009 live births in the MSBase registry, the share of pregnancies (resulting in live births) followed by a relapse within 6 months after birth varied by treatment group, from 4.1% (ocrelizumab, 3 of 73 pregnancies) to 30.5% (natalizumab stopped early, 25 of 82, a selected subset of 419 natalizumab pregnancies, not an exhaustive split). |
| [2025-pregnancy-moderate-severe-disability-shipley](../evidence/2025-pregnancy-moderate-severe-disability-shipley.md) | 3 | Overall, 575 pregnant women with EDSS 3 or more were compared with 1,056 matched non-pregnant women (MSBase); time to disability worsening did not differ significantly (HR 1.15, 95% CI 0.96 to 1.38). In the relapse analysis of 469 women with live-birth pregnancies, rates fell 75% in the first trimester and rose 36% in the first 3 postpartum months. |

### Births, onset timing and later MS course

The pregnancy and births studies are all observational, and having MS can itself shape whether and
when people have children, so those links may run in either direction. The hormone therapy rows
include one small randomized pilot (Bove 2022), one registry cohort and one marker study.

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2020-pregnancy-cis-onset-nguyen](../evidence/2020-pregnancy-cis-onset-nguyen.md) | 3 | In 2,557 women with CIS, earlier pregnancy went with a first event a median 3.3 years later (HR 0.68); more pregnancies did not delay it further. |
| [2020-parity-disability-israel-achiron](../evidence/2020-parity-disability-israel-achiron.md) | 3 | In 2,281 women with relapsing-remitting MS, giving birth after onset went with lower risk of moderate disability (HR 0.68) but not severe disability; no dose-response. |
| [2009-parity-secondary-progression-koch](../evidence/2009-parity-secondary-progression-koch.md) | 3 | In 277 women, number of births was not independently associated with secondary progression. |
| [2020-reproductive-history-progressive-ms-zeydan](../evidence/2020-reproductive-history-progressive-ms-zeydan.md) | 3 and 4 | Women with MS had fewer full-term pregnancies than controls; fewer pregnancies and earlier menopause went with earlier progressive onset in a small clinic series. |
| [2022-hormone-therapy-danish-registry-kopp](../evidence/2022-hormone-therapy-danish-registry-kopp.md) | 3 | In 3,325 Danish women with MS (333 used hormone therapy), no association with disability accrual was found, with wide confidence intervals and a trend toward more risk with longer use; observational. |
| [2022-bazedoxifene-estrogen-menopause-trial-bove](../evidence/2022-bazedoxifene-estrogen-menopause-trial-bove.md) | 2 | A 2-month randomized pilot in 24 menopausal women with MS found hormone therapy feasible and well tolerated; hot-flash interference was lower but not significantly; it measured MS symptoms, quality of life, function and MRI but was not powered to establish effects on MS activity or disability. |
| [2025-menopausal-hormone-therapy-adipokines-juutinen](../evidence/2025-menopausal-hormone-therapy-adipokines-juutinen.md) | 4 | In 16 menopausal women with MS and 15 healthy controls, all on hormone therapy, one inflammation-related adipokine fell over a year; no untreated or randomized MS comparison, and no effect on relapses or disability was tested. |

### Puberty, menopause and the pill

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2017-age-at-menarche-danish-cohort-nielsen](../evidence/2017-age-at-menarche-danish-cohort-nielsen.md) | 3 | In 77,330 Danish women followed about 12 years (226 developed MS), each year later at first period was associated with 13% lower MS risk (HR 0.87; 0.89 after further adjustment including pre-pregnancy BMI). |
| [2019-age-at-menarche-meta-analysis-azimi](../evidence/2019-age-at-menarche-meta-analysis-azimi.md) | 3 | Pooling three observational studies (two from Iran, one from Canada), each year later at first period was associated with lower odds of MS (crude, unadjusted OR 0.88). |
| [2019-age-at-puberty-mendelian-randomization-harroud](../evidence/2019-age-at-puberty-mendelian-randomization-harroud.md) | 3 | Genetically later puberty was associated with lower MS odds (OR 0.92 per year), but the association weakened and was no longer significant after accounting for body mass index (OR 0.96). |
| [2025-menopause-disability-progression-bridge](../evidence/2025-menopause-disability-progression-bridge.md) | 3 | In 987 Australian women with relapse-onset MS, menopause was not associated with confirmed disability progression (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.70 to 1.29) or conversion to secondary progressive MS. |
| [2025-menopause-ms-course-simonsen](../evidence/2025-menopause-ms-course-simonsen.md) | 3 | In 559 peri- or postmenopausal women and 386 similarly aged men with MS in Norway, EDSS progression and relapse rate did not differ significantly in the 5 years before vs after menopause; women's EDSS progressed more slowly than men's after menopause (coef -0.02, p=0.032). Observational, self-reported menopause age. |
| [2024-oral-contraceptives-uk-biobank-nova](../evidence/2024-oral-contraceptives-uk-biobank-nova.md) | 3 | In 181,058 UK Biobank women, pill use was not significantly associated with MS overall (ever use HR 1.30, 95% CI 0.93 to 1.82); higher risks appeared only in subgroups, such as women who never gave birth. |

### Sex hormone studies and trials

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2016-estriol-trial-voskuhl](../evidence/2016-estriol-trial-voskuhl.md) | 2 | In a phase 2 trial of 164 women on glatiramer acetate, relapse rate was 0.25 per year with added estriol vs 0.37 with placebo (rate ratio 0.63, 95% CI 0.37-1.05; p=0.077, against a pre-set p<0.10 threshold). |
| [2007-testosterone-pilot-sicotte](../evidence/2007-testosterone-pilot-sicotte.md) | 4 | In 10 men, a year of testosterone gel was associated with better cognitive scores and slower brain atrophy than the untreated period before it, with no change in enhancing lesions; no placebo group. |
| [2014-testosterone-gray-matter-kurth](../evidence/2014-testosterone-gray-matter-kurth.md) | 4 | A second analysis of the MRI scans from the same 10 men as Sicotte 2007: widespread gray matter loss before treatment was no longer significant in the last 6 months on testosterone, and one right frontal area increased. No placebo group; not independent of Sicotte 2007; no clinical benefit shown. |
| [2014-low-testosterone-men-bove](../evidence/2014-low-testosterone-men-bove.md) | 4 | In 96 men with early MS, 39% had low testosterone; lower testosterone was associated with higher disability after age adjustment (p=0.044), weakening to p=0.092 after also adjusting for leptin, and, in a 40-man cognitive subgroup, with more cognitive decline over time (p=0.012). |
| [2015-finger-ratio-prenatal-androgens-men-bove](../evidence/2015-finger-ratio-prenatal-androgens-men-bove.md) | 3 | In 137 men with MS and 145 without, the 2D:4D finger ratio, a debated proxy for prenatal androgens, differed between groups (p < 0.05) but did not track MS severity or adult testosterone. |
| [2025-transgender-gender-diverse-ms-cohort-balshi](../evidence/2025-transgender-gender-diverse-ms-cohort-balshi.md) | 4 | In 22 transgender and gender-diverse people with MS (21 assigned female at birth), disability was mild, and testosterone therapy was not associated with relapses, progression or new MRI activity; no comparison group. |

### X chromosome and genetic findings

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2008-sex-chromosome-complement-mice-smith-bouvier](../evidence/2008-sex-chromosome-complement-mice-smith-bouvier.md) | 5 | In mice with the same type of gonads, XX mice were more susceptible than XY mice to an MS-like disease (EAE) and to lupus. |
| [2021-xy-chromosomes-th17-mice-doss](../evidence/2021-xy-chromosomes-th17-mice-doss.md) | 5 | In mice with a chronic model of MS, Th17 immune cells from males caused more severe disease than cells from females, and XY chromosomes increased this; an X-linked gene, Jarid1c, restrained it. | [2019-genomic-map-imsgc](../evidence/2019-genomic-map-imsgc.md) | 3 | Already in the repo (question F). Of 233 MS risk variants found in 47,429 cases and 68,374 controls, one is on the X chromosome. |

### How disease course differs by sex

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2022-sex-disability-accumulation-magyari](../evidence/2022-sex-disability-accumulation-magyari.md) | 3 | In 9,647 treated Danes with relapsing MS, women had 16% more relapses (until about age 50), while men reached EDSS 4 (HR 1.34) and EDSS 6 (HR 1.43) sooner. |
| [2015-sex-disability-msbase-ribbons](../evidence/2015-sex-disability-msbase-ribbons.md) | 3 | In the international MSBase registry, EDSS rose faster in men than women with relapse-onset MS (0.124 vs 0.107 points a year), and women had a lower hazard of secondary progressive MS (HR 0.77); no sex difference in primary progressive MS. The paper has internal number mismatches. |
