# The IMSGC genome-wide map of MS risk variants

- **Citation:** International Multiple Sclerosis Genetics Consortium. Multiple sclerosis genomic map implicates peripheral immune cells and microglia in susceptibility. *Science*, 2019;365(6460):eaav7188.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1126/science.aav7188](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aav7188), PMID [31604244](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31604244/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (genetic case-control study; observational)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The consortium analyzed genetic data from 47,429 people with MS and 68,374 controls to build a
reference map of the common genetic variants linked to MS risk. It then used several methods to
rank likely susceptibility genes and compared their activity across immune cell types and purified
human microglia (the brain's own immune cells).

## What it found

- The map includes 200 risk variants on the non-sex chromosomes outside the major
  histocompatibility complex (MHC, the region that includes the HLA genes), one variant on the X
  chromosome, and 32 variants within the extended MHC.
- The authors prioritized 551 likely susceptibility genes, spread across many innate and adaptive
  immune pathways.
- MS genes were enriched in microglia. The authors propose that MS is most likely first triggered
  by changes in immune responses outside the brain, with microglia possibly helping to direct the
  process to the central nervous system.

## Limits

- Each variant is common and raises risk only slightly. The abstract does not report how much of
  MS heritability these variants explain together; a reviewer should check the full paper.
- A genetic association marks a region of the genome. Which gene and which cell it acts through are
  inferences, and the microglia role is a proposal, not shown directly in people.
- Participants were mainly of European ancestry, so the map may be incomplete for other groups.
