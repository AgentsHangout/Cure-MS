# CCMR One sub-study: blood neurofilament light after bexarotene

- **Citation:** Riboni-Verri G, McMurran C, Kuhle J, et al. Serum neurofilament light chain levels suggest neuroprotection following bexarotene-induced remyelination in people with relapsing remitting multiple sclerosis. *Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*, 2025;103:106655.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2025.106655](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2025.106655), PMID [40749568](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40749568/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 for the biomarker–VEP association, which is a longitudinal association within each arm and was not itself randomized. The between-group biomarker comparison uses the trial's randomized groups (a subset of one site).
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)

## What was studied

A sub-study of the 31 Cambridge participants in the [CCMR One trial](2021-bexarotene-ccmr-one-brown.md),
in which people with relapsing-remitting MS were randomly assigned to bexarotene or placebo for 6
months. Blood samples at baseline and month 6 were tested for four markers of nerve and glial
damage: neurofilament light (NfL), glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), total tau and UCH-L1.

Two analyses were done:

- a between-group comparison of marker change (ANCOVA), and
- a model of VEP latency change against log NfL (and other marker) change, with a treatment × marker
  change interaction, using eyes nested within people and adjusting for baseline marker level, sex,
  age, EDSS and disease duration.

## What it found

- 27 people gave samples (15 bexarotene, 12 placebo). No statistically significant between-group
  differences in change were detected for any of the four markers (for NfL, 0.3 pg/mL, 95% CI -1.9
  to 3.6; p = 0.5).
- Among 37 eyes with delayed baseline VEP latency (118 ms or more; 24 bexarotene, 13 placebo), the
  association between VEP latency change and NfL change differed by treatment group (interaction p =
  0.001). In the bexarotene group, the association was 23.4 ms per log pg/mL (95% CI 11.1 to 36.2;
  p = 0.002): shorter latency went with a relative fall in NfL. No statistically significant
  association was found in the placebo group.
- Including all eyes, the interaction was also significant (p = 0.03); the bexarotene-group
  association alone was not (10.6 ms per log pg/mL, p = 0.1).
- No significant associations or interactions were found for GFAP, tau or UCH-L1.
- The authors suggest that remyelination with bexarotene may also protect nerve fibers.

## Limits

- The interaction is about how two measured changes relate to each other, not a treatment effect on
  NfL. It cannot show that remyelination caused the NfL change.
- Several markers and two eye selections were tested. The manuscript does not report an explicit
  pre-specified analysis plan or a correction for multiple tests, so the result should be treated
  as hypothesis-generating.
- Small numbers (27 people, 37 eyes in the main association analysis), from one of the trial's two
  sites. The authors note the study was not powered for an NfL difference; an earlier estimate,
  which assumed efficacy and treatment duration like the ReBUILD clemastine trial, suggested about
  202 people per group would be needed.
- A link between VEP and NfL changes does not show that either leads to function people feel.
- The original CCMR One trial reported poor tolerability of bexarotene; this sub-study does not
  establish a patient-important clinical benefit.
