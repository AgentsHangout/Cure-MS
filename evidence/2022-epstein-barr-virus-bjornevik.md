# Epstein-Barr virus infection and later MS in US military personnel

- **Citation:** Bjornevik K, Cortese M, Healy BC, et al. Longitudinal analysis reveals high prevalence of Epstein-Barr virus associated with multiple sclerosis. *Science*, 2022;375(6578):296-301.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1126/science.abj8222](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj8222), PMID [35025605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35025605/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (cohort study using stored blood samples; observational)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors used a cohort of more than 10 million young adults on active duty in the US military,
955 of whom were diagnosed with MS during service. Using serum samples stored over time, they
looked at whether people were infected with Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), and with other viruses such
as cytomegalovirus, before MS began. They also measured neurofilament light chain, a blood marker
of nerve-fiber damage.

## What it found

- The risk of MS was 32 times higher after EBV infection.
- Risk was not raised after infection with other viruses, including cytomegalovirus, which spreads
  in a similar way.
- Neurofilament light chain levels rose only after EBV infection, so infection came before this
  sign of nerve damage.
- The authors argue that no known MS risk factor explains these findings and suggest EBV as the
  leading cause of MS. That is their interpretation; the design shows a strong association with
  infection coming first.

## Limits

- Observational. The timing and strength of the link support a causal role, but a cohort study
  cannot prove cause on its own.
- Most adults are infected with EBV and most never get MS, so EBV alone cannot explain who gets MS.
- Military personnel are not representative of everyone (for example, by age and health).
- Relevance to this question: EBV is a shared, transmissible exposure, so it could contribute to
  clustering in families and places. This study does not test clustering directly.
