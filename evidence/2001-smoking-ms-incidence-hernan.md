# Cigarette smoking and incidence of multiple sclerosis (Nurses' Health Study cohorts)

- **Citation:** Hernán MA, Olek MJ, Ascherio A. Cigarette smoking and incidence of multiple sclerosis. *Am J Epidemiol*, 2001;154(1):69-74.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/aje/154.1.69](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/154.1.69), PMID [11427406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11427406/)

## What was studied

Two prospective cohorts of US women: the Nurses' Health Study (121,700 women aged 30 to 55 at baseline in 1976) and the Nurses' Health Study II (116,671 women aged 25 to 42 at baseline in 1989). Smoking history was taken at baseline and updated on biennial questionnaires. A total of 315 definite or probable MS cases were documented. Incidence rates in smokers were compared with women who had never smoked, adjusting for age, latitude and ancestry, and then for other possible confounders.

## What it found

- Compared with never-smokers, the relative incidence rate of MS was 1.6 (95% CI 1.2 to 2.1) in current smokers and 1.2 (95% CI 0.9 to 1.6) in past smokers.
- The rate rose with cumulative smoking: 1.1 (95% CI 0.8 to 1.6) for 1 to 9 pack-years, 1.5 (1.2 to 2.1) for 10 to 24, and 1.7 (1.2 to 2.4) for 25 or more (p for trend below 0.05).
- The authors say similar results held after adjusting for other confounders, and that the prospective design does not prove cause and effect, but suggests smoking is associated with higher MS risk.

## Limits

- Association only. The authors themselves say the results do not prove a cause-and-effect relation.
- Women only, all in the US (the cohorts are named for nurses). Nothing here tests men or other populations, so it cannot be used for the sex comparison in the question directly.
- Smoking history came from biennial questionnaires. The abstract does not say how definite or probable MS cases were confirmed.
- Only 315 cases, so the confidence intervals are wide, and the past-smoker estimate includes 1 (0.9 to 1.6).
- The abstract does not list the full set of confounders adjusted for beyond age, latitude, ancestry and "other potential confounding factors"; the full text may.
