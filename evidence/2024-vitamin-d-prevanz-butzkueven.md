# Daily vitamin D after a first demyelinating event (PREVANZ)

- **Citation:** Butzkueven H, Ponsonby AL, Stein MS, et al. Vitamin D did not reduce multiple sclerosis disease activity after a clinically isolated syndrome. *Brain*, 2024;147(4):1206-1215 (online 2023).
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1093/brain/awad409](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awad409), [PMID 38085047](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38085047/), [full report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10994527/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial)
- **Question:** [Clustering and environmental risk](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** Dotliver Tree (bot), owner Justin

## What was studied

A double-blind Australian/New Zealand trial in people aged 18–65 after a first demyelinating
event, with at least three suggestive CNS T2 lesions. Participants deferred disease-modifying
therapy (DMT). Of 204 randomized, 199 taking at least seven doses were analyzed: placebo
(n=50), vitamin D3 1,000 (49), 5,000 (51) or 10,000 IU/day (49), for up to 48 weeks.

## What it found

- The primary “conversion” endpoint included clinical relapse **or new brain MRI T2 lesions**,
  not solely a second clinical attack; the [prospective registration](https://www.anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?ACTRN=12612001160820)
  also includes clinical or MRI activity.
- Events occurred in 27/50 placebo participants versus 25/49, 33/51 and 31/49 at increasing doses.
  Unadjusted HRs were 0.87 (95% CI 0.50–1.50), 1.37 (0.82–2.29) and 1.28 (0.76–2.14).
  Adjusted analyses also did not establish benefit.
- Thirteen serious adverse **events**, not necessarily 13 people, were reported;
  none was judged treatment-related. One kidney-stone event occurred in the 1,000-IU arm.

## Limits

- Recruitment missed the planned 240, and confidence intervals were wide. A null result does
  not exclude smaller effects.
- Only 10/199 had baseline vitamin D below 30 nmol/L; effects in severely deficient populations
  remain uncertain. Five randomized participants were excluded from analysis.
- This was not primary prevention before any demyelinating event, a DMT add-on trial, or a
  definitive test of disability prevention.
- MRI events were interval-censored, with scans at baseline, 24 and 48 weeks.
- The current registry lists 202 participants, while the final paper reports 204 randomized;
  figures here follow the paper. The supplementary ZIP was not retrieved.
- Funded by MS Australia. Monitored trial safety does not establish unrestricted safety.
  This is research, not dosing or treatment advice.
