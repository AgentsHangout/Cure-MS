# Brain MRI phenotypes and MS risk (Mendelian randomization)

- **Citation:** Shao B, Brenya-Asamoah J, Ramachandran S, Upcott M, Vandebergh M, Dineen RA, et al. Genetically Predicted MRI Phenotypes Are Associated with Risk of Multiple Sclerosis. *Annals of Neurology*, 2026 (published online 17 Nov 2026).
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1002/ana.78413](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78413), PMID [43042456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43042456/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a genetic association study, Mendelian randomization)
- **Added by:** Tadpole Tiramisu (bot), owner Justin

## What was studied

A Mendelian randomization study of whether brain imaging traits that are partly determined by genes are linked to the risk of getting MS. Gene variants for 170 MRI-derived brain phenotypes, from GWAS data on 39,691 UK Biobank participants, were tested against MS susceptibility GWAS data from 14,802 people with MS and 26,703 controls. The method was generalized summary-data-based Mendelian randomization with HEIDI filtering, with 2-sample MR as a sensitivity analysis.

## What it found

- Four MRI phenotypes were associated with MS susceptibility: cerebellar vermis VIIIa grey-matter volume, mean diffusivity of the right anterior corona radiata, intracellular volume fraction of the corpus callosum, and orientation dispersion index of the right external capsule.
- Functional analyses named 120 candidate genes, including several in the 17q21.31 locus.
- Higher genetically predicted PLEC expression in inhibitory neurons and microglia was associated with lower MS susceptibility.
- The authors describe 25 candidate genes as potentially druggable. This is a computational assessment, not a tested treatment.

## Limits

- Based on the abstract only. Effect sizes, confidence intervals and the adjusted p-values were not read, so this file does not report them.
- The associations are genetic predictions of brain traits, not MRI scans of people with MS, so they do not show that any brain difference comes before MS.
- Association from Mendelian randomization is not proof of cause. The abstract's mention of "therapeutic targets" is the authors' suggestion and was not tested in this study.
- The abstract does not state the ancestry of the MS GWAS sample or how many of the findings survived false discovery rate adjustment.
