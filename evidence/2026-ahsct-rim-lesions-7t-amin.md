# Paramagnetic rim lesions before and after autologous stem cell transplantation, on 7T MRI

- **Citation:** Amin M, Nakamura K, Thoomukuntla B, et al. Paramagnetic rim lesions on 7T MRI after autologous hematopoietic cell transplantation. *Multiple Sclerosis*, 2026;32(6):647-651.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/13524585261429371](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585261429371), PMID [41821410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41821410/)
- **Evidence level:** 4
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

At one MS referral center, a single rater counted paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) on 7T MRI before
and after autologous haematopoietic cell transplantation (aHSCT). Seven people were included (mean
age 38.9, median disease duration 9 years), and 5 had follow-up scans.

## What it found

- The median PRL count before transplantation was 2.
- After transplantation (median 15 months, in the 5 people followed up), none of the PRLs resolved.
- The authors call this "low-level evidence" that PRLs do not resolve after aHSCT.

## Limits

- Only 7 people, 5 with follow-up, and a single rater. It cannot show that aHSCT never affects rim
  lesions.
- Its finding differs from [Mariottini 2026](2026-ahsct-paramagnetic-rim-lesions-mariottini.md),
  where some rims disappeared after aHSCT. The two studies differ in MRI strength (7T here), follow-up
  length and size, and both are too small and uncontrolled to settle the question.
- No link to disability was tested.
