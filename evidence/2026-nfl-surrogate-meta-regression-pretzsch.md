# Do drug effects on neurofilament light track drug effects on relapses and disability? (LUMINOUS)

- **Citation:** Pretzsch R, El Mahdaoui S, Johnson P, et al. Neurofilament light chain as a surrogate candidate for disease activity in multiple sclerosis (LUMINOUS)-A meta-regression of randomized controlled trials. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2026;32(3):268-278.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/13524585251407973](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585251407973), PMID [41574448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41574448/)
- **Evidence level:** 1 by design (systematic search and meta-regression of randomized trials). What it shows is an association between trials' effects, not that acting on NfL in one person helps.
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A systematic PubMed search found randomized trials in MS from 1999 to 2023 that lasted at least 24
months and reported serum or plasma neurofilament light (NfL). Fifteen trials with 6,814 people were
included: nine in relapsing MS (5,221 people) and six in progressive MS (1,593 people). For each
trial, the authors compared the drug's effect on NfL with its effect on relapse rate, confirmed
disability worsening (CDW), new or enlarging T2 lesions on MRI and brain volume change.

## What it found

- Trials in which a drug lowered NfL more also tended to show larger effects on new or enlarging T2
  lesions (R² = 0.42), relapse rate (R² = 0.33) and CDW (R² = 0.30).
- There was no consistent link with brain volume change.
- In relapsing MS trials alone, the links with relapse rate (R² = 0.45) and CDW (R² = 0.50) were
  stronger.

## Limits

- The association is moderate: drug effects on NfL explained roughly a third to a half of the
  differences between trials in other outcomes.
- With 15 trials, the estimates rest on few data points.
- This is a trial-level analysis. It supports NfL as a candidate measure of how well a drug works in
  a group, but it does not test NfL-guided treatment decisions for individuals.
- PubMed was the only database named in the abstract.
- All numbers come from the abstract.
