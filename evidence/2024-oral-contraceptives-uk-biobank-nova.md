# Oral contraceptive use and MS risk (UK Biobank)

- **Citation:** Nova A, Di Caprio G, Baldrighi GN, et al. Investigating the influence of oral contraceptive pill use on multiple sclerosis risk using UK Biobank data. *Fertility and Sterility*, 2024;122(6):1094-1104.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.fertnstert.2024.07.999](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fertnstert.2024.07.999), PMID [39098539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39098539/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A population-based cohort of 181,058 white women born in England between 1937 and 1970 in the UK
Biobank, of whom 1,131 had MS. Oral contraceptive use was self-reported, including ages at starting
and stopping. Marginal structural time-to-event models adjusted for MS polygenic risk score,
education, number of births, smoking, fertility problems, obesity and mononucleosis.

## What it found

- Ever use and current use were not significantly associated with MS overall (ever vs never, HR 1.30,
  95% CI 0.93 to 1.82; current vs never, HR 1.35, 95% CI 0.81 to 2.25).
- Number of births modified the association. In women who had never given birth, ever use (HR 2.08,
  95% CI 1.04 to 4.17) and current use (HR 3.15, 95% CI 1.43 to 6.9) were associated with higher MS
  risk, as were longer current use and an earlier starting age.
- The association was stronger in women with a low MS polygenic risk score.

## Limits

- Observational, with self-reported pill use recalled years later.
- The strong associations come from subgroups (women who never gave birth, low genetic risk), with
  wide confidence intervals; subgroup findings are more likely to be chance.
- Women who never gave birth may differ in other ways, and having MS can itself affect decisions
  about pregnancy and contraception (reverse causation).
- White women born 1937 to 1970 only. Given their birth years, many may have used pill
  formulations different from today's, but the abstract does not report formulations. The authors
  call for cautious causal interpretation and checks in other populations and pill types.
- This is not advice about contraception, which is a decision for each person and their clinician.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
