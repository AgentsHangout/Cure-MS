# EXPAND: siponimod in secondary progressive MS

- **Citation:** Kappos L, Bar-Or A, Cree BAC, et al. Siponimod versus placebo in secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (EXPAND): a double-blind, randomised, phase 3 study. *The Lancet*, 2018;391(10127):1263-1273.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30475-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30475-6), PMID [29576505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29576505/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial
([NCT01665144](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01665144)) at 292 centers in 31 countries, in
people aged 18 to 60 with secondary progressive MS and an EDSS disability score of 3.0 to 6.5. People
were randomly assigned 2:1 to siponimod 2 mg once a day or placebo, for up to 3 years or until a
pre-set number of progression events had occurred. Siponimod is a pill that keeps some immune cells
out of the bloodstream by acting on S1P receptors.

The main outcome was the time to disability progression confirmed 3 months later.

## What it found

- In a trial of 1,651 randomized people (1,645 analyzed: 1,099 on siponimod and 546 on placebo),
  disability progression confirmed at 3 months occurred in 288 of 1,096 (26%) on siponimod vs 173 of
  545 (32%) on placebo (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.95; p = 0.013). The authors give this as a 21%
  relative risk reduction.
- At the start, people had had MS symptoms for a mean of 16.8 years and secondary progressive MS for
  3.8 years. 1,055 (64%) had not relapsed in the previous 2 years, and 918 of 1,651 (56%) needed
  help walking.
- 82% on siponimod and 78% on placebo completed the study.
- Adverse events occurred in 89% on siponimod vs 82% on placebo, and serious adverse events in 18%
  vs 15%. More common with siponimod: low lymphocyte counts, raised liver enzymes, slow heart rate
  when starting, macular edema, high blood pressure, shingles reactivation and convulsions. The
  authors report that rates of infections, cancers and deaths did not differ between groups.

## Limits

- The absolute difference was 6 percentage points (26% vs 32%) over a trial lasting up to 3 years.
- About a third of participants had relapsed in the 2 years before entry, so the trial did not
  test siponimod only in people without relapses. The outcome was confirmed disability
  progression, which counts worsening whether or not relapses occurred. It is not a measure of
  progression independent of relapse activity (PIRA), and the abstract does not report the result
  separately for people with and without recent relapses.
- Progression was confirmed at 3 months, a shorter window than the 6 months some trials use.
- The abstract gives no results for secondary outcomes such as walking speed, MRI measures or
  6-month confirmed progression.
- Only people aged 18 to 60 with an EDSS of 3.0 to 6.5 were included.
- Funded by Novartis Pharma AG, which makes siponimod.
- This is not advice about treatment.
- Numbers come from the abstract; the full text is not open access and was not read.
