# Change in blood NfL over the first treatment year and later disease activity

- **Citation:** Fernández V, Pappolla A, Carbonell-Mirabent P, et al. Refining Treatment Monitoring in Patients With Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis Based on Changes on Neurofilament Light Chain Z-Scores. *Neurology*, 2026;107(6):e218451.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000218451](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218451), PMID [42623577](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42623577/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a prospective cohort)
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A prospective, real-world cohort at the MS Center of Catalonia (Cemcat) of adults with relapsing MS
starting a disease-modifying treatment, with serum samples at the start and after 1 year. The
exposure was the change in serum NfL Z score (NfL adjusted against a reference population) over the
first year, using thresholds taken from the data: no fall (change 0 or more) or a fall of less than
0.5 points. The outcome was evidence of disease activity (relapses, MRI activity or EDSS
progression) between years 1 and 2. The study also asked whether NfL change added to existing
treatment-response scores (Rio, modified Rio and MAGNIMS).

## What it found

- 329 people started 352 treatments; 342 treatments lasted at least a year, and 182 had complete
  data on disease activity in year 2.
- NfL Z scores fell by a mean of 0.62 (SD 1.2) over the first year, with larger falls on monoclonal
  antibodies and in people new to treatment.
- No fall in NfL Z score was associated with higher odds of disease activity in year 2 (OR 3.07, 95%
  CI 1.47 to 6.39; p = 0.003). The association was also seen in people new to treatment (OR 14.04,
  95% CI 2.47 to 79.93) and in people with no evidence of disease activity in year 1 (OR 4.54, 95%
  CI 1.26 to 16.30).
- The association persisted when added to treatment-response scores that otherwise looked
  favorable (for example, Rio score: OR 2.26, 95% CI 1.12 to 4.59).

## Limits

- Only 182 of 342 treatment courses had complete year-2 data, so missing follow-up could bias the
  result. The authors name incomplete follow-up as a limit.
- The authors describe NfL change as having modest ability to tell people apart, and they support a
  complementary role alongside clinical and MRI monitoring, not a stand-alone one.
- The thresholds were derived from the same data, which can make results look stronger than they
  would in a new group.
- Subgroup estimates (for example OR 14.04) have very wide confidence intervals.
- One center, observational: it shows an association, not that acting on NfL change improves
  outcomes.
- Numbers come from the abstract; the full text was not read.
