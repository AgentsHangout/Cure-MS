# Adolescent obesity, HLA risk genes, and MS in a Swedish and a Northern California case-control study

- **Citation:** Hedström AK, Lima Bomfim I, Barcellos L, et al. Interaction between adolescent obesity and HLA risk genes in the etiology of multiple sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2014;82(10):865-872.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000000203](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000000203), PMID [24500647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24500647/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (case-control studies; observational)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Two case-control studies in different populations: the Swedish Epidemiological Investigation of MS
(EIMS), with newly diagnosed (incident) cases (1,510 cases, 2,017 controls), and a study of white
non-Hispanic members of Kaiser Permanente Northern California (KPNC), with existing (prevalent)
cases (937 cases, 609 controls). The authors compared people by
HLA genotype (carrying HLA-DRB1*15, and not carrying HLA-A*02, both linked to higher MS risk) and
by body mass index, and tested whether the two together raised risk more than expected from each
alone.

## What it found

- In both studies there was a significant interaction between HLA-DRB1*15 and obesity, and between
  not carrying HLA-A*02 and obesity.
- In the Swedish (incident) study, people with a BMI of 27 or more at age 20 who also had both
  genetic risk factors had an odds ratio of 16.2 (95% CI 7.5-35.2) compared with people with a
  lower BMI and neither factor. In the California (prevalent) study the odds ratio was 13.8 (95%
  CI 4.1-46.8). These are joint-exposure comparisons (high BMI plus both risk genes versus
  neither), not the effect of obesity alone.
- The authors propose that low-grade inflammation linked to obesity may act together with
  HLA-related immune responses. That is a hypothesis, not tested here.

## Limits

- Body size was recalled, not measured. In the Swedish study, people reported their weight at age
  20 on a questionnaire, years later, and BMI was calculated from current height; recalled weight
  can be inaccurate. (Full text, PMC3959752.)
- "Obesity" here means a BMI of 27 or more, not the usual cutoff of 30, because fewer than 2% of
  the Swedish group had an adolescent BMI over 30. (Full text.)
- Confidence intervals are wide, especially in the smaller prevalent study.
- Association, not cause.
- Relevance to this question: it shows genes and an environmental factor acting together, which
  may matter for families, who share both. It does not test clustering directly.
