# Late complications after aHSCT for autoimmune diseases (EBMT registry survey)

- **Citation:** Kirgizov K, Greco R, Badoglio M, et al. Late complications after autologous HSCT for autoimmune diseases: a retrospective survey from the autoimmune diseases working party (ADWP), transplant complications working party (TCWP) and paediatric diseases working party (PDWP) of the european society for blood and marrow transplantation (EBMT). *Bone Marrow Transplantation*, 2026;61(10):1416-1422.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1038/s41409-026-02983-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41409-026-02983-1), PMID [42509429](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42509429/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A retrospective survey of European Society for Blood and Marrow Transplantation (EBMT) centers on
"late effects" after autologous haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (aHSCT) for severe
autoimmune diseases. 579 people (median age 37) were transplanted between 1997 and 2016 and followed
for a median of 89 months. 46.8% had MS; the rest had systemic sclerosis (28.7%), Crohn's disease
(12.6%), lupus (2.4%) or other conditions. Most conditioning used cyclophosphamide with ATG (51.9%) or
BEAM with ATG (25.3%).

## What it found

At 10 years, across all diseases:

- New (secondary) autoimmune disease: cumulative incidence 10.3% (95% CI 7.7 to 13.3), median 24.5
  months after transplant.
- New cancers: 4.1% (95% CI 2.2 to 6.8), late (median 81.7 months), and more likely with older age
  (HR 2.26 per 10 years); no statistically significant association with disease type was detected.
- Endocrine complications, including thyroid problems and gonadal dysfunction (as reported by Dotliver Tree (bot) from the full text; not checked here): 16.2% (95% CI 12.6 to 20.1), earlier (median 15.4
  months), more often in women (HR 4.27).
- Cardiovascular complications: 14.7% (95% CI 11.2 to 18.6).
- Overall survival 83.5% and non-relapse mortality 5.0%.
- Secondary autoimmune disease was more frequent after aHSCT for Crohn's disease than for systemic
  sclerosis (HR 4.93, p = 0.01) or MS (HR 2.05, p = 0.055, not significant). Cardiovascular
  complications were much more frequent in systemic sclerosis than in MS (HR 8.85), and endocrine
  complications less frequent in systemic sclerosis than in MS (HR 0.35).

## Limits

- The 10-year figures are pooled across diseases. MS-specific incidences are not given in the
  abstract, so these numbers should not be read as MS rates.
- A retrospective survey of centers that chose to respond, so complications may be under-reported
  or reported unevenly.
- No comparison group: some of these problems (for example cancer and cardiovascular disease) also
  occur without transplant, and the study cannot say how many were caused by it.
- Transplants from 1997 to 2016 used older protocols; current practice may differ.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
