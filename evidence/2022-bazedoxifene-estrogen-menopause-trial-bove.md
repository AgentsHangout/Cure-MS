# Bazedoxifene plus conjugated estrogen in menopausal women with MS (phase Ib/IIa pilot trial)

- **Citation:** Bove R, Anderson A, Rowles W, et al. A hormonal therapy for menopausal women with MS: A phase Ib/IIa randomized controlled trial. *Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*, 2022;61:103747.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2022.103747](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2022.103747), PMID [35397292](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35397292/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (a small randomized, placebo-controlled pilot; see Limits)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

24 peri- or postmenopausal women with MS and troublesome hot flashes were randomized 1:1 to
conjugated estrogens with bazedoxifene (brand name Duavee) or placebo, and evaluated at baseline and
2 months. The study's aims were feasibility, tolerability and symptoms.

## What it found

- Groups were similar in age (mean 51.2), EDSS (median 3) and MS duration. 21 of 24 completed the
  study (87%).
- Enrollment took 34 months, partly because of concerns about hormone therapy safety.
- The treatment group reported greater satisfaction and fewer missed doses. Liver tests stayed
  normal. One participant on placebo developed new MRI lesions.
- Hot-flash interference with daily life at 2 months was lower on treatment than placebo (median 4
  vs 9), but the difference was not statistically significant.

## Limits

- A feasibility pilot of 24 women over 2 months. It was not designed or powered to show any effect on
  MS activity or disability, and it does not establish disease modification.
- The authors conclude it supports a longer, powered trial of whether this treatment could improve
  MS-related function.
- This is not advice about hormone therapy, which is a decision for each person and their clinician.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
