# MS incidence in Danish women and men over six decades

- **Citation:** Koch-Henriksen N, Thygesen LC, Stenager E, Laursen B, Magyari M. Incidence of MS has increased markedly over six decades in Denmark particularly with late onset and in women. *Neurology*, 2018;90(22):e1954-e1963.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000005612](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000005612), PMID [29720546](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29720546/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (descriptive, population-based registry study of incidence trends; no exposures measured)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The Danish Multiple Sclerosis Registry has prospectively recorded nearly everyone in Denmark with
MS onset since 1950, from several sources of notification. The authors counted 19,536 people with
clinical onset of confirmed MS from 1950 to 2009 and calculated yearly incidence per 100,000,
standardized to the European Standard Population, separately for women and men. They used
age-period-cohort analysis and also looked at season of birth.

## What it found

- From the 1950-1959 onset period to 2000-2009, incidence in women more than doubled, from 5.91
  (95% CI 5.60-6.24) to 12.33 (11.91-12.75) per 100,000 per year.
- In men, incidence rose by a modest 24%, from 4.52 (4.24-4.81) to 6.08 (5.79-6.38).
- For onset at age 50 or older, incidence rose 4.30-fold in women and 2.72-fold in men.
- The female-to-male ratio rose over time and with year of birth. There was a significant birth
  cohort effect in addition to age and period effects. No significant season-of-birth effect was
  found.
- The authors suggest that lifestyle changes among women, such as fewer childbirths, more obesity
  and more smoking, may play a role. These were not measured in this study, so this is a
  hypothesis.

## Limits

- Changes in diagnostic criteria, MRI and awareness over 60 years affect recorded incidence. The
  stronger rise at older onset ages could partly reflect better diagnosis.
- It shows trends by sex, not their causes.
- One country with a mainly European-ancestry population.
