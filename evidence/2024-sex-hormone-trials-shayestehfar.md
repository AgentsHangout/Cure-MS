## What was studied

A systematic review of clinical trials and randomized trials of sex hormones (estrogens, progestins, testosterone) in people with MS, searched in PubMed, EMBASE and Scopus. The PubMed abstract (checked) says 9 papers met the criteria, and that two reviewers extracted data and rated quality with the PEDro scale.

Unchecked: the full text was not read for this file. The counts below come from the paper's Results and Methods as reported, and have not been verified: 482 people enrolled in total; a mean of 43.8 people per study (range 4 to 150); publication years 2002 to 2021; countries (USA 6, France 2, Italy 1); and 6 studies in women and 3 in men.

## What it found

The abstract (checked) says the authors sorted outcomes into clinical, MRI and immune categories, and call the investigations on inflammatory and neurodegenerative components of MS "promising particularly in relapsing-remitting MS (RRMS)". The paper does not give a pooled effect size in the abstract. The outcome tables were not read, so this file does not say which outcomes changed in which study.

## Limits

- Unchecked, from the full text: the trial counts. Methods is said to give five RCTs and four clinical trials (nine in total, matching the abstract). Results is said to give seven clinical trials and three RCTs (ten in total). The abstract gives only the total of nine. This file does not pick one.
- Unchecked: the mean of 43.8 people per study does not match the total as reported. 482 people over nine studies is 53.6 per study. The 43.8 figure is kept as reported and marked unchecked. Check the study-by-study table before using either figure.
- The studies are small (mean about 44 people as reported). Several are said to be crossover designs (unchecked), so the number of trials and their designs cannot by themselves show a treatment effect.
- POPARTMUS. The review describes the postpartum POPARTMUS trial as ongoing, with no results in the outcome categories. That matches the 2009 protocol, Vukusic et al., J Neurol Sci 2009;286(1-2):114-8 (PMID 19758607, DOI 10.1016/j.jns.2009.08.056; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jns.2009.08.056). The PubMed record (checked) gives volume 286, issue 1-2, pages 114-8, and its abstract says "At present, 126 patients have been enrolled and 107 patients have completed the protocol" (patients, not women). The trial's results were published later: Vukusic et al. 2021, Multiple Sclerosis Journal 27(9):1458-1463, PMID 33269975, DOI 10.1177/1352458520978218 (https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458520978218). The PubMed abstract (checked) reports recruitment stopped early (n = 202) with no treatment effect on the 12-week annualized relapse rate (0.90 with sex steroids vs 0.97 with placebo; p = 0.79). Check this against the review's table before relying on it.
- Not checked: the quality scores (PEDro, Table 4), the study-by-study tables (Tables 1 to 3) and the search flow diagram.
- Hormone doses and comparators differ across studies, and some studies compared with healthy controls or with no treatment, so the set can't be read as one comparison (from the full text, unchecked).
