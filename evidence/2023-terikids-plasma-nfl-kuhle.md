# TERIKIDS plasma NfL: separate blinded and extension evidence

- **Citation:** Kuhle J, Chitnis T, Banwell B, et al. Plasma neurofilament light chain in children with relapsing MS receiving teriflunomide or placebo: A post hoc analysis of the randomized TERIKIDS trial. *Mult Scler*. 2023;29(3):385–394.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1177/13524585221144742](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585221144742); [PMID 36632983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36632983/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 overall; blinded treatment contrasts retain randomized provenance (level 2), while prognosis and extension findings are exploratory.
- **Question:** [Blood biomarkers](../questions/biomarkers.md)

## What was studied

Post hoc Simoa plasma-NfL analysis in 111 participants (78 teriflunomide, 33 placebo). Baseline was week 2, after treatment began. Relapse or high MRI activity permitted early open-label switching.

## What it found

The main blinded week-24 teriflunomide/placebo geometric-mean ratio was 0.74 (95% CI 0.53–1.05; p=.09); baseline-NfL-adjusted sensitivity analysis gave p<.01. The significant global test combined blinded and open-label observations. During the extension, both groups received teriflunomide.

Per baseline-NfL doubling, the age-adjusted high-MRI-activity/relapse composite HR was 1.22 (1.01–1.48). Relapse-only and extension-disability associations were nonsignificant.

## Limits

Selected subset, activity-triggered switching, missing samples and few outcomes constrain interpretation. Prognostic models did not establish added value beyond baseline MRI. Post-treatment MRI adjustment attenuated biomarker contrasts but cannot establish mediation. Neither biomarker-guided benefit nor validation of pediatric serum Z-score references was tested. Sanofi funded the study; five authors were employees. Lower NfL establishes neither safety nor neurorepair.

## Parent-trial context

The biomarker sample was a subset of 166 randomized children aged 10–17 (109 teriflunomide, 57 placebo). The parent trial did not establish a difference on its primary relapse endpoint: HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.39–1.11; p=.29), although secondary MRI outcomes favored treatment. Serious adverse events affected 12/109 versus 6/57 participants. Four teriflunomide recipients had pancreatic adverse events, including two with acute pancreatitis. This context is not a complete safety review.
