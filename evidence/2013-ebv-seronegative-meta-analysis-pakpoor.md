# Meta-analysis: MS risk in people with no sign of past EBV infection

- **Citation:** Pakpoor J, Disanto G, Gerber JE, et al. The risk of developing multiple sclerosis in individuals seronegative for Epstein-Barr virus: a meta-analysis. *Multiple Sclerosis*, 2013;19(2):162-166.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458512449682](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458512449682), PMID [22740437](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22740437/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a meta-analysis of observational studies, not of randomized trials)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A meta-analysis of studies comparing EBV antibody status in people with and without MS. It asked
how much less likely MS is in people who are EBV seronegative, meaning their blood shows no
antibodies from a past EBV infection. PubMed and EMBASE were searched, and odds ratios were pooled
with a fixed-effects model. A subgroup analysis split studies by the antibody test used.

## What it found

- 22 studies of adult-onset MS and 3 of pediatric-onset MS were included.
- EBV-seronegative people had much lower odds of MS: OR 0.18 (95% CI 0.13 to 0.26) for adult MS and
  0.18 (95% CI 0.11 to 0.30) for pediatric MS.
- The result depended on the test. Studies using immunofluorescence gave OR 0.07 (95% CI 0.03 to
  0.16), studies using ELISA gave 0.33 (95% CI 0.22 to 0.50), and studies using both gave 0.00 (95% CI
  0 to 0.43).
- The authors conclude that in studies using two independent tests, EBV appeared to be present in
  everyone with MS, and that test accuracy affects the size of the association.

## Limits

- The pooled studies are observational comparisons of people with and without MS, mostly testing
  antibodies after MS had started. They show an association, not the order of events. A later
  cohort that tested blood before MS began is
  [2022-epstein-barr-virus-bjornevik](2022-epstein-barr-virus-bjornevik.md).
- A fixed-effects model assumes the studies estimate one common effect. The differences by test
  method suggest they do not, so the pooled interval may be too narrow.
- The "both tests" subgroup has a very wide interval, so "100% of MS patients" rests on few studies.
- Most adults have had EBV, so seronegative groups are small.
- Numbers come from the abstract; the full text was not read.
