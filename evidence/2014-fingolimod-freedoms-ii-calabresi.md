# Oral fingolimod vs placebo in relapsing MS, 2-year phase 3 trial (FREEDOMS II)

- **Citation:** Calabresi PA, Radue EW, Goodin D, Jeffery D, Rammohan KW, Reder AT, et al. Safety and efficacy of fingolimod in patients with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (FREEDOMS II): a double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial. *Lancet Neurol*, 2014;13(6):545-556.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1016/S1474-4422(14)70049-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2814%2970049-3), PMID [24685276](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24685276/). Checked against Crossref and PubMed on 2026-11-26. Trial registration NCT00355134.
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial)

Abstract-level only: this file was written from the PubMed abstract. The full text was not read, so the numbers below are the abstract's, and the full adverse event tables, dropout reasons and conflicts of interest are not checked.

## What was studied

A double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial, run mostly in the USA (101 of 117 centres), in people aged 18 to 55 with relapsing-remitting MS. 1,083 people were randomized 1:1:1 to oral fingolimod 0.5 mg, fingolimod 1.25 mg, or placebo once daily. The primary end point was annualized relapse rate at month 24. Brain volume change and time to confirmed disability progression were secondary end points. Partway through, everyone on 1.25 mg was switched blind to 0.5 mg, but the primary analysis kept them in the 1.25 mg group.

## What it found

- Annualized relapse rate: 0.40 with placebo and 0.21 with 0.5 mg, a rate ratio of 0.52 (95% CI 0.40 to 0.66; p<0.0001), about a 48% reduction.
- Percentage brain volume change: -0.86 with 0.5 mg vs -1.28 with placebo (treatment difference -0.41, 95% CI -0.62 to -0.20; p=0.0002).
- Confirmed disability progression: no significant difference (hazard ratio 0.83 with 0.5 mg, 95% CI 0.61 to 1.12; p=0.227).
- More adverse events with 0.5 mg than placebo, per the abstract: lymphopenia (8% vs 0%), raised alanine aminotransferase (8% vs 2%), herpes zoster (3% vs 1%), hypertension (9% vs 3%), first-dose bradycardia (1% vs under 0.5%), and first-degree atrioventricular block (5% vs 2%).
- Serious adverse events over 24 months: 15% with 0.5 mg vs 13% with placebo. The abstract lists basal-cell carcinoma (10 vs 2 patients), macular oedema (3 vs 2), infections (11 vs 4) and neoplasms (13 vs 8) among them.

## Limits

- Two years only, so it says little about long-term risks. The authors say the findings "strengthen evidence" for relapse benefit and "expand knowledge" of safety.
- Only relapsing-remitting MS, aged 18 to 55. It does not tell us about progressive MS or people outside those ages.
- The 1.25 mg arm was switched to 0.5 mg during the trial, so the 1.25 mg results are hard to read on their own. This file uses only the 0.5 mg arm.
- Funded by Novartis Pharma AG (per the abstract). Conflicts of interest and the full safety tables are not checked here.
- Relapse rate (primary) was lower; brain volume (secondary) also favoured fingolimod; disability was not significant.
- The same trial design, with 0.5 mg and 1.25 mg arms, was also reported for FREEDOMS (2010); see [2010-fingolimod-freedoms-kappos.md](2010-fingolimod-freedoms-kappos.md). That is a separate trial, so the two should not be counted as one result.
