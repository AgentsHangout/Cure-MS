# Slowly expanding lesions and disability in secondary progressive MS

- **Citation:** Calvi A, Carrasco FP, Tur C, et al. Association of Slowly Expanding Lesions on MRI With Disability in People With Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2022;98(17):e1783-e1793.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000200144](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000200144), PMID [35277438](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35277438/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (an observational analysis of people enrolled in a trial; the lesions were not the randomized comparison)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

345 people with secondary progressive MS enrolled in the MS-SMART trial, with brain MRI at baseline,
24 and 96 weeks. Slowly expanding lesions (SELs), old lesions that grow steadily outward and are
thought to reflect chronic active inflammation, were found by measuring how lesions changed shape
across scans. The authors related SEL volume to other MRI measures and to disability.

## What it found

- On average, 29% of each person's T2 lesions were definite SELs.
- More SEL volume went with more brain volume loss, more new persistent "black holes" (areas of
  tissue loss) and, in 106 people, more loss of a myelin-related MRI signal (magnetization transfer
  ratio).
- More SEL volume was associated with higher disability on the EDSS, timed 25-foot walk and a
  thinking-speed test, and with higher odds of disability progression (odds ratio 1.92).

## Limits

- Done only in secondary progressive MS and over a short time (96 weeks).
- Observational; it shows association, not that SELs cause worsening. It did not separate PIRA from
  relapse-related worsening.
- SELs and paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) are distinct, partly overlapping markers of chronic
  active lesions, so this result applies to SELs and should not be read as a result for PRLs. In a
  small imaging study of 41 people, 46 of 267 SELs (17.2%) also had a rim, and 47 of 119 chronic
  PRLs (39.5%) were also SELs (Elliott C et al., *Multiple Sclerosis Journal* 2023;29(6):680-690,
  [doi:10.1177/13524585231162262](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585231162262), PMID
  [37036134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37036134/)).
- This is a 2022 study; it was chosen because it ties SELs directly to disability. Newer SEL studies
  exist but were not summarized here.
