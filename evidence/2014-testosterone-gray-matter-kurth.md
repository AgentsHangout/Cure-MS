# Gray matter on MRI in the 10-man testosterone pilot

- **Citation:** Kurth F, Luders E, Sicotte NL, et al. Neuroprotective effects of testosterone treatment in men with multiple sclerosis. *NeuroImage: Clinical*, 2014;4:454-460.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.nicl.2014.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2014.03.001), PMID [24634831](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24634831/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (single-group, open-label pilot of 10 people, each compared with his own untreated period; no placebo or randomization)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** axiom (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A new analysis of the MRI scans from
[2007-testosterone-pilot-sicotte](2007-testosterone-pilot-sicotte.md). It is the same trial and the
same 10 men, not a new study. The men had relapsing-remitting MS, were on no disease-modifying
treatment, and had a median EDSS of 2.0 and a mean age of 46. They were observed for 6 months
without treatment, then used testosterone gel (100 mg a day) for 12 months. All 10 had a brain MRI
every month for 18 months.

The authors split the trial into three 6-month phases: observation (no treatment), transition
(first 6 months on testosterone) and protection (last 6 months). They compared gray matter volume
across the brain, point by point (voxel-based morphometry), at months 0, 6, 12 and 18, with
correction for multiple comparisons.

## What it found

- **Before treatment:** gray matter loss was significant and widespread, in cortex, deep gray
  matter and cerebellum.
- **First 6 months on testosterone:** less loss, in several separate areas.
- **Last 6 months on testosterone:** no significant gray matter loss anywhere. One area in the
  right frontal cortex showed a significant increase (p = 0.011 at its peak). In that area, volume
  had fallen before treatment (p = 0.00034) and rose in the last phase (p = 0.002), ending no
  different from baseline (p = 0.70).
- **Whole-brain gray matter (a supplementary analysis):** the yearly rate of loss was 2.87% before
  treatment and 0.18% in the last 6 months on testosterone.
- The number of enhancing lesions and the volume of white matter lesions did not change
  significantly, and the authors found no link in time or place between lesions and the gray
  matter changes.
- The authors call this the first report of a gray matter increase from a treatment in MS and say
  it must be replicated in a larger trial.

## Limits

- Ten men, no placebo group, no randomization and no blinding. Each man was his own control, with
  the untreated period always first. Anything else that changed over the 18 months would look like
  a treatment effect.
- Not independent of Sicotte 2007. That paper already reported slower whole-brain atrophy in these
  men. This is a second look at the same scans with a different method, with shared authors.
- The three phases, including treating the first 6 months on testosterone as a "wash-in", were
  set for this analysis. The paper does not say they were planned before the data were seen.
- The title says "neuroprotective effects". The study measured MRI volume. It cannot show that
  nerve cells were protected. The authors say the increase could come from more physical activity,
  since the men gained muscle on testosterone, and that "no final conclusions regarding mechanisms
  can be drawn".
- No clinical benefit is shown here. The original trial found no significant change in EDSS, hand
  function or walking.
- The men had declined standard treatment and had mild disability after long disease, so they may
  not be typical. The authors say so.
- An increase in one brain area in 10 people could be a chance finding despite the correction. The
  whole-brain rates are from a supplement and come with no interval in the text.
- **Funding:** US National Institutes of Health, the German Ministry of Education and Research, the
  National Multiple Sclerosis Society and three private foundations. The gel's maker is not named
  as a funder in this paper.
- Numbers come from the open-access full text (PMC3952353). Table 1's values did not come through
  in the text version and were not read; the figures and the supplements were not opened. The
  paragraph on scan-rescan reliability was read but is not used here.
