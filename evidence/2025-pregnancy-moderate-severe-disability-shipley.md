# Pregnancy in women with moderate to severe MS disability (MSBase)

- **Citation:** Shipley J, Beadnall HN, Sanfilippo PG, et al. Pregnancy-Related Disease Outcomes in Women With Moderate to Severe Multiple Sclerosis Disability. *JAMA Network Open*, 2025;8(9):e2531581.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.31581](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.31581), PMID [40952740](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40952740/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A multicenter retrospective cohort from the MSBase registry (1984 to 2024). 575 women with MS who
became pregnant with an EDSS of 3 or more before conception (median 3.5, range 3.0 to 7.5) were
compared with 1,056 propensity score-matched women with MS who were not pregnant. Outcomes were
relapse rates around pregnancy and time to 6-month confirmed disability worsening.

## What it found

- Relapses fell during pregnancy, by 75% in the first trimester (rate ratio 0.25, 95% CI 0.15 to
  0.43), and rose to 36% above preconception levels in the first 3 months after birth (rate ratio
  1.36, 95% CI 1.06 to 1.75), a pattern like that seen in women with less disability.
- Relapse during pregnancy was more likely with more relapses before conception (OR 1.56) and with
  natalizumab (OR 4.42) or fingolimod (OR 14.07) before conception, and less likely with older age
  (OR 0.92) and with continuing treatment into pregnancy (OR 0.42, 95% CI 0.19 to 1.00).
- Restarting treatment within 1 month after birth was associated with lower odds of early
  postpartum relapse (OR 0.45, 95% CI 0.23 to 0.86).
- Time to confirmed disability worsening did not differ significantly between pregnant and
  non-pregnant women (HR 1.15, 95% CI 0.96 to 1.38). Relapses during pregnancy (HR 1.37) and a
  postpartum EDSS above 4 (HR 2.69) went with faster worsening.

## Limits

- Observational and retrospective. Women chose whether to become pregnant and which treatments to
  use, so these are associations, not effects of pregnancy or of particular treatment choices.
- The confidence interval for disability worsening reaches a 38% higher hazard, so a modest harm is
  not ruled out.
- The very wide intervals for natalizumab and fingolimod reflect small subgroups.
- This is not advice about pregnancy or treatment, which are decisions for each person and their
  clinician.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
