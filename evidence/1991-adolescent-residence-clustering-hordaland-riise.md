# Where people with MS lived at ages 13 to 20 (Hordaland, Norway)

- **Citation:** Riise T, Grønning M, Klauber MR, et al. Clustering of residence of multiple sclerosis patients at age 13 to 20 years in Hordaland, Norway. *American Journal of Epidemiology*, 1991;133(9):932-939.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a115972](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a115972), PMID [1851394](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1851394/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

381 people with MS onset between 1953 and 1987 in Hordaland county, Norway. The authors tested whether
people born in the same years had lived closer together than expected at different ages, compared
with age-, sex- and area-matched hospital controls without MS.

## What it found

- People with MS from the same birth cohort had lived significantly closer to each other than
  expected at ages 13 to 20, with the strongest clustering at age 18 (p = 0.002).
- Clustering also appeared between pairs with relapsing and progressive onset, and between cases
  with very different onset dates.
- No similar clustering was seen in the matched controls, or among cases when using a fixed number
  of years before onset.

## Limits

- One county, cases from 1953 to 1987, and many statistical comparisons across ages.
- The authors suggest an infectious agent such as EBV acquired in adolescence. That is their
  interpretation; the study did not measure any infection.
- Residence history depends on records and recall.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
