# Meta-analysis: MS risk after infectious mononucleosis

- **Citation:** Handel AE, Williamson AJ, Disanto G, et al. An updated meta-analysis of risk of multiple sclerosis following infectious mononucleosis. *PLoS One*, 2010;5(9):e12496.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0012496](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0012496), PMID [20824132](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20824132/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a meta-analysis of observational studies, not of randomized trials)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

An update of an earlier meta-analysis of whether people who have had infectious
mononucleosis, the illness that Epstein-Barr virus can cause when first caught in the teens or
later, are more likely to develop MS. Medline was searched from January 2006 to April 2010 for
cohort and case-control studies, adding 4 studies to the 14 in the earlier analysis. Relative risks
were pooled with a random-effects model.

## What it found

- 18 studies were included: 14 case-control and 4 cohort. The abstract gives 19,390 people with MS
  and 16,007 controls for all 18; the full text gives those counts when describing the case-control
  studies, so they may not cover the cohort studies.
- People with a history of infectious mononucleosis were about twice as likely to have MS
  (relative risk 2.17, 95% CI 1.97 to 2.39). Results were similar in case-control studies (2.11,
  1.82 to 2.43) and cohort studies (2.33, 1.96 to 2.78), with no significant heterogeneity between
  studies (p = 0.47).
- The authors conclude that a history of infectious mononucleosis increases the risk of MS.

## Limits

- The pooled studies are observational, so the result is an association. Shared causes, such as
  genes or the age at which EBV was caught, could contribute.
- Most of the pooled studies are case-control. Where those relied on people recalling past
  mononucleosis, people with MS may remember it differently from controls. The four cohort studies
  gave a similar result.
- No formal risk-of-bias assessment or test for publication bias is reported.
- The search used one database (Medline) and ended in April 2010. Later large cohorts, such as
  [2024-mononucleosis-cohort-england-goldacre](2024-mononucleosis-cohort-england-goldacre.md), are
  not included.
- Mononucleosis is the symptomatic form of EBV infection. This does not measure the risk from EBV
  infection without symptoms.
- Numbers come from the abstract and the open-access full text (PMC2931696). The full-text check
  was done by axiom (bot, owner morgkorg) and confirmed by ron. Supplementary Table S1 was not read.
