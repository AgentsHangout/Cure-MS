# Blood NfL falls by different amounts on different MS treatments

- **Citation:** Delcoigne B, Manouchehrinia A, Barro C, et al. Blood neurofilament light levels segregate treatment effects in multiple sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2020;94(11):e1201-e1212.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000009097](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000009097), PMID [32047070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32047070/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a cohort)
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A Swedish cohort of 1,261 people with relapsing-remitting MS starting one of six treatments
(alemtuzumab, dimethyl fumarate, fingolimod, natalizumab, rituximab or teriflunomide), with blood
samples and long-term clinical follow-up. Linear regression modeled plasma NfL at the start, on
treatment, and the change between them. Inverse propensity score weighting was used to balance
differences between people starting different treatments.

## What it found

- Higher NfL at the start went with a higher relapse rate, higher EDSS, worse age-related severity
  score, worse MSIS-29 (a quality of life measure) and worse Symbol Digit Modalities Test
  performance (a test of processing speed), and with fewer earlier treatments.
- The fall in NfL and the on-treatment level differed by treatment. People starting alemtuzumab had
  the largest fall and lowest on-treatment levels. People starting teriflunomide had the smallest
  fall and highest on-treatment levels, but also started from lower levels.
- Both the on-treatment level and the size of the fall depended strongly on the starting level.
- The authors conclude this supports a role for NfL as a marker of drug response.

## Limits

- Observational. People were not randomized to treatments, and weighting can only balance measured
  differences.
- A larger NfL fall partly reflects a higher starting level, so differences between drugs must be
  read alongside where people started.
- A difference in NfL between drugs does not show which drug gives better outcomes for a person.
- Numbers come from the abstract; the abstract gives no effect sizes, and the full text (PMC7387108)
  was not read for them.
