# EBV-specific T cells and antibodies across anti-CD20 drugs in MS

- **Citation:** Stögbauer J, Bronder S, Saenz Kämpfer N, et al. The influence of anti-CD20-directed B cell depletion across different agents on Epstein-Barr virus-specific humoral and cellular immunity in patients with multiple sclerosis. *Journal of Neuroinflammation*, 2026;23(1):326.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1186/s12974-026-04062-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12974-026-04062-0), PMID [42806375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42806375/)
- **Evidence level:** 4
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A cross-sectional study of 183 people: 54 with MS on anti-CD20 drugs (mean age 47), 22 with newly
diagnosed, untreated MS (mean age 33) and 107 controls without MS. The authors stimulated blood cells
with EBV peptides, mostly ones shown to CD8+ T cells, and measured EBV-specific CD8+ and CD4+ T cells.
They also measured antibodies to two EBV proteins, EBNA1 and VCA.

## What it found

- EBV-specific T cells were similar in controls and untreated MS.
- People on anti-CD20 drugs had fewer EBV-specific CD8+ T cells (p < 0.0001) and CD4+ T cells
  (p = 0.0004) than controls, and fewer EBV-specific CD8+ T cells than untreated MS (p = 0.0023).
  General (polyclonal) T-cell responses were largely similar across groups.
- Longer anti-CD20 treatment went with fewer EBV-specific CD8+ T cells (p = 0.0003). People on
  ofatumumab had more of these cells than people on ocrelizumab (p < 0.0001).
- Antibodies to EBNA1 and VCA stayed higher in treated MS than in untreated MS or controls, and were
  unrelated to treatment duration, disease duration, disability or drug.

## Limits

- Cross-sectional: each person was measured once, so it cannot show that treatment caused the
  lower T-cell levels. The treated group was also about 14 years older than the untreated MS group.
- Fewer EBV-specific T cells is a change in the immune response to EBV, not a measure of how active
  the virus is. The study did not measure EBV DNA.
- The authors suggest fewer EBV-reactive T cells may help explain how well anti-CD20 drugs work. That
  is a proposal; the study did not test whether people with bigger drops did better.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
