# INSPIRE: an HIV drug (raltegravir) in active relapsing MS

- **Citation:** Gold J, Marta M, Meier UC, et al. A phase II baseline versus treatment study to determine the efficacy of raltegravir (Isentress) in preventing progression of relapsing remitting multiple sclerosis as determined by gadolinium-enhanced MRI: The INSPIRE study. *Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*, 2018;24:123-128.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2018.06.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2018.06.002), PMID [29990894](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29990894/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (a single-group study comparing each person's treatment phase with their own baseline phase; no randomization or placebo)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A phase 2a trial ([NCT01767701](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01767701)) in 20 people with
active relapsing MS. Each person had a 3-month baseline phase with no study drug, then 3 months of
raltegravir 400 mg twice a day. Raltegravir is an HIV drug. The idea was that HIV drugs might
suppress human endogenous retroviruses (HERVs), old viral sequences in human DNA that some
researchers link to MS. Everyone had monthly MRI with contrast, plus saliva tests for EBV shedding
and blood tests for HERV and inflammation markers.

The main outcome was the number of gadolinium-enhancing lesions (a sign of active inflammation) in
the treatment phase compared with the baseline phase.

## What it found

- All 20 people completed the 6 months.
- Raltegravir had no significant effect on the rate of new gadolinium-enhancing lesions compared
  with each person's baseline phase.
- Disability and quality of life showed no change that could reasonably be attributed to the drug.
- Inflammation markers and serum CD163 were unchanged.
- Separately from the drug, a HERV-W marker on B cells was associated with the number of enhancing
  lesions (p = 0.029). The amount of EBV shed in saliva was not significantly correlated with lesion
  numbers; the authors describe a borderline association with total lesions.
- The authors conclude that raltegravir did not affect MS disease activity.

## Limits

- 20 people, 3 months of treatment, no placebo group and no blinding. Each person was their own
  control, so changes over time, such as activity settling on its own after an active period, cannot
  be separated from a drug effect. That applies to a null result too.
- One drug at one dose. The authors note that a different drug, a combination as used for HIV, or
  longer treatment might behave differently, and that HERVs may not matter once MS is established.
- This tested a HERV idea, not EBV directly. Raltegravir is not an anti-EBV drug, so the result says
  little about antivirals aimed at EBV.
- The HERV and EBV associations are exploratory correlations among several markers, with no
  correction for multiple tests reported in the abstract.
- The ClinicalTrials.gov record lists 23 enrolled and 20 started and completed. Its posted adverse
  event table gives a serious event in 1 person, with 31 listed as at risk, which does not match
  either count. This is not reconciled here.
- Numbers come from the abstract and the registry API. The full text is not open access and was not
  read, so the lesion counts themselves are not given.
