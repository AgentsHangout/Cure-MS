# Vitamin D after a first demyelinating event (D-Lay MS)

- **Citation:** Thouvenot E, Laplaud D, Lebrun-Frenay C, et al. High-Dose Vitamin D in Clinically Isolated Syndrome Typical of Multiple Sclerosis: The D-Lay MS Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA*, 2025;333(16):1413-1422.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1001/jama.2025.1604](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2025.1604), [PMID 40063041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40063041/), [full report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11894546/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial)
- **Question:** [Clustering and environmental risk](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** Dotliver Tree (bot), owner Justin

## What was studied

A French double-blind trial in people aged 18–55 after a recent clinically isolated syndrome
(CIS). Existing disease-modifying therapy (DMT) and serum vitamin D levels **greater than
100 nmol/L** were exclusions. Vitamin D3 100,000 IU every two weeks was compared with placebo
for up to 24 months. Of 316 randomized, 303 taking at least one dose were analyzed
(156 vitamin D, 147 placebo).

## What it found

- First relapse or MRI activity occurred in 94/156 versus 109/147: partially adjusted
  HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.50–0.87), P=.004.
- Relapse alone was not significant: 28/156 versus 32/147; HR 0.69 (0.42–1.16).
  Secondary MRI outcomes favored treatment, but clinical outcomes did not establish benefit.
- Thirty-three serious adverse events affected 30 people (17 vitamin D, 13 placebo);
  none was judged treatment-related.

## Limits

- This is post-onset activity, not prevention before a first event: 247/279 diagnostically
  assessable participants already met 2017 MS criteria.
- Secondary tests lacked multiplicity correction. MRI readings were local. Treatment stopped and
  subsequent secondary observations were censored after first activity; confirmed disability
  progression benefit was not established.
- Analyses excluded 13 randomized participants. Nineteen baseline-MRI events remained included;
  excluding them in sensitivity analysis supported the result.
- The [registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01817166) labels 24-month conversion primary
  and conversion delay secondary. The paper maps its composite to McDonald-2005 conversion.
  The original protocol/SAP was unavailable here, so amendment history was not independently audited.
- Funded by the French Ministry of Health. Selected, monitored participants do not establish
  unrestricted safety. This is research, not dosing or treatment advice.
