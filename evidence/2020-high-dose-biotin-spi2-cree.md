# SPI2: high-dose biotin (MD1003) in progressive MS

- **Citation:** Cree BAC, Cutter G, Wolinsky JS, et al. Safety and efficacy of MD1003 (high-dose biotin) in patients with progressive multiple sclerosis (SPI2): a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial. *The Lancet Neurology*, 2020;19(12):988-997.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(20)30347-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(20)30347-1), PMID [33222767](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33222767/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at 90 clinics in 13 countries in 642 people aged
18 to 65 with primary or secondary progressive MS, an EDSS score of 3.5 to 6.5, worsening disability
and no relapses in the previous 2 years. MD1003 is pharmaceutical-grade biotin at a very high dose
(100 mg three times a day), proposed to improve the energy supply of nerve and myelin-making cells.
An earlier, smaller trial (MS-SPI) had reported improvement. People were assigned to MD1003 (326) or
placebo (316). The main outcome was the share of people whose EDSS or timed 25-foot walk improved
at month 12 and stayed improved at month 15, which is an outcome people could feel.

## What it found

- In a trial of 642 people, improvement confirmed at month 15 was seen in 12% on high-dose biotin
  vs 9% on placebo (odds ratio 1.35, 95% CI 0.81 to 2.26), which was not a significant difference.
- Adverse events were similar (84% vs 85%), and serious adverse events were 26% in both groups. One
  person on biotin died.
- High-dose biotin made some lab tests (those using biotinylated antibodies) give inaccurate results,
  despite steps to prevent this.
- The authors concluded that MD1003 cannot be recommended for progressive MS.

## Limits

- A large trial that did not repeat the earlier positive result.
- People who had relapses recently, or whose disability was outside the EDSS range, were not
  included.
- Funded by the drug's maker (MedDay Pharmaceuticals).
