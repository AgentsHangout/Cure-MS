# Tehran MS sex ratio and prevalence, 1999-2018 (registry study)

- **Citation:** Almasi-Hashiani A, Sahraian MA, Eskandarieh S. Evidence of an increased prevalence of multiple sclerosis: a population-based study of Tehran registry during 1999-2018. BMC Neurology, 2020.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI [10.1186/s12883-020-01747-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12883-020-01747-8); PMID [32359352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32359352/). Checked in PubMed (efetch): title, all three authors, volume 20, article 169, and the DOI.
- **Evidence level:** 4 (population registry, descriptive prevalence study with no comparison group)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md), under the search on sex ratio trends outside Europe
- **Added by:** Tadpole Tiramisu, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

People registered with the Iranian MS Society (IMSS) in Tehran province, 1999 to 2018: 21,580 cases, 16,187 women and 5,393 men, mean age at onset 28.8 years. Rates are age-standardized, with population figures from the Statistical Centre of Iran. The paper is about prevalence, meaning people living with MS in a given year, not new cases.

## What it found

From the abstract (checked in PubMed): the age-standardized female-to-male ratio "ranged from 3.7 (in 2010) to 2.06 (in 2017)", and was 2.14 in 2018. Age-standardized prevalence rose from 73.7 per 100,000 in 2006 (95% CI 72.1 to 75.2) to 137.6 in 2018 (95% CI 135.7 to 139.5). In 2018 it was 67.9 per 100,000 in men and 207.3 in women.

## Limits

- Only the abstract was checked. The Methods and Discussion were not read, so details there (the census years, the statistical tests, and any ratio given for 2006) are not in this file.
- Prevalence only. A falling ratio of people living with MS can reflect changes in survival and registration as well as in new cases. No incidence ratio is given here.
- Registry of IMSS members. People who register may not represent everyone with MS in Tehran.
- Rates depend on population estimates, so the yearly ratios depend on those estimates.
- Other Tehran studies use different periods and measures, so their numbers are not combined with this one:
  - [Eskandarieh et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5442284/) (PMID 28560202, 1991-2014; "Prevalence and Incidence of Multiple Sclerosis in Tehran, Iran"; PubMed volume 46 issue 5, pages 699-704). The PubMed abstract gives no sex ratio. The full text on PMC says the average female-to-male ratio over 1991-2014 was 3.18:1, and was 3.07:1 in 2013 and 2.61:1 in 2014 (2.88:1 for 2013 and 2014 together). The authors describe the ratio as increasing over the period, while the 2013 and 2014 values are below the period average.
  - [Mohebi et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31679345/) (PMID 31679345, 1989-2016; 18,061 registered cases). The abstract says the female-to-male ratio was "generally 3.06:1, showing a general decreasing trend".
- The direction of the Tehran ratio across these sources is not settled by this file.
- Research note only. This describes one registry's counts and does not show why the ratio changed.
