# Genetically lower vitamin D and MS risk (Mendelian randomization)

- **Citation:** Mokry LE, Ross S, Ahmad OS, et al. Vitamin D and Risk of Multiple Sclerosis: A Mendelian Randomization Study. *PLoS Medicine*, 2015;12(8):e1001866.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001866](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001866), PMID [26305103](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26305103/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a genetic association study; Mendelian randomization reduces but does not remove confounding)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Mendelian randomization uses gene variants that affect an exposure as a natural experiment, because
variants are assigned at conception and are less tied to lifestyle than measured blood levels. The
authors took four variants linked to blood 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25OHD) in the SUNLIGHT genetic study
(33,996 people), confirmed their effect on 25OHD in 2,347 people from a Canadian cohort, and tested
them in the International MS Genetics Consortium data (up to 14,498 people with MS and 24,091
controls).

## What it found

- Each genetically determined one-standard-deviation decrease in log 25OHD was associated with
  about twice the odds of MS (OR 2.0, 95% CI 1.7 to 2.5; p = 7.7 × 10⁻¹²).
- The result held when variants possibly affected by population structure or pleiotropy were left
  out (OR 1.7, 95% CI 1.3 to 2.2), and when only variants in vitamin D synthesis (OR 2.1) or
  metabolism (OR 1.9) were used.

## Limits

- Only four variants, and pleiotropy (variants affecting MS through other paths) cannot be fully
  excluded, as the authors say.
- It estimates the effect of lifelong, genetically lower vitamin D, not of taking supplements as an
  adult, so it does not show that supplements prevent MS. The authors call for long-term randomized
  trials.
- Mostly people of European ancestry.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
- A correction was published (*PLoS Medicine* 2016;13(3):e1001981,
  [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001981](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001981), PMID 26963823).
  It adds an author who was left off the byline and corrects a diagram of the vitamin D pathway.
  It changes none of the numbers above. Read 11/10/2026.
