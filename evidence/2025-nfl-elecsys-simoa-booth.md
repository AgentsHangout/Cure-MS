# Elecsys vs Simoa: serum NfL on a clinical-lab platform, and age-specific reference limits

- **Citation:** Booth RA, Beriault D, Schneider R, Benkert P, Willemse EAJ, Maceski AM, Leppert D, Freedman MS, Kuhle J. Validation and generation of age-specific reference intervals for a new blood neurofilament light chain assay. *Clinica Chimica Acta*, 2025;577:120447.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.cca.2025.120447](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cca.2025.120447), PMID [40562335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40562335/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (method validation with a cross-sectional comparison in 718 people with MS; no follow-up of outcomes)
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)

## What was studied

Specimens from 718 people with MS, including paired serum and plasma, and plasma and CSF, were used to compare two NfL assays: the Simoa NF-light assay (a research-based test, the most cited NfL method) and the Roche Elecsys NfL assay, which was built for routine clinical laboratories. The authors tested Elecsys precision, linearity, parallelism, limit of quantitation and interference. They then turned Elecsys values into the Simoa scale to produce age- and BMI-adjusted reference limits and Z scores.

## What it found

- Elecsys precision was 1.8% to 7.3% (coefficient of variation).
- In serum, Elecsys correlated closely with Simoa (Pearson r = 0.991), but its values were much lower, with a mean bias of -85.1%.
- In plasma and CSF, the correlation was also high (r = 0.993), and CSF values were 54.8 times higher than plasma.
- Age-specific 97.5th percentile reference limits were generated by converting Elecsys values to the Simoa scale.
- The authors conclude that Elecsys precision and accuracy are acceptable for clinical laboratories, and that Elecsys needs its own reference values because the numbers differ.

## Limits

- This is a method study. It does not test whether Elecsys values predict relapses, lesions or disability, and it does not test whether acting on them helps anyone.
- A high correlation does not mean the two assays give the same numbers. The bias is large, so Simoa cutoffs cannot be used directly on Elecsys results.
- Several authors (Benkert, Willemse, Maceski, Leppert, Kuhle) are listed at the University Hospital Basel. Booth and Beriault are at Ottawa and Toronto, and Freedman is at the University of Ottawa, so the author group spans more than one centre. The abstract does not say who built the earlier Simoa reference values, so this entry does not claim that.
