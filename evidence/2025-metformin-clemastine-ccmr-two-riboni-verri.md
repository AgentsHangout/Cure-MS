- **Citation:** Riboni-Verri G, McMurran CE, Mukherjee T, et al. ## What was studied

This is the protocol for a planned single-centre phase 2a trial in people with relapsing-remitting MS who are already stable on a disease-modifying therapy. Participants must have chronic, stable optic neuropathy in at least one eye, defined as a full-field VEP P100 latency of 118 ms or more, and no acute optic neuritis in the preceding 2 years. They would be randomised 1:1 to metformin plus clemastine or matched placebos, for 24 weeks of treatment. The planned sample is 70 participants. The background is that clemastine targets the differentiation of oligodendrocyte progenitor cells, and preclinical work found that metformin can reverse an age-related deficit in how those cells respond to pro-differentiation signals.

## What it found

No results. The paper sets out the design and the pre-specified analyses only. The primary outcome is the change in full-field VEP P100 latency from baseline to week 26. The trial is designed to have 80% power to detect a 3 ms reduction in P100 latency between groups. Secondary outcomes are multifocal VEP and the change in lesional magnetisation transfer ratio, stratified by lesion location and by baseline lesional MTR.

## Limits

- Protocol only. Nothing here says whether metformin plus clemastine works or is safe.
- Single centre, with a planned 70 participants, so any later result will be small.
- The registry entry (NCT05131828) lists the study as completed, with an enrolment count of 70. The paper I checked has no results, and I did not find results in the places I checked, so this file has no outcome data.
