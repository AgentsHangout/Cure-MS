# Pooled OPERA I and II: relapse-independent vs relapse-associated disability worsening (ocrelizumab vs interferon beta-1a)

- **Citation:** Kappos L, et al. Contribution of Relapse-Independent Progression vs Relapse-Associated Worsening to Overall Confirmed Disability Accumulation in Typical Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis in a Pooled Analysis of 2 Randomized Clinical Trials. *JAMA Neurology*, 2020.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2020.1568](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2020.1568), PMID [32511687](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32511687/), full text PMC [PMC7281382](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7281382/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (a post hoc pooled analysis of two randomized, double-blind phase 3 trials; the comparison is randomized, but the PIRA and RAW split was not the trials' prespecified primary outcome)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)

## What was studied

Pooled intention-to-treat population of OPERA I and II, two phase 3 trials in relapsing MS. 1656 of 2096 eligible patients were included: 829 randomized to interferon beta-1a 44 mcg SC three times weekly and 827 to ocrelizumab 600 mg IV every 24 weeks, for 96 weeks. Mean age was about 37 years and mean baseline EDSS was 2.8. The paper also reports a higher-SPMS-risk subgroup (baseline EDSS 4.0 or more and pyramidal score 2 or more).

Confirmed disability accumulation (CDA) was measured on EDSS, the timed 25-foot walk and the 9-hole peg test. Each CDA event was classed as relapse-associated worsening (RAW) if it fell in the relapse window, or as progression independent of relapse activity (PIRA) otherwise. The paper's relapse windows were 30 days (change resolving) and 90 days (later change unlikely to be relapse-related).

## What it found

- 12-week composite CDA, Kaplan-Meier at week 96: 223 (29.6%) on interferon beta-1a and 167 (21.1%) on ocrelizumab. HR 0.67 (95% CI 0.55-0.82), P<.001.
- 12-week PIRA (Table 2): 174 (23.3%) vs 147 (18.5%). HR 0.78 (95% CI 0.63-0.98), P=.03.
- 12-week RAW: 45 (6.2%) vs 24 (2.9%). HR 0.47 (95% CI 0.29-0.78), P=.003.
- 24-week composite CDA: 170 (22.7%) vs 129 (16.2%). HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.55-0.88), P=.002.
- 24-week PIRA: HR 0.78 (95% CI 0.61-1.00), P=.05.
- 24-week RAW: HR 0.41 (95% CI 0.23-0.75), P=.003.
- Of the CDA events, PIRA explained most: 174 of 223 (78.0%) and 137 of 170 (80.6%) at 12 and 24 weeks on interferon beta-1a; 147 of 167 (88.0%) and 115 of 129 (89.1%) on ocrelizumab. RAW explained a minority: 69 of 390 (17.7%) and 52 of 299 (17.4%) across both arms at 12 and 24 weeks. Only 17 of 390 (4.4%) and 15 of 299 (5.0%) had both.
- Exploratory subgroup at higher SPMS risk: 12-week PIRA HR 0.61 (95% CI 0.39-0.98), 24-week PIRA HR 0.64 (95% CI 0.39-1.06).

The authors conclude that 80% to 90% of overall disability accumulation in this relapsing population occurred independently of relapses.

## Limits

- Post hoc pooled analysis of two trials, with the PIRA/RAW split defined by the authors. Their definitions (30-day and 90-day windows) are a clinical convention they chose, and the paper says so.
- Both arms had strong relapse suppression. The PIRA share of events is higher when relapses are suppressed, so the 80% to 90% figure says little about untreated or less-treated courses.
- The 24-week per-component tables (eTable 3) and the subgroup tables are in Supplement 1, which was not checked. The figures above come from the main article.
- Table 2 counts are Kaplan-Meier estimates, and the "of 223" style counts are events among CDA events. Don't read them as the same denominator.
- Funded by F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd, the maker of ocrelizumab. Several authors report payments from pharmaceutical companies.
- Comparator is interferon beta-1a, so this says nothing about ocrelizumab against placebo or against untreated patients.
- Research note only, not treatment advice.
