# Estriol added to glatiramer acetate in women with relapsing-remitting MS: a phase 2 trial

- **Citation:** Voskuhl RR, Wang H, Wu TC, et al. Estriol combined with glatiramer acetate for women with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis: a randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 2 trial. *Lancet Neurology*, 2016;15(1):35-46.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(15)00322-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(15)00322-1), PMID [26621682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26621682/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Relapses fall during pregnancy, when levels of the hormone estriol rise. The trial tested whether
estriol reduces relapses. At 16 US academic centers, from 2007 to 2014, 164 women aged 18 to 50 with
relapsing-remitting MS were randomly assigned to daily oral estriol 8 mg (83 women) or placebo (81),
each added to daily injected glatiramer acetate 20 mg. The main outcome was the yearly rate of
confirmed relapses over 24 months, with a pre-set significance level of p=0.10 (less strict than
the usual 0.05). ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00451204.

## What it found

- Confirmed relapse rate: 0.25 per year (95% CI 0.17-0.37) with estriol against 0.37 (0.25-0.53)
  with placebo; adjusted rate ratio 0.63 (95% CI 0.37-1.05), p=0.077.
- That met the trial's own p<0.10 threshold, but the confidence interval includes no difference
  (1.0), so by the usual 0.05 standard the result is not conclusive.
- Serious adverse events were similar (8 of 82 vs 10 of 76). Irregular menstrual periods were more
  common with estriol (23% vs 4%); vaginal infections were less common (1% vs 11%). No differences
  were seen in breast, uterine or endometrial measures that were checked.
- The authors call for a phase 3 trial.

## Limits

- Phase 2, 164 women, and a relaxed significance threshold. A larger trial would be needed to
  confirm any effect.
- Estriol was tested only on top of glatiramer acetate and only in women.
- The trial tests one pregnancy hormone; it does not show that estriol explains why relapses fall
  in pregnancy or why MS is more common in women.
