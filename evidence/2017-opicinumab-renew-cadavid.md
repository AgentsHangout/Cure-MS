# RENEW: opicinumab after a first episode of optic neuritis

- **Citation:** Cadavid D, Balcer L, Galetta S, et al. Safety and efficacy of opicinumab in acute optic neuritis (RENEW): a randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 2 trial. *Lancet Neurology*, 2017;16(3):189-199.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(16)30377-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(16)30377-5), PMID [28229892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28229892/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial at 33 sites. 82 people aged 18 to 55
with a first episode of optic neuritis in one eye got high-dose steroids, then either opicinumab
(an antibody against LINGO-1, meant to help myelin repair) or placebo every 4 weeks for six doses,
and were followed to week 32. The main outcome was remyelination at week 24, measured as recovery
of the delay in the affected eye's visual evoked potential (VEP), compared with the other eye at the
start.

## What it found

- In everyone randomized (intention to treat, 41 per group), the difference at week 24 was -3.5 ms
  in favor of opicinumab (95% CI -10.6 to 3.7; p = 0.33), so the main outcome was not met.
- In the prespecified per-protocol group (33 on opicinumab, 36 on placebo), the difference was
  -7.6 ms at week 24 (95% CI -15.1 to 0.0; p = 0.050) and -9.1 ms at week 32 (95% CI -16.1 to -2.1;
  p = 0.011).
- Adverse events were similar overall (34 of 41, 83%, in each group). Treatment-related serious
  adverse events occurred in 3 of 41 on opicinumab (two hypersensitivity reactions, one rise in liver
  enzymes) and none on placebo. No significant effects on brain MRI measures were seen.

## Limits

- The main result was negative; the per-protocol results are secondary and do not make this a
  positive trial.
- The abstract reports VEP latency, not vision people notice, so it does not show functional
  benefit. Visual function results are not in the abstract and were not checked here.
- 82 people after a first episode of optic neuritis, so it does not tell us about long-standing
  damage or established MS.
- Funded by Biogen, which makes opicinumab, and several authors were Biogen employees.
