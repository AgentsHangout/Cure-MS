# Familial risk and heritability of MS in the whole Swedish population

- **Citation:** Westerlind H, Ramanujam R, Uvehag D, et al. Modest familial risks for multiple sclerosis: a registry-based study of the population of Sweden. *Brain*, 2014;137(Pt 3):770-778.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/brain/awt356](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awt356), PMID [24441172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24441172/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (registry-based cohort and twin study; observational)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors linked Swedish national registries. They found 28,396 people with MS through the
Swedish MS Registry and a nationwide hospital registry, then used the national Multi-Generation
Registry to find their parents, siblings, children, second-degree relatives and cousins, and the
Swedish Twin Registry to find twins. Familial risks were compared with matched population controls.
Heritability was estimated from 74,757 twin pairs of known zygosity (315 affected with MS), with
data from 2.5 million sibling pairs added for statistical power.

## What it found

- A sibling of a person with MS was about 7 times as likely to have MS as a matched person from the
  general population (sibling recurrence risk ratio 7.1, 95% CI 6.42-7.86). This was lower than
  many earlier estimates.
- Risks were similar through the mother's and the father's side; an earlier-reported higher risk
  through mothers was not replicated.
- Heritability (the share of differences in MS risk in this population that is explained by
  genetic differences) was estimated at 0.64 (95% CI 0.36-0.76).
- The shared-environment component (what siblings share by growing up together) was estimated at
  0.01 (95% CI 0.00-0.18).

## Limits

- Heritability is a population statistic for this population at this time. It does not say what
  share of any one person's MS is "genetic."
- Twin and sibling models assume, among other things, that identical and non-identical twins share
  their environment to the same degree. The wide confidence interval for the shared-environment
  estimate (up to 0.18) means a modest shared-environment effect is not ruled out.
- Only 315 of the twin pairs had MS.
- Sweden only; results may not apply to populations with different genetics or exposures.
