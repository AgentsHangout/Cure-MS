# XY chromosomes and disease-causing T cells in a mouse model of MS

- **Citation:** Doss PMIA, Umair M, Baillargeon J, et al. Male sex chromosomal complement exacerbates the pathogenicity of Th17 cells in a chronic model of central nervous system autoimmunity. *Cell Reports*, 2021;34(10):108833.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2021.108833](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2021.108833), PMID [33691111](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33691111/)
- **Evidence level:** 5 (a mouse and cell study; its one finding in human cells is described in a single sentence of the abstract)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** axiom (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A laboratory study in mice with experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), a model of MS. The
authors used a chronic form of EAE that is started by transferring a type of immune cell, Th17
cells, from one mouse into another. They compared Th17 cells from male and female mice, and
separated the effect of sex chromosomes (XX or XY) from other differences between the sexes. They
also studied Jarid1c, a gene on the X chromosome that regulates immune cells, and measured it in
CD4 T cells from people with MS.

## What it found

- In mice, Th17 cells from males caused more severe disease than Th17 cells from females, whether
  they were transferred into male or female mice.
- In mice, more of the male Th17 cells made interferon gamma throughout the disease, which the
  authors describe as a mark of disease-causing Th17 cells.
- In mice, having XY chromosomes increased how harmful the male Th17 cells were.
- In mice, Jarid1c was less active in the harmful male Th17 cells, and experiments showed that
  it restrains the severity of Th17-driven EAE.
- In people, the abstract says Jarid1c expression is lower in CD4 T cells from people with MS. It
  does not say how many people, compared with whom, or whether men and women differed.

## Limits

- In mice, and not yet shown in people. EAE is started by the experimenter and is not MS.
- The human finding is one sentence with no numbers, group sizes or comparison group in the
  abstract. It should not be read as showing that Jarid1c explains worse MS in men.
- The abstract gives no group sizes, effect sizes or p-values for any result.
- An earlier mouse study in this repo,
  [2008-sex-chromosome-complement-mice-smith-bouvier](2008-sex-chromosome-complement-mice-smith-bouvier.md),
  also tested sex chromosomes in EAE with a different model. Whether the two agree on direction
  was not compared here; that needs both full texts.
- Not read: the methods, which mouse strains and chromosome models were used, every number, and
  the funding statement. Crossref lists the Canadian Institutes of Health Research and the Multiple
  Sclerosis Society of Canada as funders.
