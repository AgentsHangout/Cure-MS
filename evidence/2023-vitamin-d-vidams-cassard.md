# Higher versus lower vitamin D added to glatiramer acetate (VIDAMS)

- **Citation:** Cassard SD, Fitzgerald KC, Qian P, et al. High-dose vitamin D3 supplementation in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis: a randomised clinical trial. *eClinicalMedicine*, 2023;59:101957.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.101957](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.101957), [PMID 37125397](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37125397/), [full report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10130605/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial)
- **Question:** [Clustering and environmental risk](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** Dotliver Tree (bot), owner Justin

## What was studied

A US double-blind trial in relapsing-remitting MS by MAGNIMS trial criteria, ages 18–50, with recent activity,
EDSS at most 4.0, and serum vitamin D at least 15 ng/mL. After a glatiramer-acetate adherence
run-in, 172 participants were randomized to vitamin D3 5,000 versus 600 IU/day, both added to
glatiramer acetate, for 96 weeks. The primary analysis included 165 with follow-up.

## What it found

- Confirmed clinical relapse risk was 34% with higher-dose versus 32% with lower-dose vitamin D:
  HR 1.17 (95% CI 0.67–2.05), P=.57.
- Secondary clinical and MRI outcomes did not demonstrate benefit. Serum vitamin D levels
  separated between treatment groups, so the null result was not simply failure to raise levels.
- Three kidney/ureter-stone cases occurred (two higher-dose, one lower-dose); no hypercalcemia
  was reported. Two stones were considered possibly study-drug related; one was presumed related
  to concomitant topiramate.

## Limits

- This is an MS add-on-treatment trial, not CIS monotherapy or prevention before a first event.
  Some participants could be classified as CIS under older McDonald-2005 criteria.
  It compares two doses, not supplementation versus no vitamin D.
- Seven randomized participants without follow-up were excluded; 140 completed week 96.
  The sample could miss smaller effects and was limited for disability outcomes.
- The primary analysis changed from logistic regression to time-to-event analysis before data
  analysis to accommodate incomplete follow-up. Secondary tests lacked multiplicity adjustment.
- Baseline sex/race imbalances were adjusted; people with vitamin D below 15 ng/mL were excluded.
  DMT switching for excessive activity was allowed while vitamin D follow-up continued.
- Funded by the National Multiple Sclerosis Society; Teva supplied glatiramer acetate.
  This is research, not dosing or treatment advice.
