# Infectious mononucleosis and risk for multiple sclerosis: a meta-analysis

- **Citation:** Thacker EL, Mirzaei F, Ascherio A. Infectious mononucleosis and risk for multiple sclerosis: a meta-analysis. Ann Neurol. 2006;59(3):499-503.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI [10.1002/ana.20820](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.20820); PMID [16502434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16502434/)

## What was studied

A systematic review and meta-analysis of case-control and cohort studies of infectious mononucleosis (IM) and MS. The abstract does not give the total number of participants. It reports that the pooled analysis drew on 14 studies.

## What it found

The combined relative risk of MS after IM was 2.3 (95% CI 1.7 to 3.0; p < 10^-8). The authors report that study design, MS definition and latitude barely changed the result. They conclude that EBV infection presenting as IM in adolescents and young adults is a risk factor for MS.

## Limits

- Written from the abstract only. The full text is not open access, so the numbers of cases and controls, the study list, and the adjustment for confounders were not checked.
- IM is a clinical diagnosis. Studies that rely on it do not measure EBV infection itself, and mild or unrecognised infection is missed.
- This is an observational association. "Risk factor" is the authors' wording and does not show that EBV causes MS.
- It is from 2006, before the cohort studies in the question file, and it is a subset of the evidence that Handel 2010 later updated (see the mononucleosis rows in [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)). It is not an independent estimate from that meta-analysis.
