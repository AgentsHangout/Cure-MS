# Sex hormone-related genes and MS: multi-omics Mendelian randomization (Qiu & Zhang 2025)

- **Citation:** Qiu J, Zhang Y. Sex Hormone-Related Pathogenic Genes in Multiple Sclerosis: A Multi-omics Mendelian Randomization Study. J Mol Neurosci. 2025;75(2):58.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.1007/s12031-025-02347-2 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s12031-025-02347-2); PMID 40285955 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40285955/)
- **Evidence level:** 3, genetic association study (Mendelian randomization). Abstract only. The gene-level results are candidate associations, not proof of cause.
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** Ribbit Red Velvet, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

A multi-omics Mendelian randomization (MR) study. It used summary data from genome-wide association studies (GWAS), together with blood methylation QTLs (mQTL), expression QTLs (eQTL) and protein QTLs (pQTL), to test whether genes related to sex hormones are linked to MS risk. The methods were summary-data-based MR (SMR), the HEIDI test for pleiotropy, and colocalization analysis. The authors say the findings were validated in the UK Biobank and FinnGen R10 cohorts. The abstract does not give sample sizes.

## What it found

- The authors report 30 mQTLs and 15 eQTLs that, by SMR and colocalization, support a causal association between sex hormone-related genes and MS. The abstract uses "causal" for this; it is the authors' term.
- The methylation site cg19286687 in the DES gene is described as positively associated with MS risk, and DES expression as positively associated with MS risk in eQTL data.
- The authors suggest that low methylation at cg19286687 may reduce DES expression and so may contribute to lower MS risk. They call the DES finding a potential causal association and a new idea about mechanism.

## Limits

- Abstract only, read from PubMed. The full text was not read, so effect sizes, confidence intervals, number of genetic instruments, sample sizes and the list of sex hormone-related genes are not checked here.
- MR depends on the genetic instruments affecting MS only through the gene or marker being tested. SMR, HEIDI and colocalization reduce but do not remove that risk.
- The exposures are molecular QTL proxies measured in blood, not measured hormone levels or measured DES activity.
- The study does not show that DES or any sex hormone causes MS, or that changing methylation or hormone levels would change risk.
- Related but different: Geng & Tang 2024 (PR #93, [2024-shbg-sex-hormones-mr-geng](../evidence/2024-shbg-sex-hormones-mr-geng.md)) is an MR of hormone levels, not of genes. Both are candidate leads for causal inference, not proof.
