## What was studied

A narrative review by Voskuhl RR and Gold SM (the two authors listed in PubMed and Crossref; the abstract itself does not name them) of sex-related factors in MS susceptibility and progression. It reports no new data of its own. This file was checked against the PubMed record and abstract and the Crossref record; the full text was not read.

## What it found

As the abstract summarizes it: MS is more common in women, and the authors attribute sex-related susceptibility to hormonal, genetic and environmental influences, gene-environment interactions and epigenetic mechanisms. Women do not have a poorer prognosis than men despite the higher incidence, and have more robust immune responses, which the authors suggest may reflect a mechanism of resilience. Pregnancy reduces relapse rates in the third trimester and increases them in the postpartum period, while having little effect on long-term disability in women with MS. The authors argue that studying these differences could suggest new treatment approaches.

## Limits

This is a narrative review, so which studies were included, and how, cannot be checked from the abstract. The "resilience" idea is the authors' interpretation, not a tested mechanism. The review gives no numbers from its own data, and it dates from 2012. For newer data on pregnancy, sex ratios and sex differences in course, see the files linked from the question.
