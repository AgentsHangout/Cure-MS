# A critique of the Faroe Islands MS "epidemic"

- **Citation:** Poser CM, Hibberd PL, Benedikz J, Gudmundsson G. Analysis of the 'epidemic' of multiple sclerosis in the Faroe Islands. I. Clinical and epidemiological aspects. *Neuroepidemiology*, 1988;7(4):168-180. And: Poser CM, Hibberd PL. Analysis of the 'epidemic' of multiple sclerosis in the Faroe Islands. II. Biostatistical aspects. *Neuroepidemiology*, 1988;7(4):181-189.

## What was studied

A two-part reanalysis of the data behind the claim that British troops introduced MS to the Faroe
Islands in World War II (see [2001-faroe-islands-epidemics-kurtzke](2001-faroe-islands-epidemics-kurtzke.md)).
Part I looks at case counting and assumptions. Part II applies statistical tests for clusters to
the 32 cases from 1943 to 1973.

## What it found

- **Case counting.** The authors argue that the exclusion of Faroese born in Denmark or away for 3
  or more years was arbitrary. They count 42 cases instead of 32, including at least 2 with onset
  before 1940. They doubt that MS was absent before 1940 and that ascertainment was complete.
- **Timing.** They argue that onset dates say little about when MS was acquired, which they place
  at ages 5 to 15. Only 15 of the 42 were aged 5 to 14 during the occupation.
- **Statistics.** Common temporal cluster tests were unsuitable for 32 cases. One method found a
  cluster of 16 cases with onset in 1941 to 1950 (p < 0.05), but it did not support the epidemic
  hypothesis once one questionable case was removed. Exposure to British troops was only borderline
  significant, and moving one case between exposed and unexposed removed that support. Analyses
  using the probable age of acquisition also did not support an epidemic.
- They conclude there was no epidemic of MS in the Faroes.

## Limits

- A critique of the same very small data set, so it cannot prove there was no epidemic either; it
  shows how fragile the epidemic conclusion is to case definitions.
- Their assumed age of acquisition (5 to 15) is itself an assumption.
- Kurtzke and colleagues responded in later papers, which are not summarized here.
- Numbers come from the abstracts.
