# Systematic review of whether approved MS treatments promote remyelination

- **Citation:** De Keersmaecker AV, van Doninck E, Wens I, et al. Recent Advances in Interventions Targeting Remyelination and a Systematic Review of Remyelinating Effects of Approved Disease-Modifying Treatments for Multiple Sclerosis. *European Journal of Neurology*, 2025;32(11):e70397.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1111/ene.70397](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.70397), PMID [41216863](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41216863/)
- **Evidence level:** 3, provisional, for the review as a whole. It is a mixed-design systematic review without meta-analysis. Its components differ: 3 randomized trials, 29 non-randomized clinical studies, 57 preclinical studies (level 5) and 8 reviews. It is not treated here as a level-1 synthesis of randomized trials.
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)

## What was studied

A systematic review, registered in advance on PROSPERO and reported with the "Synthesis without
meta-analysis" (SWiM) guideline, with a last search in July 2025. It asked which FDA- or
EMA-approved disease-modifying treatments show evidence of promoting remyelination, to inform
combinations with future repair drugs. The authors judged the models and outcomes too varied for
meta-analysis. The paper also gives a narrative overview of newer remyelination approaches.

## What it found

- 97 manuscripts were included: 57 preclinical, 3 randomized trials, 29 non-randomized clinical
  studies and 8 reviews.
- **Preclinical (level 5):** in the authors' synthesis, convincing evidence of remyelination in cell
  or animal studies was found only for glatiramer acetate, teriflunomide, fingolimod, siponimod,
  ponesimod and alemtuzumab. Results were not uniformly positive; for fingolimod, for example, the
  authors describe conflicting findings across more than 15 preclinical studies.
- **Clinical (mixed randomized and non-randomized):** studies using imaging and nerve-signal
  measures of myelin repair showed promising results for glatiramer acetate, fingolimod, siponimod,
  natalizumab, alemtuzumab and ocrelizumab.
- The authors found insufficient proof that these measures change because of remyelination alone.
  They propose that future trials combine several measures into a composite endpoint.

## Limits

- In people, the signals come from surrogate measures that do not separate remyelination from
  reduced inflammation, natural repair or other tissue changes. The proposed composite endpoint is a
  research proposal, not a validated surrogate for disability benefit.
- Only 3 randomized trials were included, so the clinical conclusions rest mostly on non-randomized
  studies.
- A single reviewer did the initial title and abstract screening, data collection and bias
  assessment; two reviewers independently screened full texts.
- Only English articles from the last 15 years were included, and registered studies with no
  update or publication for over 2 years were excluded. The authors note this could miss negative
  trials.
- The review covers approved treatments only, not new repair drugs like those in this question's
  trial table.
