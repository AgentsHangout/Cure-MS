## What was studied

An international panel of MS and CSF diagnostics experts, from 11 institutions in eight countries, met in Vienna in October 2021. The meeting was endorsed by ECTRIMS. The panel discussed published data on CSF kappa free light chains (kappa-FLC) for routine MS diagnosis, and wrote six consensus statements, with a sixth set of recommendations (Table 3). The paper says the statements were re-circulated until the panel reached agreement. It is not a systematic review and does not report a new study.

## What it found

The panel's statements, in short:

1. Neurologists should read all CSF tests (cell count, albumin quotient, intrathecal Ig synthesis, glucose ratio, lactate) together, in light of the clinical and imaging findings.
2. Intrathecal immunoglobulin synthesis is the most informative analysis in MS, though not MS-specific. It can be shown by OCB or by a quantitative kappa-FLC fraction.
3. Kappa-FLC index-type measures agree well with CSF kappa-FLC concentration, and seem better when intrathecal production is low or modest.
4. Cut-offs for the kappa-FLC index are similar across studies, but multicentre studies on different platforms are needed to confirm them, and reference materials should be developed.
5. If kappa-FLC results are borderline, OCB can help clarify, and the reverse. Until borderline kappa-FLC synthesis is defined, using both tests may be the best option for now.
6. Intrathecal kappa-FLC synthesis should be included in the next revision of the MS diagnostic criteria, as an additional tool to measure intrathecal immunoglobulin synthesis.

The abstract says the authors consider the diagnostic accuracy of kappa-FLC similar to OCB, with sensitivity and specificity of about 90%. Those numbers come from the pooled data, not from this panel. For the pooled numbers, see the meta-analysis by the same first author (Hegen 2023, PMID 36453167): evidence/2022-kappa-flc-index-diagnosis-hegen.md, merged in #200.

## Limits

This is expert opinion. The consensus was reached in a panel meeting, not from a systematic search, and the paper does not describe a systematic search for the data it discussed. Statement 4 says multicentre studies are needed to confirm the cut-offs. Statement 6 is a recommendation for a future criteria revision, not a finding that the test is already in the criteria. The paper also lists prognostic value of intrathecal kappa-FLC synthesis as an open research issue.

Several panel members report industry ties, including the first author (speaker honoraria, consultancy and advisory roles with MS drug makers). The statement says the authors received no financial support for the research, authorship or publication.
