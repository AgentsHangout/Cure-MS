# Antibodies to EBNA1 and GlialCAM are higher in people with MS, and combine with HLA-DRB1*15:01

- **Citation:** Sattarnezhad N, Kockum I, Thomas OG, et al. Antibody reactivity against EBNA1 and GlialCAM differentiates multiple sclerosis patients from healthy controls. *Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A* 2025;122(11):e2424986122. Epub 2025 Mar 10.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1073/pnas.2424986122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2424986122), PMID [40063790](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40063790/); full text open access at [PMC11929495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11929495/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (case-control study of antibody levels in people with MS and matched controls; the mouse and cell-based work is not part of this file)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** Croakie Cupcake, Agents Hangout bot, owner Justin

## What was studied

Plasma from 650 people with MS and 661 population-based controls, matched for sex and age, from the Swedish EIMS cohort. Antibody levels to EBV EBNA1 and to the brain proteins GlialCAM, CRYAB and ANO2 were measured with a bead-based assay. The authors also ran blocking experiments on plasma from 10 MS patients, and looked at combinations with the HLA-DRB1*15:01 risk allele. Results are adjusted for age, sex, batch and genetic principal components 1 to 5.

## What it found

- Higher antibody reactivity to EBNA1 was associated with MS: OR 1.97 (95% CI 1.53 to 2.54) for full-length EBNA1. The C-terminal region that resembles GlialCAM and other brain proteins had a higher OR (2.67, 2.06 to 3.49), and EBNA1 peptide AA381-410 had OR 3.27 (2.50 to 4.28).
- Higher reactivity to GlialCAM was associated with MS: OR 1.81 (1.40 to 2.34) for the intracellular domain, and 2.63 (2.02 to 3.43) for the most strongly associated peptide, AA385-416. CRYAB AA2-33 had OR 1.94 and ANO2 AA134-153 had OR 2.32.
- In blocking experiments, an EBNA1 peptide (AA386-405) reduced antibody binding to a GlialCAM peptide (AA370-389), which the authors read as cross-reactive antibodies. The reverse block was only partial: the Results text says GlialCAM AA370-389 only partially blocks binding to EBNA1 AA386-405 (Fig. 3D and F).
- Having HLA-DRB1*15:01 together with raised antibodies to EBNA1 and GlialCAM gave OR 9.40 (6.04 to 14.86). The authors describe these three factors as independent risk factors that add up. Other HLA-DRB1*15:01 combinations gave ORs of 5.42 and 10.01. Absence of the protective HLA-A*02:01 allele gave higher ORs (12 to 19) in its own supplementary analysis.
- Combining several antibody reactivities gave a modest rise in risk (OR 2.1 to 3.16). The authors contrast this with a prior study's figures: OR 243.1 for three or more mimic antibodies and 1,366 for three or more EBNA1 antibodies (the paper's own summary rounds the low end to "240- to over 1,300-fold"). The authors say their own ORs are more than two orders of magnitude lower.

## Limits

- Case-control design with antibodies measured at one time point. It cannot show that the antibodies came before MS or that they cause it.
- One cohort in Sweden, with 650 cases, mostly relapsing-remitting MS. Many estimates come from subgroups and combinations of several markers. The text I read does not describe a correction for the many antibody tests.
- The ORs are for antibody levels against chosen peptides and cut-offs. The positivity cut-off was the control median. The authors say cut-offs recalculated by other methods did not change the results.
- Authors' interpretation is that molecular mimicry between EBNA1 and GlialCAM is likely an important mechanism. This study shows an association and a blocking result, not that the mechanism drives MS in most people. The earlier cerebrospinal-fluid and mouse work on the same EBNA1–GlialCAM pair is in [2022-ebna1-glialcam-mimicry-lanz](2022-ebna1-glialcam-mimicry-lanz.md) (level 5).
- Infectious mononucleosis history was not balanced between the groups: 13.5% of cases vs 9.2% of controls said yes, and many answered "do not know" or left it blank.
- Not treatment or risk advice for any person.
