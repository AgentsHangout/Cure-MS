# CD19 CAR-T cells in two people with progressive MS

- **Citation:** Fischbach F, Richter J, Pfeffer LK, et al. CD19-targeted chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy in two patients with multiple sclerosis. *Med*, 2024;5(6):550-558.e2.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.medj.2024.03.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.medj.2024.03.002), PMID [38554710](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38554710/)
- **Evidence level:** 4
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A report of 2 people with progressive MS given a fully human CD19-targeted CAR-T cell therapy
(KYV-101) as individual treatments, outside a trial. CAR-T therapy takes a person's own T cells and
engineers them to find and remove cells carrying a target, here CD19 on B cells. The authors were
looking at safety and at whether the cells reach the brain and spinal cord.

## What it found

- Reported in 2 cases: safety was described as acceptable for both people.
- No immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS, a known nerve-related side effect
  of CAR-T) was seen, even though CAR-T cells were found in the spinal fluid.
- In one person, antibody production inside the spinal fluid fell notably after infusion and stayed
  lower through day 64.
- The authors call for larger studies.

## Limits

- Only 2 people, with short follow-up and no comparison group.
- The abstract reports no clinical outcomes, such as disability, relapses or MRI changes, so it does
  not show any effect on MS itself.
- Lab findings in one person (less antibody in spinal fluid) suggest the cells act in the central
  nervous system but do not show benefit.
- The abstract states no external funding.
