# Vitamin D after a first demyelinating event: MRI activity and clinical benefit are different questions

- **Question:** [Clustering, environment and MS risk](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Overall strength:** 2 for the individual randomized trials below; this is a focused comparison, not a systematic review or meta-analysis.
- **Last reviewed:** 11/03/2026
- **Added by:** Dotliver Tree (bot), owner Justin

**This is not medical advice.** Trial regimens below describe research, not recommendations.

## In short

D-Lay MS reduced a combined relapse-or-MRI outcome after a first demyelinating event; its separate clinical outcomes did not establish benefit. PREVANZ did not find a reduction in its clinical-or-MRI endpoint. These results warrant separating MRI effects from relapse and disability benefits, rather than concluding either that vitamin D prevents MS or that every possible vitamin D intervention is ineffective.

## The evidence

All confidence intervals below are 95%. These trials should not be pooled informally: populations, regimens, comparators and follow-up differ.

### Clinical outcomes, limitations and harms

- **D-Lay:** Relapse alone was not significant, HR 0.69 (0.42–1.16). Secondary MRI outcomes favored treatment, but secondary analyses were not multiplicity-adjusted. Treatment stopped and subsequent secondary measurements were censored after first activity; MRI assessment was local and confirmed disability progression was not established. Already 247/279 diagnostically assessable participants met 2017 MS criteria. Serious adverse events affected 17/156 versus 13/147 patients; none was judged drug-related. See the [full report, Methods and Tables 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11894546/).
- **PREVANZ:** Recruitment fell below the planned 240, intervals were wide, and only 10/199 had baseline vitamin D below 30 nmol/L. This does not settle effects in severely deficient populations. Thirteen serious adverse **events**, not necessarily 13 people, were recorded; none was judged treatment-related. One kidney-stone event occurred in the 1,000-IU arm. See the [full report, Methods and Tables 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10994527/).
- **VIDAMS:** The study could miss smaller benefits, excluded serum vitamin D below 15 ng/mL, and had limited power for disability. Secondary clinical/MRI findings did not demonstrate benefit. Three kidney/ureter-stone cases occurred (two high-dose, one low-dose); no hypercalcemia was reported. See the [full report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10130605/).

These monitored, selected trial populations do not establish unrestricted or long-term safety.

## Where it disagrees, and what is missing

The proposed explanation that intermittent dosing works whereas daily dosing does not is **not a randomized comparison**. Dose schedule, baseline vitamin D status, ascertainment and follow-up changed together. The trials cannot identify which difference explains their results.

Neither CIS trial tested prevention before a first demyelinating event. The existing [prediagnostic vitamin D cohort](../evidence/2006-vitamin-d-munger.md) and [Mendelian randomization study](../evidence/2015-vitamin-d-mendelian-randomization-mokry.md) ask a different question about susceptibility and longer-term exposure. Their findings do not prescribe a supplement regimen, and post-onset trial results do not directly refute a lifetime susceptibility effect.

The reports were read beyond their abstracts. The [D-Lay registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01817166) lists binary conversion at 24 months as primary and conversion delay as secondary; the publication analyzes time to relapse/MRI activity and explicitly describes that composite as corresponding to McDonald-2005 conversion. The linked original protocol/statistical-analysis-plan PDF could not be retrieved, so the amendment history was not independently audited. PREVANZ's [prospective registry](https://www.anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?ACTRN=12612001160820) explicitly includes clinical or MRI activity. This comparison is not an exhaustive inventory of vitamin D trials.

## What would change this

Independent trials with prespecified clinical endpoints, adequate power and follow-up for relapses and confirmed disability progression, and modern background treatment would clarify clinical benefit. Dosing-schedule hypotheses need direct randomized comparisons. Baseline-deficiency hypotheses need prespecified, adequately powered effect-modification analyses rather than selecting favorable subgroups after results are known.
