# Pooled transplant-related deaths and outcomes after stem cell transplant

- **Citation:** Sormani MP, Muraro PA, Schiavetti I, et al. Autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplantation in multiple sclerosis: A meta-analysis. *Neurology*, 2017;88(22):2115-2122.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000003987](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000003987), PMID [28455383](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28455383/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A meta-analysis of all published studies of autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant (aHSCT) in
any form of MS from 1995 to 2016, leaving out reports later updated by newer ones. It pooled 15
studies with 764 people. Outcomes were transplant-related mortality (deaths caused by the
treatment), disability progression, and "no evidence of disease activity" (NEDA). It also tested
which study features were linked to more deaths and progression.

## What it found

- Pooled transplant-related mortality was 2.1% (95% CI 1.3% to 3.4%).
- Deaths were more common in older studies, in studies with fewer people with relapsing-remitting
  MS, and in studies of people with higher disability before transplant.
- Pooled disability progression was 17.1% at 2 years and 23.3% at 5 years.
- Progression at 2 years was lower in studies with more people with relapsing-remitting MS.
- The pooled share of people with no evidence of disease activity was 83% at 2 years and 67% at
  5 years.
- The authors conclude the best balance of benefit and risk is likely in people with aggressive
  relapsing-remitting MS who do not yet have high disability.

## Limits

- Pools mostly single-arm observational studies, not randomized trials, so it is rated level 3 and
  cannot compare aHSCT with drugs.
- The studies differed in who was treated, which regimen was used and how outcomes were defined.
- Links between study features and death rates are at the level of studies, not individual people.
- Covers studies up to 2016; it does not include the MIST trial or later cohorts.
- Muraro, an author here, also led the international long-term cohort
  ([2017-ahsct-long-term-outcomes-muraro](2017-ahsct-long-term-outcomes-muraro.md)); the abstract does
  not list which 15 studies were pooled, so overlap between the two is not known.
- A correction was published (*Neurology* 2017;89(2):215,
  [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000004169](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000004169), PMID 28696936).
  It is not open access and was not read, so it is not known whether it changes any figure above.
