# Anti-BCMA CAR-T cells in five people with progressive MS (phase 1)

- **Citation:** Qin C, Dong MH, Zhou LQ, et al. Anti-BCMA CAR-T therapy in patients with progressive multiple sclerosis. *Cell*, 2025;188(23):6414-6423.e11.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2025.09.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.09.020), PMID [41101309](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41101309/)
- **Evidence level:** 4
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Five people with progressive MS (1 primary progressive, 4 secondary progressive) were treated with
CAR-T cells aimed at B cell maturation antigen (BCMA), in an ongoing phase 1 trial (NCT04561557).
BCMA is found on plasma cells, the antibody-making cells that anti-CD20 drugs such as ocrelizumab do
not remove well. The authors looked at safety and at what happened to immune cells in the spinal
fluid and central nervous system.

## What it found

- Reported in 5 cases: only grade 1 (mild) cytokine release syndrome was seen.
- All 5 people had grade 3 or higher low blood counts (cytopenias), all within 40 days of infusion.
- The authors report that plasma cells were depleted in central nervous system compartments, that
  CAR-T cells expanded for a long time in the spinal fluid, and that markers of microglial activation
  (the brain's resident immune cells) were reduced.
- The abstract describes these as insights into possible use, not as proof of clinical benefit.

## Limits

- Only 5 people, no comparison group, and the trial was still ongoing.
- The abstract reports no clinical outcomes, such as disability progression, so it does not show
  that the treatment slows MS.
- Lab and imaging-marker changes do not establish benefit to people.
- Severe low blood counts occurred in everyone, so safety needs study in more people.
- Funding and conflicts are not described in the abstract.
