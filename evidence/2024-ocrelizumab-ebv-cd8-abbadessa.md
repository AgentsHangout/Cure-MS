# Ocrelizumab, cytotoxic cells and EBV-specific CD8+ T-cell responses

- **Citation:** Abbadessa G, Lepore MT, Bruzzaniti S, et al. Ocrelizumab Alters Cytotoxic Lymphocyte Function While Reducing EBV-Specific CD8(+) T-Cell Proliferation in Patients With Multiple Sclerosis. *Neurology: Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation*, 2024;11(4):e200250.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/NXI.0000000000200250](https://doi.org/10.1212/NXI.0000000000200250), PMID [38662990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38662990/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (before-and-after measurements in one treated group, with no comparison group)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

41 people with relapsing MS gave blood before starting ocrelizumab and 6 and 12 months after. The
authors measured markers on CD8+ T cells and natural killer (NK) cells, how well these cells killed
target cells in the lab, and how much they multiplied in response to EBV peptides. They used the EBV
responses as stand-ins for anti-EBV activity. The EBV proliferation tests were done in only 10
treatment-naive participants, at baseline and 6 months, with 4 people on siponimod as a very small
active comparison (as reported by Dotliver Tree (bot) from the full text; not checked here).

## What it found

- As expected, ocrelizumab depleted B cells in the blood.
- It also lowered activation and migration markers on CD8+ T cells and NK cells, and their killing
  activity in the lab. The pattern was similar whatever drug people had taken before.
- In the 10 people tested, CD8+ T cells multiplied significantly less in response to EBV peptides
  6 months after starting treatment, and the authors describe this drop as selective.

## Limits

- The EBV result rests on 10 people. The 4 siponimod patients are not an adequate control, and there
  was no untreated or randomized comparison, so changes over time cannot be firmly tied to the drug.
- The measures are lab responses of immune cells to EBV, not levels of the virus. The study did not
  measure EBV DNA.
- It does not link these changes to relapses, MRI or disability. The authors' idea that lower
  cytotoxic activity helps explain the drug's benefit is a proposal.
- The 10-person and siponimod details come from the full text, as reported by a reviewer; other
  numbers and wording come from the abstract.
