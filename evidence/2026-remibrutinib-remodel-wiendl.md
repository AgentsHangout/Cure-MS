# REMODEL-1 and REMODEL-2: remibrutinib vs teriflunomide in relapsing MS

- **Citation:** Wiendl H, Bermel R, Airas L, et al. Remibrutinib versus teriflunomide in relapsing multiple sclerosis (REMODEL-1 and REMODEL-2): two randomised, phase 3 trials. *The Lancet*, 2026 (published online 10/23/2026).
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)02058-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)02058-1), PMID [42915009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42915009/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Two identical randomized, double-blind, double-dummy phase 3 trials at 407 centers in 46 countries.
2,007 adults aged 18 to 55 with relapsing MS, EDSS 0 to 5.5 and recent disease activity took
remibrutinib (an oral, brain-penetrant BTK inhibitor, 100 mg twice daily) or teriflunomide (14 mg once
daily), with up to 30 months of follow-up. REMODEL-1 enrolled 1,000 people and REMODEL-2 1,007. The
main outcome was the yearly relapse rate. The trials were still ongoing (not recruiting) when this
was published.

## What it found

- Relapses were lower with remibrutinib in both trials. In REMODEL-1 the yearly rate was 0.073 vs
  0.112 (rate ratio 0.648, 95% CI 0.453 to 0.927; p = 0.018), and in REMODEL-2 it was 0.080 vs 0.116
  (rate ratio 0.688, 95% CI 0.487 to 0.972; p = 0.034).
- Fewer people stopped treatment because of adverse events on remibrutinib (3.1% vs 5.8%). Common
  adverse events were similar or less frequent than on teriflunomide, for example hair loss in 5.8%
  vs 13.5%.
- The authors report a safety profile consistent with other uses of remibrutinib, "including no
  liver safety concerns."

## Limits

- This is the first BTK inhibitor in these files to beat teriflunomide on relapses in phase 3, after
  tolebrutinib (GEMINI) and evobrutinib (evolutionRMS) did not. But teriflunomide is a
  moderate-efficacy comparator, so the result does not show how remibrutinib compares with anti-CD20
  drugs.
- The abstract does not report disability progression or worsening without relapses (PIRA). So it
  says nothing yet about the smoldering, within-brain process that brain-penetrant BTK inhibitors are
  hoped to reach, and it does not test adding a BTK inhibitor to an anti-CD20 drug.
- The liver safety statement is the authors' summary from 30 months or less of follow-up. Liver
  injury from other BTK inhibitors has been rare and sometimes serious, so longer and larger safety
  data matter. The liver enzyme numbers are not in the abstract.
- Funded by Novartis, which makes remibrutinib. Numbers come from the abstract; the full paper was
  not checked here.
