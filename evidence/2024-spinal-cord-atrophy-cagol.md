# Spinal cord shrinkage and rim lesions in people with PIRA

- **Citation:** Cagol A, Benkert P, Melie-Garcia L, et al. Association of Spinal Cord Atrophy and Brain Paramagnetic Rim Lesions With Progression Independent of Relapse Activity in People With MS. *Neurology*, 2024;102(1):e207768.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000207768](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000207768), PMID [38165377](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165377/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

445 people with MS (11.2% with progressive MS) from an ongoing multicenter cohort study, each with an
iron-sensitive MRI scan and regular clinical and MRI follow-up over the 4 years before it. The
authors compared spinal cord shrinkage (atrophy) rates and the number of paramagnetic rim lesions
(PRLs) between people with PIRA and people with no confirmed disability worsening. They also tested
whether brain and cord volumes at baseline predicted time to PIRA.

## What it found

- People with PIRA had faster cervical spinal cord atrophy than people without worsening (mean
  difference -1.39% per year) and about twice as many PRLs (incidence rate ratio 1.95).
- These differences held when looking only at people with relapsing-remitting MS.
- Smaller cervical cord, whole brain and cortex volumes at baseline predicted earlier PIRA.
- People with progressive MS had faster cord atrophy and more PRLs than people with
  relapsing-remitting MS.

## Limits

- Observational; PIRA was associated with nerve tissue loss and chronic active lesions, but this does
  not show which comes first or what causes what.
- PRLs were counted on a single scan, not tracked over time.
- People with PIRA were compared with people with no worsening at all, not with people whose
  worsening followed relapses.
