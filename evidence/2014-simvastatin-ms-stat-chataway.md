# MS-STAT: high-dose simvastatin and brain shrinkage in secondary progressive MS

- **Citation:** Chataway J, Schuerer N, Alsanousi A, et al. Effect of high-dose simvastatin on brain atrophy and disability in secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (MS-STAT): a randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 2 trial. *The Lancet*, 2014;383(9936):2213-2221.

## What was studied

A phase 2, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at three UK neuroscience centers, run from
January 28, 2008 to November 4, 2011, in 140 people aged 18 to 65 with secondary progressive MS.
Simvastatin is a cholesterol drug. People were assigned 1:1 to simvastatin 80 mg (70) or placebo
(70). The main outcome was the yearly rate of whole-brain shrinkage (atrophy), measured from
repeated MRI scans. Registered as NCT00647348.

## What it found

- In a trial of 140 people, the brain shrank more slowly on simvastatin: a mean of 0.288% a year
  (SD 0.521) vs 0.584% a year (SD 0.498) on placebo. The adjusted difference was -0.254% a year
  (95% CI -0.422 to -0.087; p = 0.003), which the authors describe as a 43% reduction.
- Serious adverse events were reported in 14 people (20%) in one group and 9 (13%) in the other,
  given in the abstract in the order placebo, then simvastatin. The authors report no difference
  between the groups and describe simvastatin as well tolerated.
- The authors concluded that the result supported a phase 3 trial. That trial,
  [MS-STAT2](2025-simvastatin-ms-stat2-chataway.md), found that simvastatin did not slow
  disability progression in 964 people.

## Limits

- **The main outcome was an MRI measure, not disability.** The title names disability, but the
  abstract gives no disability result, and this file doesn't report one. Not read: the disability
  and other secondary outcomes, how long people were followed, how many finished, and what the
  adverse events were.
- A phase 2 trial of 140 people at three centers in one country.
- Slower brain shrinkage here did not carry over to slower disability in the phase 3 trial, so this
  result on its own is not evidence that simvastatin helps people with MS.
- Only secondary progressive MS was studied.

  Trials Collaboration and the Rosetrees Trust), a personal contribution from one individual, and
  the UK National Institute for Health Research.
