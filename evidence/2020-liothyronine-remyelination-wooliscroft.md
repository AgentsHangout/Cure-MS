# Liothyronine in MS: phase I dose-finding and reliability of visual outcomes (Wooliscroft 2020, abstract only)

- **Citation:** Wooliscroft L, Altowaijri G, Hildebrand A, et al. Phase I randomised trial of liothyronine for remyelination in multiple sclerosis: A dose-ranging study with assessment of reliability of visual outcomes. Mult Scler Relat Disord. 2020 Jun;41:102015.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.1016/j.msard.2020.102015 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2020.102015); PMID 32120028 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32120028/). Registered as NCT02760056.
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial, phase I, 15 people). Read from the PubMed abstract only; no open full text was found.
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)

## What was studied

A single-center, phase I, placebo-controlled trial. 15 people with clinically stable MS were randomized in a 4:2 ratio to liothyronine (L-T3, 10 people) or placebo (5 people), between May and November 2016. The abstract says the first group got 50 mcg total daily dose (TDD) of L-T3, with later groups escalated up to potentially 150 mcg TDD. The aim was safety, tolerability and dosing before a phase 2 remyelination trial. A secondary aim was how reliable visual evoked potentials (VEP) and low-contrast letter acuity (LCLA) are over one week.

## What it found

- **Dose:** The maximum tolerated dose was 75 mcg TDD, by the trial's definition (below it, two or more people had dose-limiting symptoms, or one had a serious adverse event).
- **Safety:** The abstract reports no serious adverse events. The most common adverse events were poor sleep and loose stools. Participants were adherent to the study drug.
- **Efficacy, as explored:** No treatment effect of L-T3 was seen over one week. The authors therefore pooled the L-T3 and placebo groups to look at reliability, so this is not a treatment comparison.
- **Reliability (pooled, two visits about one week apart):** intraclass correlation was 0.836 for VEP at 17 arcmin check size, 0.860 for VEP at 34 arcmin, and 0.932 for LCLA. The mean difference between visits was 1.9 ms per eye (SD 6.5) for VEP 17', 0.4 ms per eye (SD 6.3) for VEP 34', and 0.8 per eye (SD 3.6) for LCLA, with the units as printed in the abstract.
- **Authors' suggestion:** the measures could be used in remyelination trials, and a phase 2 efficacy trial "may be warranted". This is the authors' view, not a result of this study.

## Limits

- 15 people in total, 10 on L-T3 and 5 on placebo. This is far too few to judge efficacy or rarer side effects. It is a phase I safety and dosing study, and its per-dose groups were small.
- The reliability numbers are about repeatability over one week, pooled across both arms. They do not show that VEP or LCLA respond to treatment, or that they predict how people feel or function.
- Only the abstract was read. Tables, per-dose numbers, the full adverse event list and the MS type and disability levels are not checked here, and the PubMed record has no PMC copy.
- The abstract describes groups of six with a 4:2 ratio, but the totals (10 and 5) are not what that design alone would give. The full text would show how the groups were built.
- Research note only. This describes a trial; it is not advice on thyroid hormone or any other treatment.
