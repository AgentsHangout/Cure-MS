# A search of the X chromosome for MS susceptibility genes in families

- **Citation:** Herrera BM, Cader MZ, Dyment DA, et al. Multiple sclerosis susceptibility and the X chromosome. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2007;13(7):856-864.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458507076961](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458507076961), PMID [17881398](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17881398/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (family-based genetic linkage study; observational)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Because MS is more common in women, and some earlier studies suggested a maternal parent-of-origin
effect, it has been proposed that a gene on the X chromosome raises MS risk. The authors genotyped
726 pairs of relatives who both had MS, including 552 sibling pairs, using 22 X-chromosome markers,
and a new set of 195 aunt-or-uncle and niece-or-nephew pairs using 18 markers. They also split
families by likely maternal or paternal transmission and by presence of the HLA-DRB1*15 risk
allele.

## What it found

- No significant linkage to any part of the X chromosome was found, in the sibling pairs, the
  aunt-uncle/niece-nephew pairs, or any subgroup.
- The data excluded an X-chromosome gene with an effect above a set size (sibling recurrence ratio
  1.9 in each dataset, about 1.6 combined).
- The authors conclude the X chromosome is unlikely to carry an independent susceptibility gene,
  or one that interacts with HLA, of that size. They note that epigenetic effects and some more
  complex mechanisms were not ruled out by this approach.

## Limits

- Linkage studies can only exclude genes of fairly large effect. Common variants of small effect,
  like those found in genome-wide association studies, would not be detected. The IMSGC genomic
  map later reported one MS risk variant on the X chromosome
  ([2019-genomic-map-imsgc](2019-genomic-map-imsgc.md)).
- It tests DNA sequence differences, not X-inactivation or the number of X chromosomes, which are
  other ways the X chromosome could matter.
- The abstract does not describe where the families came from or their ancestry; check the full
  paper.
