# GEMINI 1 and 2: tolebrutinib vs teriflunomide in relapsing MS

- **Citation:** Oh J, Arnold DL, Cree BAC, et al. Tolebrutinib versus Teriflunomide in Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis. *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2025;392(19):1893-1904.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2415985](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2415985), PMID [40202623](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40202623/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Two randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, event-driven phase 3 trials in relapsing MS. 1,873 people
(974 in GEMINI 1, 899 in GEMINI 2) were randomized to tolebrutinib, a brain-penetrant BTK inhibitor
(60 mg once daily), or teriflunomide (14 mg once daily). Median follow-up was 139 weeks. The main outcome was the
yearly relapse rate. The key secondary outcome was disability worsening confirmed over at least 6
months, pooled across both trials.

## What it found

- No statistically significant difference in relapses was demonstrated: yearly rates were 0.13 vs 0.12 in
  GEMINI 1 (rate ratio 1.06, 95% CI 0.81 to 1.39; p = 0.67) and 0.11 vs 0.11 in GEMINI 2 (rate ratio
  1.00, 95% CI 0.75 to 1.32; p = 0.98).
- Pooled 6-month confirmed disability worsening was 8.3% with tolebrutinib vs 11.3% with
  teriflunomide (hazard ratio 0.71, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.95). Because the primary outcome failed, no
  formal hypothesis test was done for this under the prespecified testing order, and the confidence
  interval is not adjusted for multiple testing. It is exploratory.
- Adverse events overall were similar. Minor bleeding was more common with tolebrutinib (petechiae
  4.5% vs 0.3%, heavy menstrual bleeding 2.6% vs 1.0%).

## Limits

- The disability result is a nominal, exploratory finding, not a demonstrated benefit. It is
  interesting because no statistically significant difference in relapses was demonstrated, which fits the idea that tolebrutinib acts on
  worsening through something other than relapses, but this trial cannot show that.
- Teriflunomide is a moderate-efficacy comparator, and no anti-CD20 arm or add-on design was used.
- Liver safety is covered across the tolebrutinib program in the state-of-the-art finding, section 3
  (Maxwell 2026); the liver numbers for these trials alone are not in the abstract.
- Funded by Sanofi, which makes tolebrutinib. Numbers come from the abstract.
