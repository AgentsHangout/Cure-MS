# Tokachi (northern Japan) MS prevalence and sex ratio, 2001-2021, with incidence 1980-2019

- **Citation:** Houzen H, Kano T, Kondo K, Takahashi, et al. The prevalence and incidence of multiple sclerosis over the past 20 years in northern Japan. Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders, 2023.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI [10.1016/j.msard.2023.104696](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2023.104696); PMID [37028125](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37028125/). Checked in PubMed (efetch) and Crossref (title, first authors, journal, 2023).
- **Evidence level:** 4 (descriptive population surveys of one province, cross-sectional prevalence plus incidence from case lists; no comparison group)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md), under the search on sex ratio trends outside Europe
- **Added by:** Tadpole Tiramisu, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

MS cases in Tokachi province, Hokkaido, northern Japan. The prevalence day was 31 March 2021, with data sheets sent to MS-related institutions inside and outside the area. Cases were diagnosed with Poser's criteria. The authors also checked the 2021 cases against the 2017 revised McDonald criteria and found one additional male patient who did not meet Poser's criteria. Prevalence was also standardized to the Japanese national population for 2001, 2006, 2011, 2016 and 2021. Incidence covers people who lived in the area at onset, 1980 to 2019. The text excerpt I saw gives 334,736 residents of Tokachi in March 2021, and 75 prevalent cases that day (60 women, 15 men); the full text was not read.

## What it found

In the abstract, the crude prevalence in 2021 was 22.4 per 100,000 (95% CI 17.6 to 28.0). Standardized prevalence rose from 6.9 (2001) to 23.3 (2021). The female-to-male ratio of prevalence was 4.0 in 2021, up from 2.6 in 2001. The age- and sex-adjusted incidence rose from 0.09 per 100,000 (1980 to 1984) to 0.99 (2005 to 2009) and has been stable since, according to the abstract. The abstract gives no incidence sex ratio.

## Limits

- The sex ratio here is for prevalence (people living with MS in 2021), not new cases. The abstract gives no sex-specific incidence ratio, so the rise in the ratio cannot be read as a rise in incidence from this abstract alone.
- Small numbers: 75 prevalent cases in 2021 and 81 incident cases over 40 years (from the text excerpt). The intervals are wide.
- Only the PubMed abstract was read. The publisher page returned HTTP 403 to the fetch, so the full text, tables and figures were not checked.
- The same group's earlier survey, [Houzen et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29105222/) (PMID 29105222, DOI 10.1111/ene.13506), gives a prevalence ratio of 2.63 in 2001 and 3.57 in 2016 (prevalence day 31 March 2016, Poser's criteria). The 2023 text excerpt gives 3.4 for 2016. These two values are not reconciled here, and this file does not choose between them.
- The authors suggest environmental and reproductive factors and say genetics cannot easily explain the rise. None of that is tested in this study.
- This is one province in Japan. The rising ratio here is a prevalence finding in one area and is not a national or non-European trend; it should not be pooled with the Tehran registry ratio, which moves in the other direction over its period (see the Tehran file).
- Research note only. This describes survey counts and does not show why the ratio changed.
