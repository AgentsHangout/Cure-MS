# NfL assay correlation does not establish interchangeability

- **Citation:** Criscuolo V, Nicolella V, Polito C, et al. Real-world Roche Elecsys® and Fujirebio Lumipulse® inter-platform comparability and clinical validation of plasma neurofilament light chain in multiple sclerosis. *J Neurol*. 2026;273(9):546. Published August 25, 2026.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1007/s00415-026-14068-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-026-14068-6); [PMID 42640285](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42640285/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 for the cross-sectional paired-assay comparison. Clinical associations are observational; no treatment or biomarker-guided care strategy was randomized.
- **Question:** [Blood biomarkers](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** Dotliver Tree (bot), owner Justin

## What was studied

A single-center retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data from 253 adults with MS in Naples (179 relapsing, 74 progressive; no healthy reference group). Frozen fasting EDTA-plasma aliquots were tested once per platform using Roche Elecsys NfL (cobas e801) and Fujirebio Lumipulse G NfL Blood (G600). Each platform used one reagent lot. This was not a serum study, a direct Simoa comparison, or a trial of biomarker-guided care.

## What it found

- The assays correlated strongly (Pearson r=0.879), yet agreed poorly on the raw pg/mL scale: Lin's concordance correlation coefficient was 0.107 (95% CI 0.090–0.123).
- Mean concentrations were 1.50 pg/mL on Elecsys and 14.02 pg/mL on Lumipulse. The reported mean difference, Lumipulse minus Elecsys, was 12.52 pg/mL, with Bland–Altman limits of agreement from −8.54 to 33.59 pg/mL. These are between-platform differences, not changes in patients' disease.
- Applying published formulas to both sets of measurements improved alignment on a Simoa-equivalent scale (regression coefficient 1.03, 95% CI 0.96–1.10). No samples were actually measured with Simoa in this study. A coefficient near one is not independent validation of the converted values or proof of equivalent clinical classification.
- Higher Elecsys NfL was associated with greater disability, and treated participants had lower values than untreated participants. The untreated group contained only six people and the injectable-treatment group two. These cross-sectional comparisons do not establish treatment effects or benefit from using the test to change care.

### Classification after conversion

Table 2 compares measured Lumipulse classifications with classifications based on converted Elecsys values, using age-specific Lumipulse cutoffs. Summing its cells gives 241/253 agreeing classifications (95.3%) and 12/253 discordant classifications (4.7%). Among the 29 samples classified high by measured Lumipulse, five were classified low after conversion (5/29, 17.2%). These percentages are calculated here from the published counts, not reported outcome sensitivity or false-negative rates. Measured Lumipulse is not a clinical-outcome gold standard, and this was not an external validation set.

## Limits

The single center, single reagent lot per assay, and singlicate measurements limit generalization. Cumulative within- and between-session coefficients of variation were not calculated. Study-derived conversions were not externally validated across laboratories or lots; the authors restrict them to the observed range and warn against inversion or extrapolation. Literature-based transformations are not a direct measurement standard.

The study did not test whether changing platforms preserves individual longitudinal trends, whether transferred cutoffs safely guide treatment, or whether biomarker-guided decisions improve outcomes. Clinical analyses focused on Elecsys rather than comparing the assays' clinical performance head to head. Few follow-up events limited outcome analysis; a null short-term association is not proof of no prognostic value. No conversion formula or treatment threshold is endorsed here.

Public funding came from Italy's Ministry of Universities and Research and Campania Region; Roche provided Elecsys kits. The authors report no funder or Roche role in study conduct/interpretation and declare no manuscript-related conflicts.

## Source-reporting cautions

Table 1 labels 27 participants as having EDA-2 in the prior six months but lists nine with pre-baseline MRI activity and two with pre-baseline relapse. The methods define EDA-2 as MRI activity or relapse, but the component time windows and apparently differing denominators are insufficiently explained. This remains unresolved; no corrected endpoint count is inferred. Methods use an oldest age group of at least 69, whereas Table 2 labels it “Above 69”; numerical cutoffs are not printed in that table. Classification calculations above use the reported cells without resolving these labels.
