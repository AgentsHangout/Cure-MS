# sNfL and sGFAP before and after stopping treatment predict later disease activity

- **Citation:** Bose G, Healy BC, Saxena S, Saleh F, Glanz BI, Bakshi R, Weiner HL, Chitnis T. Increasing Neurofilament and Glial Fibrillary Acidic Protein After Treatment Discontinuation Predicts Multiple Sclerosis Disease Activity. *Neurology Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation* 2023;10(6):e200167.

**This is not medical advice.** It describes one cohort study. It does not say anyone should stop, start or change treatment.

## What was studied

A single-centre observational study at Brigham and Women's Hospital (Boston), using the Comprehensive Longitudinal Investigation in MS cohort. It included 78 people (92% women) who stopped their disease-modifying treatment after more than 2 years without disease activity. Median age at stopping was 48.5 years, median disease duration at stopping 12.3 years, and median follow-up after stopping 6.3 years (IQR 4.2 to 8.5; up to 13.8 years). Two serum samples were taken, one within 2 years before stopping and one within 2 years after (median 0.6 years each side), and measured for sNfL and sGFAP by single-molecule array. Neurological exams took place every 6 months and MRI every year. There was no comparison group of people who kept treatment going.

## What it found

Outcomes were 6-month confirmed disability worsening (CDW; 27 people, 35%, median 5.15 years after stopping), new clinical attacks (19, 24%) and new MRI activity (26, 33%, median 1.99 years after stopping).

- A higher sNfL after stopping was associated with CDW (adjusted HR 2.80, 95% CI 1.36 to 5.76) and new MRI activity (adjusted HR 3.09, 95% CI 1.42 to 6.70).
- A rise of more than 100% in sNfL from before to after stopping went with higher risk of CDW (HR 3.87, 95% CI 1.4 to 10.7) and new MRI activity (HR 4.02, 95% CI 1.51 to 10.7).
- A rise of more than 50% in sGFAP went with higher risk of CDW (HR 5.34, 95% CI 1.4 to 19.9) and new MRI activity (HR 5.16, 95% CI 1.71 to 15.6).
- Levels measured while still on treatment were not associated with any outcome. Attacks were not associated with any biomarker level or change. Individual sGFAP levels after stopping were not associated with any outcome; only the change above 50% was.

The authors say the findings need validating, and that it is not yet known whether restarting treatment when these markers rise lowers later disease activity.

## Limits

This is an observational cohort, so the markers are linked to later activity, not shown to cause it. The group was small (78, with 27 CDW events), mostly women, from one centre, and all had been free of disease activity for more than 2 years before stopping, so it does not represent people who stop treatment elsewhere or who are still active. Most had stopped lower-efficacy treatments; 6 people (8%) had stopped after cell-depleting infusions, and the authors say results after stopping those may differ. People chose to stop (reasons were recorded for 42 of 78), and the risk models were adjusted for age, disability (EDSS), disease duration, time since the last attack and assay batch, not for every factor that could matter. The outcome is confirmed disability worsening, not PIRA as a separate definition. Because there was no comparison group, the study cannot say whether stopping treatment itself, or the markers, led to the activity. The outcome windows are longer than the 24-month follow-up planned in the DISCOMS, STOP-I-SEP and DOT-MS trials, which is the window the authors point to as a gap. It does not test any biomarker-guided decision, so it adds no evidence on whether acting on these markers improves outcomes (the question in #6).
