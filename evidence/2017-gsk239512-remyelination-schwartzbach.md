# GSK239512 (histamine H3 antagonist) and lesion remyelination in relapsing-remitting MS: phase II trial

- **Citation:** Schwartzbach CJ, Grove RA, Brown R, Tompson D, Then Bergh F, Arnold DL. Lesion remyelinating activity of GSK239512 versus placebo in patients with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis: a randomised, single-blind, phase II study. J Neurol. 2017;264(2):304-315. Trial registration NCT01772199.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI [10.1007/s00415-016-8341-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-016-8341-7); PMID [27888416](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27888416/). Full text checked in PMC: [PMC5306088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5306088/).
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomised controlled trial)

## What was studied

A phase II, randomised (1:1), parallel-group, placebo-controlled, multicentre trial at 34 sites. 131 adults aged 18 to 50 with relapsing-remitting MS, all receiving intramuscular interferon beta-1a or glatiramer acetate, were randomised to once-daily oral GSK239512 (65 people; up-titrated over 4 to 5 weeks to a maximum tolerable dose of up to 80 µg) or placebo (66 people). Treatment was maintained to week 48. GSK239512 is a histamine H3 receptor antagonist/inverse agonist. The trial looked at MRI myelin imaging (magnetisation transfer ratio, MTR) in lesions, not at how people felt or functioned.

## What it found

The two co-primary endpoints were mean changes in post-lesion MTR in gadolinium-enhanced (GdE) lesions and in Delta-MTR lesions, analysed in the intent-to-treat population, with MRI data within 70 days of the reference lesion excluded.

- **GdE lesions:** 27 people on GSK239512 (92 lesions) and 28 on placebo (97 lesions) contributed. Adjusted difference in normalised MTR 0.149 (90% CI 0.008 to 0.289). Effect size 0.344 (90% CI 0.018 to 0.671); posterior probability 0.955 that the effect size is above 0.
- **Delta-MTR lesions:** 24 on GSK239512 (69 lesions) and 29 on placebo (77 lesions) contributed. Adjusted difference 0.105 (90% CI -0.049 to 0.257 in the text; -0.048 in Table 2). Effect size 0.243 (90% CI -0.112 to 0.598); posterior probability 0.877.

The authors call the result "a small but positive effect" of GSK239512 on remyelination observed on MRI. Their Discussion refers to "change in MTR, a marker of lesion remyelination".

Secondary results at week 48: the rate of new GdE lesions was higher on GSK239512 (rate ratio 1.72, 90% CI 1.05 to 2.82; mean 1.14 vs 0.66), and the authors say lesion development trended in favour of placebo. Relapse rates were similar (0.417 vs 0.400 per 48 weeks). The authors report no meaningful change from baseline on the EDSS or the CogState battery.

Adverse events, treatment phase (65 GSK239512, 66 placebo): any AE 74% vs 76%. Treatment-related AEs 42% vs 12%. Insomnia 34% (22 people) vs 11% (7); relative risk 3.19 (95% CI 1.57 to 6.95). Most insomnia was in the titration period (31% vs 9%). Serious AEs 3% (2 people) vs 2% (1). AEs leading to withdrawal 11% (7 people) on GSK239512 vs none on placebo. Completed the study: 51 of 65 (78%) vs 63 of 66 (95%).

## Limits

- This is an imaging biomarker result, not clinical benefit. The trial's main outcome was an MRI measure of remyelination. The paper reports no meaningful change in disability or cognition, and does not show that an MTR change predicts a change people feel.
- Small numbers. Only 27 and 28 people (GdE), and 24 and 29 (Delta-MTR), contributed to the primary analyses. The results use 90% CIs. The Delta-MTR effect-size CI includes 0, and so does the 90% CI for the Delta-MTR difference.
- Blinding: the title says "single-blind", but the abstract and the Methods (Study design) both say "double-blind (sponsor-unblinded)", so the title is the only single-blind wording.
- The authors are from GSK, the sponsor.
- A post hoc finding: in the small subgroup with no GdE lesions on the screening MRI, people on GSK239512 had more than double the new lesions compared with placebo. This was not a planned analysis.
- Phase II, 48 weeks, 131 people. Not tested in progressive MS.
- More people on GSK239512 dropped out and had insomnia, which affects how the results should be read.
