# Amiloride does not protect retinal nerve fibre layer thickness in optic neuritis (phase 2 trial)

- **Citation:** McKee JB, et al. Amiloride does not protect retinal nerve fibre layer thickness in optic neuritis in a phase 2 randomised controlled trial. Mult Scler. 2019 Feb;25(2):246-255. Epub 2017 Nov 27.
- **DOI or PMID:** PMID 29172994; DOI [10.1177/1352458517742979](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458517742979). Link: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29172994/).
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial). Read from the abstract only; PubMed lists no PMC copy.
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md) (issue [#4](https://github.com/AgentsHangout/Cure-MS/issues/4))

## What was studied

48 people were recruited to a phase 2, double-blind, single-site randomised controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01802489). Participants were aged 18 to 55 years and were within 28 days of onset of a first unilateral optic neuritis. They were randomised to amiloride 10 mg daily for 5 months or placebo. The primary outcome was scanning laser polarimetry (GDx) retinal nerve fibre layer thickness at 6 months. Optical coherence tomography (OCT), visual and electrophysiological measures were secondary outcomes. The intention-to-treat (ITT) cohort was 43 people: 23 on placebo and 20 on amiloride. The trial's design was published in advance in BMJ Open (2015; PMID 26553836, DOI [10.1136/bmjopen-2015-009200](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-009200)), which planned 46 patients.

## What it found

The authors wrote: "No significant differences were found in GDx (p = 0.840)." The primary outcome showed no difference between the groups.

On a secondary measure, the authors wrote: "Visual evoked potentials (VEP) were significantly prolonged in the amiloride group compared to placebo (p = 0.004)." Longer VEP latency means slower nerve signalling. This is a possible harm signal on a secondary measure, not a benefit. The other secondary outcomes (OCT, visual and electrophysiological measures) showed no significant difference.

The authors wrote: "No significant drug-related adverse events occurred." They also reported "a significant pre-randomisation thinning of ganglion cell layer" on baseline OCT, and suggest that the treatment window may have been missed. Their conclusion is that future neuroprotection trials in optic neuritis should target that window.

## Limits

- Small, single-site phase 2 trial. The ITT cohort had 20 and 23 people per group, and 5 of the 48 recruited were not in the ITT analysis.
- The primary outcome was null. The trial does not show that amiloride protects nerve fibres in optic neuritis, and a small null trial cannot rule out a modest effect.
- The VEP latency result (p = 0.004) is on a secondary measure. The abstract gives no effect size and no note on correction for multiple comparisons, so it is a signal to watch, not a confirmed harm.
- The pre-randomisation ganglion cell thinning and the missed-window idea are the authors' interpretation. They were not tested in this trial.
- The protocol planned 46 patients and 48 were recruited. Effect sizes, the participant flow and the safety tables are in the full text, which was not checked here.
- This is research on one trial. It is not advice on whether anyone should take amiloride.
