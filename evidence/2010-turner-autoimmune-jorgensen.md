# Autoimmune diseases in women with Turner's syndrome

- **Citation:** Jørgensen KT, Rostgaard K, Bache I, Biggar RJ, Nielsen NM, Tommerup N, Frisch M. Autoimmune diseases in women with Turner's syndrome. Arthritis Rheum. 2010;62(3):658-66.
- **DOI or PMID:** PMID 20187158, DOI 10.1002/art.27270. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20187158/ and https://doi.org/10.1002/art.27270
- **Evidence level:** 3 (cohort study; association only)
- **Question:** questions/sex-differences.md
- **Added by:** Ribbit Red Velvet, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

A national cohort of 798 Danish women with Turner's syndrome, followed for 12,461 person-years between 1980 and 2004. Diagnoses of 46 autoimmune diseases came from the Danish National Patient Register, and standardized incidence ratios (SIRs) were calculated against Danish women in general. The study asked whether the autoimmune profile in Turner's syndrome looks more like diseases that are female-predominant or male-predominant.

## What it found

Women with Turner's syndrome had about twice the overall risk of autoimmune disease (SIR 2.1, 95% CI 1.6-2.7). For diseases with female predominance the SIR was 1.7 (1.2-2.4); for diseases with male predominance it was 3.9 (2.5-5.8). The strongest associations were Hashimoto thyroiditis (SIR 14.6, 6.7-27.1) and type 1 diabetes (SIR 4.1, 2.5-6.3).

## Limits

This check used the PubMed abstract only. The full text is closed access and was not read, so no MS-specific estimate has been verified: the abstract does not name multiple sclerosis, and no MS SIR or CI is reported here. Do not read the male-predominant group as MS risk. The findings are about autoimmune disease in women with Turner's syndrome, a rare chromosome condition, and say nothing about MS in the general population. The comparison is with Danish women overall, and the study is observational, so it shows association only.
