# Progression without relapses in early MS: a 5-year-plus Italian registry cohort

- **Citation:** Portaccio E, Bellinvia A, Fonderico M, et al. Progression is independent of relapse activity in early multiple sclerosis: a real-life cohort study. *Brain*, 2022;145(8):2796-2805.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/brain/awac111](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awac111), PMID [35325059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35325059/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** Ribbit Red Velvet, bot; owner Justin

Note: this file is based on the abstract only (read from the PubMed record). The full text was not read, so the study's PIRA and relapse-association definitions, and its model details, are not checked here.

## What was studied

5,169 people with clinically isolated syndrome or early relapsing-remitting MS, assessed within one year of onset, from the Italian Multiple Sclerosis Register. Each had at least 5 years of follow-up (mean 11.5 ± 5.5 years). Confirmed disability accumulation was an EDSS rise confirmed at 6 months, sorted by its timing relative to relapses. Factors were looked at with multivariable Cox regression. This is a retrospective observational cohort.

## What it found

- Progression independent of relapse activity (PIRA) occurred in 1,427 people (27.6%).
- Relapse-associated worsening (RAW) occurred in 922 people (17.8%).
- PIRA was associated with older age at baseline (HR 1.19 per the paper's unit), relapsing-remitting course at baseline (HR 1.44), longer disease duration at baseline (HR 1.56), lower baseline EDSS (HR 0.92) and fewer relapses before the event (HR 0.76).
- RAW was associated with younger age (HR 0.87), relapsing-remitting course (HR 1.55), lower baseline EDSS (HR 0.94) and more prior relapses (HR 1.04).
- Longer exposure to disease-modifying drugs was associated with lower risk of both PIRA and RAW (P < 0.001).
- The authors conclude that PIRA was an important contributor to confirmed disability accumulation in this early-stage cohort, and suggest inflammation and neurodegeneration may form one disease continuum.

The 27.6% here is the share of people with a PIRA event. It is a different measure from the 27.6% in the 2024 Portaccio file ([2024-pira-frequency-portaccio.md](2024-pira-frequency-portaccio.md)), which is the share who moved to secondary progressive MS.

## Limits

- Retrospective registry data from routine care. Visit timing and relapse records can be incomplete.
- Associations only; the factors listed are not shown to cause PIRA or RAW.
- Hazard ratios are quoted from the abstract; the units and reference categories are in the full text, which was not read.
- The Italian register is also the source for the 2024 Portaccio paper (PMID 38805052) and the 2025 unified-definition paper (PMID 40982769). We have not checked how far the patients overlap, so these should not be counted as independent confirmation of each other.
- Follow-up varied widely (SD 5.5 years).
