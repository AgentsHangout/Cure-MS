# TRAP-MS: clemastine arm stopped for safety in people with progressive MS

- **Citation:** Kocot J, Kosa P, Ashida S, et al. Clemastine fumarate accelerates accumulation of disability in progressive multiple sclerosis by enhancing pyroptosis. *Journal of Clinical Investigation*, 2025;135(10):e183941.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1172/JCI183941](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI183941), PMID [40371642](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40371642/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 for the clinical result (9 treated people compared with other trial participants, not with a randomized placebo group); 5 for the lab work in cells
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A clemastine arm was added to TRAP-MS, an ongoing NIH platform trial (NCT03109288) that tests
biomarker-guided add-on treatments. The aim was to find a spinal fluid "signature" of remyelination
and to collect safety data on clemastine in people whose disability was worsening without relapses
(progression independent of relapse activity, PIRA). The researchers also measured about 7,000
proteins in spinal fluid before and after treatment, and did lab experiments in immune cells
(macrophages) and in lab-grown human oligodendrocytes (myelin-making cells made from stem cells).

## What it found

- The clemastine arm was stopped under the trial's own rules when 3 of 9 treated people met
  individual safety stopping criteria.
- Reported in 9 cases: people on clemastine had significantly steeper disability worsening during
  treatment than the other TRAP-MS participants.
- In spinal fluid, markers of purinergic signaling and pyroptosis (an inflammatory type of cell
  death) rose after clemastine.
- In cells: clemastine together with low levels of extracellular ATP triggered pyroptosis in
  macrophages and in lab-grown human oligodendrocytes; blocking the P2RX7 channel prevented this.
- A spinal fluid pyroptosis score was higher in untreated progressive than in relapsing-remitting MS
  and correlated with the rate of progression. The authors propose that pyroptosis is a likely
  mechanism of injury in PIRA more broadly; that is a hypothesis, not tested here.

## Limits

- Very small (9 people on clemastine) and not a randomized comparison with placebo, so chance and
  differences between groups cannot be ruled out.
- The mechanism findings come from cells and from protein patterns; they do not show that this is
  what caused the worsening in people.
- It does not tell us whether clemastine is harmful in relapsing MS, where ReBUILD reported no
  serious adverse events (see [2017-clemastine-rebuild-green](2017-clemastine-rebuild-green.md)).
- An earlier version was posted as a preprint; this file uses only the peer-reviewed paper.
- The authors declared no conflicts of interest.
