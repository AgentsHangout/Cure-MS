# Changes in VEP over time, relapses and disability in relapsing-remitting MS

- **Citation:** Öncel S, Seferoğlu M, Ömerhoca S, et al. Longitudinal Visual Evoked Potential Changes Show Distinct Associations with Relapse Activity and Disability in Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis: A Multicenter Retrospective Cohort Study. *Medicina (Kaunas)*, 2026;62(9):1750.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.3390/medicina62091750](https://doi.org/10.3390/medicina62091750), PMID [42796356](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42796356/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A retrospective cohort of 83 people with relapsing-remitting MS from three centers in Turkey, each
with at least two pattern-reversal VEP tests a year or more apart (median follow-up 2 years). People
with recent optic neuritis were left out. The authors compared changes in VEP latency and amplitude
with relapses in the same period and with EDSS disability.

## What it found

- 43.4% developed a longer P100 latency, which went with a higher relapse rate in the same period
  (odds ratio 2.91, p = 0.005) but not with worsening disability.
- 56.6% had a fall in VEP amplitude, which went with higher EDSS at follow-up at the group level. The
  correlation between amplitude decline and EDSS change was only nominally significant (rho = -0.231,
  p = 0.036), did not survive correction for multiple comparisons, and weakened after adjusting for
  starting EDSS.
- Only 3 formally defined PIRA events occurred, too few to draw conclusions.

## Limits

- Retrospective, 83 people, about 2 years. The authors call it exploratory and say it needs
  prospective confirmation.
- It looks at VEP getting worse, not recovering after a repair treatment, so it does not close the
  repair gap. In this cohort, latency changes tracked relapses rather than disability.
- One author works for a health technology company.
