# A valacyclovir vs placebo MRI trial in relapsing-remitting MS

- **Citation:** Bech E, Lycke J, Gadeberg P, et al. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled MRI study of anti-herpes virus therapy in MS. *Neurology*, 2002;58(1):31-36.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/wnl.58.1.31](https://doi.org/10.1212/wnl.58.1.31), PMID [11781402](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11781402/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 for the main outcome (randomized controlled trial). The subgroup result is exploratory.
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A phase 2, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Denmark and Sweden in 70 people with
relapsing-remitting MS who had had two or more relapses in the 2 years before (EDSS 0 to 5.5). They
took the antiviral valacyclovir (1 gram three times a day; 36 people) or placebo (34 people) for 24
weeks. Brain MRI was done every 4 weeks for 32 weeks. The main outcome was the number of new active
MRI lesions during the 24 weeks of treatment. It tested the idea that herpes viruses play a role in
MS; it was not designed around EBV in particular.

## What it found

- **Main outcome, no effect.** New active lesions per person averaged 11.9 (SD 17.6) on
  valacyclovir and 14.5 (SD 21.4) on placebo. The authors conclude that valacyclovir "did not
  reduce" new active lesions.
- **Exploratory subgroup.** In a protocol-planned exploratory analysis of people with high disease
  activity at the start (17 on valacyclovir, 11 on placebo), those on valacyclovir had fewer new
  active lesions per scan (median 2.0 vs 6.5) and more scans without new active lesions. This is a
  small subgroup and does not show a benefit on its own.
- No clinical benefit (relapses or disability) is reported in the abstract.

## Limits

- Short (24 weeks of treatment) and small. MRI lesions are a marker, not relapses or disability.
- The only positive result is in a small subgroup of 28 people, which may be chance.
- Not an EBV trial. EBV results from saliva and plasma samples in what appears to be this trial are
  in a separate paper ([2005-ebv-hhv6-dna-valacyclovir-hollsberg](2005-ebv-hhv6-dna-valacyclovir-hollsberg.md)).
- All numbers are from the PubMed abstract. The full text is not open in PMC or Europe PMC, so
  dropouts, side effects and funding were not checked.
