# X chromosome inactivation in women with MS (Knudsen 2007)

- **Citation:** Knudsen GP, Harbo HF, Smestad C, Celius EG, Akesson E, Oturai A, Ryder LP, Spurkland A, Ørstavik KH. X chromosome inactivation in females with multiple sclerosis. Eur J Neurol. 2007;14(12):1392-1396.
- **DOI or PMID:** PMID 17970735, [doi:10.1111/j.1468-1331.2007.01987.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-1331.2007.01987.x) (both checked: PubMed record lists this DOI, a PubMed search on the DOI returns PMID 17970735, and Crossref gives the same title and journal).
- **Evidence level:** 3 (comparison of patients with controls, observational)

## What was studied

568 female patients with MS compared with female controls, measuring how skewed their X-chromosome inactivation pattern was. Patients were then grouped by disease course (relapsing-remitting, secondary progressive, primary progressive) and compared. Read from the abstract only; the full text was not read.

## What it found

The degree of skewing did not differ between patients (median 64%) and controls (median 65%), P = 0.474. In the subgroup comparison, the authors report a possible difference in skewing between patients with a progressive and those with a relapsing course (P = 0.05).

## Limits

The abstract does not give the number of controls, the method used to measure inactivation, or how the subgroups were chosen; the full text was not read, so these are not in this file. Women only, so it says nothing about men. The subgroup result sits right at P = 0.05 and is not a finding about cause. The study measures one feature of X inactivation in blood-derived cells at one point in time. It does not show whether X inactivation affects MS risk, course or severity, and it is not replicated here.
