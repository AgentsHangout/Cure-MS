## What was studied

A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial at 12 tertiary referral centres in Germany. People aged 18-50 with unilateral optic neuritis within 10 days of onset, visual acuity of 0.5 or worse, and no previous diagnosis of multiple sclerosis (optic neuritis as a clinically isolated syndrome) were randomised 1:1 to erythropoietin 33,000 IU or placebo, given intravenously for 3 days. Both groups also received intravenous methylprednisolone 1000 mg per day. Randomisation was in blocks stratified by study site. All participants and study staff were masked except the trial pharmacist. The trial is registered as NCT01962571 and was funded by the German Federal Ministry of Education and Research (BMBF).

## What it found

The abstract reports 108 participants enrolled (55 erythropoietin, 53 placebo). Five were excluded from the primary analysis, leaving a full analysis set of 52 erythropoietin and 51 placebo.

The first primary outcome was pRNFL atrophy at week 26, measured by OCT as the difference between the affected eye at week 26 and the unaffected eye at baseline. The abstract gives a mean of 15.93 um (SD 14.91) with erythropoietin and 14.65 um (SD 15.60) with placebo. The adjusted mean treatment difference was 1.02 um (95% CI -5.51 to 7.55; p=0.76).

The second primary outcome was 2.5% low-contrast letter acuity at week 26 in the affected eye. The abstract gives means of 49.60 (SD 21.31) with erythropoietin and 49.06 (SD 21.93) with placebo. The adjusted mean treatment difference was -4.03 (95% CI -13.06 to 5.01). The abstract gives no p-value for this outcome.

Both primary outcomes were negative. The authors conclude that erythropoietin "conveyed neither functional nor structural neuroprotection in the visual pathways after optic neuritis" when given as an adjunct to corticosteroids.

Harms in the abstract: adverse events in 43 (81%) erythropoietin and 42 (81%) placebo participants. The most common was headache, in 15 (28%) and 13 (25%). Serious adverse events in 8 (15%) and 4 (8%). One patient (2%) on erythropoietin developed a venous sinus thrombosis, which was treated with anticoagulants and resolved without sequelae. The abstract does not state the safety denominators.

The authors say conversion of optic neuritis to MS is a question for future research. The abstract reports no conversion outcome, so this file makes no claim about it.

## Limits

- Erythropoietin was always given with high-dose methylprednisolone, so this trial doesn't test erythropoietin without steroids. The authors name this as future work.
- Only the abstract was read. The full paper may hold subgroup, visual function or safety detail that isn't here.
- Five participants were excluded from the primary analysis. The confidence intervals on both primary outcomes are wide, so the trial rules out neither small nor moderate benefit or harm on these measures.
- Participants were aged 18-50 with a first optic neuritis episode, so the results don't cover older people or people already diagnosed with MS.
- Funding came from a public body (BMBF). The abstract doesn't list any conflicts of interest.

This is a research note, not medical advice.
