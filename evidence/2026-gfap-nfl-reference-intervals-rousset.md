# Reference intervals for blood GFAP and neurofilament light, and what shifts them

- **Citation:** Rousset RZ, Honey MIJ, den Braber A, et al. Glial Fibrillary Acidic Protein and Neurofilament Light Reference Intervals in Healthy Individuals. *JAMA Network Open*, 2026;9(5):e2612793.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.12793](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.12793), PMID [42133312](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42133312/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (cross-sectional study)
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The authors built reference intervals (normal ranges) for plasma neurofilament light (NfL) and glial
fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), measured on one lab platform (Simoa HD-X), in people without
cognitive impairment (median age 54, range 30 to 90; 61.3% female). The full-text participant table
and Discussion give 7,958 people with NfL results and 7,810 with GFAP results; the abstract gives
7,989. They looked at how age, sex, body-mass index (BMI) and kidney function (estimated glomerular
filtration rate, eGFR) shift the values. They then compared people with several brain conditions, including 82 people with MS,
against these ranges.

## What it found

- Older age, lower BMI and poorer kidney function were each associated with higher NfL and GFAP.
  Female sex was associated with higher GFAP.
- Compared with an eGFR of 90 (mL/min per 1.73 square meters of body surface), an eGFR of 45 went
  with median NfL 6.58 pg/mL higher and GFAP 23.4 pg/mL higher. Between eGFR 90 and 60, the differences were small (NfL 1.43 pg/mL,
  GFAP 8.10 pg/mL).
- Compared with a BMI of 20, a BMI of 30 went with median NfL 1.72 pg/mL lower and GFAP 12.6 pg/mL
  lower.
- Median GFAP was 6.17 pg/mL higher in women than in men.
- NfL was most raised in frontotemporal and vascular dementia, and GFAP in Alzheimer disease
  dementia. In the small MS comparison group (82 people), 12.2% had NfL above the 95th percentile;
  the full text describes GFAP as more markedly raised than NfL in this group.
- The authors built an online tool to place a value in the context of a person's age, BMI, kidney
  function and sex.

## Limits

- Cross-sectional, and focused on dementia. Only 82 people with MS were included, as a comparison
  group.
- These are plasma reference intervals for healthy people, not validated thresholds for treatment
  decisions. The authors did not define any percentile as a cause for concern and say further
  validation is needed before setting clinical cutoffs.
- Age, kidney function, BMI and sex all shift the values, so a single result needs context. Other
  factors such as smoking or diabetes may also affect interpretation (raised by a reviewer; not
  modeled in this study and not checked here).
- One lab platform, so the ranges may not carry over to other assays.
- The paper is not internally consistent on sample size: the abstract and Results text give 7,989
  (7,841 with GFAP in the Results text), while the participant table and Discussion give 7,958 with
  NfL and 7,810 with GFAP. This file uses the table figures.
- Numbers were checked against the full text in PubMed Central
  ([PMC13177032](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13177032/)).
