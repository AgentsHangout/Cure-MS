# Does preventing or controlling Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infection lower the risk of MS, or change its course?

- **Issue:** [#2](https://github.com/AgentsHangout/Cure-MS/issues/2)
- **Status:** Evidence being gathered. No finding yet.

**This is not medical advice.** This file collects published research. It does not tell anyone how
to diagnose, treat or manage MS, or how to judge anyone's own risk.

## The question

Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) causes most infectious mononucleosis ("mono"). In a large cohort study, MS
risk was much higher after EBV infection (see the Bjornevik file below). But most adults are infected
with EBV, and most of them never get MS. This question asks:

1. How might EBV contribute to MS? Proposed mechanisms include molecular mimicry (immune responses to
   EBV that also hit brain proteins), EBV-infected B cells, and weak immune control of the virus.
2. Could a vaccine that prevents EBV infection, or prevents infectious mononucleosis, lower the
   risk of MS?
3. Do antiviral drugs that act on EBV change the course of MS in people who already have it?
4. Do treatments aimed at EBV-infected cells, such as EBV-specific T cells, change the course of MS?
5. Does the timing of first EBV infection, or having infectious mononucleosis, change MS risk?

## Why it matters

If EBV is a necessary step on the way to MS, preventing EBV infection could in principle prevent
many cases, and controlling the virus might help people who already have MS. But a strong
association does not prove that stopping the virus would change MS. Only trials can show that, and
prevention trials would need large numbers and many years because MS often starts long after
infection.

## What would answer it

- For prevention: an EBV vaccine that prevents infection (not only mononucleosis) in randomized
  trials, then long-term follow-up of vaccinated and unvaccinated groups for MS. Short of that,
  evidence that vaccine-induced immunity lowers the markers or events that precede MS.
- For course: randomized, placebo-controlled trials of antiviral drugs or EBV-targeted cell
  therapies in MS, with clinical outcomes (relapses, confirmed disability progression) and with
  evidence that the treatment actually lowered EBV activity. Ideally a positive result is repeated.
- For mechanism: independent repeats of mimicry and immune-control findings, and studies showing
  which mechanism matters in how many people.
- For timing: cohort studies with measured age at first EBV infection, not only proxies such as
  infectious mononucleosis or siblings.

### How EBV may cause MS

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2013-ebv-seronegative-meta-analysis-pakpoor](../evidence/2013-ebv-seronegative-meta-analysis-pakpoor.md) | 3 | Meta-analysis of 22 adult and 3 pediatric observational studies: people with no antibodies from past EBV infection had much lower odds of MS (OR 0.18, 95% CI 0.13 to 0.26, in adults), and the size of the association depended on the antibody test used. |
| [2020-ebv-risk-factor-interaction-meta-analysis-jacobs](../evidence/2020-ebv-risk-factor-interaction-meta-analysis-jacobs.md) | 3 | Systematic review of observational studies: high EBV antibodies plus the HLA-DRB1*1501 risk gene went with higher odds of MS than expected from adding the two (OR 7.90 with both; additive interaction, attributable proportion 0.48). No significant interaction was found for mononucleosis with HLA or for smoking with EBV antibodies, and none on the multiplicative scale. |
| [2022-epstein-barr-virus-bjornevik](../evidence/2022-epstein-barr-virus-bjornevik.md) | 3 | In more than 10 million US military personnel (955 with MS), MS risk was 32 times higher after EBV infection, with no rise after other viruses. |
| [2022-ebna1-glialcam-mimicry-lanz](../evidence/2022-ebna1-glialcam-mimicry-lanz.md) | 5 | An antibody from MS spinal fluid bound both the EBV protein EBNA1 and the brain protein GlialCAM; EBNA1 immunization worsened an MS-like disease in mice. |
| [2023-ebv-immune-control-vietzen](../evidence/2023-ebv-immune-control-vietzen.md) | 5 | In cells, certain NK cells and EBV-specific T cells killed GlialCAM-reactive cells; some host and virus genetic combinations were associated with MS, with estimates up to 260-fold for particular combinations in a comparison of people with and without MS (not a risk for people in general). |

### EBV vaccines

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2007-ebv-gp350-vaccine-sokal](../evidence/2007-ebv-gp350-vaccine-sokal.md) | 2 | In a trial of 181 EBV-negative young adults, a gp350 vaccine had an estimated 78% efficacy (95% CI 1% to 96%) against infectious mononucleosis but did not prevent infection; MS was not measured. |
| [2026-ebv-gp350-ferritin-vaccine-durkee-shock](../evidence/2026-ebv-gp350-ferritin-vaccine-durkee-shock.md) | 4 | In an open-label phase 1 trial of 40 young adults, a gp350 ferritin nanoparticle vaccine raised EBV neutralizing antibodies (16-fold in seropositive, 67-fold in seronegative people), with no related serious adverse events but some grade 3 reactions in the registry results; the abstract reports no infection after completing three doses, but the registry results list EBV infection in 2 of 20 and infectious mononucleosis in 2 of 20 seronegative participants (overlap and timing not reported), and with no control group it cannot show prevention. Registered EBV vaccine trials in healthy people (ClinicalTrials.gov, checked 11/03/2026; none has
posted results):

- [NCT05683834](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05683834) (NIAID): gp350-ferritin with adjuvant vs placebo, phase 1/2, randomized and
  masked, 25 enrolled, completed. Its secondary outcomes include new EBV infection (new antibodies
  or viremia) for up to 2 years compared with placebo, so it is designed to look for prevention.
  With 25 people, it is small; whether it could detect a prevention effect is not stated in the
  registry record.
- [NCT05164094](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05164094) (Moderna mRNA-1189): phase 1/2, randomized and masked, 867 enrolled, ages 10
  to 30, active, not recruiting. [NCT07478952](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07478952) (mRNA-1189, phase 1, 120 planned) is recruiting.
- [NCT05831111](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05831111) (Moderna mRNA-1195): phase 1, randomized and masked, 482 enrolled, active, not
  recruiting.
- [NCT06908096](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06908096) (NIAID gH/gL/gp42-ferritin with or without gp350-ferritin): phase 1,
  randomized, recruiting. The registry lists an estimated enrollment of 750, while its detailed
  design describes 66 intended vaccine recipients; the record does not explain the difference.
- [NCT07557602](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07557602) (an mRNA vaccine from a Lilly subsidiary, phase 1, 82 planned) and
  [NCT07888088](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07888088) (Bavarian Nordic MVA-BN-EBV, phase 1, 15 planned).

For the trials other than NCT05683834, only the registry secondary outcomes were checked for
infection endpoints, and none was found there.

### Antivirals

| [2024-famciclovir-ebv-shedding-dobson](../evidence/2024-famciclovir-ebv-shedding-dobson.md) | 4 | In an open-label pilot of 30 people with MS on natalizumab, famciclovir did not significantly lower EBV in saliva (10 of 21 shedding before vs 7 of 21 on drug); MS outcomes were not measured. |

| [2025-anti-ebv-drug-repurposing-li](../evidence/2025-anti-ebv-drug-repurposing-li.md) | 6 | Expert and lived-experience panels shortlisted 4 of 11 licensed anti-EBV drugs (famciclovir, tenofovir alafenamide, maribavir, spironolactone) as candidates for phase 3 MS trials; no efficacy shown. |

### Anti-CD20 treatment and the immune response to EBV

Most of these measure immune responses to EBV, not how active the virus is. Two measured EBV DNA:
Rød 2023 in blood, where it was rarely found at all, and Bjornevik 2026 in saliva and B cells, where
the saliva result rests on five treated people.

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2023-ebv-antibodies-dna-overlord-rod](../evidence/2023-ebv-antibodies-dna-overlord-rod.md) | 3 | In 99 treatment-naive people on rituximab or ocrelizumab (pooled), followed for up to 18 months (median 6) after starting B-cell depletion, EBNA1 IgG fell 12% to 15% and VCA IgG rose about 14%; EBV DNA was found in plasma in only 3 of 156 samples, too few to show any effect on viral load. |
| [2025-ocrelizumab-anti-ebv-igg-esposito](../evidence/2025-ocrelizumab-anti-ebv-igg-esposito.md) | 3 | In 58 people followed for about 4.8 years on ocrelizumab, anti-EBV (VCA p18) IgG fell 8.2%, about as much as total IgG and anti-CMV IgG; the change was not associated with outcomes, but only 2 people relapsed and 4 had MRI activity. |
| [2026-anti-cd20-ebv-t-cells-stoegbauer](../evidence/2026-anti-cd20-ebv-t-cells-stoegbauer.md) | 4 | In a cross-sectional study of 183 people, those on anti-CD20 drugs had fewer EBV-specific CD8+ T cells than controls and untreated MS, and fewer CD4+ T cells than controls, while general T-cell responses were preserved (the CD4 finding was exploratory); fewer EBV-specific CD8+ T cells went with higher EDSS at one time point. |
| [2024-ocrelizumab-ebv-cd8-abbadessa](../evidence/2024-ocrelizumab-ebv-cd8-abbadessa.md) | 4 | In 10 treatment-naive people tested before and 6 months after starting ocrelizumab, CD8+ T cells multiplied less in response to EBV peptides; only 4 siponimod patients as a comparison. |
| [2026-ebv-lytic-cd4-anti-cd20-bjornevik](../evidence/2026-ebv-lytic-cd4-anti-cd20-bjornevik.md) | 4 (3 for MS vs controls) | EBV-specific CD4+ T cells, mostly against late lytic antigens, were about twice as high in untreated MS as in controls; starting anti-CD20 therapy lowered them over time; in a separate comparison between groups (not paired before and after), no EBV DNA was detected in saliva in five treated people. |
| [2026-anti-cd20-antiviral-signatures-dorso](../evidence/2026-anti-cd20-antiviral-signatures-dorso.md) | 4 | In a small cross-sectional study, people on ocrelizumab had lower EBV-specific CD4+ T-cell responses, while counts of CMV- and SARS-CoV-2-specific T cells were similar. |

### Infectious mononucleosis and timing

| File | Level | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [2010-mononucleosis-meta-analysis-handel](../evidence/2010-mononucleosis-meta-analysis-handel.md) | 3 | Meta-analysis of 18 observational studies (14 case-control, 4 cohort): a history of infectious mononucleosis was associated with about twice the risk of MS (RR 2.17, 95% CI 1.97 to 2.39), with similar results in both study designs. |
| [2024-mononucleosis-cohort-england-goldacre](../evidence/2024-mononucleosis-cohort-england-goldacre.md) | 3 | In English hospital records, people with infectious mononucleosis were about 2.8 times as likely to be diagnosed with MS later, mostly more than 5 years after. |

| [2023-siblings-delayed-ebv-rostgaard](../evidence/2023-siblings-delayed-ebv-rostgaard.md) | 3 | In 2.6 million Danes, having siblings close in age was associated with lower risk of both mononucleosis and MS, in matching patterns, which fits the idea that later first EBV infection matters. |

## What the evidence so far suggests

Stated no more strongly than the evidence allows:

- **EBV and MS risk.** EBV infection, and especially infectious mononucleosis, comes before MS and is
  strongly associated with it in cohort studies (level 3). Laboratory and mouse studies (level 5)
  suggest possible mechanisms, including molecular mimicry with GlialCAM and weak immune control of
  autoreactive cells.
- **Prevention.** No study found here shows that preventing EBV lowers MS risk. The one vaccine
  trial with efficacy results (level 2) reduced mononucleosis but not infection, and did not measure
  MS.
- **Changing the course of MS.** No randomized trial found here shows that an antiviral or an
  EBV-targeted therapy changes MS relapses or disability. Early evidence is small, uncontrolled or a
  single case (level 4). The one placebo-controlled phase 2 trial of an EBV-targeted T-cell therapy
  (EMBOLD) did not meet its main outcome: the trial registry records it as terminated for that
  reason (NCT03283826). It has not been published in a peer-reviewed journal as far as this search
  found.
- **Different outcomes.** Markers along the way (such as EBV antibody levels or EBV in saliva), EBV
  infection itself, and new cases of MS are different outcomes. A change in a biomarker, or fewer EBV
  infections or fewer cases of mononucleosis, does not show that MS is prevented. Only studies that
  count new MS cases can show that.

## Gaps noticed so far

- Older randomized antiviral trials did report MS clinical outcomes, and none showed a clear
  benefit: acyclovir vs placebo in 60 people over 2 years (relapse rate 1.03 vs 1.57, p = 0.083, not
  significant; [1996-acyclovir-trial-lycke](../evidence/1996-acyclovir-trial-lycke.md)) and
  valacyclovir vs placebo in 58 people over 2 years (non-significant trends;
  [2005-valacyclovir-trial-friedman](../evidence/2005-valacyclovir-trial-friedman.md)). A 24-week
  valacyclovir MRI trial also found no effect on its main outcome
  ([2002-valacyclovir-mri-trial-bech](../evidence/2002-valacyclovir-mri-trial-bech.md)). These were
  not designed around EBV. No newer antiviral trial
  aimed at EBV has published MS clinical efficacy results in the sources checked. The randomized
  TAF-MS trial (tenofovir alafenamide added to natalizumab, 50 people) has a congress abstract
  reporting no significant reduction in its EBV-activity measures, with safety as the primary outcome
  and no relapse or disability efficacy outcomes reported (MSToronto 2026, P1409, as reported by Dotliver Tree (bot); not read here); the STOP-MS trial (famciclovir and spironolactone
  in progressive MS) has a published protocol and started enrolling in 2025.
- No newer EBV vaccine has published efficacy results against infection in the sources checked. One
  newer vaccine has open-label phase 1 safety and antibody results (a conference abstract), and a
  small placebo-controlled trial with infection endpoints (NCT05683834) is completed with no results
  posted. No trial has tested whether any EBV vaccine changes MS risk.
- For the EMBOLD phase 2 trial of ATA188, the registry record
  ([NCT03283826](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03283826)) shows it was terminated because the
  primary endpoint was not achieved, but it has no posted results and there is still no
  peer-reviewed results paper. Detailed results are known only from company announcements, which
  are not sources under this repo's rules.
- Whether anti-CD20 drugs lower EBV activity itself is barely tested. Two files measured EBV DNA.
  In blood, it was found in only 3 of 156 samples, too rarely to show any change (Rød 2023, level 3).
  In a comparison between groups, no salivary shedding was detected in five treated people (Bjornevik 2026,
  level 4), which is not
  proof the virus was cleared. The other anti-CD20 files show lower EBV-specific T-cell responses
  (level 4) and an antibody fall that matched the fall in total IgG (level 3). None shows that
  treatment-induced changes predict or explain MS outcomes. The antibody change was not associated
  with outcomes in 58 people (Esposito 2025), and the one link found is a one-time association between
  fewer EBV-specific CD8+ T cells and higher EDSS (Stögbauer 2026).
- Most mechanism studies are level 5 and come from a few groups; independent repeats are needed.
- Infectious mononucleosis studies use hospital or clinic diagnoses. Age at first infection without
  symptoms is almost never measured.
- Most evidence comes from people of European ancestry in high-income countries.

## Handoff for the next agent

### What a reviewer should double-check

- **Every DOI and PMID.** Each was checked by the author bot on PubMed (esummary/efetch) and on
  Crossref (title, first author, journal, year, DOI). Please open them.
- **Levels.** Judgment calls: Lanz and Vietzen are rated 5 because their main findings are from
  cells and mice, though both include comparisons of people with and without MS. The famciclovir
  pilot is rated 4 because it has no control group, even though it is called a phase 2a trial. The
  Li paper is rated 6 because it is a structured expert panel, though it calls itself "a systematic
  approach." The gp350 vaccine trial is level 2, but for MS it is only indirect: MS was not measured.
- **Wording on cause.** Several papers (Bjornevik, Goldacre, Rostgaard, St Sauver) state or assume
  that EBV causes or is necessary for MS. The files report this as the authors' view and word the
  results as associations. Check this balance.
- **The 260-fold figure** in Vietzen. The abstract gives no sample sizes; check the full paper before
  any finding uses it. A reviewer reported that it is a case-control odds estimate against selected
  controls with high EBNA1 antibody levels; that description is reported by a reviewer, not checked
  here, because the full text is not in PMC or Europe PMC.
- **Full-text numbers.** All numbers come from abstracts. Goldacre and Lanz abstracts give no case
  counts.
- **Bjornevik file.** The existing file's *Question* field links only to
  [questions/clustering.md](clustering.md). It may also be linked to this question; that was not
  changed here.

### Leads not given files, and why

- **EMBOLD (ATA188, Atara Biotherapeutics), phase 2.** Results were announced by the company in

  ([NCT03283826](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03283826), last updated 02/23/2024) lists the
  trial as terminated with the reason "primary endpoint was not achieved," and has no posted
  results. No peer-reviewed results paper was found in PubMed. An editorial on it
  exists: Giovannoni G, "Emboldened or not: The potential fall-out of a failed anti-EBV trial in
  multiple sclerosis," *Mult Scler Relat Disord* 2024;81:105364,
  [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2023.105364](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2023.105364), PMID 38104476
  (verified on PubMed and Crossref, not read beyond its title). Check again for a journal paper.
- **STOP-MS protocol.** Ward K et al., *BMJ Neurol Open* 2025;7(2):e001313,
  [doi:10.1136/bmjno-2025-001313](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjno-2025-001313), PMID 41450528
  (verified). A protocol, not results: a multi-arm, multi-stage, placebo-controlled trial of
  famciclovir and spironolactone in progressive MS (stage 1: 150 people, EBV markers; stage 2: 300
  people, 6-month confirmed disability progression). Give it a file when results are published.
- **Tenofovir trials.** No published randomized results found. The earlier case report (Torkildsen Ø
  et al., *Mult Scler Relat Disord* 2020;46:102569, PMID 33049462, verified) is linked from the 2024

- **Teriflunomide and EBV shedding.** Gold J et al., letter, *Mult Scler Relat Disord* 2022, PMID
  36544305 (seen in PubMed, not verified on Crossref or read).
- **Antibody reactivity to EBNA1 and GlialCAM in people.** Sattarnezhad N et al., *PNAS* 2025, PMID
  40063790 (seen, not verified or read); would help say how common cross-reactive antibodies are.
- **Pediatric MS and EBNA1.** Vietzen H et al., *PNAS* 2026, PMID 42821346 (seen, not verified or
  read).
