# Long-term outcomes after stem cell transplant in Italy

- **Citation:** Boffa G, Massacesi L, Inglese M, et al. Long-term Clinical Outcomes of Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation in Multiple Sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2021;96(8):e1215-e1226.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000011461](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000011461), PMID [33472915](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33472915/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

An observational cohort of 210 people with MS who had autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant
(aHSCT) in Italy; 122 (58%) had relapsing-remitting MS and the rest progressive MS. Median EDSS before
treatment was 6 (range 1 to 9). Mean follow-up was 6.2 years. The main outcome was survival without
disability worsening. There was no comparison group.

## What it found

- In relapsing-remitting MS, an estimated 85.5% had no disability worsening at 5 years and 71.3% at
  10 years. Mean EDSS fell slightly over time (by about 0.09 points a year).
- In progressive MS, an estimated 71.0% had no disability worsening at 5 years and 57.2% at 10 years.
- In relapsing-remitting MS, one conditioning regimen (BEAM plus antithymocyte globulin) was
  associated with less relapse and MRI activity than other regimens.
- 3 people (1.4%) died within 100 days of transplant. No deaths occurred in people transplanted
  after 2007.

## Limits

- No comparison group, so it cannot show whether aHSCT did better than drugs.
- The authors themselves classify it as Class IV evidence (the lowest level in the American Academy
  of Neurology scheme). Single-arm cohort; a reviewer may judge level 4 (case series) a better fit
  than 3 here.
- Confidence intervals at 10 years are wide (for example 57.8% to 84.8% in relapsing-remitting MS),
  because fewer people were followed that long.
- Regimens changed over time, so results mix older and newer practice.
- Funding and conflicts are not described in the abstract.
- A correction was published (*Neurology* 2021;97(4):203,
  [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000011825](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000011825), PMID 33947786).
  It is not open access and was not read, so it is not known whether it changes any figure above.
