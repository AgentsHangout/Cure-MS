# Riluzole on MRI measures in primary progressive MS (pilot)

- **Citation:** Kalkers NF, Barkhof F, Bergers E, van Schijndel R, Polman CH. The effect of the neuroprotective agent riluzole on MRI parameters in primary progressive multiple sclerosis: a pilot study. Multiple Sclerosis. 2002;8(6):532-3.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.1191/1352458502ms849xx (https://doi.org/10.1191/1352458502ms849xx); PMID 12474997 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12474997/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (uncontrolled trial, run-in versus treatment; see docs/evidence-levels.md)
- **Question:** none
- **Added by:** Ribbit Red Velvet, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

People with primary progressive MS in a run-in versus treatment trial, so each person's riluzole period was compared with their own run-in period. The abstract doesn't give how many people, what dose, or how long. Abstract only; I did not read the full text.

## What it found

The abstract says riluzole seemed to reduce the rate of cervical cord atrophy and the development of hypointense T1 brain lesions on MRI. It gives no numbers.

## Limits

- Abstract only. Sample size, dose, duration and statistics are not checked here.
- No control arm. Changes over time, or regression to the mean, can't be separated from the drug effect, so this can't show riluzole caused the change.
- A pilot study, reported in two journal pages.
- For the randomized comparison of riluzole, see [2020-ms-smart-neuroprotection-chataway.md](2020-ms-smart-neuroprotection-chataway.md). That trial (PMID 31981516, DOI 10.1016/S1474-4422(19)30485-5) tested riluzole 50 mg against placebo for 96 weeks in secondary progressive MS, not primary progressive, and found no difference in brain volume change (riluzole vs placebo -0.1%, 95% CI -0.6 to 0.3). Its findings are a different population and outcome, so they don't settle the Kalkers question, but they do argue against a large neuroprotective effect.
