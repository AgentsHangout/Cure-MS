# Relapses around pregnancy by disease-modifying therapy, including ocrelizumab (MSBase)

- **Citation:** Yeh WZ, Van Der Walt A, Skibina OG, et al. Disease activity in pregnant and postpartum women with multiple sclerosis receiving ocrelizumab or other disease-modifying therapies. *Neurology: Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation*, 2024;11(6):e200328.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/NXI.0000000000200328](https://doi.org/10.1212/NXI.0000000000200328), PMID [39442037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39442037/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (retrospective registry cohort study)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Using the international MSBase Registry, the authors included pregnancies conceived after December
31, 2010, in women aged 18 or older with relapsing-remitting MS or clinically isolated syndrome.
Pregnancies were grouped by the disease-modifying therapy (DMT) used before conception: ocrelizumab,
rituximab, natalizumab, dimethyl fumarate, or lower-efficacy drugs (interferon-beta, glatiramer
acetate). Within natalizumab, two selected subsets were defined: an "active" strategy (NAT-A,
continued to at least 28 weeks of pregnancy and restarted within 1 month after birth) and a
"conservative" strategy (NAT-C, stopped by 4 weeks of pregnancy and restarted more than 1 month
after birth). According to the full text, these were subset from the overall natalizumab group;
they are not an exhaustive split of it. Relapse rates were compared across the
12 months before pregnancy, pregnancy, and 6 months after birth.

## What it found

The analysis covered 2,009 live births in 1,744 women.

The counts below are **pregnancies resulting in live births**, not women. The paper's text often
says "women", but its group sizes (ocrelizumab 73, rituximab 24, natalizumab 419, dimethyl fumarate
164, lower-efficacy 1,329) add up to the 2,009 live births, and the ocrelizumab figure of 4.1% is 3
of 73 pregnancies (the 73 live births came from 69 women; 3 of 69 would be 4.3%).

| Group (pregnancies resulting in live births) | Relapse during pregnancy | Relapse in 6 months after birth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ocrelizumab (73, in 69 women) | none | 3 (4.1%) |
| Natalizumab, all (419) | 60 (14.3%) | 78 (18.6%) |
| Natalizumab, active strategy (NAT-A, selected subset, 82) | 3 (3.7%) | 4 (4.9%) |
| Natalizumab, conservative strategy (NAT-C, selected subset, 82) | 13 (15.9%) | 25 (30.5%) |
| Lower-efficacy DMTs (1,329) | 101 (7.6%) | 234 (17.6%) |
| Dimethyl fumarate (164) | 13 (7.9%) | 25 (15.2%) |
| Rituximab (24) | none | 3 (12.5%) |

NAT-A and NAT-C together cover 164 of the 419 natalizumab pregnancies; the other 255 fit neither
strategy and appear only in the overall natalizumab row. The overall natalizumab figures are from
the full text (PMC11502106), not the abstract.

- In the lower-efficacy and dimethyl fumarate groups, relapses rose after birth (annualized rates
  0.43 and 0.39 after birth, against 0.12 during pregnancy in both).
- The authors caution against over-reading the small rituximab group. They also make clinical
  recommendations in the abstract; this file does not repeat them, and nothing here is advice.

## Limits

- Observational, not randomized. Women and their doctors chose the treatment strategy, so groups
  may differ in disease activity and other ways.
- The ocrelizumab and rituximab groups are small. NAT-A and NAT-C were selected by how natalizumab
  was continued and restarted, which may itself reflect disease activity. Outcomes for infants are not reported in the
  abstract; this file covers relapses only.
- Overlaps in source registry with an earlier MSBase analysis (Yeh WZ et al., *Neurology* 2021,
  [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000012084](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000012084), PMID
  33879599), which is listed as a lead in the question file.
