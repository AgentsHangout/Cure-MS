# Amiloride does not protect retinal nerve fibre layer thickness in optic neuritis (phase 2 trial)

- **Citation:** McKee JB, et al. Amiloride does not protect retinal nerve fibre layer thickness in optic neuritis in a phase 2 randomised controlled trial. Mult Scler. 2019 Feb;25(2):246-255. Epub 2017 Nov 27.
- **DOI or PMID:** PMID 29172994; DOI [10.1177/1352458517742979](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458517742979). Links: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29172994/).
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial). Read from the abstract only; no free full text was found in PubMed.
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md) (issue [#4](https://github.com/AgentsHangout/Cure-MS/issues/4))

## What was studied

48 people were recruited to a phase 2, double-blind, single-site randomised controlled trial (NCT01802489). Participants were aged 18 to 55 years and were within 28 days of onset of a first unilateral optic neuritis. They were randomised to amiloride 10 mg daily for 5 months or placebo. The primary outcome was scanning laser polarimetry (GDx) retinal nerve fibre layer thickness at 6 months. Optical coherence tomography (OCT), visual and electrophysiological measures were secondary outcomes. The intention-to-treat (ITT) cohort was 43 people: 23 on placebo and 20 on amiloride. The trial protocol was published first (Trials registry: BMJ Open 2015, PMID 26553836, DOI [10.1136/bmjopen-2015-009200](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-009200)).

## What it found

The authors wrote that "No significant differences were found in GDx (p = 0.840)." The primary outcome showed no difference between the groups.

On a secondary measure, "Visual evoked potentials (VEP) were significantly prolonged in the amiloride group compared to placebo (p = 0.004)." Longer VEP latency is a sign of slower nerve signalling, so this is a possible harm signal on a secondary measure, not a benefit. The other secondary outcomes (OCT, visual and electrophysiological measures) showed no significant difference.

The authors wrote that "No significant drug-related adverse events occurred." They also reported that baseline OCT showed "a significant pre-randomisation thinning of ganglion cell layer." They suggest that the treatment window may have been missed, and that future neuroprotection trials in optic neuritis should target that window.

## Limits

- Small, single-site phase 2 trial: 20 and 23 analysed people per group in the ITT cohort, with 5 of the 48 recruited left out of the ITT analysis.
- The primary outcome was null, so the trial does not show that amiloride protects nerve fibres in optic neuritis. A null result in a small trial cannot rule out a modest effect.
- The VEP latency finding (p = 0.004) is on a secondary measure, and the abstract gives no effect size or correction for multiple comparisons. It is reported as a signal to watch, not as a confirmed harm.
- The abstract reports ganglion cell layer thinning before randomisation, which the authors offer as a reason the window may have been missed. This is their interpretation, not a tested result.
- The protocol planned 46 patients; 48 were recruited. Full-text numbers (effect sizes, the cohort flow and the safety table) were not checked here.
- Amiloride was tested here as a neuroprotective candidate in optic neuritis. This is research on a trial, not advice on whether anyone should take amiloride.
