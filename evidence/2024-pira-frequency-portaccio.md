# PIRA in the Italian MS Registry: how much worsening happens without relapses

- **Citation:** Portaccio E, Betti M, De Meo E, et al. Progression independent of relapse activity in relapsing multiple sclerosis: impact and relationship with secondary progression. *Journal of Neurology*, 2024;271(8):5074-5082.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1007/s00415-024-12448-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-024-12448-4), PMID [38805052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38805052/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

16,130 people with relapsing-onset MS in the Italian MS Registry, each followed for at least 5 years
(average about 11.8 years). Disability worsening was a 6-month confirmed rise in the Expanded
Disability Status Scale (EDSS) score. Each worsening event was sorted as relapse-associated worsening
(RAW) or PIRA. PIRA meant a confirmed rise that began more than 90 days after and more than 30 days
before the start of a relapse; this is a study-specific window, and it does not prove that no relapse
or inflammation happened. "Sustained" worsening meant no later improvement at any visit. The authors
also looked at who went on to secondary progressive MS. Per the full text, moving to secondary
progressive MS was defined by an algorithm that required a PIRA event, with an EDSS score of at
least 4.0 at that time and at the end of follow-up.

## What it found

- 16,731 confirmed worsening events happened in 8,998 people (55.8%).
- PIRA made up 72.3% of these events (12,175), as the paper reports. Note: 12,175 of 16,731 is
  about 72.8%, so the paper's percentage does not match its own counts; we did not find an
  explanation.
- PIRA events were more often sustained than RAW events (73.2% vs 56.7%).
- 27.6% of people moved to secondary progressive MS. This happened in 73.2% of people with sustained
  PIRA and 24.8% of people with non-sustained PIRA. Because the study's definition of secondary
  progressive MS requires a PIRA event, this link is partly built into the method. It is not
  independent evidence that PIRA marks the start of progressive MS.
- PIRA was associated with a higher final EDSS score after adjusting for other factors.

## Limits

- Registry data from routine care: visits were not at fixed intervals, and relapses or worsening can
  be missed or misclassified.
- Only people followed for 5 or more years were included, which may not reflect everyone with MS.
- Association only; it does not show what causes PIRA.
- The journal later published a correction to this article
  ([doi:10.1007/s00415-024-12627-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-024-12627-3), PMID
  [39231861](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39231861/)). It fixes author affiliations only and does
  not change any results. The numbers above are from the original article
  ([full text in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11319422/)).
