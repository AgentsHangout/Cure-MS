# Menopause and disability progression in relapse-onset MS (MSBase, Australia)

- **Citation:** Bridge F, Sanfilippo PG, Zhu C, et al. Menopause Impact on Multiple Sclerosis Disability Progression. *JAMA Neurology*, 2025;82(12):1219-1226.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.3538](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.3538), PMID [41021242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41021242/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A retrospective cohort using prospectively collected MSBase registry data from 8 Australian MS
centers. 1,468 women completed women's health surveys, and 987 women with relapse-onset MS, at least
three EDSS scores and known menopausal status were in the main analysis (583 premenopausal and 404
postmenopausal). Menopause was modeled as a change over time, with adjustment for age at onset,
disease duration, baseline EDSS, baseline relapse and use of high-efficacy treatment. A second
analysis followed 209 women with EDSS scores before and after menopause.

## What it found

- The median age at menopause was 48.5 years.
- After adjustment, menopause was not associated with a higher risk of 6-month confirmed disability
  progression (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.70 to 1.29) or of conversion to secondary progressive MS (HR 1.00,
  95% CI 0.60 to 1.67).
- In the 209 women followed through menopause, it did not mark a turning point in EDSS worsening.

## Limits

- Observational, and menopause timing and status came from retrospective surveys.
- The confidence intervals allow modest effects in either direction, so "not associated" is not
  proof of no effect.
- Women who completed the surveys may differ from those who did not.
- EDSS is a coarse measure. Effects on cognition, fatigue or other symptoms are not addressed in the
  abstract.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
