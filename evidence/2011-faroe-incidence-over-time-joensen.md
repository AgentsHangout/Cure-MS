# MS incidence over 60 years in the Faroe Islands

- **Citation:** Joensen P. Multiple sclerosis: variation of incidence of onset over time in the Faroe Islands. *Multiple Sclerosis*, 2011;17(2):241-244.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458510386997](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458510386997), PMID [20978036](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20978036/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (descriptive incidence over time in a small population)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

All people diagnosed with MS in the Faroe Islands between 1943 and 2007. The author estimated the
yearly incidence of MS onset by decade and for 2003 to 2007.

## What it found

- Before 1943, incidence was around 0.2 per 100,000 a year.
- 1943 to 1962: 4.4 per 100,000 (95% CI 2.9 to 6.1).
- 1963 to 1982: a significant fall to about 0.6 (0.3 to 1.5; p < 0.001).
- 1983 to 2002: a significant, sevenfold rise to 4.6 (3.2 to 6.2; p < 0.001).
- 2003 to 2007: a fall again to 2.4 (2.1 to 3.3; p = 0.003).
- The author argues that genes change too slowly to explain this, so environmental factors likely
  contribute.

## Limits

- Very small numbers. Diagnostic criteria and ascertainment methods differed across periods,
  complicating comparison; their contribution to the reported variation is not quantified here.
- It shows that incidence varied, not what caused the variation.
- Two published comments with an author reply exist (*Mult Scler* 2011;17(11):1393-1396), including
  one by Wallin and Kurtzke; they were not read here.
- Numerical results come from the abstract; diagnostic and ascertainment methods were also
  checked in the full text.
