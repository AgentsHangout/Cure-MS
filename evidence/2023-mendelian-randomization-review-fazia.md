# Systematic review of Mendelian randomization studies of MS risk

- **Citation:** Fazia T, Baldrighi GN, Nova A, et al. A systematic review of Mendelian randomization studies on multiple sclerosis. *European Journal of Neuroscience*, 2023;58(4):3172-3194.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1111/ejn.16088](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.16088), PMID [37463755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37463755/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a systematic review of genetic association studies, not of randomized trials)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A systematic review of Mendelian randomization studies on genetic and non-genetic exposures and MS
risk. Of 107 papers found, 42 were eligible and summarized qualitatively.

## What it found

- Across the reviewed studies, low vitamin D levels and high adult body mass index stood out as the
  most consistent risk factors for MS.

## Limits

- A qualitative synthesis with no pooled estimates in the abstract.
- Many Mendelian randomization studies reuse the same genetic data sets, so agreement between them
  is less independent than it looks.
- The abstract calls these risk factors "uncontested," which is stronger than Mendelian randomization
  alone can establish, because pleiotropy and weak instruments remain possible.
- Rated 3, not 1: the rubric's level 1 is for reviews of randomized trials.
