# Infectious mononucleosis and later MS in English hospital records

- **Citation:** Goldacre R. Risk of multiple sclerosis in individuals with infectious mononucleosis: a national population-based cohort study using hospital records in England, 2003-2023. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2024;30(4-5):489-495.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/13524585241237707](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585241237707), PMID [38511730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38511730/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (retrospective cohort study)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Using England's national hospital records (Hospital Episode Statistics) from 2003 to 2023, people
with a hospital diagnosis of infectious mononucleosis (IM, "mono," usually caused by a first EBV
infection in adolescence or adulthood) were compared with the general population for how often MS
was diagnosed later. The study looked especially at the time since IM.

## What it found

- People with hospital-diagnosed IM were more likely to be diagnosed with MS later: adjusted hazard
  ratio 2.8 (95% CI 2.3 to 3.4), or nearly three times the general population.
- The link was driven by long gaps, more than 5 years, between IM and MS diagnosis.
- The author suggests that the immune response during IM, not only EBV infection itself, may be
  involved over the long term. That is an interpretation of an association.

## Limits

- Observational, so it shows association, not cause.
- Only IM serious enough to appear in hospital records was counted, so milder IM was missed and the
  comparison group includes people who had IM outside hospital.
- The abstract gives no counts of people with IM or of MS cases. Check the full paper.
- The abstract does not say that IM was confirmed as EBV-caused by laboratory tests.
