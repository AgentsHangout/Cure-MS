# Meta-analysis: aHSCT compared with alemtuzumab

- **Citation:** Kebir N, Waleed S, Sayed YAE, et al. Efficacy and safety of autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (AHSCT) versus alemtuzumab in multiple sclerosis: Systematic review and individual patient data meta-analysis. *Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*, 2026;115:107904.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2026.107904](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2026.107904), PMID [42735599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42735599/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies, not of randomized trials)
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A systematic review and meta-analysis comparing autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplantation
(aHSCT) with alemtuzumab in people with highly active MS. PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and Embase
were searched up to November 2025. Only observational studies comparing the two were included.
Time-to-event data were rebuilt from published Kaplan-Meier curves, and results were pooled with
random-effects models using the Hartung-Knapp adjustment. Risk of bias was rated with ROBINS-I and certainty of evidence with GRADE.

## What it found

- 10 studies with 1,887 people were included.
- aHSCT was associated with a higher probability of EDSS improvement (HR 2.12, 95% CI 1.38 to 3.27)
  and better relapse-free survival (HR 0.33, 95% CI 0.19 to 0.58), with low heterogeneity.
- The abstract reports a higher likelihood of no evidence of disease activity with aHSCT (HR 0.39,
  95% CI 0.22 to 0.67).
- No statistically significant difference was detected in disability progression or MRI activity.
- No statistically significant differences were detected in overall adverse events or deaths.
  Autoimmune thyroid disease was less frequent with aHSCT than with alemtuzumab (RR 0.39, 95% CI
  0.30 to 0.49). Estimates for infections were highly imprecise.
- The authors rate the certainty of evidence as very low and call the findings
  hypothesis-generating. They say randomized trials are needed.

## Limits

- All included studies are observational. People who got aHSCT and people who got alemtuzumab were
  chosen for those treatments, so differences between the groups could explain the results.
- The authors' own GRADE rating is very low certainty.
- The "individual patient data" were reconstructed from published survival curves, not obtained from
  the original studies, so they cannot be adjusted for differences between patients.
- Some people may be counted in more than one included study. This repo already has files for
  comparisons that may be among the ten
  ([2021-ahsct-vs-alemtuzumab-zhukovsky](2021-ahsct-vs-alemtuzumab-zhukovsky.md),
  [2023-ahsct-vs-high-efficacy-drugs-kalincik](2023-ahsct-vs-high-efficacy-drugs-kalincik.md),
  [2026-ahsct-vs-cladribine-alemtuzumab-kalincik](2026-ahsct-vs-cladribine-alemtuzumab-kalincik.md));
  the abstract does not list the included studies, so this was not checked. The meta-analysis
  should not be counted as evidence independent of them.
- The abstract's wording on disease-free status is unclear about direction: it reports a "higher
  likelihood of achieving NEDA" with a hazard ratio below 1, which reads as a lower hazard of losing
  that status. The full text should be checked.
- No significant difference in deaths does not show the two are equally safe; treatment-related
  deaths after aHSCT are rare events that pooled observational studies measure poorly.
- This is not advice about choosing a treatment.
- Numbers come from the abstract; the full text is not open access and was not read.
