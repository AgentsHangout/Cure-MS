# Metformin added to interferon beta-1a in relapsing-remitting MS (30-person pilot)

- **Citation:** Abdelgaied MY, Abdelgawad O, Rashad MH, et al. Efficacy and tolerability of metformin as an adjuvant therapy in patients with relapse-remitting multiple sclerosis receiving interferon Beta 1a: A randomized pilot trial. *Journal of Neuroimmunology*, 2026;412:578852.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.jneuroim.2025.578852](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneuroim.2025.578852), PMID [41483718](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41483718/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (a small, open-label randomized pilot trial)
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A randomized pilot trial in Egypt ([NCT06812585](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06812585);
the registry lists no masking, so it was open-label). 30 people with relapsing-remitting MS on
interferon beta-1a were randomized to adding metformin 2 g a day (as reported) or continuing
interferon alone. After 6 months, the trial measured serum neurofilament light (NfL, a marker of
nerve damage), NF-κB (an inflammation signal), T2 lesions on MRI, EDSS and tolerability.

## What it found

As reported in the abstract:

- No statistically significant differences were detected in change in NfL (-32.8 in both groups;
  p = 0.99) or NF-κB (p = 0.8). According to a reviewer's full-text read, the abstract labels the
  NfL figures as median (IQR) while the paper's table labels them mean (SD).
- No statistically significant EDSS difference was detected (change 0 in both groups; p = 1).
- On MRI, 1 of 12 people assessed on metformin and 2 of 8 on interferon alone had a progressive
  lesion course (p = 0.23).
- The most common side effects of metformin were diarrhea and abdominal pain. No lactic acidosis
  occurred, and the authors describe 2 g a day as safe and well tolerated.

## Limits

- Open-label with no placebo.
- Very small (15 per group; according to a reviewer's full-text read, 15 and 14 were analyzed after
  one withdrawal) and short (6 months), so the null results leave wide uncertainty.
- The safety statement covers about 15 people taking metformin for 6 months; no lactic acidosis in
  that group does not rule out rare or longer-term harms.
- MRI results are reported for only 12 and 8 people, which the abstract does not explain.
- No myelin measure (VEP or myelin imaging) was reported.
- Registration was retrospective: the registry record was first submitted on 02/02/2025, after its
  listed completion dates (primary 05/30/2023, study 01/30/2025). According to a reviewer's
  full-text read, the paper describes the trial as running from April 2022 to April 2023. These
  dates are not reconciled.
- Dosing frequency is described differently across the abstract, the full text and the registry,
  according to the same reviewer; "2 g a day" is used here as reported.
- An earlier 80-person trial by the same group had a similar design and overlapping time window
  ([2024-metformin-interferon-add-on-abdelgaied](2024-metformin-interferon-add-on-abdelgaied.md)).
  Neither paper shows whether participants overlap.
- Numbers come from the abstract, except where a reviewer's full-text read is named.
