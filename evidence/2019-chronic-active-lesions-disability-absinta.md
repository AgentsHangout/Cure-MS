# Chronic active MS lesions with paramagnetic rims, and disability (Absinta 2019)

- **Citation:** Absinta M, Sati P, Masuzzo F, Nair G, Sethi V, Kolb H, Ohayon J, Wu T, Cortese ICM, Reich DS. Association of Chronic Active Multiple Sclerosis Lesions With Disability In Vivo. *JAMA Neurology*, 2019;76(12):1474-1483.

## What was studied

Three studies at one centre, the NIH Clinical Center.

1. A prospective clinical and MRI cohort of 209 adults with MS, enrolled 2012 to 2018, with 7-T or 3-T susceptibility-based brain MRI. 17 (8%) were excluded for uninterpretable scans, leaving 192 for the main analysis of rim counts and disability.
2. A pathology study of 10 expanding lesions from one adult with progressive MS, who had serial in vivo MRI for 7 years before autopsy.
3. A retrospective study of 27 rim lesions and 27 rimless lesions in 23 people with MS who had yearly MRI for 10 years or more (earliest scan 1992).

## What it found

- 117 of 209 people (56%) had at least one rim lesion, whatever their treatment. 84 (40%) had none, 66 (32%) had 1 to 3, and 42 (20%) had 4 or more.
- People with 4 or more rims reached motor and cognitive disability at an earlier age.
- Normalised brain, white matter and basal ganglia volumes were lower in people with rim lesions.
- Rimless lesions shrank over time (-3.6% a year), while rim lesions were stable or grew (+2.2% a year; P < .001).
- Rim lesions had longer T1 times than rimless lesions, which the authors read as more tissue destruction.
- In the autopsy case, all 10 rim lesions that grew in vivo showed chronic active inflammation.
- The authors conclude that chronic active lesions are common, go with more aggressive disease and ongoing tissue damage, and occur even in people on effective disease-modifying therapies.

## Limits

- Observational, at one centre, and association rather than cause. It does not show that treating rim lesions would prevent disability.
- The abstract does not give the size of the disability association (no effect estimate or confidence interval), and it does not use the term PIRA. Which disability measure was used, and what was adjusted for, is not checked here.
- The 8% excluded for unreadable scans could bias the result, though the abstract does not say how.
- The pathology result is one patient.
- The lesion-growth comparison (27 rim vs 27 rimless lesions in 23 people) is the primary analysis for annual lesion volume change, as the abstract lists it. Only the T1 comparison is exploratory. It is small and comes from 23 people.
- Needs susceptibility MRI at 3 T or 7 T, which is not routine everywhere.
- The treatment claim (rims occur on effective therapy) rests on the abstract's conclusion; the treatment breakdown is not checked.
- The journal lists an erratum (JAMA Neurol. 2019;76(12):1520, doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2019.4331). Its content has not been read.
