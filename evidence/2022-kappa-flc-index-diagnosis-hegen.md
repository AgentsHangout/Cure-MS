# Kappa free light chain index vs OCB for diagnosing MS (meta-analysis)

- **Citation:** Hegen H, Walde J, Berek K, et al. Cerebrospinal fluid kappa free light chains for the diagnosis of multiple sclerosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Mult Scler. 2023;29(2):169-181. Published online 2022.

## What was studied

A systematic review of PubMed-indexed studies that measured the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of the CSF kappa free light chain (kappa-FLC) index and of oligoclonal bands (OCB), to tell people with clinically isolated syndrome (CIS) or MS apart from control subjects. Two reviewers screened and extracted data following PRISMA, and the pooled analysis used bivariate mixed models. The kappa-FLC index analysis included 32 studies, with 3,322 CIS/MS patients and 5,849 controls.

## What it found

Across the 32 studies, the kappa-FLC index had a weighted-average sensitivity of 88% (range 52% to 100%) and specificity of 89% (range 69% to 100%). OCB had a weighted-average sensitivity of 85% (range 37% to 100%) and specificity of 92% (range 74% to 100%). The average difference was +2 percentage points for sensitivity and -4 for specificity (kappa-FLC index minus OCB). The mixed model found no significant difference in diagnostic accuracy between the two. The authors report a discriminatory kappa-FLC index cut-off of 6.1. The paper's conclusion is that the kappa-FLC index has similar diagnostic accuracy in MS to OCB.

The same paper's intrathecal kappa-FLC fraction analysis (13 studies) and CSF kappa-FLC concentration analysis (10 studies) are not covered here.

## Limits

This is a pooled look at diagnostic accuracy, not a trial. It says nothing about treatment or outcomes. The studies used different patient groups (MS, CIS and mixed cohorts), different control groups, different assays (Freelite and N Latex, nephelometry and turbidimetry) and different cut-off formulas, so the pooled numbers are averages across quite different settings. The authors say the power of their model was under 80% for the fraction comparison because there were few studies, and the cut-off of 6.1 was derived from the same pooled data. The conflicts section lists several authors with industry ties (including the first author), and the funding statement says no financial support was received.
