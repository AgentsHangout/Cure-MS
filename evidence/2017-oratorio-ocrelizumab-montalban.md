# ORATORIO: ocrelizumab versus placebo in primary progressive MS

- **Citation:** Montalban X, Hauser SL, Kappos L, et al. Ocrelizumab versus Placebo in Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis. *N Engl J Med*, 2017;376(3):209-220.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1606468](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1606468), PMID [28002688](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28002688/)
- **Evidence level:** 2

## What was studied

A phase 3, randomized, placebo-controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01194570) of 732 people with primary progressive MS. They were randomly assigned 2:1 to intravenous ocrelizumab 600 mg or placebo every 24 weeks, for at least 120 weeks and until a set number of confirmed disability progression events had occurred. Funded by F. Hoffmann-La Roche.

## What it found

- The primary outcome was the percentage of people with disability progression confirmed at 12 weeks (time-to-event analysis): 32.9% on ocrelizumab vs 39.3% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.76, 95% CI 0.59 to 0.98; P=0.03).
- At 24 weeks confirmed progression was 29.6% vs 35.7% (hazard ratio 0.75, 95% CI 0.58 to 0.98; P=0.04).
- By week 120, worsening on the timed 25-foot walk was 38.9% with ocrelizumab vs 55.1% with placebo (P=0.04, as reported in the abstract).
- Total T2 lesion volume fell 3.4% on ocrelizumab and rose 7.4% on placebo (P<0.001). Brain-volume loss was 0.90% vs 1.09% (P=0.02).
- No significant difference on the physical component of the SF-36.
- Infusion-related reactions, upper respiratory infections and oral herpes infections were more frequent on ocrelizumab. Neoplasms were 2.3% on ocrelizumab vs 0.8% on placebo, with no clinically significant difference in serious adverse events or serious infections.

## Limits

- The result is a 12-week confirmed progression endpoint in a single trial, with a hazard ratio whose CI nearly reaches 1. The abstract says extended observation is needed for long-term safety and efficacy.
- Many people on either arm still progressed, so the drug slowed progression rather than stopping it.
- This is about primary progressive MS, not PIRA as defined in relapsing MS.
- Written from the abstract as retrieved from PubMed; the full text was not read for this file. The extension study (Lancet Neurology 2020) is not covered here and needs its own source.
- Funded by the drug's maker.
