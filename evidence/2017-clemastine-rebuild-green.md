# ReBUILD: clemastine as a remyelinating drug in relapsing MS

- **Citation:** Green AJ, Gelfand JM, Cree BA, et al. Clemastine fumarate as a remyelinating therapy for multiple sclerosis (ReBUILD): a randomised, controlled, double-blind, crossover trial. *The Lancet*, 2017;390(10111):2481-2489.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32346-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32346-2), PMID [29029896](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29029896/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A single-center, 150-day, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 50 people with
relapsing MS (disease duration under 15 years) who had long-standing damage to the optic nerve
(chronic demyelinating optic neuropathy) and were on stable immune treatment. Clemastine fumarate is
an older, over-the-counter antihistamine that helped myelin-making precursor cells mature in lab and
animal studies. Half took clemastine (5.36 mg twice daily) for 90 days and then placebo for 60 days;
the other half took placebo first and then clemastine for 60 days. The main outcome was a shorter
delay (P100 latency) on visual evoked potentials (VEP), an electrical test of how fast signals travel
from the eye to the brain. A faster signal is read as a sign of new myelin.

## What it found

- In a trial of 50 people, clemastine shortened the VEP latency delay by 1.7 ms per eye (95% CI 0.5
  to 2.9; p=0.0048), analyzed as a crossover.
- All 50 people completed the study. Clemastine was associated with fatigue; no serious adverse
  events were reported.
- The authors described this as the first randomized trial to show efficacy of a remyelinating drug
  for chronic demyelinating injury in MS.

## Limits

- Small (50 people), single center and short (150 days).
- The main outcome is a nerve-signal measure, not something people feel. The abstract reports no
  change in vision or disability that people would notice.
- Only people with relapsing MS and optic nerve damage were included, so it does not show an effect
  in progressive MS or in other nerve pathways.
- A later study gave clemastine to people with progressive MS and stopped that arm for safety (see
  [2025-clemastine-trap-ms-kocot](2025-clemastine-trap-ms-kocot.md)).
- Funded by the University of California, San Francisco and a family foundation.
