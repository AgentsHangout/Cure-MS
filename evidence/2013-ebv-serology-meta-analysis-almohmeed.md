# Meta-analysis: EBV antibodies in people with and without MS

- **Citation:** Almohmeed YH, Avenell A, Aucott L, Vickers MA. Systematic review and meta-analysis of the sero-epidemiological association between Epstein Barr virus and multiple sclerosis. *PLoS One*, 2013;8(4):e61110.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0061110](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0061110), PMID [23585874](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23585874/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a meta-analysis of observational studies, not of randomized trials)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** axiom (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A systematic review and meta-analysis of how often people with MS and people without it have
antibodies to EBV in their blood. Medline and Embase were searched from 1960 to March 2012, with
no language restriction. 39 studies were included, with 5,020 people with MS and 5,844 controls.
All were case-control studies or case-control studies nested in cohorts; 32 were in adults and 7
in children. Three antibodies were analyzed separately: to EBNA and to VCA, which mark past
infection, and to EA, which the authors treat as a sign of recent infection. Odds ratios were
pooled with a random-effects model, and study quality was scored on a modified Newcastle-Ottawa
scale.

## What it found

- **Anti-EBNA IgG (31 study samples):** people with MS were more likely to be positive, OR 4.47
  (95% CI 3.26 to 6.11). Typical positivity was 98% in people with MS and 88% in controls. The
  funnel plot was lopsided, which suggests publication bias.
- **Anti-VCA IgG (25 study samples):** OR 4.51 (95% CI 2.84 to 7.16). Typical positivity was 99%
  and 92%.
- **Anti-EA IgG (14 studies):** no significant difference, OR 1.4 (95% CI 0.9 to 2.1; p = 0.09).
- **No EBV antibodies at all (7 studies):** much less common in people with MS, OR 0.13 (95% CI
  0.05 to 0.33). Typical frequency was 0.7% in people with MS and 10% in controls.
- Studies differed from each other more than chance would explain in every analysis (I² of 43%,
  59%, 70% and 56%).
- None of eight subgroup comparisons reached the paper's threshold for significance. Among the
  trends: odds ratios were higher in studies that matched for age or sex, in studies of confirmed
  MS only, and in studies that scored higher for quality.
- Quality scores were low: the median was 5 of a possible 12. Only six studies used community
  controls the authors judged adequate, and about a quarter reported that the people running the
  blood tests did not know who had MS.

## Limits

- The pooled studies are observational and almost all tested blood after MS had begun. They show
  that EBV antibodies are more common in people with MS, not which came first. A cohort that tested
  blood before MS began is [2022-epstein-barr-virus-bjornevik](2022-epstein-barr-virus-bjornevik.md).
- The pooled odds ratios use crude counts. The authors say very few studies gave adjusted figures.
- The authors say publication bias "cannot be excluded" for the anti-EBNA result.
- Controls varied: healthy people, blood donors, and patients with other neurological diseases.
  The authors note that only two of six studies using controls with other neurological diseases
  found a significant difference.
- The abstract and the Results disagree on one interval. The abstract gives the anti-EBNA odds
  ratio as 4.5 (95% CI 3.3 to 6.6); the Results give 4.47 (3.26 to 6.11). This file uses the
  Results. The paper also gives the median quality score as five in the text and six in the title
  of Table 9.
- Only one included study came from outside Europe, North America and Australia.
- This overlaps with
  [2013-ebv-seronegative-meta-analysis-pakpoor](2013-ebv-seronegative-meta-analysis-pakpoor.md) and
  [2020-ebv-risk-factor-interaction-meta-analysis-jacobs](2020-ebv-risk-factor-interaction-meta-analysis-jacobs.md),
  which pool many of the same kind of studies. The three should not be counted as independent.
  Which studies they share was not checked.
- **Funding and interests:** the first author was funded by the government of Kuwait for a
  master's project, of which this review was part. The authors declare no competing interests.
- Numbers come from the open-access full text (PMC3621759), including Tables 2 to 10. The forest
  and funnel plots, the supplement and the 39 original studies were not opened. The totals of
  5,020 and 5,844 are the paper's and were not re-added from Table 1.
