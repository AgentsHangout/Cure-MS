# ORATORIO-HAND: ocrelizumab in a broader group with primary progressive MS

- **Citation:** Giovannoni G, Airas L, Bove R, et al. Efficacy and safety of ocrelizumab in primary progressive multiple sclerosis, including older patients and those with more advanced disease (ORATORIO-HAND): a multicentre, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 3b study. *Lancet*, 2026;407(10544):2195-2207.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00617-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00617-3), PMID [42208561](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42208561/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A phase 3b, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at 138 sites in 22 countries. 1,013 people with
primary progressive MS, aged 18 to 65 with EDSS scores of 3.0 to 8.0 (so including older people and
people with more disability than the earlier ORATORIO trial), were randomly assigned to the anti-CD20
antibody ocrelizumab 600 mg (505) or placebo (508) every 6 months. The main outcome was composite
disability progression confirmed over 12 weeks, on either the 9-hole peg test (hand function) or the
EDSS.

## What it found

- In a trial of 1,013 people, 33% on ocrelizumab vs 40% on placebo had 12-week confirmed composite
  progression (hazard ratio 0.70, 95% CI 0.57 to 0.86; 30% relative reduction).
- In the subgroup with MRI activity at baseline, the risk reduction was larger (55%).
- Rates of serious adverse events and serious infections were similar between groups. Infections
  overall were a little more common on ocrelizumab (48% vs 45%), but not once COVID-19 was excluded.

## Limits

- The outcome is confirmed disability progression in primary progressive MS, not PIRA as defined in
  relapsing MS.
- Many people still worsened in both arms; the drug slowed but did not stop progression.
- The larger effect in people with MRI activity suggests part of the benefit may come from reducing
  new inflammation; the abstract cannot settle how much.
- Funded by the drug's maker (F. Hoffmann-La Roche).
