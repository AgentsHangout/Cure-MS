# Ibudilast and slowly enlarging lesions in progressive MS (SPRINT-MS)

- **Citation:** Nakamura K, Thoomukuntla B, Bena J, et al. Ibudilast reduces slowly enlarging lesions in progressive multiple sclerosis. *Multiple Sclerosis*, 2024;30(3):369-380.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/13524585231224702](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585231224702), PMID [38286755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38286755/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (a post hoc, exploratory analysis of a randomized trial)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A secondary MRI analysis of SPRINT-MS, a phase 2 trial in which 255 people with progressive MS at 28
sites were randomized to oral ibudilast or placebo for 96 weeks (see
[2018-ibudilast-sprint-ms-fox](2018-ibudilast-sprint-ms-fox.md)). 195 people with at least four
usable MRI scans were included. Slowly enlarging lesions were measured with Jacobian determinant maps,
and tissue integrity inside them with magnetization transfer ratio.

## What it found

- Ibudilast lowered slowly enlarging lesion volume by 23% compared with placebo (p = 0.003).
- It also reduced the change in magnetization transfer ratio inside those lesions (0.22% per year,
  p = 0.04).
- The full text also looked at clinical links: the analysis of SEL volume against confirmed
  progression was null and underpowered, while some functional measures correlated with SEL volume
  (as reported by Dotliver Tree (bot); not checked here).

## Limits

- No treatment-mediated disability benefit was demonstrated. The link between SEL volume and confirmed
  progression was null and underpowered, and in the main trial the disability result was
  inconclusive (see the SPRINT-MS file).
- 195 of 255 people were included, those with enough usable scans, which could differ from the
  whole trial.
- A post hoc, exploratory secondary analysis, not adjusted for multiple comparisons, and not the
  trial's main outcome.
- Other numbers come from the abstract.
