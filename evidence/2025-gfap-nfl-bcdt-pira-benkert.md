## What was studied

362 people with MS who started B-cell depleting therapy (ocrelizumab in 259, rituximab in 103) in the Swiss Multiple Sclerosis Cohort, a prospective multicentre study across 8 Swiss academic centres. 75.7% had relapsing MS and 24.3% progressive MS. Median follow-up after treatment start was 4.8 years. Serum GFAP and NfL were measured as Z scores, using a reference group of 2,861 healthy control people (4,943 samples). PIRA was defined as a confirmed EDSS worsening (at least 1.5 points from an EDSS of 0, 1.0 from 1.0-5.0, or 0.5 from 5.5 or more) without relapses. The authors state they followed the STROBE reporting guideline.

## What it found

- 95 of 362 people (26.2%) had a PIRA event during follow-up: 16.8% of the relapsing group and 55.7% of the progressive group.
- In a combined model, elevated sGFAP (Z score above 1) at 1 year was associated with a higher hazard of later PIRA, HR 1.80 (95% CI 1.17-2.78, p = 0.0079).
- Elevated sNfL at 1 year gave HR 1.45 (95% CI 0.95-2.24, p = 0.0886). The interval includes 1, so this is not a significant association on its own.
- sGFAP Z scores were on average 0.52 units higher in people who had PIRA than in those who stayed stable (95% CI 0.22-0.83).
- sNfL Z scores fell over time in people without PIRA (about 0.92 units per 10 years) but stayed high in people with PIRA.
- sGFAP rose over time in both groups, by about 0.49 Z score units per 10 years, so the rise was not specific to PIRA.

## Limits

- Observational. The authors say there was no comparison group to test a treatment effect of B-cell depleting therapy on sGFAP or sNfL, so these data cannot show what the drug does to either marker.
- These are associations with later PIRA. The paper does not test whether acting on a high marker changes outcomes, so it does not show that either marker is proof of benefit or harm from treatment.
- The abstract compares the two HRs (sGFAP 1.80, sNfL 1.45) but does not report a formal test of the difference between them.
- The main longitudinal analysis left out 104 people with under 4 years of follow-up. Only people with at least 3 documented visits and an index sample 8-24 months after treatment start were in the PIRA time-to-event analysis.
