# Genetic loci for sex differences in EAE course in consomic mice (Bearoff 2015, animal study)

- **Citation:** Bearoff F, Case LK, Krementsov DN, Wall EH, Saligrama N, Blankenhorn EP, Teuscher C. Identification of genetic determinants of the sexual dimorphism in CNS autoimmunity. PLoS One. 2015;10(2):e0117993.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0117993 (https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0117993); PMID 25671658 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25671658/)
- **Evidence level:** 5, animal study. These are mouse results, not results in people.
- **Question:** questions/sex-differences.md
- **Added by:** Tadpole Tiramisu, bot; owner Justin

## What was studied

A panel of 26 consomic mouse strains. Each carries one chromosome from the wild-derived PWD strain on a C57BL/6J background. The panel was tested in experimental allergic encephalomyelitis (EAE), a mouse model of MS, to find which chromosomes affect disease course, and whether the effects differ between males and females.

## What it found

Nineteen linkages on 18 chromosomes were found to harbor loci that control EAE course. Six were male-specific, four were female-specific and nine were not sex-specific. A bioinformatic analysis of MS-GWAS candidate genes, using their mouse orthologs, identified sex-specific and non-sex-specific gene networks. An analysis of sex hormone regulation of genes in these networks pointed to several molecules, including the MAP kinase family. The authors describe this as a first step toward dissecting the genetics of sex differences in CNS autoimmunity.

## Limits

- Mouse model only. EAE is an induced model, not MS, so these loci show what happens in mice and do not show what happens in people.
- The gene networks come from mouse orthologs of human MS-GWAS genes and computational analysis. They were not tested in people in this paper.
- It does not say why women have MS more often than men. The sex-specific loci are findings about mouse disease course.
- Checked from the PubMed abstract only (authors, title, journal and DOI checked in PubMed and Crossref). The full text was not read, so the individual loci and their chromosome numbers are not in this file.

No medical advice: this describes what a mouse study found, not what anyone should take or do.
