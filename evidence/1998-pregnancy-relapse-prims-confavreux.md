# Relapse rates during and after pregnancy: the PRIMS study

- **Citation:** Confavreux C, Hutchinson M, Hours MM, Cortinovis-Tourniaire P, Moreau T; Pregnancy in Multiple Sclerosis Group. Rate of pregnancy-related relapse in multiple sclerosis. *New England Journal of Medicine*, 1998;339(5):285-291.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1056/NEJM199807303390501](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199807303390501), PMID [9682040](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9682040/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (prospective cohort study; each woman compared with her own year before pregnancy)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The Pregnancy in Multiple Sclerosis (PRIMS) study followed 254 women with MS through 269
pregnancies in 12 European countries, during pregnancy and for up to 12 months after delivery. It
compared the relapse rate in each trimester and after birth with the rate in the year before
pregnancy, and tracked disability on the Kurtzke Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS). It also
looked at epidural analgesia and breastfeeding in relation to early postpartum relapses and
disability at 12 months.

## What it found

- Mean relapse rate per woman per year (± SD):
  - Year before pregnancy: 0.7 ± 0.9.
  - First trimester: 0.5 ± 1.3 (p=0.03 compared with before pregnancy).
  - Second trimester: 0.6 ± 1.6 (p=0.17).
  - Third trimester: 0.2 ± 1.0 (p<0.001).
  - First three months after delivery: 1.2 ± 2.0 (p<0.001), then back to the prepregnancy rate.
- Mean EDSS worsened by 0.7 point over 33 months of follow-up, with no clear speeding up after
  delivery.
- Neither breastfeeding nor epidural analgesia was associated with more relapses or faster
  disability worsening.

## Limits

- The women were studied before disease-modifying drugs were widely used, so the results describe
  largely untreated MS. Later cohorts (see the question file) report lower rates.
- The pattern of fewer relapses late in pregnancy and more after birth is consistent with a role
  for pregnancy hormones or immune changes, but this study measured neither, so it cannot say which.
- Breastfeeding was not randomized; women who chose it may differ in other ways.
