# Sex differences in relapses and disability worsening in treated relapsing MS in Denmark

- **Citation:** Magyari M, Koch-Henriksen N. Quantitative effect of sex on disease activity and disability accumulation in multiple sclerosis. *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry*, 2022;93(7):716-722.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1136/jnnp-2022-328994](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2022-328994), PMID [35393340](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35393340/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (nationwide registry cohort study)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The study included all Danish citizens with relapsing MS onset since 1996 who had received
disease-modifying therapy and were followed at required yearly or twice-yearly visits reported to
the Danish Multiple Sclerosis Registry: 3,028 men and 6,619 women. Men and women were compared using
weights based on the probability of being female, for relapse rates, yearly change in EDSS, and time
to EDSS 4 and EDSS 6.

## What it found

- Women had more relapses: weighted female-to-male relapse rate ratio 1.16 (95% CI 1.10-1.22). The
  difference disappeared after age 50.
- Disability rose faster in men: yearly EDSS increase 0.07 (0.05-0.08) in men against 0.05
  (0.04-0.06) in women (p=0.017).
- Compared with women, men reached EDSS 4 sooner (HR 1.34, 95% CI 1.23-1.45) and EDSS 6 sooner
  (HR 1.43, 1.28-1.61).
- Delay to diagnosis did not differ significantly by sex.
- The authors suggest that sex hormones may play a role in women's higher relapse rate up to the
  age of menopause, and that men are more affected by the neurodegenerative part of MS, especially
  after 45. Hormones were not measured; these are interpretations.

## Limits

- Observational; men and women may differ in treatment, smoking, other illnesses and other factors
  not fully captured by the weights.
- Only people who received disease-modifying therapy, so it may not describe everyone with MS.
- The age-50 cutoff is a stand-in for menopause, which was not recorded.
- An earlier international MSBase study found a similar pattern (Ribbons KA et al., *PLoS One*
  2015, [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0122686](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0122686), PMID
  26046348); it is listed as a lead and has not been given its own file.
