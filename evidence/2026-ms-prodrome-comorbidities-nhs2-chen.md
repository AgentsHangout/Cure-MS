## What was studied

113,876 women in the Nurses' Health Study II, followed prospectively for lifestyle, health indicators and diet. The authors looked at 336 women who later developed clinical MS and compared how these factors changed in the years before their first MS symptom with the underlying cohort. Women only.

## What it found

Several conditions were more common in women who later developed MS than in the cohort, including migraine, depression, urinary leakage and restless legs syndrome. Migraine prevalence rose above the cohort's about 9 to 10 years before onset, reaching 22.8% versus 19.3% at the time of first symptom. Physical activity, physical function and vitality declined years before first symptoms. Dietary changes were less pronounced. The authors describe a prodromal phase with both specific and non-specific signs.

## Limits

These are prevalence comparisons against the cohort over time, not risk factors or risk estimates, and the abstract gives no adjusted effect sizes. Early symptoms may reflect MS itself or more medical checks before diagnosis (detection), and conditions present early may be a sign of the disease rather than a cause (reverse causation). Women only, from one cohort of nurses. I checked the abstract and the DOI, not the full text, so the subgroup and adjustment details are not verified here.
