# Ublituximab vs teriflunomide in relapsing MS (ULTIMATE I and II)

- **Citation:** Steinman L, Fox E, Hartung H-P, et al. Ublituximab versus Teriflunomide in Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis. *N Engl J Med*, 2022;387(8):704-714.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2201904](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2201904), PMID [36001711](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36001711/). Trial registrations NCT03277261 and NCT03277248.
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trials, two identical phase 3 trials)
- **Question:** Lab question #5 (comparing the best current drugs). There is no questions/ file for it yet, so this refs #5 and is not an answer to #4 (remyelination).

## What was studied

Two identical phase 3, double-blind, double-dummy trials (ULTIMATE I and II) randomized 1:1 people with relapsing multiple sclerosis to IV ublituximab (150 mg on day 1, then 450 mg on day 15 and at weeks 24, 48 and 72, with oral placebo) or oral teriflunomide 14 mg once daily (with IV placebo). 549 people were enrolled in ULTIMATE I and 545 in ULTIMATE II; median follow-up was 95 weeks.

## What it found

- Annualized relapse rate, ULTIMATE I: 0.08 with ublituximab vs 0.19 with teriflunomide (rate ratio 0.41, 95% CI 0.27 to 0.62; P<0.001).
- Annualized relapse rate, ULTIMATE II: 0.09 vs 0.18 (rate ratio 0.51, 95% CI 0.33 to 0.78; P=0.002).
- Gadolinium-enhancing lesions on MRI, "mean number": 0.02 vs 0.49 in ULTIMATE I (rate ratio 0.03), and 0.01 vs 0.25 in ULTIMATE II (rate ratio 0.04).
- Worsening of disability at 12 weeks, pooled: 5.2% with ublituximab vs 5.9% with teriflunomide (hazard ratio 0.84, 95% CI 0.50 to 1.41; P=0.51). The authors say ublituximab "did not result in a significantly lower risk of worsening of disability".
- Infusion-related reactions occurred in 47.7% of the ublituximab group.
- Serious infections: 5.0% with ublituximab vs 2.9% with teriflunomide.

## Limits

- Relapse and MRI endpoints favoured ublituximab. The disability endpoint did not show a significant difference, so the trials do not show that ublituximab slows disability worsening compared with teriflunomide.
- The comparator was teriflunomide, a lower-efficacy oral drug. These trials cannot rank ublituximab against other high-efficacy treatments such as ocrelizumab or autologous haematopoietic stem cell transplant.
- This is not a remyelination trial. It does not speak to repair of lesions.
- Sponsor-funded by TG Therapeutics. Several authors are listed with a TG Therapeutics affiliation.
- Follow-up was about 95 weeks, so long-term effects are not shown.
