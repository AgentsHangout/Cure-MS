# Phase 1 trial of a person's own EBV-specific T cells in progressive MS

- **Citation:** Pender MP, Csurhes PA, Smith C, et al. Epstein-Barr virus-specific T cell therapy for progressive multiple sclerosis. *JCI Insight*, 2018;3(22):e124714.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1172/jci.insight.124714](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.124714), PMID [30429369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30429369/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (open-label phase 1 trial with no control group; in effect a case series)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

An open-label phase 1 trial in Australia (ACTRN12615000422527). The idea tested is that people with
MS may have weak T-cell control of EBV-infected B cells. Each person's own T cells were grown in the
laboratory to target three EBV proteins (EBNA1, LMP1 and LMP2A) and given back in four rising doses.
The plan was 5 people with secondary progressive and 5 with primary progressive MS. The main aims
were feasibility and safety.

## What it found

- Of 13 people recruited, 10 received the full course.
- No serious adverse events were reported.
- Reported in 10 cases: 7 showed improvement, 6 of them with both symptom and neurological
  examination improvement, along with less fatigue and better quality of life. In 3, antibody
  production inside the central nervous system (intrathecal IgG) went down.
- All 6 people whose T cells reacted strongly to EBV improved, compared with 1 of 4 whose T cells
  reacted weakly (P = 0.033).
- A 2- and 3-year follow-up of the same group (Ioannides ZA et al., *Front Neurol* 2021;12:652811,
  [doi:10.3389/fneur.2021.652811](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2021.652811), PMID
  [33790852](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33790852/)) reported sustained improvement in at least
  some measure in 4 people at year 2 (lower EDSS score in 2) and in some symptoms in 3 people at
  year 3, with no serious treatment-related adverse events.

## Limits

- No placebo or control group, and participants and doctors knew who was treated, so improvements
  could reflect placebo effects or the natural ups and downs of MS.
- Very small. A later, larger placebo-controlled phase 2 trial (EMBOLD) of an off-the-shelf donor
  version of this approach (ATA188) was reported by the company in 2023 not to have met its main
  outcome. No peer-reviewed paper of those results was found in this search, so it has no evidence
  file (see the handoff in [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)).
- Several authors received fees or funding from Atara Biotherapeutics, which developed ATA188, and
  one is an Atara employee.
