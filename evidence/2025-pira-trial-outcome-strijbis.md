# Is PIRA a reliable trial outcome? A reanalysis of two natalizumab trials

- **Citation:** Strijbis EMM, Mostert J, Comtois J, et al. Utility of Progression Independent of Relapse Activity as a Trial Outcome in Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2025;104(2):e210153.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000210153](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000210153), PMID [39960455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39960455/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (uses data from randomized trials, but this question was not the randomized comparison, so it is an observational analysis)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Individual data from 2,113 people with relapsing-remitting MS in two randomized trials of
natalizumab, AFFIRM and SENTINEL, with visits every 3 months for 2 years. The authors counted
3-month confirmed disability worsening, RAW and PIRA using the EDSS, 9-hole peg test and timed
25-foot walk. They checked how often PIRA came with new MRI activity, and they counted "improvement"
events defined the same way, to see how much of the signal could be measurement noise.

## What it found

- Only 8% of participants had a 3-month confirmed worsening event; most were PIRA (6.8%) rather than
  RAW (0.9%).
- Of people with PIRA, 42.2% had MRI activity in the first year and 30.9% in the second, so many
  "PIRA" events happened alongside new inflammatory lesions.
- Improvement events outnumbered PIRA events throughout follow-up, in every trial arm.
- Time to PIRA did not differ between people with and without MRI activity.
- The authors conclude that PIRA and RAW, as currently defined, do not reliably separate worsening
  driven by inflammation from worsening driven by neurodegeneration, and share the noise problems of
  existing trial outcomes.

## Limits

- Only 2 years of follow-up and relatively few events.
- Done in relapsing-remitting MS, with older trial designs and MRI schedules; the authors say it
  needs confirmation in other data sets with more complete MRI monitoring.
