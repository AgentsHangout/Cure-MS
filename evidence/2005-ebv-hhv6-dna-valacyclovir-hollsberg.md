# EBV and HHV-6B DNA in saliva and plasma within a valacyclovir trial in MS

- **Citation:** Höllsberg P, Kusk M, Bech E, et al. Presence of Epstein-Barr virus and human herpesvirus 6B DNA in multiple sclerosis patients: associations with disease activity. *Acta Neurologica Scandinavica*, 2005;112(6):395-402.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1111/j.1600-0404.2005.00516.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0404.2005.00516.x), PMID [16281923](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16281923/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (a secondary lab analysis of samples from a randomized trial; see Limits for why it is not rated 2)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Saliva and plasma samples from people with MS in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
valacyclovir trial were tested for DNA of EBV and of human herpesvirus 6B (HHV-6B), using real-time
PCR. The abstract does not name the parent trial or say how many people gave samples. It is likely
the trial reported by Bech et al. in 2002
([2002-valacyclovir-mri-trial-bech](2002-valacyclovir-mri-trial-bech.md)): both come from the same
Aarhus group and share several authors. That link was not confirmed, because the full text is not
open. Healthy controls were also tested.

## What it found

All percentages are of **samples**, not of people.

- **Saliva.** EBV DNA was found in 41% of saliva samples and HHV-6B DNA in 65%. In people on
  valacyclovir, the share of saliva samples with EBV was much lower (9%, p = 0.000017), and fell
  over time on treatment. The HHV-6B share was not changed (57%, p = 0.38).
- **Plasma.** EBV DNA was in 17% of plasma samples and HHV-6B DNA in 25%. On valacyclovir these were
  13% and 16%, not significantly lower. They also did not differ from healthy controls (6% and 39%).
- **Disease activity.** People with high disease activity had a higher share of samples with EBV
  (p = 0.018) and HHV-6B (p = 0.023) than people with low activity. EBV and HHV-6B were strongly
  linked to each other in plasma but not in saliva.
- No MS benefit is shown. This paper reports viral DNA, not relapses, disability or MRI outcomes.

## Limits

- **Why level 4, not 2.** The trial was randomized, but this is a secondary lab analysis. The
  abstract does not show the saliva result as a valacyclovir vs placebo comparison of people: the
  9% could be compared with placebo, with samples before treatment, or with all samples. Results are
  counted per sample, and the number of people is not given, so a few people giving many samples
  could drive them. The disease-activity links are observational. The level for the antiviral
  effect on saliva EBV could be higher if the full text shows a clear between-group comparison.
- Lower EBV in saliva is a marker of less virus copying in the mouth. Plasma EBV was not
  significantly lower, and the paper does not show that lowering EBV changed MS.
- The parent trial itself found no effect on its main MRI outcome (see the Bech file).
- All numbers are from the PubMed abstract. The full text is not open in PMC or Europe PMC, so the
  number of people, number of samples per person and funding were not checked.
