# Propionate and disease activity in pediatric-onset MS

- **Citation:** Schoeps VA, Bhargava P, Chang G, et al. Propionate, a short-chain fatty acid, is associated with disease activity in pediatric-onset multiple sclerosis. Cell Reports Medicine, 2026; 7(11): 103136. Published 17 Nov 2026.

## What was studied

Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) were measured by targeted mass spectrometry in plasma and in paired stool samples. The participants were a large cohort of deeply phenotyped people shortly after MS onset, with MS onset in childhood (per the title). The findings were reproduced in a second external cohort of similar size, for plasma and stool. The abstract does not give the number of participants, the outcome measures or the comparison used.

## What it found

Per the abstract, propionate was consistently associated with lower disease activity across three clinical and imaging activity-related outcomes. This held in the external cohort for plasma and stool. Propionate levels were not correlated with dietary fiber. The authors suggest that giving propionate directly might be more likely than fiber supplementation to affect MS activity, and propose testing this in proof-of-concept trials.

## Limits

This is an observational association in cohorts, and it does not show that propionate causes lower disease activity. Nothing here shows that diet, fiber or any supplement would help anyone; the idea of giving propionate directly is an untested hypothesis from the authors, not a result. The findings are for pediatric-onset MS.
