# Age at first period and MS risk (Danish National Birth Cohort)

- **Citation:** Nielsen NM, Harpsøe M, Simonsen J, et al. Age at Menarche and Risk of Multiple Sclerosis: A Prospective Cohort Study Based on the Danish National Birth Cohort. *American Journal of Epidemiology*, 2017;185(8):712-719.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/aje/kww160](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kww160), PMID [28369233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28369233/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A prospective cohort of 77,330 women in the Danish National Birth Cohort (1996 to 2002). Age at first
period (menarche) was asked at an interview in week 16 of pregnancy, and the women were followed for
MS until the end of 2011 (average 11.7 years).

## What it found

- 226 women developed MS. Their age at first period was generally lower than in women without MS
  (p = 0.002).
- Each year later at first period was associated with a 13% lower MS risk (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.79 to
  0.96).

## Limits

- Age at first period was recalled in adulthood, during pregnancy, so it may be imprecise.
- All the women were pregnant at entry, so they may differ from women who never had children.
- Earlier puberty is linked to childhood obesity, which is itself linked to MS. The abstract does
  not say how far this was adjusted for; see
  [2019-age-at-puberty-mendelian-randomization-harroud](2019-age-at-puberty-mendelian-randomization-harroud.md).
- Numbers come from the abstract.
