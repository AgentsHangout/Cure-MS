# Childbirth rates in MS from the prodromal phase (population-based cohort)

- **Citation:** Simonsen CS, Flemmen HØ, Broch L, Brekke K, Myklebust H, Berg-Hansen P, Brunborg C, Celius EG. ## What was studied

A cohort study of 1599 people with MS (1118 women, 481 men) from three hospitals in southeast Norway, with clinical data collected prospectively. They were compared with 23,855 controls matched for age, sex and place of residence at age 16. Statistics Norway supplied live births and marital status. The abstract does not give the follow-up length.

## What it found

The abstract reports these numbers. Source: abstract only.

- Mean number of live births: 1.5 (SD 1.2) in people with MS vs 1.8 (SD 1.2) in controls (p < 0.001).
- In women with MS, birth rates declined from 3 years before onset. Two years before onset, 4.5% gave birth vs 8.4% of controls (p = 0.046).
- Birth rates in women with MS were also lower 1 year after onset (p = 0.002).
- Men with MS showed a dip 2 years before onset (p = 0.002), otherwise similar to controls.
- No differences in marital status.

The authors conclude that women with MS have reduced childbirth rates compared with controls, and that this is already visible in the prodromal phase.

## Limits

- Observational cohort: it shows an association in timing, not a cause. Reduced births before diagnosis could reflect early symptoms rather than an effect of MS itself. The abstract does not say how this was handled.
- Abstract only. The full text was not checked, so the secondary analyses, the exact definition of onset and any adjustment are not confirmed here.
- Three hospitals in one region of Norway. Results may not carry over to other countries or health systems.
- Births are not the same as fertility intentions or pregnancy outcomes, and the abstract does not give the reasons for fewer births.
- The men with MS group is smaller (481), and its pattern is described only briefly in the abstract.
