# RENEWED: 2-year follow-up of the RENEW opicinumab trial

- **Citation:** Aktas O, Ziemssen F, Ziemssen T, et al. RENEWED: A follow-up study of the opicinumab phase 2 RENEW study in participants with acute optic neuritis. *Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*, 2025;93:106185.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/j.msard.2024.106185](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2024.106185), PMID [39662163](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39662163/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (follow-up of a randomized trial, with the limits below)
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A single follow-up visit about 2 years after the end of [RENEW](2017-opicinumab-renew-cadavid.md),
a randomized trial of opicinumab or placebo after a first episode of optic neuritis. 52 of the 82
RENEW participants (63%; 28 who had opicinumab and 24 who had placebo) took part. The main outcome
was the affected eye's VEP latency delay. The study also looked at progression to clinically
definite MS, visual acuity and other neurological function.

## What it found

- The VEP latency delay was numerically smaller in the opicinumab group, but not significantly so:
  -6.0 ms (95% CI -14.6 to 2.6; p = 0.165) in the per-protocol group and -4.5 ms (95% CI -12.6 to
  3.7; p = 0.274) in everyone followed.
- In a multifocal VEP substudy, an exploratory outcome, the improvement with opicinumab was nominally
  significant (p = 0.009).
- In the per-protocol group without clinically definite MS at the start, 12 (55%) on opicinumab and
  12 (67%) on placebo developed it (hazard ratio p = 0.23, not significant).
- The authors report no benefit on visual acuity or other neurological functions with opicinumab
  compared with placebo.

## Limits

- This is the closest file here to the repair gap, but it does not close it. It compares the two
  randomized groups; it does not test whether the people whose VEP latency recovered more went on to
  see or function better. A numerical VEP difference with no functional difference fits a marker that
  does not translate into benefit, but 52 people cannot settle that.
- Only 63% of RENEW participants took part, so the groups may no longer be balanced as randomized.
- The authors themselves ask for caution because of the follow-up design and the small sample.
- Biogen, which makes opicinumab, sponsored the work, and several authors are current or former
  Biogen employees.
