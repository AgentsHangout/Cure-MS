# Sex differences in relapse-independent and relapse-associated disability progression in relapsing-remitting MS (real-world, inverse-probability weighted)

- **Citation:** Foschi M, Marastoni D, Panzera I, Mancinelli L, Ganino C, Abbadessa G, D'Anna L, Gabriele F, Sacco S, Signoriello E, Lugaresi A, Tsantes E, Piscaglia MG, Surcinelli A. Sex differences in relapse-independent and relapse-associated disability progression in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis: a real-world inverse-probability weighted study. *Ther Adv Neurol Disord*. 2025;18:17562864251376807.
- **DOI or PMID:** doi:10.1177/17562864251376807 (https://doi.org/10.1177/17562864251376807); PMID 41000265 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41000265/); PMC12457755.
- **Evidence level:** 3 (cohort study, observational)

## What was studied

Adults with relapsing-remitting MS from a local registry, who had their first recorded neurological visit within 12 months of MS onset (492 people; median age 44.0 years; 68.9% women). Median follow-up was 5.1 years (IQR 3.1 to 7.2). The authors used inverse-probability weighting and weighted conditional proportional hazards models for repeated events, adjusted for visit and MRI frequency, to compare women and men. Outcomes were progression independent of relapse activity (PIRA), progression independent of relapse and MRI activity (PIRMA), and relapse-associated worsening (RAW). They also tested whether the sex comparison differed across prespecified subgroups.

## What it found

- Women had higher hazards of PIRA (HR 2.44, 95% CI 1.56 to 3.70; p<0.001) and PIRMA (HR 2.13, 95% CI 1.25 to 3.70; p<0.001). The abstract says these events were mainly postmenopausal (56.6% and 52.4%).
- RAW risk was similar (HR 1.07, 95% CI 0.54 to 2.11; p=0.843), although women had higher relapse rates (0.13 plus or minus 0.18 vs 0.06 plus or minus 0.16; p<0.001).
- Men had greater EDSS worsening per event: +0.29 plus or minus 0.71 vs +0.16 plus or minus 0.53 per PIRA (p=0.023), and +0.25 plus or minus 0.71 vs +0.09 plus or minus 0.38 per PIRMA (p=0.001).
- Age 50 or older at onset raised PIRA and PIRMA risk, with no sex interaction. The sex comparison differed by symptom location at onset (p<0.001 for both outcomes) and by initial disease-modifying therapy (p=0.013 for PIRA; p=0.022 for PIRMA).

The authors summarize this as women having higher risk of progression without relapses, while disability worsening per event was greater in men.

## Limits

This is an observational registry study, so the sex differences are associations and not causes. Weighting does not remove confounding by factors not measured. Treatment was not randomized, and the subgroup interactions were tested in several subgroups, so they need replication. The cohort included only relapsing-remitting MS, and the follow-up is about 5 years. The numbers here come from the PubMed abstract only; the full text was not read for this file. Nothing here is advice about treatment, and it is not a comment on any person's own care.
