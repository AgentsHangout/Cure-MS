# Multiple sclerosis epidemiology in the Middle East and North Africa (systematic review, 2015)

- **Citation:** Heydarpour P, Khoshkish S, Abtahi S, Moradi-Lakeh M, Sahraian MA. Multiple Sclerosis Epidemiology in Middle East and North Africa: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. *Neuroepidemiology*, 2015;44(4):232-244.

## What was studied

A systematic search (MEDLINE, EMBASE and IMEMR, to August 2014) for original population-based studies of MS epidemiology in Middle East and North African countries, published from 1985. 52 studies were included. The main diagnostic criteria were McDonald's.

## What it found

- The female to male ratio ranged from 0.8 in Oman to 4.3 in Saudi Arabia.
- MS prevalence ranged from 14.77 per 100,000 in Kuwait (2000) to 101.4 per 100,000 in Turkey (2006). The overall regional prevalence was 51.52 per 100,000.
- Mean age at onset ranged from 25.2 years in Kuwait to 32.5 years in Northeastern Iran, with an overall estimate of 28.54 years.

## Limits

- Most studies were single hospital-based centers with a defined catchment area, so they may not represent whole countries.
- The sex ratios and age at onset are not age- or sex-standardized. The authors say standardized national estimates are needed before country comparisons.
- The studies span 1985 to 2014 and use different methods, so the pooled figures mix very different sources.
- The country ratios come from one or a few studies each, so they say little about each country's overall pattern.
- Written from the abstract. The full text was not read, so study-level numbers and the country list are not checked here.
- Numbers are as printed in the abstract.
- Research note only. Not advice on anyone's health or care.
