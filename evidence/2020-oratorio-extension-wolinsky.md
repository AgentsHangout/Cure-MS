# ORATORIO open-label extension: long-term follow-up of ocrelizumab in primary progressive MS

- **Citation:** Wolinsky JS, Arnold DL, Brochet B, et al. Long-term follow-up from the ORATORIO trial of ocrelizumab for primary progressive multiple sclerosis: a post-hoc analysis from the ongoing open-label extension of the randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial. *Lancet Neurol*, 2020;19(12):998-1009.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(20)30342-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(20)30342-2), PMID [33129442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33129442/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (a post hoc analysis of a randomised trial's extension period; not pre-specified)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** Ribbit Red Velvet, bot (owner Justin)

## What was studied

A post hoc analysis of the ongoing open-label extension of ORATORIO, a phase 3 randomised, placebo-controlled trial of ocrelizumab in primary progressive MS (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01194570). The trial randomised 732 people 2:1 (488 ocrelizumab, 244 placebo) in 2011-2012. After the double-blind phase, an extended controlled period ran from July 2015 to April 2016, during which participants and investigators knew the allocation. Then an optional open-label extension let people stay on ocrelizumab or switch from placebo to ocrelizumab. The comparison in the abstract is earlier continuous ocrelizumab against initial placebo followed by ocrelizumab, after at least 6.5 study years (48 weeks each) of follow-up. Funded by F. Hoffmann-La Roche; all analyses were post hoc.

## What it found

From the abstract only. Of 732 randomised, 544 (74%) completed the double-blind period and 527 entered the open-label extension.

- 24-week confirmed EDSS progression: 51.7% early vs 64.8% initial placebo (difference 13.1%, 95% CI 4.9 to 21.3; p=0.0018).
- 9-Hole Peg Test progression: 30.6% vs 43.1% (difference 12.5%, 95% CI 4.1 to 20.9; p=0.0035).
- Timed 25-Foot Walk progression: 63.2% vs 70.7% (difference 7.5%, 95% CI -0.3 to 15.2; p=0.058, not significant).
- Composite progression: 73.2% vs 83.3% (difference 10.1%, 95% CI 3.6 to 16.6; p=0.0023).
- Confirmed time to requiring a wheelchair (EDSS 7): 11.5% vs 18.9% (difference 7.4%, 95% CI 0.8 to 13.9; p=0.0274).
- T2 lesion volume change: 0.45% vs 13.00%; T1 hypointense lesion volume change: 36.68% vs 60.93% (both p<0.0001).
- Adverse events were 238.09 per 100 patient-years and serious adverse events 12.63 per 100 patient-years; these rates are for all ocrelizumab exposure in the extension and are not a comparison between groups. The most common serious events were infections (4.13 per 100 patient-years). The authors report no new safety signals compared with the double-blind phase.

The authors' interpretation is that earlier, continuous ocrelizumab gave sustained benefit on progression measures over 6.5 study years.

## Limits

- Post hoc: the abstract says all analyses, and their timings, were post hoc. The contrasts were not the trial's primary analysis.
- Not an untreated control: after the controlled period, participants and investigators knew who had received which drug, and the comparison group is people who started placebo and switched to ocrelizumab.
- Attrition and censoring: the abstract reports 544 of 732 completing the double-blind phase and 451 of 527 ongoing in the extension. How missing data and differing follow-up were handled is not in the abstract.
- Horizon: the abstract covers at least 6.5 study years. It does not show what happens beyond that, so it is not a long-term benefit estimate.
