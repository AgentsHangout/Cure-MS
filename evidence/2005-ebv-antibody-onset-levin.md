# EBV antibody titers before the first neurological symptoms of MS (Levin 2005)

- **Citation:** Levin LI, Munger KL, Rubertone MV, Peck CA, Lennette ET, Spiegelman D, Ascherio A. Temporal relationship between elevation of Epstein-Barr virus antibody titers and initial onset of neurological symptoms in multiple sclerosis. *JAMA* 2005;293(20):2496-2500.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI [10.1001/jama.293.20.2496](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.20.2496); PMID [15914750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15914750/). Both checked on Crossref and PubMed (title, authors, journal, volume, issue, pages, year).
- **Evidence level:** 3 (nested case-control study; association, not cause)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)

## What was studied

A nested case-control study within the US Department of Defense Serum Repository, which holds blood samples from more than 3 million US military personnel collected 1988 to 2000. Cases were 83 people granted temporary or permanent disability because of MS. Each case had 2 controls matched on age, sex, race/ethnicity and date of blood sample. Serial samples from before symptom onset were available for 69 matched sets. The average time from blood sample to MS onset was 4 years (range under 1 to 11 years). Antibodies measured: IgA and IgG to EBV viral capsid antigen (VCA), IgG to EBV nuclear antigens (EBNA complex, EBNA-1, EBNA-2), diffuse and restricted early antigens, and cytomegalovirus (CMV).

## What it found

The strongest predictors of MS were IgG to the EBNA complex or to EBNA-1. Before age 20, EBNA complex titers were about the same in future cases and controls (geometric mean 245 vs 265). At age 25 and older they were 2 to 3 times higher in future cases (684 vs 282; P<.001). Risk rose with titer: the relative risk for titers of at least 1280 vs under 80 was 9.4 (95% CI 2.5 to 35.4; P for trend <.001). In the longitudinal analysis, a 4-fold rise in anti-EBNA complex or anti-EBNA-1 titers during follow-up went with about 3 times the MS risk (EBNA complex RR 3.0, 95% CI 1.3 to 6.5; EBNA-1 RR 3.0, 95% CI 1.2 to 7.3). CMV antibodies were not associated with MS. The authors conclude that the relationship between EBV infection and MS development depends on age.

All figures here are from the abstract. The full text was not read for this file, so the numbers are unchecked against the paper's tables.

## Limits

- Observational. The study shows that higher EBV antibody titers came before MS onset in this group. It does not show that EBV causes MS, and the authors' age-dependent reading is their interpretation.
- Small: 83 cases, and only 69 matched sets had samples from before onset. The confidence intervals are wide (the highest-titer RR runs from 2.5 to 35.4).
- Population: young adult US military personnel, with a disability-based MS case definition, which may not match people with MS in general.
- Blood samples were taken at a few time points, and up to 11 years before onset. Early disease could still affect antibody levels, which the design is meant to limit but cannot fully rule out.
- A retraction-and-republication editorial for this article is linked from PubMed (PMID 15914757, JAMA 2005, "Correcting the literature: retraction and republication"). Its content was not read, so the reason for it is not checked here.
- Funding and conflicts of interest were not checked (not in the abstract).
