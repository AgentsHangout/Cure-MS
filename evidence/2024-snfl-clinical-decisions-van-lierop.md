# How often serum neurofilament light results changed clinical decisions in one MS center

- **Citation:** van Lierop ZY, Wessels MH, Lekranty WM, et al. Impact of serum neurofilament light on clinical decisions in a tertiary multiple sclerosis clinic. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2024;30(13):1620-1629.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/13524585241277044](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585241277044), PMID [39420574](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39420574/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (case series of tests with before-and-after questionnaires; no comparison group and no patient outcomes)
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

From August 2021 to December 2022, the MS Center Amsterdam added serum neurofilament light (sNfL)
tests to routine care. Before and after seeing each result, clinicians filled in questionnaires on
why they tested, what they planned to do, how certain they were, whether they expected MRI activity,
and how useful they found the result. sNfL was tested in 166 cases (mean age 41, 68% female, 64% on a
disease-modifying drug). Reasons were drug monitoring (55%), new symptoms (18%), help telling MS
apart from other conditions (17%) and a baseline before starting a drug (11%).

## What it found

- Clinical decisions changed in 19.3% of cases after the result was seen. Changes were most common
  when testing for new symptoms (38%) and with higher sNfL levels.
- Clinicians' certainty rose after seeing the result.
- When sNfL was low, clinicians expected MRI activity less often.

## Limits

- Measures whether decisions changed, not whether patients did better. No outcomes for patients were
  reported.
- One tertiary center, no comparison group, and clinicians rated their own decisions.
- The abstract does not say which way decisions changed (for example, more or fewer MRI scans, or
  treatment changes).
- The authors themselves urge caution and more study in other settings.
