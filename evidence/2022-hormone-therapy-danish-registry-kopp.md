# Menopausal hormone therapy and MS activity (Danish registry)

- **Citation:** Kopp TI, Lidegaard Ø, Magyari M. Hormone therapy and disease activity in Danish women with multiple sclerosis: A population-based cohort study. *European Journal of Neurology*, 2022;29(6):1753-1762.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1111/ene.15299](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.15299), PMID [35196406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35196406/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A register-based cohort of 3,325 women with MS from the Danish MS registry. Hormone therapy use came
from the National Prescription Registry; 333 (10%) used it at some time during follow-up. Outcomes
were relapse rates, recurrent relapses, 6-month confirmed and sustained EDSS 4 and 6, and recurrent
EDSS worsening.

## What it found

- No association was found between hormone therapy and disability accrual, though risk trended up
  with longer use: for reaching EDSS 4, the estimate was 0.6 (95% CI 0.3 to 1.2) after less than 1
  year of use and 1.4 (95% CI 0.9 to 2.2) after more than 5 years, compared with never use.
- Current users had a 20% higher risk of recurrent relapses (95% CI 1.0 to 1.4), but this was driven
  by 1996 to 2005, before high-efficacy treatments were introduced.

## Limits

- Observational: women who choose hormone therapy may differ from those who don't, so the results
  cannot show cause in either direction.
- The confidence intervals are wide and include both benefit and harm for disability; "no
  association" is not proof of no effect.
- Prescriptions show that therapy was dispensed, not that it was taken. The abstract does not
  describe formulations.
- This is not advice about hormone therapy, which is a decision for each person and their clinician.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
