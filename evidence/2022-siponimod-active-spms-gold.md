# EXPAND active-SPMS subgroup: disability and cognition findings

- **Citation:** Gold R, Piani-Meier D, Kappos L, et al. Siponimod vs placebo in active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis: a post hoc analysis from the phase 3 EXPAND study. *Journal of Neurology*. 2022;269(9):5093–5104.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1007/s00415-022-11166-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-022-11166-z); [PMID 35639197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35639197/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 for randomized treatment comparisons, but an exploratory, post hoc subgroup analysis with no multiplicity correction, not a separate confirmatory trial.
- **Question:** [Progression independent of relapse activity](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** Dotliver Tree (bot), owner Justin

## What was studied

This analysis reused the phase 3 [EXPAND trial](2018-siponimod-expand-kappos.md), rather than recruiting a new population. Of 1,645 randomized participants who received study drug, 779 met the analysis definition of active secondary progressive MS: at least one relapse in the two years before screening and/or at least one gadolinium-enhancing T1 MRI lesion at baseline. The treated analysis set comprised 516 siponimod and 263 placebo participants.

The parent trial enrolled adults aged 18–60 with EDSS 3.0–6.5 at screening and no relapse in the preceding three months. Treatment was oral siponimod 2 mg/day or placebo for a variable period of up to three years. The article reports median exposure of 18 months and median core-study duration of 21 months. In the active subgroup, 621/779 completed the study.

Confirmed disability progression (CDP) meant an EDSS increase of at least 1.0 point for baseline EDSS 3.0–5.0 or 0.5 point for baseline EDSS 5.5–6.5, sustained to a three- or six-month confirmation. This is not a PIRA endpoint requiring progression events to fall outside specified relapse windows.

## What it found

All p values below are nominal: the authors made no correction for multiple comparisons.

- **Three-month CDP:** 128/515 (24.9%) with siponimod versus 91/263 (34.6%) with placebo; adjusted hazard ratio (HR) 0.69, 95% CI 0.53–0.91; p=.0094.
- **Six-month CDP:** 98/515 (19.0%) versus 74/263 (28.1%); HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.47–0.86; p=.0040. Table 2 defines these denominators as participants with non-missing model covariates. These observed proportions are not fixed-time absolute risks in a trial with variable follow-up.
- **Walking:** no significant difference in three-month confirmed worsening of at least 20% on the Timed 25-Foot Walk: 213/511 versus 120/263; HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.68–1.07; p=.1747.
- **Exploratory cognition:** six-month confirmed improvement of at least four points on the Symbol Digit Modalities Test (SDMT) was more frequent with siponimod (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.14–2.29; p=.007). Six-month confirmed worsening did not reach nominal significance (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.53–1.01; p=.060). Mean SDMT change at month 24 differed by 2.34 points (95% CI 0.66–4.02). The group mean difference is not the same quantity as an individual's four-point response threshold.
- **MRI:** adjusted T2 lesion-volume increase at month 24 was 13.3 versus 1,316.3 mm³, a difference of −1,303.0 mm³ (95% CI −1,675.8 to −930.31; p<.0001). This is an imaging outcome, not proof of myelin repair.
- **Reported safety:** adverse events occurred in 448/516 (86.8%) versus 206/263 (78.3%), serious adverse events in 78/516 (15.1%) versus 41/263 (15.6%), and adverse events leading to discontinuation in 30/516 (5.8%) versus 16/263 (6.1%). Supplement Table S5 reports more liver-related events (14.0% versus 4.6%), initiation-phase bradyarrhythmias including conduction defects (9.3% versus 3.4%) and macular oedema (1.4% versus 0.4%). These descriptive subgroup figures do not establish equivalent safety or exclude rare harms.

## Limits

- The active-subgroup analysis was post hoc. Although its treatment comparison retains randomized provenance, the authors call the analysis hypothesis-generating and state that EXPAND was not powered to assess active and non-active groups separately.
- A larger apparent effect here than in the overall trial does not itself establish treatment-effect modification. Significance in one subgroup and not another is not an interaction test. No formal active-versus-non-active treatment interaction was identified in the inspected main text and supplement.
- Baseline activity is a time-dependent classification, not proof of a permanently distinct disease mechanism. Non-active-SPMS CDP results were inconclusive (Table S2: three-month HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.71–1.23; six-month HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.64–1.19). Table S3 does show a nominal mean SDMT advantage in non-active SPMS (2.44 points, 95% CI 0.67–4.22; p=.007), although its confirmed four-point improvement/worsening results were nonsignificant. It would be inaccurate to describe every non-active-subgroup outcome as negative or to infer no effect from a nonsignificant estimate.
- Variable follow-up, incomplete observations and permitted switching to open-label siponimod after confirmed progression complicate timepoint comparisons. Cognition analyses were exploratory and included several definitions of improvement and worsening.
- EDSS progression, an SDMT test response and MRI change are different outcomes. This analysis does not isolate a relapse-independent mechanism or establish remyelination, neurorepair, employment benefit or a cure. The separate [Cree 2021 analysis](2021-siponimod-disability-apart-from-relapses-cree.md) addresses relapse-independent effects through additional modeling assumptions.
- **Funding and interests:** Novartis Pharma AG funded the study and medical writing; several authors were current or former Novartis employees. Other authors reported industry relationships.
