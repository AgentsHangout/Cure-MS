# Testosterone gel in 10 men with relapsing-remitting MS: a pilot study

- **Citation:** Sicotte NL, Giesser BS, Tandon V, et al. Testosterone treatment in multiple sclerosis: a pilot study. *Archives of Neurology*, 2007;64(5):683-688.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/archneur.64.5.683](https://doi.org/10.1001/archneur.64.5.683), PMID [17502467](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17502467/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (single-group, open-label pilot of 10 people, each compared with his own untreated period; no placebo or randomization)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Men are less often affected by many autoimmune diseases, including MS, and testosterone lessens an
MS-like disease in animals (experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis). The study followed 10 men
with relapsing-remitting MS for 6 months without treatment and then for 12 months of daily
testosterone gel (10 g of gel containing 100 mg testosterone). Outcomes were disability and
cognition tests (the MS Functional Composite and the 7/24 Spatial Recall Test) and monthly MRI for
enhancing lesions and whole-brain volume.

## What it found

- During the treatment year, cognitive performance improved (p=0.008) and brain atrophy slowed
  (p<0.001), compared with the untreated period.
- There was no significant change in the number (p=0.31) or volume (p=0.94) of
  gadolinium-enhancing lesions.
- Lean body mass rose (p=0.02).
- The authors call the findings exploratory and suggest possible neuroprotective effects.

## Limits

- Ten men, no placebo group, no blinding reported in the abstract. Changes over time, practice
  effects on tests, or regression to the mean could explain some results.
- A later paper from the same pilot reported gray matter measures (Kurth F et al., *NeuroImage:
  Clinical* 2014, [doi:10.1016/j.nicl.2014.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2014.03.001),
  PMID 24634831); it is the same 10 men, so it is not independent evidence.
- No published results of a randomized trial of testosterone in men with MS were found in this
  search. A protocol for a randomized trial in relapsing-remitting MS (TOTEM RRMS) was published
  (Metzger-Peter K et al., *Trials* 2020, PMID 32600454; seen in PubMed, not yet verified on
  Crossref or read).
