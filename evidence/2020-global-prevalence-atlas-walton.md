# Worldwide MS prevalence by sex: the Atlas of MS, third edition

- **Citation:** Walton C, King R, Rechtman L, et al. Rising prevalence of multiple sclerosis worldwide: Insights from the Atlas of MS, third edition. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2020;26(14):1816-1821.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458520970841](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458520970841), PMID [33174475](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33174475/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (survey of country representatives compiling prevalence and incidence data; descriptive)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

The Atlas of MS is a global compendium of MS data. Country representatives reported prevalence and
incidence in males, females and children, age and type of MS at diagnosis, and their data sources,
in a survey run from September 2019 to March 2020. The authors made regional summaries and compared
them with the 2013 edition.

## What it found

- An estimated 2.8 million people live with MS worldwide (35.9 per 100,000 population).
- Females are twice as likely as males to live with MS.
- Prevalence had risen in every world region since 2013. The pooled incidence across 75 reporting
  countries was 2.1 per 100,000 per year, and the mean age at diagnosis was 32 years.

## Limits

- Figures come from what each country reported, from sources of uneven quality; the authors note
  that good surveillance data are not available everywhere.
- A ratio of people *living with* MS (prevalence) is not the same as a ratio of new cases
  (incidence); survival and disease length also affect it.
- The abstract does not give the female-to-male ratio by region or how it changed since 2013;
  check the full paper (open access, PMC7720355).
