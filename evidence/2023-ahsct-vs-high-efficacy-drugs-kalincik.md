# Stem cell transplant vs fingolimod, natalizumab and ocrelizumab

- **Citation:** Kalincik T, Sharmin S, Roos I, et al. Comparative Effectiveness of Autologous Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant vs Fingolimod, Natalizumab, and Ocrelizumab in Highly Active Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis. *JAMA Neurology*, 2023;80(7):702-713.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2023.1184](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2023.1184), PMID [37437240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37437240/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A registry study that tried to imitate head-to-head trials (trial emulation), using data from 6
specialist MS centers with aHSCT programs and the international MSBase registry, 2006 to 2021. It
included 4,915 people with relapsing-remitting MS treated with autologous hematopoietic stem cell
transplant (aHSCT, 167), fingolimod (2,558), natalizumab (1,490) or ocrelizumab (700), each with at
least 2 years of follow-up. aHSCT was compared with each drug in turn, after matching people on a
propensity score built from clinical and demographic features. Before matching, people given aHSCT
were younger and more disabled. Outcomes were relapses and 6-month confirmed disability worsening and
improvement (EDSS).

## What it found

- **vs fingolimod (5 years):** people treated with aHSCT had fewer relapses (yearly rate 0.09 vs
  0.20) and were more likely to have disability improvement (HR 2.70, 95% CI 1.71 to 4.26). Risk of
  disability worsening did not differ clearly (HR 1.70, 95% CI 0.91 to 3.17).
- **vs natalizumab (5 years):** relapse rate was slightly lower with aHSCT (0.08 vs 0.10), and
  disability improvement was more likely (HR 2.68, 95% CI 1.72 to 4.18). Risk of worsening did not
  differ clearly (HR 1.06, 95% CI 0.54 to 2.09).
- **vs ocrelizumab (3 years):** no clear difference in relapses (0.09 vs 0.06), worsening (HR 1.77,
  95% CI 0.61 to 5.08) or improvement (HR 1.37, 95% CI 0.66 to 2.82).
- Death related to aHSCT occurred in 1 of 159 people (0.6%).
- The authors conclude aHSCT was associated with considerably better results than fingolimod and
  marginally better results than natalizumab, and that they found no evidence of a difference from
  ocrelizumab over the shorter follow-up available.

## Limits

- Observational, not randomized. Matching balances measured features only; people chosen for a
  transplant may differ in ways not recorded.
- Small aHSCT group (167), and fewer still in each matched comparison (110 in the ocrelizumab
  comparison).
- Follow-up in the ocrelizumab comparison was only 3 years, and the wide confidence intervals cannot
  rule out either a benefit or a harm.
- "No difference found" with ocrelizumab is not the same as "shown to be equal."
- Funding and conflicts are not described in the abstract.
