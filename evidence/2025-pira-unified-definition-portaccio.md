# A unified PIRA definition tested in the Italian MS Register (30,203 people)

- **Citation:** Portaccio E, Betti M, De Meo E, Pastò L, Prestipino E, Lugaresi A, et al. (Italian MS and Related Disorders Register). Toward a Unified Definition of Progression Independent of Relapse Activity in Multiple Sclerosis. *Neurology*, 2025 (electronic publication 22 Sep 2025; PubMed publication date 21 Oct 2025).
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000213977](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000213977), PMID [40982769](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40982769/). Full text open at [PMC12477792](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12477792/). DOI and PMID checked against the PubMed record (efetch) and the PMC record.
- **Evidence level:** 3 (retrospective cohort analysis of registry data, observational, no randomization)
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)

## What was studied

People with relapsing MS from the Italian MS and Related Disorders Register, with a first visit on or after 1 January 2000, at least 3 EDSS assessments, and at least 5 years of follow-up. The register was extracted on 29 September 2023. That gave 30,203 patients, followed for 11.3 years on average (SD 4.3).

The authors built 18 PIRA definitions from combinations of: a fixed or roving baseline; 24-week or 48-week confirmed or sustained disability accrual; and a relapse window (no relapse 90 days before and 30 days after the event, or 180 days before and 30 days after, or no relapse from baseline to confirmation). They then checked each definition against reaching EDSS 6.0, a later disability milestone.

## What it found

- PIRA frequency ranged from 38.8% to 74.1% across the 18 definitions. EDSS 6.0 was reached by 4,401 people (15%).
- The 90-day relapse window gave higher sensitivity for EDSS 6.0 (66.7% to 69.4%). The no-relapse-from-baseline window gave higher specificity (55.3% to 62.2%).
- The authors' best-performing definition, for predictive value and feasibility, combines a roving baseline, no relapse 90 days before and 30 days after the event, and 24-week confirmation.
- These figures are from the abstract and Results text of the full article. The rest of the full text was not checked line by line.

## Limits

- The register is retrospective, from routine care. Visits were not at fixed intervals, and relapses or worsening can be missed.
- The outcome used to judge definitions is reaching EDSS 6.0. That is a proxy for long-term disability. A definition that predicts EDSS 6.0 better is not thereby shown to be the correct definition of PIRA.
- The EDSS alone was used. The authors note it may under-count PIRA by leaving out other measures.
- The authors call the best definition the one with "best predictive value and feasibility". It is one trade-off, not a settled standard.
- Overlap with the 2024 Italian register analysis is not stated. Both draw on the same register, but the 2024 study used 16,130 people and a different selection, and the 2025 paper does not say whether the two samples share people. See [evidence/2024-pira-frequency-portaccio.md](2024-pira-frequency-portaccio.md). Do not add the two as independent samples.
- Several authors report industry compensation in the disclosures. The paper is not a trial and does not compare treatments.

Research note only. This does not say which PIRA definition any study or person should use, and it is not medical advice.
