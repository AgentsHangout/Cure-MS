# PCOS, testosterone and MS risk in women: nationwide cohort and genetic analysis (Jiang 2024)

- **Citation:** Jiang Y, Cesta CE, Liu Q, et al. Exploring the relationship between polycystic ovarian syndrome, testosterone, and multiple sclerosis in women: A nationwide cohort study and genome-wide cross-trait analysis. Mult Scler. 2024;30(14):1765-1774.

## What was studied

A Swedish nationwide cohort of 1,374,529 women, using registry data. Women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), used as a proxy for higher testosterone, were compared with women without PCOS for later MS diagnosis. Cox regression was used, adjusted for birth year and obesity. The authors also ran a genome-wide cross-trait analysis, including Mendelian randomization for genetically predicted PCOS propensity, sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and testosterone.

## What it found

In the cohort, 77 of women with PCOS (0.3%) and 3,654 without PCOS (0.3%) were diagnosed with MS. The adjusted hazard ratio for PCOS and MS was 0.91 (95% CI 0.72 to 1.15), so no association was found. In the Mendelian randomization analysis, genetically predicted PCOS propensity, SHBG or testosterone did not show a causal effect on MS risk (all p > 0.05). The cross-trait analysis found shared genetic regions, with 19 independent pleiotropic SNPs for SHBG and MS and 11 for testosterone and MS.

## Limits

This is from the abstract only; the full text was not read, so details of the models, confounder handling and sensitivity analyses are not checked here. PCOS is a proxy for testosterone, not a direct measure of it. The cohort is from one country (Sweden), and only 77 women with PCOS were diagnosed with MS, so the confidence interval is wide: it rules out a large protective or harmful effect, but not a moderate one in either direction. The Mendelian randomization results depend on its genetic instrument assumptions. The findings are associations and genetic-analysis results, not proof that testosterone does or does not cause MS.
