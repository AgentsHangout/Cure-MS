# Oral fingolimod vs placebo in relapsing MS (FREEDOMS)

- **Citation:** Kappos L, Radue EW, O'Connor P, et al. A placebo-controlled trial of oral fingolimod in relapsing multiple sclerosis. *N Engl J Med*, 2010;362(5):387-401. FREEDOMS Study Group.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa0909494](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0909494), PMID [20089952](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20089952/). Trial registration NCT00289978.

## What was studied

A 24-month, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial in people aged 18 to 55 with relapsing-remitting MS, EDSS score 0 to 5.5, and one or more relapses in the previous year or two or more in the previous two years. 1,272 people enrolled; 1,033 (81.2%) completed. Participants received oral fingolimod 0.5 mg or 1.25 mg daily, or placebo.

## What it found

- Annualized relapse rate (primary end point): 0.18 with 0.5 mg, 0.16 with 1.25 mg, and 0.40 with placebo (P<0.001 for each dose vs placebo).
- Time to disability progression (secondary end point): hazard ratio 0.70 (0.5 mg) and 0.68 (1.25 mg) vs placebo, P=0.02 for both. Confirmed progression at 24 months was 17.7%, 16.6% and 24.1%.
- MRI measures (new or enlarged T2 lesions, gadolinium-enhancing lesions, brain volume loss) were better than placebo at both doses (P<0.001).
- Adverse events related to fingolimod named in the abstract: bradycardia and atrioventricular conduction block at the start of treatment, macular edema, raised liver enzymes, and mild hypertension.

## Limits

- Two-year trial, so it says little about long-term risks. The abstract itself says these benefits "will need to be weighed against possible long-term risks".
- Relapse and MRI results are the main effects; the disability result is a secondary end point with a P value of 0.02.
- Only relapsing-remitting MS, aged 18 to 55 and with low to moderate disability (EDSS 0 to 5.5). It does not tell us about progressive MS or people outside those limits.
