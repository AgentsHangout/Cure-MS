# MS-STAT2: simvastatin in secondary progressive MS

- **Citation:** Chataway J, Williams T, Blackstone J, et al. Effect of repurposed simvastatin on disability progression in secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (MS-STAT2): a phase 3, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. *The Lancet*, 2025;406(10512):1611-1624.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(25)01039-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)01039-6), PMID [41045938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41045938/)
- **Evidence level:** 2
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at 31 UK hospitals in 964 people aged 18 to 65
with secondary progressive MS and an EDSS disability score of 4.0 to 6.5 (mean age 54; 73% women).
Simvastatin is a cholesterol drug. An earlier phase 2b trial (MS-STAT) had reported slower brain
shrinkage with it, so this trial tested whether it slows disability. People were assigned 1:1 to
simvastatin 80 mg a day (482) or placebo (482) for up to 4.5 years. The main outcome was time to
disability progression on the EDSS confirmed after 6 months.

## What it found

- In a trial of 964 people, simvastatin did not slow disability progression: 6-month confirmed
  progression occurred in 40% on simvastatin vs 36% on placebo (adjusted hazard ratio 1.13, 95% CI
  0.91 to 1.39; p=0.26).
- No new safety issues were seen. There was one serious adverse reaction (rhabdomyolysis, muscle
  breakdown) on simvastatin. Cardiovascular serious adverse events: 1% on simvastatin vs 2% on
  placebo.
- The authors concluded that simvastatin use in MS should be limited to existing heart and blood
  vessel reasons.

## Limits

- A large, well-designed trial with a clear negative result; it shows that a positive brain
  shrinkage result in a phase 2 trial did not carry over to disability in phase 3.
- Only secondary progressive MS was studied.
- Publicly funded (UK National Institute for Health and Care Research, the UK MS Society and the US
  National MS Society).
