# Increasing female preponderance of MS in Isfahan, Iran: a population-based study

- **Citation:** Maghzi AH, Ghazavi H, Ahsan M, Etemadifar M, Mousavi S, Khorvash F, Minagar A. Increasing female preponderance of multiple sclerosis in Isfahan, Iran: a population-based study. Mult Scler. 2010;16(3):359-361.
- **DOI or PMID:** PMID 20086021, DOI 10.1177/1352458509358092 (https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458509358092; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20086021/)
- **Evidence level:** 4 (descriptive registry study; cross-sectional analysis of registered cases)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)

## What was studied

Female/male ratio by year of birth in 1,584 people with MS registered with the Isfahan Multiple Sclerosis Society, Iran, between April 2003 and August 2007. The authors compared the sex ratio across birth-year groups to look at trends over recent decades. Abstract only; the full text was not checked for this file.

## What it found

The female/male ratio rose across birth years in a significant, gradual way, with an apparent break in the late 1960s. Year of birth was a significant predictor of the sex ratio (p < 0.001, chi-squared = 17.130, Spearman's rank correlation r = 0.893). The authors describe the rise as an increase in female incidence and suggest environmental change, vitamin D insufficiency, better diagnosis and lifestyle as possible causes. They did not test those causes in this study.

## Limits

This is a registry of people who registered with one patient society in one city, not a full population count, so it can't give a population incidence. Registration and survival can differ by sex and by birth year, and better diagnosis over time could also shift the ratio, so the trend may partly reflect how cases were found. The abstract gives no denominators. The vitamin D, diagnosis and lifestyle explanations are the authors' suggestions, not findings. This is an association over time, not a cause. It overlaps with the Isfahan registry in Barzegar 2024 (PMID 38359695, #125), so it is not independent evidence of the same trend. Figures are taken from the abstract only.
