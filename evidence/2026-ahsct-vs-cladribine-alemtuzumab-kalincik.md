# Stem cell transplant vs cladribine and alemtuzumab

- **Citation:** Kalincik T, Sharmin S, Roos I, et al. Haematopoietic stem cell transplant versus immune-reconstitution therapy in relapsing multiple sclerosis. *Brain*, 2026;149(3):951-962.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/brain/awaf286](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaf286), PMID [40755195](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40755195/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/cell-therapy.md](../questions/cell-therapy.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A registry study imitating head-to-head trials, using data from 7 specialist MS centers with aHSCT
programs (RESCUE-MS) and the international MSBase registry, 2006 to 2023. It compared autologous
hematopoietic stem cell transplant (aHSCT) with two "immune-reconstitution" drugs, cladribine and
alemtuzumab, in people with relapsing-remitting MS. People were matched on a propensity score:
143 aHSCT to 283 cladribine, and 134 aHSCT to 562 alemtuzumab. Matched people had active disease
(more than 0.8 relapses in the prior 2 years) and moderate disability (mean EDSS 3 to 4). Mean
follow-up was 3.8 to 3.9 years after aHSCT, 1.9 years on cladribine and 4.5 years on alemtuzumab.

## What it found

- **vs cladribine:** people treated with aHSCT had fewer relapses (yearly rate 0.05 vs 0.16; HR 0.24,
  95% CI 0.15 to 0.41) and were more likely to have disability improvement (HR 2.19, 95% CI 1.31 to
  3.66). Risk of disability worsening did not differ clearly (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.34 to 1.43).
- **vs alemtuzumab:** fewer relapses after aHSCT (0.04 vs 0.09; HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.29 to 0.93) and
  more disability improvement (HR 2.03, 95% CI 1.23 to 3.34). Risk of worsening did not differ
  clearly (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.53 to 1.72).
- 34% of people treated with aHSCT had delayed complications, mainly infections.
- No treatment-related deaths were reported.
- The authors describe aHSCT as superior to both drugs at suppressing relapses and enabling recovery.
  Because this is an observational study, this file words that as an association.

## Limits

- Observational, not randomized; matching cannot balance features that were not measured.
- Follow-up on cladribine was short (1.9 years) compared with aHSCT (about 3.9 years).
- Same research network and registry as the 2023 comparison with fingolimod, natalizumab and
  ocrelizumab ([2023-ahsct-vs-high-efficacy-drugs-kalincik](2023-ahsct-vs-high-efficacy-drugs-kalincik.md)),
  so the two studies may share aHSCT patients and are not fully independent.
- Did not compare aHSCT with anti-CD20 antibodies such as ocrelizumab.
- Funding and conflicts are not described in the abstract.
