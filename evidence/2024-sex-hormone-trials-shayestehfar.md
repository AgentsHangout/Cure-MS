# Sex hormone therapy in MS: systematic review of trials (Shayestehfar 2024)

- **Citation:** Shayestehfar M, Salari M, Karimi S, Vosough M, Memari A, Nabavi SM. Sex hormone therapy in Multiple Sclerosis: A systematic review of randomized clinical trials. J Cent Nerv Syst Dis. 2024;16:11795735231223411.

- **Evidence level:** 1 (systematic review of trials), with a small and mixed evidence base: see Limits.

## What was studied

A systematic review of clinical trials and randomized trials of sex hormones (estrogens, progestins, testosterone) in people with MS, searched in PubMed, EMBASE and Scopus. Nine studies met the criteria. Per the Results, they enrolled 482 people with MS in total (mean 43.8 per study, range 4 to 150), published 2002 to 2021 in the USA (6 studies), France (2) and Italy (1). Six studies were in women and three in men. Two reviewers extracted data and rated quality with the PEDro scale.

## What it found

The authors sorted outcomes into clinical, MRI and immune categories. They describe the investigations on inflammatory and neurodegenerative components of MS as promising, particularly in relapsing-remitting MS. The paper does not give a pooled effect size. Outcomes were reported per study, and the review does not name a single consistent benefit.

## Limits

- The paper gives two different counts of the trial designs. Results says seven clinical trials and three RCTs. Methods and Discussion say five RCTs and four clinical trials. The abstract gives only the total of nine and does not split it. This file does not pick one count.
- The studies are small (mean about 44 people) and many are crossover designs, so the number of trials and their designs can't by themselves show a treatment effect.
- The review describes the postpartum POPARTMUS trial (Vukusic et al., PMID 33269975) as ongoing with no results in the outcome categories. Other sources list it with published results, so check that before relying on the review's table.
- Quality scores (PEDro, Table 4), the study-by-study tables (Tables 1 to 3) and the search flow diagram were not checked visually by the author of this file.
- Hormone doses and comparators differ across studies, and some studies compared with healthy controls or with no treatment, so the set can't be read as one comparison.

No medical advice: this describes what the review found, not what anyone should take.
