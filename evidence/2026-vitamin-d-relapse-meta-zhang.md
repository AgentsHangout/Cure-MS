# Vitamin D supplementation and relapse rate: meta-analysis of 9 RCTs (Zhang 2026)

- **Citation:** Zhang Y, Yu S, Zu Y, Lv J, Chen J, Feng X. Vitamin D deficiency and multiple sclerosis relapse: a meta-analysis. Front Neurol. 2025;16:1727615. Epub 2026 Jan 2.
- **DOI or PMID:** DOI 10.3389/fneur.2025.1727615, PMID [41551317](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41551317/). Full text open: [PMC12807898](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12807898/).
- **Evidence level:** 1 (systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials), see docs/evidence-levels.md
- **Question:** none

## What was studied

Nine randomized controlled trials with 1,078 people with MS (mostly RRMS; the table also lists CIS and a few SPMS), comparing vitamin D supplementation with placebo or a lower dose. Searches ran from inception to April 2025, risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2, and pooling was done in RevMan 5.3. The primary outcome was annualized relapse rate (ARR). EDSS and serum 25(OH)D were secondary.

## What it found

Overall, there was no significant difference in ARR between supplementation and control (MD -0.07, 95% CI -0.18 to 0.03, p = 0.17). There was no significant difference in EDSS (MD -0.11, 95% CI -0.40 to 0.18, p = 0.47). Supplementation raised serum 25(OH)D (pooled MD 44.97, 95% CI 29.93 to 60.01, in the paper's units).

In a subgroup analysis by dose, low/medium-dose trials showed a lower ARR (MD -0.14, 95% CI -0.26 to -0.03, p = 0.01), while high-dose trials did not (MD 0.05, 95% CI -0.08 to 0.18, p = 0.42). This is a subgroup finding that generates a hypothesis. It is not a dose recommendation, because the overall ARR comparison was null.

## Limits

The title says "deficiency", but these are supplementation trials in people with MS, not a study of people with low vitamin D. Nine trials is a small set, and most are small: four have 53 people or fewer, and the largest has 468. Doses, populations, control arms and follow-up differ, and the dose subgroup is a split of the trials, not a randomized comparison. The paper is a meta-analysis, so it is no stronger than the trials it pools.

The nine trials are listed in Table 1 by author and year only. None is labelled as VIDAMS (see [2023-vitamin-d-vidams-cassard.md](evidence/2023-vitamin-d-vidams-cassard.md)), PREVANZ (see [2024-vitamin-d-prevanz-butzkueven.md](evidence/2024-vitamin-d-prevanz-butzkueven.md)) or D-Lay (see [2025-vitamin-d-d-lay-ms-thouvenot.md](evidence/2025-vitamin-d-d-lay-ms-thouvenot.md)). So this review does not clearly include those trials, but the table does not name trials by acronym, so check the full text before relying on that.
