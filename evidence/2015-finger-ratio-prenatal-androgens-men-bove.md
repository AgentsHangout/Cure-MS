# Finger-length ratio as a marker of prenatal androgens in men with and without MS

- **Citation:** Bove R, Malik MT, Diaz-Cruz C, et al. The 2D:4D ratio, a proxy for prenatal androgen levels, differs in men with and without MS. *Neurology*, 2015;85(14):1209-1213.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000001990](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000001990), PMID [26341868](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26341868/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (case-control)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A case-control study comparing the ratio of the second to the fourth finger length (2D:4D) in the
right hand of 137 men with MS at a referral center and 145 men without autoimmune or endocrine
disease, aged 18 to 65. A higher 2D:4D ratio is used as a proxy for lower prenatal androgen relative
to estrogen. In 51 men with MS, the ratio was compared with a testosterone level measured within 10
years of symptom onset.

## What it found

- The 2D:4D ratio was significantly associated with MS status in a univariate model (p < 0.05), and
  the difference was not explained by age or race, which differed between groups.
- In men with MS, the ratio did not correlate with MS type, age at first symptoms, MS Severity Score
  (p > 0.15 for each) or adult testosterone (r = 0.06, p = 0.68, n = 51).
- The authors suggest that low prenatal androgens could be a risk factor for MS.

## Limits

- 2D:4D is an indirect proxy for prenatal hormones, and its validity as a marker is debated.
- One referral center, with controls recruited differently from cases, so selection could explain
  part of the difference.
- The abstract gives only p < 0.05 from a univariate model, with no effect size.
- Men only, so it does not address women.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
