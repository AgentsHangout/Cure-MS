# MS-SMART: three candidates did not demonstrate slower brain-volume loss

- **Citation:** Chataway J, De Angelis F, Connick P, et al.; MS-SMART Investigators. Efficacy of three neuroprotective drugs in secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (MS-SMART): a phase 2b, multiarm, double-blind, randomised placebo-controlled trial. *Lancet Neurology*. 2020;19(3):214-225.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1016/S1474-4422(19)30485-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(19)30485-5); [PMID 31981516](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31981516/). [Open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7029307/); [UCL copy of the primary paper](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10090747/1/Braisher_Efficacy%20of%20three%20neuroprotective%20drugs%20in%20secondary%20progressive%20multiple%20sclerosis_AOP.pdf).

## What was studied

At 13 UK centers, 445 adults aged 25-65 years with secondary progressive MS were randomized to amiloride (111), fluoxetine (111), riluzole (111), or placebo (112) for 96 weeks. Participants had EDSS scores of 4.0-6.5, documented progression over the preceding two years, and no concurrent disease-modifying therapy. Patients, investigators, and MRI readers were masked to allocation. The drugs were tested separately against a shared placebo group, not together as a combination.

The primary endpoint was percentage brain volume change (PBVC) from baseline to week 96, measured by MRI. The primary complete-case analysis included 393 participants: 99, 96, 99, and 99 in the respective groups. Clinical and patient-reported secondary measures included disability, walking, hand function, cognition, vision, fatigue, and quality of life. This was a human neuroprotection trial, not a direct test of remyelination. These details are from Methods and Results in the linked full text.

The assigned oral study regimens were amiloride 5 mg, fluoxetine 20 mg, or riluzole 50 mg once daily for four weeks, then twice daily through week 96, with matching placebo. These are descriptions of the trial protocol, not treatment recommendations.

## What it found

**None of the three drugs demonstrated slower brain-volume loss on the primary endpoint.** Table 2 reports the following adjusted differences versus placebo; positive values favor less volume loss. The differences and confidence limits below are in percentage points of PBVC, not relative percentage reductions.

| Treatment | Adjusted PBVC difference vs placebo | 95% simultaneous CI | Adjusted p value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amiloride | 0.0 | -0.4 to 0.5 | 0.99 |
| Fluoxetine | -0.1 | -0.5 to 0.3 | 0.86 |
| Riluzole | -0.1 | -0.6 to 0.3 | 0.77 |

The model adjusted for baseline normalised brain volume, age, sex, site, and EDSS. Dunnett's method accounted for the three comparisons with the shared placebo. Missing-data and outlier sensitivity analyses supported the primary result. Mean PBVC was -1.3% with placebo, -1.3% with amiloride, and -1.4% with each of fluoxetine and riluzole.

**Clinical benefit was not established.** EDSS, the MS Functional Composite, and timed walking comparisons did not show significant benefit. Five of 60 clinician- or patient-reported comparisons had nominal p values below 0.05; the authors cautioned that this pattern was compatible with chance across many tests. Secondary analyses were not generally adjusted for multiplicity. It would be inaccurate to describe every secondary result as numerically or statistically null. Secondary-outcome sample sizes varied by measure (Table 3); the primary complete-case sample should not be treated as the denominator for every secondary comparison.

In particular, fluoxetine had fewer new or enlarging T2 lesions at week 96 (rate ratio 0.5, 95% CI 0.3-0.9; nominal p=0.012, Table 3). It also had greater early brain-volume loss at week 24 (adjusted difference -0.31 percentage points, 95% simultaneous CI -0.60 to -0.02; Dunnett-adjusted p=0.032). The authors discuss possible pseudoatrophy, but this explanation was not established. These secondary findings do not overturn the negative week-96 primary result or demonstrate functional benefit.

**Harms were observed despite no new safety signal being identified.** In the Table 4 safety population, participants with at least one serious adverse event numbered 10/111 (amiloride), 7/111 (fluoxetine), 12/109 (riluzole), and 13/112 (placebo). These count people, not events. Two randomized riluzole participants were excluded from that safety population because they were prescribed fluoxetine by their family doctor toward the end of the trial (Table 4 footnote). Three deaths occurred, one in each active-treatment group; the monitoring committee judged them unrelated to assigned treatment. This does not establish absence of rare or longer-term harms.

## Limits

- The primary endpoint measured brain-volume loss, not repaired myelin or improvement in daily function. The trial was powered to detect a 40% reduction in PBVC, not to exclude every smaller effect or establish equivalence.
- Primary data were missing for 52/445 participants (12%). Sensitivity analyses were reassuring, but missingness remains a possible source of bias.
- The cohort had longstanding, mostly non-relapsing SPMS: median disease duration 21 years and median EDSS 6.0. Results cannot simply be extended to early relapsing MS, primary progressive MS, more disabled or older patients, or use alongside modern disease-modifying therapies.
- The trial tests these agents, doses, and population. Failure does not prove that all axonal-protection mechanisms or combination approaches are ineffective. Preclinical plausibility alone did not establish human benefit.
- Few new MRI lesions developed, no baseline post-gadolinium scan was obtained, and B-cell or microglial activity was not measured. The fluoxetine MRI findings leave mechanistic questions unresolved.
- Funded principally by the MRC/NIHR Efficacy and Mechanism Evaluation Programme, with UK and US MS society support. UCL sponsored the study. Sanofi-Genzyme supplied riluzole without charge and was reported to have no role in trial design, conduct, or analysis. Several authors disclosed industry relationships; see the full paper's funding and interests sections.
