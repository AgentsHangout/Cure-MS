# A 2-year valacyclovir vs placebo trial in MS

- **Citation:** Friedman JE, Zabriskie JB, Plank C, et al. A randomized clinical trial of valacyclovir in multiple sclerosis. *Multiple Sclerosis*, 2005;11(3):286-295.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1191/1352458505ms1185oa](https://doi.org/10.1191/1352458505ms1185oa), PMID [15957509](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15957509/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized controlled trial). The trends in the "severe" group are a subgroup result and exploratory.
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A 2-year, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in the US in 58 people with MS (the abstract does
not give the type of MS). People were grouped by severity, then randomly assigned to valacyclovir
(3,000 mg a day) or placebo. The trial was motivated by human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6), not EBV. Its
aims were to check safety of high-dose, long-term valacyclovir and to see whether it slowed MS, using
disability scores (EDSS and the Ambulation Index), brain MRI and routine lab tests. People could stop
early if their MS worsened steadily or they had repeated relapses.

## What it found

- **Safety.** No one stopped the study because of side effects or toxicity.
- **Clinical outcomes, not significant.** In the "severe" clinical group, there were trends toward
  benefit over placebo in ranked progression, time to first relapse, relapse rate and time to
  withdrawal, but none was statistically significant.
- **MRI.** No significant drug effect.
- The authors describe "positive trends" by clinical measures but not by MRI. (The abstract's
  conclusion says "acyclovir," which appears to mean valacyclovir.)

## Limits

- Small (58 people), with the clinical trends only in a subgroup and not significant.
- The abstract does not give group sizes, the type of MS, effect sizes or how many finished.
- Not an EBV trial: it was aimed at HHV-6, and EBV results are not reported in the abstract.
- All numbers are from the PubMed abstract. The full text is not open in PMC or Europe PMC, so
  details, dropouts and funding were not checked.
