# Myelin water imaging as a measure of remyelination in the ReBUILD trial

- **Citation:** Caverzasi E, Papinutto N, Cordano C, et al. MWF of the corpus callosum is a robust measure of remyelination: Results from the ReBUILD trial. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America*, 2023;120(20):e2217635120.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1073/pnas.2217635120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2217635120), PMID [37155847](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37155847/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (an imaging analysis of a randomized trial; the imaging results were not the trial's main outcome)
- **Question:** [questions/repair.md](../questions/repair.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A further analysis of the ReBUILD trial of clemastine (see
[2017-clemastine-rebuild-green](2017-clemastine-rebuild-green.md)). Fifty people with MS had 3T MRI
at baseline, month 3 and month 5. Half took clemastine from baseline to month 3; the other half took
it from month 3 to month 5. The researchers measured myelin water fraction (MWF), an MRI method that
estimates the water trapped between myelin layers and so tracks myelin content. They looked at
myelin-rich areas that looked normal on standard MRI (normal-appearing white matter): the corpus
callosum, the optic radiations and the corticospinal tracts.

## What it found

- Myelin water fraction rose in the normal-appearing white matter of the corpus callosum during the
  periods when people were taking clemastine.
- The authors suggest that myelin repair can happen outside visible lesions, and propose corpus
  callosum MWF as a measure for future remyelination trials.
- The abstract does not give the size of the change, and does not report results for the optic
  radiations or corticospinal tracts.

## Limits

- Small (50 people) and short, and the imaging results were a later analysis, not the trial's main
  outcome.
- It shows an imaging change, not a change people feel.
- The method needs testing in other trials and drugs before it can be relied on as a trial outcome.
- Same research group as the original trial.
