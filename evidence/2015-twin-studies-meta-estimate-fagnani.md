# Pooled twin studies: how much of MS risk is genes, shared surroundings or neither

- **Citation:** Fagnani C, Neale MC, Nisticò L, et al. Twin studies in multiple sclerosis: A meta-estimation of heritability and environmentality. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2015;21(11):1404-1413.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458514564492](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458514564492), PMID [25583848](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25583848/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (a meta-analysis of observational twin studies)
- **Question:** [questions/clustering.md](../questions/clustering.md)
- **Added by:** axiom (bot), owner morgkorg

**Read from the PubMed abstract only.** The full text needs a subscription, and no open copy was
found. Anything the abstract doesn't state is left out and listed under Limits.

## What was studied

A meta-analysis of eight earlier twin studies of MS, found by a MEDLINE search, from France, the
UK, Canada, Denmark, North America, Italy, Finland and Sweden. The authors asked how much of the
difference in who gets MS is explained by genes (heritability), by surroundings twins share, such
as the home they grew up in (shared environment), and by everything that differs between twins
(unique environment). They fitted one statistical model across all eight studies, allowing for how
each study found its twins and for how common MS is in each country.

## What it found

- Identical twins were more alike in MS risk than non-identical twins: the pooled correlation was
  0.71 (95% CI 0.67 to 0.74) in identical pairs and 0.46 (95% CI 0.41 to 0.50) in non-identical
  pairs.
- Heritability was estimated at 0.50 (95% CI 0.39 to 0.61).
- The shared-environment component was estimated at 0.21 (95% CI 0.11 to 0.30).
- The unique-environment component was estimated at 0.29 (95% CI 0.26 to 0.33).
- The authors conclude that non-inherited factors deserve more study, both those that twins share
  and those that differ between them.

## Limits

- The underlying studies are observational. These are shares of the variation in risk across a
  population under a statistical model. They do not say how much of any one person's MS is due to
  genes.
- The model assumes identical and non-identical twins share their surroundings to the same degree.
  If identical twins share more, heritability is overestimated. The abstract does not discuss this.
- This disagrees with a file already in this repo.
  [2014-familial-risk-heritability-westerlind](2014-familial-risk-heritability-westerlind.md)
  estimated shared environment at 0.01 (95% CI 0.00 to 0.18) and heritability at 0.64 in Swedish
  registries. The two intervals for shared environment overlap between 0.11 and 0.18, so the
  studies are not flatly contradictory, but they point different ways on whether shared
  surroundings matter. Sweden is one of the eight countries pooled here; whether the same Swedish
  twins are in both was not checked.
- Not independent of
  [2003-twin-concordance-canada-willer](2003-twin-concordance-canada-willer.md) and
  [2005-twin-concordance-denmark-hansen](2005-twin-concordance-denmark-hansen.md). Canada and
  Denmark are among the eight countries, so those studies or earlier reports from the same twin
  series are probably pooled here. The abstract does not name the eight studies.
- Twin studies that recruit by asking for volunteers tend to over-count pairs where both twins
  have MS. The abstract says the model took account of how each study found its twins, but not
  how.
- Not read: which eight studies, how many twin pairs, how MS was confirmed, heterogeneity between
  studies, and the funding and interests statements. One database (MEDLINE) was searched, per the
  abstract.
