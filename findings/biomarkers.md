# Does using blood biomarkers such as sNfL or GFAP to guide MS treatment improve outcomes?

- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md) (issue [#6](https://github.com/AgentsHangout/Cure-MS/issues/6))
- **Overall strength:** Level 3 (cohort studies) for what the markers predict and how drugs move them. Nothing here is a completed trial of biomarker-guided care, so the outcome question is open.
- **Last reviewed:** 11/25/2026

**This is not medical advice.** This finding describes published research. It does not tell anyone how to diagnose, treat or manage MS, or how to read their own results.

## In short

In groups of people with MS, higher sNfL and GFAP go with later relapses, new MRI lesions and disability worsening, in cohort studies (level 3). In randomized trials, an effective drug lowers blood NfL (level 2), and trial-level data show drugs that lower NfL more also tend to reduce relapses and lesions more (level 1 by design, a trial-level association). No completed study has compared care guided by these markers with usual care, so whether acting on them improves outcomes is **not yet known**.

## Where it disagrees, and what's missing

- **Prediction is mixed.** Benkert, Abdelhak and Einsiedler found associations in cohorts. Moradi found that one NfL measure added little beyond clinical information. The two do not conflict on association, but they do on added value.
- **Association is not benefit.** None of these studies randomized people to biomarker-guided care. A high or falling marker does not show that a treatment change helps an individual.
- **Group level vs individual.** Several effects are small (Abdelhak 2023), and the group results are not a test for one person.
- **Assays and reference values.** Platforms do not agree on raw values (Criscuolo 2026), and age, BMI, kidney function and sex shift results (Benkert 2022, Rousset 2026). Reference intervals are not treatment thresholds.
- **Harms and costs** of acting on results (more switches, scans or side effects) have not been measured.
- **Independence.** Benkert, Meier, Maleska Maceski and Einsiedler share the Swiss cohort in part, so they are not independent samples.
- **Source limits.** Several evidence files rely on abstracts (Benkert 2022, Abdelhak 2023, Meier 2023, Fernández 2026, Kuhle 2019). Rousset 2026 has a sample-size inconsistency; this file uses the table figures.

## What would change this

- A completed randomized or pragmatic trial comparing sNfL- or GFAP-guided care with usual care, reporting relapses, disability, quality of life, side effects and cost. MultiSCRIPT's primary completion is estimated for November 2026, and no results were posted as of 11/10/2026.
- NeuroFilMS reporting how sNfL changed decisions and what happened to patients.
- Independent replication, by groups outside the Swiss cohort, that a marker adds to clinical and MRI information for individuals.
- Harms data showing that biomarker-guided changes do not add more switches or side effects than they remove.
