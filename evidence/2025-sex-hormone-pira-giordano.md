# Sex hormone-related factors and later progression (PIRA) in women with MS (San Raffaele, Milan)

- **Citation:** Giordano A, Gilberti A, Clarelli F, et al. Sex hormone-related factors and the risk of PIRA in women with multiple sclerosis. *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery and Psychiatry*, 2025 (online 13 March 2025).
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1136/jnnp-2024-335547](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2024-335547), PMID [40081962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40081962/)

## What was studied

A retrospective cohort of 1,210 women with MS from the San Raffaele MS Center in Milan, followed over the long term. Hormone-related factors came from an environmental survey (2019 to 2023). The outcome was PIRA: 12-week confirmed disability progression that is not tied to a relapse within the 30 days before it. Cox proportional-hazards models were adjusted for confounding factors.

## What it found

- Oral contraceptive use before MS diagnosis went with a lower risk of PIRA (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.61 to 0.89, p=0.0018). The median time to the first PIRA event was 9.94 years, against 7.5 years.
- Menopause at diagnosis went with a shorter time to PIRA (HR 1.82, 95% CI 1.24 to 2.67, p=0.0022).
- Pregnancy before diagnosis went with a shorter time to PIRA (HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.006 to 1.47, p=0.043).
- Abortion, menstrual irregularity and fertility therapy showed no significant difference.

## Limits

- Observational. These are associations, not causes, and the authors' suggestion that oral contraceptives may slow progression needs prospective testing.
- Single centre. Women who used oral contraceptives may differ from those who did not in ways the model does not capture.
- Exposures were recorded by a survey, not measured hormone levels, and were taken before or at diagnosis. This is not a measure of hormone levels before onset.
- No men are included, so this says nothing about sex differences in PIRA on its own.
- Written from the abstract. The full text was not read, so treatment adjustment, follow-up details and the survey's wording are not checked here.
- Numbers are as printed in the abstract.
- Research note only. Not advice on contraception, pregnancy, hormones or treatment.
