# Siblings, timing of first EBV infection and MS risk in Denmark

- **Citation:** Rostgaard K, Nielsen NM, Melbye M, Frisch M, Hjalgrim H. Siblings reduce multiple sclerosis risk by preventing delayed primary Epstein-Barr virus infection. *Brain*, 2023;146(5):1993-2002.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/brain/awac401](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awac401), PMID [36317463](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36317463/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (retrospective nationwide cohort study)
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

Almost nobody knows when they first caught EBV unless they had infectious mononucleosis (IM). So the
authors tested an indirect idea. Siblings, especially those close in age, tend to pass EBV on early
in childhood, which makes a later (delayed) first infection and IM less likely. If delayed EBV
infection matters for MS, then the pattern of MS risk by number and age of siblings should match the
pattern for IM. They followed all 2,576,011 Danes born from 1971 to 2018, using national registers
from 1977 to 2018, and counted 23,905 people with a hospital IM diagnosis and 4,442 with an MS
diagnosis.

## What it found

- The links between sibling pattern and IM, and between sibling pattern and MS, could be treated as
  the same (test for a difference P = 0.19).
- Having a sibling 0 to 2 years younger was associated with the lowest risk: hazard ratio 0.80
  (95% CI 0.76 to 0.85) per such sibling. For a sibling 0 to 2 years older: 0.91 (0.86 to 0.96).
- The authors suggest an EBV vaccine given before the teenage years might nearly eliminate MS, but
  say this needs replication and an explanation of why early infection seems less risky. That is a
  hypothesis, not a tested result.

## Limits

- Indirect: no one's age at EBV infection was measured. Siblings could affect MS risk through other
  routes, such as other childhood infections or family circumstances.
- Observational, so it shows association, not cause.
- Only hospital-diagnosed IM was counted.
- Danish population only; not yet repeated elsewhere, as far as this search found.
