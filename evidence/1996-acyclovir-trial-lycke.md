# An acyclovir vs placebo trial in relapsing-remitting MS

- **Citation:** Lycke J, Svennerholm B, Hjelmquist E, et al. Acyclovir treatment of relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis. A randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind study. *Journal of Neurology*, 1996;243(3):214-224.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1007/BF00868517](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00868517), PMID [8936350](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8936350/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 for the main outcome (randomized controlled trial). The subgroup and regrouped analyses are exploratory.
- **Question:** [questions/ebv.md](../questions/ebv.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Sweden in 60 people with relapsing-remitting
MS. Half took the antiviral acyclovir (800 mg three times a day by mouth) and half took placebo, for
2 years. The trial tested the broad idea that herpes viruses are involved in MS. It was not designed
around EBV in particular and, as far as the abstract shows, did not measure EBV. The main outcome was
the relapse ("exacerbation") rate, analyzed by intention to treat. Other measures included
neurological exams, eye and thinking tests, and evoked potentials.

## What it found

- **Main outcome, not significant.** The acyclovir group (30 people) had 62 relapses, an annual rate
  of 1.03. The placebo group (30 people) had 94 relapses, an annual rate of 1.57. That is 34% fewer
  relapses on acyclovir, but the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.083).
- **Exploratory analyses.** When people were regrouped by how many relapses they had (0 to 2, 3 to
  5, 6 to 8), the difference was significant (p = 0.017). In a subgroup with at least 2 years of MS
  before the trial (19 on acyclovir, 20 on placebo), relapses fell more from the 2 years before the
  trial on acyclovir (p = 0.024). These were not the main outcome and should be read as
  hypothesis-generating.
- Other neurological measures were "essentially unaffected," with no convincing sign of slower
  worsening on acyclovir.
- The authors suggest acyclovir "might" lower relapses and call for more study.

## Limits

- Small (60 people), so a modest effect could be missed, and the main result was not significant.
- The significant results come from regrouping and subgroup analyses, which carry a higher risk of
  chance findings.
- Not an EBV trial: EBV activity was not reported in the abstract, so it cannot show whether any
  effect came through EBV.
- No MRI outcomes are reported in the abstract.
- All numbers are from the PubMed abstract. The full text is not open in PMC or Europe PMC, so
  dropouts, side effects and funding were not checked.
