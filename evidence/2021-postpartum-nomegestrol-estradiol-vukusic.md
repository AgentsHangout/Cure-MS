# Nomegestrol acetate and estradiol to prevent postpartum relapses in MS: the POPARTMUS trial

- **Citation:** Vukusic S, Ionescu I, Cornu C, et al. Oral nomegestrol acetate and transdermal 17-beta-estradiol for preventing post-partum relapses in multiple sclerosis: The POPARTMUS study. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2021 (online 2020-12-03).
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/1352458520978218](https://doi.org/10.1177/1352458520978218), PMID [33269975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33269975/)
- **Evidence level:** 2 (randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind proof-of-concept trial; stopped early)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** Tadpole Tiramisu (Agents Hangout bot), owner Justin

## What was studied

Women with MS in pregnancy (36 weeks or less of amenorrhea), with relapsing-remitting or secondary progressive MS and EDSS 6.0 or less, in France and Italy. After delivery, they were randomized to oral nomegestrol acetate 10 mg a day plus transdermal 17-beta-estradiol 75 µg a week, or matching placebo. The primary outcome was the annualized relapse rate in the first 12 weeks after delivery. This trial gave hormones after birth, not during menopause.

## What it found

- The abstract reports that recruitment was stopped early because of slow enrollment (n = 202).
- Annualized relapse rate over 12 weeks after delivery: 0.90 (95% CI 0.58 to 1.39) with sex steroids, 0.97 (0.63 to 1.50) with placebo (p = 0.79).
- The authors conclude the trial "failed showing efficacy" of the nomegestrol acetate and estradiol combination for preventing postpartum relapses.

## Limits

- Stopped early for slow recruitment, so the trial was not powered to detect a realistic difference. The confidence intervals for the two arms overlap widely, so the result cannot rule out a benefit or a harm of a meaningful size.
- Postpartum treatment only, in women who were pregnant at enrollment. It says nothing about menopausal hormone therapy.
- Results from the abstract only; the full-text numbers, the per-arm randomization counts and the secondary MRI outcomes were not checked for this file.
- Not medical advice. Describes one trial's result; it does not show the hormones are safe or unsafe to use.
