# Expert consensus on treatment algorithms that use serum neurofilament light (Delphi study)

- **Citation:** Yaldizli Ö, Benkert P, Achtnichts L, et al. Personalized treatment decision algorithms for the clinical application of serum neurofilament light chain in multiple sclerosis: A modified Delphi Study. *Multiple Sclerosis Journal*, 2025;31(8):932-943.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/13524585251335466](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585251335466), PMID [40296363](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40296363/)
- **Evidence level:** 6 (structured expert consensus)
- **Question:** [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A modified Delphi study, a structured way of reaching agreement over several voting rounds. Over three
rounds, 10 international and 18 Swiss MS experts and 3 patient consultants rated how much they agreed
with proposed treatment algorithms that use serum neurofilament light (sNfL) in typical clinical
situations. sNfL was called "high" above the 90th percentile and "normal" below the 80th percentile
of values in control persons.

## What it found

The panel reached consensus on:

- 9 escalation algorithms (for example, starting treatment because sNfL is high),
- 11 horizontal switch algorithms (for example, switching from natalizumab to another high-efficacy
  drug because sNfL is high), and
- 3 de-escalation algorithms (for example, stopping a drug, or extending the dosing interval of
  B-cell-depleting drugs).

The authors state that these algorithms are meant to be tested in a randomized pragmatic trial.

## Limits

- Expert agreement, not evidence that the algorithms improve outcomes. The authors themselves present
  it as a basis for a trial.
- Most panelists were from Switzerland, and the panel was small.
- The abstract does not say how many algorithms reached each level of consensus (moderate, broad,
  strong or full).
- The trial these algorithms feed into appears to be MultiSCRIPT (see
  [questions/biomarkers.md](../questions/biomarkers.md)); that link is an inference and should be
  checked in the full paper.
