# Disease-modifying treatments and chronic lesion tissue expansion (MSBase)

- **Citation:** Guilfoyle D, Ward K, Klistorner S, et al. Impact of multiple sclerosis disease-modifying therapies on chronic lesion tissue expansion. *Multiple Sclerosis*, 2026;32(11):1145-1154.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1177/13524585261459228](https://doi.org/10.1177/13524585261459228), PMID [42384032](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42384032/)
- **Evidence level:** 3
- **Question:** [questions/pira.md](../questions/pira.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A retrospective, observational study using linked clinical data from MSBase and the MSBase Imaging
Repository. 564 adults with at least three MRI scans contributed 1,648 stable treatment periods across
7 therapies. The authors compared chronic lesion tissue expansion (CLTE), the slow outward growth of
established lesions, as well as new T2 lesions and brain atrophy, between treatments, with fingolimod
as the comparator, adjusting for demographic, clinical and imaging factors.

## What it found

- B-cell depleting therapy was the only treatment associated with significantly lower CLTE than
  fingolimod (beta = -4.03, p = 0.017).
- CLTE was also independently associated with age, baseline lesion volume and imaging center.

## Limits

- Observational: people were not randomized to treatments, so differences between treatment groups,
  despite adjustment, may explain the result.
- CLTE was associated with imaging center, which shows the measure is sensitive to how scans are
  taken.
- The abstract does not link lower CLTE on B-cell depletion to slower disability or PIRA.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
