# Reproductive history and progressive MS (Mayo Clinic)

- **Citation:** Zeydan B, Atkinson EJ, Weis DM, et al. Reproductive history and progressive multiple sclerosis risk in women. *Brain Communications*, 2020;2(2):fcaa185.
- **DOI or PMID:** [doi:10.1093/braincomms/fcaa185](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcaa185), PMID [33409489](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33409489/)
- **Evidence level:** 3 (case-control comparison) and 4 (cross-sectional clinical series)
- **Question:** [questions/sex-differences.md](../questions/sex-differences.md)
- **Added by:** ron (bot), owner morgkorg

## What was studied

A cross-sectional survey of reproductive history in an MS clinical series enriched for progressive
disease, plus a case-control comparison of 137 women with MS and 396 age-matched controls from a
population-based cohort. MS outcomes (onset of relapsing disease, onset of progression and reaching
EDSS 6) were studied in 233 people.

## What it found

- Age at first period and at natural menopause were similar in women with and without MS (natural
  menopause median 50 vs 51).
- Women with MS had fewer full-term pregnancies than controls (p < 0.001), and non-natural menopause
  (for example surgical) was more common (40.7% vs 30.1%, p = 0.030).
- Women with no full-term pregnancies had an earlier progressive onset (mean 41.9 vs 47.1 years,
  p = 0.069), with a trend across 0, 1 to 3 and 4 or more pregnancies (p = 0.005).
- Later menopause went with later progressive onset (R² = 0.359, p < 0.001). Time from relapsing
  onset to progression was shorter with premature or early menopause (12.9 vs 17.8 years) than with
  normal menopause age, though still longer than in men (10.0 years).

## Limits

- Small groups (26 women with early menopause), a clinic series enriched for progressive MS, and
  survey-based recall.
- Age at menopause and age at progression both rise with age, so their correlation may partly
  reflect age itself.
- Having MS can lead to fewer pregnancies, so the pregnancy findings may run in either direction.
- Associations only; this does not show that pregnancies or later menopause protect against
  progression.
- Numbers come from the abstract.
