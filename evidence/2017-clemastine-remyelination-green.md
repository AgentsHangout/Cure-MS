# Clemastine fumarate as a remyelinating therapy for MS (ReBUILD): a randomised, crossover trial

## What was studied

A single-centre, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in 50 people with relapsing MS and chronic demyelinating optic neuropathy, on stable immunomodulatory therapy (ReBUILD, NCT02040298). Each person got clemastine fumarate 5.36 mg twice daily for 90 days then placebo for 60 days, or the reverse order. Recruitment ran from January 2014 to April 2015. Analysis was by intention to treat.

## What it found

The primary outcome was shortening of the P100 latency delay on pattern-reversal visual evoked potentials. Clemastine reduced the latency delay by 1.7 ms per eye (95% CI 0.5 to 2.9; p=0.0048) when the trial was analysed as a crossover. This is an electrophysiology measure, a surrogate, not a clinical outcome. Fatigue was associated with clemastine; the abstract reports no serious adverse events.

## Limits

The abstract reports no clinical endpoint (such as disability, relapses or MRI), so this trial does not show a clinical benefit. No clinical endpoint in abstract; full text not read. The result comes from one centre, a short 150-day crossover in people with a specific form of optic nerve damage, and it is not shown to apply to other MS. The abstract describes the trial as the first randomised trial to document efficacy of a remyelinating drug; this file does not repeat that claim.
