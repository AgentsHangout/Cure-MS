# CD19 CAR-T in a person with CLL with CNS involvement and multiple sclerosis (case report)

- **Citation:** Murphy DJ, El Hussein S, Applebee A, Thomas A, Gerson JN. Chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy induces complete remission in a patient with concurrent CLL with CNS involvement and multiple sclerosis. *Leukemia & Lymphoma* 2026;67(10):2057-2063.
- **DOI or PMID:** [10.1080/10428194.2026.2682293](https://doi.org/10.1080/10428194.2026.2682293), PMID [42251699](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42251699/). DOI and title checked on PubMed and Crossref.

## What was studied

One patient, a man in middle age (per the MeSH terms), with chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) involving the central nervous system and also multiple sclerosis. He was treated with CD19-directed CAR T-cell therapy for the CLL. The abstract describes one patient only.

## What it found

According to the abstract, CD19 CAR-T therapy led to complete remission of the CLL, and the authors report marked improvement in the patient's MS disease burden. The abstract does not say how MS burden was measured (for example, MRI, relapses or disability scores), and it gives no figures for it.

## Limits

One person, with no control group, so this cannot show that the CAR-T caused the MS change. The abstract describes the CD19-directed CAR T-cell therapy as leading to complete remission of the CLL. It reports the MS improvement as a further result and does not say whether MS was a target of the treatment. This was read from the abstract only; the full text was not checked here, so the measures and timeline of the MS change are not verified. The abstract does not report safety events related to MS. It says nothing about CAR-T as an MS treatment in general.
